The story so far

Always looking ahead

After a 6-11 season that ended in a 14–12 loss to the Raiders, the 2026 Chiefs opened the exhibition slate the way too many recent Augusts have gone: backups on the field, a 20-12 loss to the Rams, and 12 penalties that turned a dress-rehearsal week into a cleanup week. The score is noise. The tape is not. Rookie back Emmett Johnson ran angry, Cyrus Allen stacked a route and a highlight catch, Garrett Nussmeier looked comfortable in his first NFL snaps, and Kahlil Benson gave the offensive line a reason to keep him in the conversation. Steve Spagnuolo’s remade secondary is already producing surprises. What it has not produced yet is a first-team identity — especially the under-center, downhill run game Andy Reid and Eric Bieniemy have been selling since camp opened in St. Joseph.

Tampa is the traditional dress rehearsal. Starters should play a series or two more than they did against Los Angeles. Patrick Mahomes needs a live pocket, the offensive line needs a real front, and Spagnuolo needs to see whether the young defensive backs can communicate when the script is no longer padded. DraftKings has Kansas City -1.5 with a 36.5 total — a preseason number that assumes neither staff is hunting points. Treat it as a practice with officials, not a referendum on the 6-11 hangover.

The through-line is Week 1 against Denver on September 15 at Arrowhead, with a final exhibition versus Seattle on August 29 that will be about cuts, not concepts. Between now and then the Chiefs have $4.2 million in cap space and a tight end room that already looks like a roster problem. Every snap in Tampa that is not a starter evaluation is a bubble evaluation. Johnson, Allen, Nussmeier, Benson, and the young corners are playing for a 53 that has almost no money left to paper over mistakes.

Polymarket has this team at 6.0 percent to win the 2027 Super Bowl and 9.5 percent to come out of the AFC. That is not a window number; that is a prove-it number after 6-11. Saturday does not change the odds. It changes the film Denver will put on the projector. If Reid actually boots off play-action, if the inside zone shows up on early downs, if the secondary holds a Cover-2 shell without a bust — then the opener has a script. If it looks like last Saturday, the market already priced that in.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-center inside zone — the identity snap

Situation 1st-and-10, opening first-team series at Raymond James, 12 or 21 personnel

Reid and Bieniemy have sold a revitalized run game and more under-center work since camp in St. Joseph; the Rams tape opened with the familiar shotgun menu instead. Dress rehearsal is the last chance to put that identity on film before Denver. Emmett Johnson already ran hard and aggressive with the twos. Kahlil Benson’s encouraging opener is the interior tell. A physical Bucs front will not scheme them open — they have to win the first hat.

Coaching point Zone steps, one cut, no bounce. Johnson: trust the track you already showed. Line: combo to the second level and finish; this is a temperament snap, not a scheme snap. Bieniemy should keep Mahomes under center for the first two series so the boot off this look is a real constraint later, not a decoy.

Offense

Play-action boot off the zone fake

Situation 2nd-and-5/6 after a live inside-zone look, first-team 12 personnel, early script

If they actually run the ball under center, the boot is the constraint that makes Tampa’s edge players wrong. Mahomes on the perimeter with an easy read is still this staff’s highest-leverage call. The warning from the Rams night is that Reid’s playcalling looked painfully familiar — if they skip the run fake, Tampa will sit on the shotgun game and this boot dies. Denver will do the same on September 15 if the fake never shows.

Coaching point Same first two steps as the zone. TE leak, flat from the Z, glance for the safety. Mahomes: eyes to the flat defender, throw on time, live for Denver. Do not hunt a shot in an August pocket. If the edge crashes, pull it and take the four-yard throw. That is the win.

Offense

Mesh — Allen sets the rub

Situation 2nd-and-7 vs likely man from Tampa’s sub package, 11 personnel, second first-team series

Reid already said Cyrus Allen knows how to set routes up; mesh is where that skill lives. The Rams highlight catch was the splash. Dress rehearsal is the first time Allen can win on a first-team call against a starting nickel. After 6-11, this offense needs a weekly man-beater that does not require Mahomes to invent. The RB checkdown is the outlet if Tampa’s second-level players jump the crossers.

Coaching point Inside receiver scrapes the hip and does not pick. Allen: sell the stem, sit if the linebacker trails, keep running if they switch. Mahomes throws the first open window — no hero ball on a preseason second down. If they pull the starters, this is also a Nussmeier progressions tape: mesh, sit, checkdown, in that order.

Offense

Four verts vs a single-high shell

Situation 3rd-and-6, first-team third-down or two-minute script vs Tampa Cover-3

Dress-rehearsal third downs are the closest thing to September this staff will allow. If Tampa sits in a single-high shell — standard when a defense is evaluating and not hunting — four verts stresses the deep middle and tells you whether this passing game still has a vertical answer after a 6-11 year. Nussmeier can run a simplified version with the twos once Mahomes is done. Denver will see every seam throw.

Coaching point Seams bend to the hash; outside receivers eat the corners’ cushion. The TE or slot owns the hole player. Mahomes: one hitch, throw the seam or check it down — no late ball over the middle in an August pocket. If they sit him, this is the read Nussmeier already showed he can handle. Protect the football first; the answer is the completion, not the shot.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 shell — evaluate the new pieces

Situation 1st-and-10, Bucs opening first-team script, Chiefs base or nickel

The secondary was remade and is already producing surprises; Cover-2 is the safe shell that lets young corners play with help and rally to tackle. Dress rehearsal on the road is a live look at whether those new pieces can stay in a two-deep without a bust when Tampa motions and shifts. If this is sloppy, Denver will live off missed assignments in Week 1. Two deep, five under, take away the shot, live for the next snap.

Coaching point Corners squat and funnel; safeties split the posts; five under rally and tackle. Spagnuolo: keep it simple early — no late rotations the new DBs have not run at game speed. This is a communication call, not a takeaway call. Do not hunt interceptions in August. Fit the run first; the Bucs will test the edges to see if the young corners abandon leverage.

Defense

Simulated pressure — drop a lineman, rush the second level

Situation 3rd-and-5, Bucs 11 personnel, Spags showing a five-man look

Simulated pressure is Spagnuolo’s signature and the fastest way to evaluate whether a rebuilt secondary can play zone behind a four-man rush that is not four. Tampa’s dress-rehearsal protection will be vanilla; this is about Kansas City communication, not a sack stat. If a young DB fails to replace the dropping lineman, that is a Week 1 problem against Denver’s quick game. The 12-penalty opener already showed sloppy operation — this call punishes sloppy communication even harder.

Coaching point Show five, drop the end or a tackle into the hook, bring a backer from the second level. The replacement defender has to be in the window before the ball comes out. Safeties: do not bite play-action; the blitz is the run fit. Call it, do not freestyle it. Young corners play their zone landmark, not the quarterback’s eyes. Survive the snap, then tag the busts on Monday.

The storylines

What we’re watching

RB bubble

Emmett Johnson ran like the job was open

The rookie back operated mostly with the second unit against the Rams and still forced his way onto every winners list: hard, aggressive, one-cut temperament. Dress rehearsal is the first time he might share a series with the starters, and that is the look Denver will actually study. If Bieniemy is serious about a downhill identity, Johnson just auditioned to be the early-down answer.

WR rook

Cyrus Allen already speaks Reid’s language

Reid’s postgame note was specific: Allen knows how to set routes up. That is not a compliment you give a highlight-reel catch; that is a compliment you give a guy who can live in mesh, choice, and third-down spacing. Tampa is the first time opposing corners will see him on a scripted first-team series. The catch was the splash. The stems are the roster spot.

QB2

Garrett Nussmeier’s debut was the quiet win

The 2026 draft class got its first live snaps in red and gold, and Nussmeier handled the moment. Preseason Week 3 is when the backup job stops being theoretical — Mahomes will come out, the pocket will get dirty, and someone has to run the two-minute. A clean second half in Tampa is how you avoid a quarterback controversy of the worst kind: nobody you trust behind 15.

Flags

Twelve penalties is the actual story of Week 1

Tried-and-true fans know the 20-12 final is meaningless. Twelve flags are not. Pre-snap, holds, and wasted downs against the Rams turned a evaluation night into a lecture. Tampa’s officials will not be friendlier, and Denver will not be. Clean operation is the only stat that travels from Raymond James to Arrowhead.

Secondary

The DB room is already producing surprises

Kansas City remade much of the defense, and the secondary shakeup is the loudest camp story that actually showed up on tape. Young players are being thrust into vital roles; Spagnuolo is going to find out on the road whether those surprises are real. Cover-2 and simulated pressure only work if the new pieces speak the same language on first and second sound.

Identity

The run-game reset still has not shown up

Camp talk was under-center, downhill, a revitalized run game. The Rams opener started with more of the same familiar Reid menu. That is the most important coaching tell heading to Tampa. If the first two first-team series are shotgun empty again, believe the tape, not the quotes — and assume Denver will.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid’s call sheet vs. the camp quote

The Rams night looked painfully familiar for a staff that has spent camp talking under-center and downhill. Dress rehearsal is the last time Reid can put that identity on a first-team script before Denver films it. If the opening series is shotgun empty again, believe the tape.

Bieniemy’s run-game installation

This is the offensive coordinator’s tell. Inside zone and the boot off it are how you find out whether Emmett Johnson and the interior line are a plan or a press-conference slogan. Keep Mahomes under center long enough for the fake to matter.

Spagnuolo simplifying for a new secondary

The room was remade; surprises are already showing up. Week 3 is not the week to layer late rotations. Cover-2 and tagged simulated pressures let the young pieces play fast. Communication errors in Tampa become Denver explosives.

Mahomes snap-count management

Traditional dress rehearsal is two to three series plus a two-minute. After a 6-11 year the temptation is to chase rhythm; the job is to leave Raymond James healthy. Nussmeier’s debut bought Reid the right to sit 15 earlier than pride would like.

Penalty cleanup as a staff problem

Twelve flags are not a player-only issue. Pre-snap operation, substitution timing, and hold discipline have to be coached this week or they will travel to Seattle and then to the opener. Officials will not give August a pass twice.

Roster moves

Personnel

  • Garrett Nussmeier pushing the QB2 conversation

    A clean NFL debut is not a job won, but it is a job opened. Dress rehearsal is when the backup has to run a live two-minute after Mahomes sits. That tape matters more than the Rams cameo.

  • Emmett Johnson vs. the early-down role

    He ran hard and aggressive with the seconds. The next ask is a first-team series under center. If the run identity is real, this is how a rookie back forces September snaps.

  • Cyrus Allen as a route-skill WR, not just a splash play

    Reid’s compliment was about setting routes up, not the highlight catch. Mesh, third down, and the last WR spots on a tight 53 are the actual audition in Tampa.

  • Kahlil Benson staying in the OL conversation

    The sloppy-but-promising opener flagged Benson as an encouraging interior piece. Dress-rehearsal run downs are the first time that evaluation happens next to the starters.

  • Young secondary competing through a remade room

    The shakeup is already producing surprises. Road communication against Tampa’s first-team motion will decide which of those surprises are on the 53 and which are practice-squad hopes.

  • $4.2 million in cap space and a thin tight end room

    As of August 16 the club is working with about $4.2 million. That is not enough to paper over a bubble miss, and the tight end room has already been flagged as a sneaky roster problem. Tampa and Seattle are the last free looks before the cut.

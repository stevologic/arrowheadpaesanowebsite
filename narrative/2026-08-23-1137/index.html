The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Inside zone — stay on schedule

Situation 1st down / 2nd & short

Early-down efficiency keeps the play-action and boot menu honest and the offense out of obvious passing downs — where opponents want it.

Coaching point One cut, downhill — let the double-teams move the line and take the four yards every time.

Offense

Under-center boot — the early-down answer

Situation 1st & 10 off a run look vs. Seattle Seahawks

Moves the launch point away from the rush and gives a defined half-field read — the cheapest explosive in the menu once the run game has landed.

Coaching point The fake has to match the run action on tape or the backside end never bites.

Offense

4 verticals vs. Cover 3

Situation 2nd & medium, spread look

Stretches the deep zones and forces the safety to choose — a staple answer when the defense sits back.

Coaching point Bend the seams to the hash and read the safety's leverage.

Offense

Mesh vs. man

Situation 3rd & short/medium

Rub-heavy crossers beat man coverage and give a clean, quick answer with an RB checkdown as the outlet.

Coaching point Set the mesh depth tight so the pick is legal and natural.

Defense

Simulated pressure on the money down

Situation 3rd & 5-8, protection stressed

Five-man picture, four-man rush: force the hot throw into a capped window without selling out the coverage behind it.

Coaching point The dropper must land in the throwing lane the protection leaves open — that's where the hot ball goes.

Defense

Spagnuolo Cover-2 shell

Situation Protect a lead / obvious passing down

Takes the top off, rallies to tackle, and dares the offense to be patient — the situational change-up.

Coaching point Sink the corners, funnel everything to the deep halves.

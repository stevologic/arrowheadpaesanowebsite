The story so far

Always looking ahead

The 2025 hangover is still in the building: first losing season of the Patrick Mahomes era, first under Andy Reid in Kansas City, and a roster that spent the spring mostly running it back. Training camp at Missouri Western is not a victory lap — it is a repair shop. Day 8 went light after three straight padded practices, and the defensive message was blunt: there is a long way to go. That is the honest frame for a fanbase that watched eight of the final games slip away and now has to watch Brett Veach and the staff prove the nightmare was a one-year glitch, not a new baseline.

The clearest structural edge on the roster still lives on the interior. First-round rookie Peter Woods has already framed the assignment the right way — playing next to Chris Jones is a daily masterclass in how offenses are forced to slide protection, chip, and abandon clean seven-man rules. Jones landing at No. 43 on the NFL Top 100 is a reminder he remains the gravity piece; Woods’ development is how Spagnuolo gets back to inventing numbers in the pocket instead of just surviving early downs. If that pair hits, the rest of the pressure package — simulated pressures, second-level add-ons — suddenly looks like 2023–24 again instead of last year’s chase-from-behind tape.

Everyone else is auditioning for the holes that actually sank 2025. The wide receiver depth chart is the same old refrain: talent without a true separator, and enough smoke that names like Andrew Armstrong and Cyrus Allen are getting real camp oxygen while trade chatter around veterans like Calvin Ridley should be treated as noise. Tight end has been quiet in free agency — which is why Will Dissly’s cold market keeps resurfacing in fan circles — and the linebacker room still has to answer the speed question that has aged the defense in space. Special teams is not exempt: Harrison Butker, after nine misses a year ago, is openly tweaking his routine because a bounce-back season for the kicker is non-negotiable on a team that will live in one-score games again.

Zoom out and the market is not buying a straight-line return to the throne — and it should not. A 6.7% championship price and 9.5% AFC price on Polymarket is the sober read after 6-11: Mahomes and Reid travel, but the supporting cast has to prove it in August, not in January nostalgia. The stadium branding flip back to plain Arrowhead Stadium for 2026 is pure signal to the base; the football signal has to come from the 53-man cutdowns, Mansoor Delane’s recovery timeline, and whether the defense Spagnuolo is installing can close space before the opener. Everything from here is path-to-September, not parade talk.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Jones/Woods A-Gap Steal → Boot Away

Situation Camp install / early-down 1st-and-10 looking ahead to Week 1 scripts when defenses overload the interior

With Peter Woods earning snaps next to Chris Jones, offenses will start sliding toward the duo. Reid’s counter is under-center play-action boot away from the slide, putting Mahomes on the perimeter with the TE and backside WR in easy ladder reads while the interior gravity of Jones/Woods sells the run fake.

Coaching point Mahomes must sell the mesh point half-second longer; the TE (whatever camp winner holds the Y) cannot drift — flat then raise. Woods’ job on the fake is to stay tight so the Mike does not free-release into the boot lane.

Offense

Seam Stress for the New Outside WRs

Situation 3rd-and-6 vs. Cover-3 teams that sat on intermediate in 2025

If Armstrong or Allen is going to prove the Diggs-like separator talk, it shows up here: four verticals that bend the seams and force the free safety to pick a hash. Mahomes’ 2025 tape died when defenses could bracket without respecting a true vertical threat off the line.

Coaching point Outside WRs must win release vs. press — no wasted steps. Seam stem from the slot/TE has to hold the safety until the break of the hash; Bieniemy’s timing rule is Mahomes throws landmark, not body.

Offense

Mesh to Expose LB Speed

Situation 2nd-and-7 vs. man or match looks — the exact leverage test for KC’s linebacker question

Arrowhead Addict’s linebacker speed question is not abstract: mesh crossers are how opposing coordinators will hunt slow backers all year. Installing it cleanly on offense also teaches Spagnuolo’s unit what the rub looks like before they face it.

Coaching point Inside crosser must flatten at 6 yards and not raise early; outside corner/sit combo clears the backer. RB outlet is live if the Mike trails — Mahomes’ eyes go rub first, check second, never force into the trail tech.

Offense

Easy Button vs. the Jones Slide

Situation 2nd-and-long when opponents commit extra to Jones/Woods and leave the perimeter soft

Aggressive slides toward Chris Jones and Peter Woods create the exact numbers advantage for an RB screen. Emari Demarcado’s camp identity — niche specialist who does the little things — fits the lead-block-and-catch role if the perimeter WRs sustain the first block.

Coaching point Guard and tackle must get to the second level without holding; Demarcado’s path is outside the tackle’s hip, not east-west dancing. Mahomes’ fake punch has to freeze the end one count or the screen dies in the backfield.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 Rally Rule

Situation Early downs defending 12p play-action after three straight padded days exposed pursuit gaps

Day 8’s lighter work still carried the theme that the defense has a long way to go. Cover-2 is Spagnuolo’s cleanest answer while linebacker speed is sorted: two deep take away the shot, five under must finish tackles — exactly where 2025 leaked explosives.

Coaching point Corners squat and funnel; safeties split the posts and drive on the first throw window. Linebackers cannot catch-and-drive late — the coaching point from Spags is hit the landmark on the three-step, then rally. Delane’s availability timeline makes the nickel depth part of this conversation for Week 1.

Defense

Simulated Heat Off Jones’ Gravity

Situation 3rd-and-5+ when protection slides to Jones and Woods occupies the other A-gap

Woods explained the priceless part himself: Jones draws the plan. That is when Spagnuolo’s signature simulated pressure — show five or six, drop a lineman, send a backer — wins without needing a pure edge ace. It is also the scheme answer while the linebacker speed rebuild is incomplete.

Coaching point The dropped end must get depth into the throwing lane Mahomes loves on scramble drills; the add-on backer times the snap off the guard’s set, not a guess. If the Mike is a step slow this camp, Spags will tag the faster nickel instead — that is the Week 1 roster tell.

The storylines

What we’re watching

DL Duo

Woods–Jones is the real camp dual threat

Peter Woods has been blunt: reps beside Chris Jones are a priceless advantage because offenses cannot ignore the veteran’s interior wreckage. If Woods wins early-down snaps, Spagnuolo can return to pure chaos on passing downs instead of manufactured pressure that arrives a half-beat late.

WR Search

Armstrong, Allen, and the missing separator

The receiver room still carries last year’s questions. Andrew Armstrong is being cast as a possible answer to what the group lacked, while Cyrus Allen has drawn internal Stefon Diggs comparisons for how he works. That is camp hype until it shows up in the red zone and on third-and-seven.

Kicker

Butker’s routine reset

Nine misses made Harrison Butker one of the highest-stakes names on the roster. He is tweaking his process in St. Joe because Reid’s offense still needs a closer, not a question mark, when Mahomes drives stall at the 35.

LB Speed

The linebacker question that shapes the defense

Spagnuolo’s best units fly downhill and erase boots and mesh crossers. The lingering speed issue at linebacker is the difference between Cover-2 rallies that finish and explosive plays that defined the 2025 tape.

Brand

Arrowhead is Arrowhead again

The club and G.E.H.A. are restoring the Arrowhead Stadium name for 2026, dropping the field title from the marquees. It is a fan-facing win while the football product still has to earn the noise back.

Polymarket is not buying the dynasty default

Chiefs sit at 6.7% to win the 2027 champion market and 9.5% to take the AFC. That is underdog pricing after 6-11 — respect for Mahomes, skepticism that the supporting cast is fixed.

Coaching & adjustments

Spagnuolo’s patience vs. the calendar

Spags has already flagged that Mansoor Delane still has plenty of work despite progress — a direct Week 1 availability cloud. The lighter Day 8 practice after three padded sessions was mandatory, but the public ‘long way to go’ line is the defensive staff setting a standard, not a panic.

Reid / Bieniemy skill-gap problem

Running back the receiver group puts the burden on camp risers (Armstrong, Allen) and scheme simplicity — boots, screens, and mesh — until a separator emerges. Forcing a Calvin Ridley-type trade would be solving optics, not the release-and-separation issue on tape.

Special teams accountability

Butker openly tweaking his routine after nine misses is the staff treating the kicker as a high-stakes roster piece, not an automatic. Reid’s late-game aggression only works if the long kick is live again.

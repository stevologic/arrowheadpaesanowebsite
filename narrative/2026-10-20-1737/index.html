Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

5-0

The Chiefs are 5-0, ranked No. 2 in Arrowhead Pride’s Week 7 sampling, and priced as a road underdog. The offense can move the ball — 422 against the Chargers, and 399 and 189 rushing yards in 24:18 at Las Vegas the week before (ESPN) — and it is not finishing at the same rate.

What we need to work on

Red-zone finishes

Arrowhead Pride flagged the red-zone offense after 422 yards became 22 points. The 2-yard touchdown cashed. The snap at the 1, Tonga eligible, did not. Seattle laying 2.5 means four-point swings from a stalled series matter.

Third-and-manageable answers

Kansas City was 3-of-10 on third down against Los Angeles. Worthy, Kelce, Gray, Allen, and Royals are the catch distribution. Bieniemy needs a mesh or screen that does not require a 23-of-30 heater every series.

Special teams details

Arrowhead Addict’s Week 6 special-teams piece and the Remigio column match the film: missed extra point, missed 52-yard try, and a return that did not survive review. The returner job is a staff question before Lumen’s crowd.

Penalty discipline on the money plays

Gillotte’s roughing wiped a Roland-Wallace interception. Fulton’s pass interference and Jones’ roughing came late. Spagnuolo cannot trade nine hits and four Herbert sacks for flags that hand Seattle hidden yards.

What to think about

How much Alohi Gilman plays

Arrowhead Addict notes Gilman went from 90 percent of defensive snaps in Week 4 to 49 percent in Week 6. That is a secondary shift before a road game in which a 57-yard Johnston catch already showed a vertical leak.

Whether the under-center look is the identity

Arrowhead Pride reports Mahomes has taken to the under-center offense. Walker’s 128 yards support staying on that path. The question is whether boot and inside zone are the Seattle script, or whether shotgun volume returns when the red zone tightens.

Trade-deadline noise versus the 5-0 room

Columns on Chargers sellers, Cam Thomas, and Chris Ballard are conversation, not transactions. The live roster question is Armstrong and Allen’s snap share, per the Week 6 snap-count pieces, and whether Kelce’s ankle tweak changes the tight-end plan.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Seattle Seahawks

Kansas City goes to Seattle on Sun Oct 25, 7:20 PM CT, NBC, at 5-0 and as a 2.5-point underdog on a 42.5 total. FPI has the Chiefs at 52.8 percent. The assignment is to cash Walker’s under-center runs, finish drives that looked like the 422-yard afternoon, and keep explosives in a cover-2 and zone-blitz plan.

How they match up

Kansas City brings a 150-yard rushing afternoon, a quarterback who was not sacked, and a defense that held a division opponent to 73 rushing yards and 6 points. Seattle brings home field and the points. The Chiefs’ leverage is early-down runs and a pass rush that produced four sacks and nine hits against Herbert. The soft spot is finishing — 3-of-10 on third down, a failed fourth down at the 1, a missed extra point — and the vertical leak Johnston exploited for 57. Gilman’s reduced snap share is the personnel variable in the back end.

Stay on Walker early

Nineteen carries and 128 yards earned another early-down diet. Inside zone under center, with Emmett Johnson (five touches) as the changeup, keeps Mahomes out of third-and-long after a 3-of-10 day.

Use Worthy and Kelce as the move-the-chains pair

Worthy’s seven catches for 73 and Kelce’s four catches are the trusted outlets. Allen’s 23-yard catch is a shot to keep in the script if Seattle squeezes the hashes. Armstrong’s one-target interception is a reminder to be careful with the third wideout on short-right throws.

Rush without the flag

Karlaftis, Gillotte, Conner, and Jones can heat the pocket the way they did to Herbert. The coaching constraint is the roughing calls.

Two-deep against the shot

Johnston’s 57-yarder is the teaching clip. Cover-2, rally to tackle, and do not let a similar explosive flip a 2.5-point game.

  1. Open under center with Walker on inside zone and keep Johnson in for a change-of-pace carry on second down.
  2. Call a play-action boot off that look for Kelce or Worthy on the perimeter once Seattle flows to Walker.
  3. On third-and-medium, mesh with a running-back checkdown rather than another Armstrong isolation after the Hart interception.
  4. Steal a screen for Walker or Johnson if the Seattle rush declares, the same idea that fits a quarterback who took three hits and zero sacks against Los Angeles.
  5. Sit in cover-2 on early downs and rally Bolton and Conner to the catch.
  6. Call a zone blitz on a Seattle passing down: show heat, drop a lineman, rush the second level.

Los Angeles Chargers Week 6 · Sun, Oct 18 · 3:25 PM CT
KC 22–6

At Arrowhead on Sun Oct 18, 3:25 PM CT, Kansas City beat the Chargers 22-6 and moved to 5-0. The Chiefs’ box was 22 first downs, 150 rushing yards, 272 net passing yards, 422 total yards, 3-of-10 on third down, two turnovers, 11 drives, and 30:26 of possession. Los Angeles had 15 first downs, 73 rushing yards, 186 net passing yards, 259 total yards, 5-of-16 on third down, two turnovers, 12 drives, and 29:34.

Walker was the offense. Nineteen rushes for 128 yards and two touchdowns, plus two catches, 21 touches. The 33-yard score at Q1 7:21 came on a shotgun run up the middle. Earlier he had a 13-yard run up the middle and a left-end gain of 2. Later he ripped a left-end 11, a left-end 13, and a 16-yard run up the middle. The goal-line failure sits next to that tape: at Q3 6:34, with K. Tonga reported eligible, Walker went left guard to the 1 for no gain. That is a red-zone stall.

Mahomes was efficient and untouched in the sack column: 23-of-30, 272 yards, rating 89.9, zero sacks, three hits, five rushes, one interception. Xavier Worthy led the receivers with seven catches for 73 yards. Travis Kelce had four catches. Noah Gray had three. Cyrus Allen had two, including a 23-yarder to the Los Angeles 49 after the Tranquill recovery. Jalen Royals had two. Andrew Armstrong had one catch and was the intended receiver on the C. Hart interception that became a touchback. Creed Humphrey’s fumble on a shotgun snap was recovered by Mahomes, who then found Kelce for 16. The box still lists two Kansas City turnovers; the listed interception is the Hart play.

The defensive scoreboard was field goals, and the disruption was specific. Cochrane’s interception of a short-right throw intended for Lambert-Smith, with A. Gillotte in the rush notation, went 48 yards to the Los Angeles 17. A separate Roland-Wallace interception was wiped by Ashton Gillotte’s roughing-the-passer penalty and is not a takeaway. At Q4 9:50, L. Sneed forced the Lambert-Smith fumble and Tranquill recovered at the Kansas City 38. A punt-return fumble involving Nikko Remigio and T. Dye was reversed, with the recovery ruled to Los Angeles’ C. Murphy. Chamarri Conner was credited with one sack. Herbert was sacked four times. Kansas City recorded nine quarterback hits. Conner also tackled Herbert on a 7-yard scramble. Bolton’s 11 tackles are the volume play in the fit.

Special teams and flags kept 422 from becoming a larger margin. Butker missed the extra point wide left after the 33-yard touchdown and missed a 52-yard field goal wide right. Harrison Butker made field goals of 44, 50, and 30 yards. Penalties included Kelce’s illegal block above the waist, Kingsley Suamataia holding that wiped a play, Kristian Fulton’s defensive pass interference, and Chris Jones’ roughing the passer. Gillotte’s roughing call cost a would-be interception. That is sloppy around a clean sack line for Mahomes.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Kansas City is 5-0 because the Chargers afternoon was controlled, not because the scoreboard matched the yardage. The Chiefs posted 22 first downs, 150 rushing yards on 27 attempts, 272 net passing yards, and 422 total yards in 30:26, and they still walked off 22-6. Kenneth Walker III carried the rushing line — 19 carries, 128 yards, two touchdowns, 21 touches — while Patrick Mahomes went 23-of-30 for 272 yards, a rating of 89.9, zero sacks, and one interception, with three quarterback hits on him.

The score sequence is the film. A 44-yard field goal at Q1 11:20 made it 3-0. Walker’s 33-yard touchdown at Q1 7:21 made it 9-0 after Harrison Butker’s extra point went wide left. Los Angeles answered with a 23-yard field goal at Q1 1:33, 9-3. A 2-yard touchdown at Q2 1:49 (Kansas City 16, Los Angeles 3), a 50-yard field goal at Q2 0:02 (Kansas City 19, Los Angeles 3), a Chargers 30-yard field goal at Q3 10:44 (Kansas City 19, Los Angeles 6), and a 30-yard field goal at Q4 3:52 closed it at 22-6. Los Angeles’ points on the scoring table were those two field goals.

Arrowhead Pride’s red-zone read is the right one: 422 yards, the second-most in a game this season behind the 523 in the overtime win over Indianapolis (ESPN 401872945), did not become a blowout because finishes stalled. Third down was 3-of-10. A fourth-down snap at the 1, with K. Tonga reported eligible, ended with Walker left guard for no gain. Butker also missed a 52-yard field goal wide right. That is the tape Andy Reid and Eric Bieniemy take to Seattle.

Steve Spagnuolo’s defense did its job on this opponent. Los Angeles managed 15 first downs, 73 rushing yards on 22 attempts, 186 net passing yards, 259 total yards, and 29:34 of possession across 12 drives. Justin Herbert was 22-of-37 with four sacks, a 63.3 rating, and five rushes. Kansas City recorded nine quarterback hits. Jack Cochrane intercepted him, and L. Sneed forced the KeAndre Lambert-Smith fumble that Drue Tranquill recovered. Nick Bolton’s 11 tackles set a franchise mark for 10-plus-tackle games. The leak was still real: Quentin Johnston’s 57-yard catch put Los Angeles on the 1.

National rankings have Kansas City at No. 2, per Arrowhead Pride’s Week 7 roundup, and Mahomes has matched Tom Brady for fastest to 100 career wins. The market has not joined the coronation. Seattle lays 2.5 at home on Sun Oct 25, 7:20 PM CT, NBC, on a 42.5 total, while FPI has Kansas City at 52.8 percent. The job is to turn the under-center run game Mahomes has taken to into points, and to carry a cover-2 and zone-blitz plan that does not give up the explosive Seattle will hunt.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-center inside zone for Walker

Situation 1st-and-10 early at Seattle, after a 19-carry, 128-yard afternoon and a left-guard stop at the 1 that cannot be the goal-line answer

Walker and Humphrey just moved a defense for 150 team rushing yards; Bieniemy should keep the under-center downhill look Mahomes has taken to, and use Johnson on the changeup so 21 touches do not become a worn path.

Coaching point Zone steps, one cut, declare the point. Walker presses the play-side hip and gets north. Johnson enters on the second early down so Seattle cannot sit on 19 Walker carries.

Offense

Boot off the Walker action

Situation 2nd-and-6 to 2nd-and-8 after a successful inside zone, with Kelce (four catches) and Worthy (seven for 73) as the perimeter answers

This is an install off the run Seattle will see on film. Mahomes on the perimeter gives Kelce the easy flat and Worthy the deep out without asking Armstrong to win a short-right isolation.

Coaching point Sell Walker, move the pocket, eyes to Kelce then Worthy. If both are capped, scramble rules apply — Mahomes had five rushes and a 10-yard left-end gain on the Chargers tape.

Offense

Mesh versus man on third down

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 3rd-and-7 after Kansas City went 3-of-10, with the Hart interception on an Armstrong target still fresh

Rub routes for Worthy and Kelce, with Royals or Allen as the sit, give Mahomes a defined read instead of another one-on-one to Armstrong. Gray’s three catches are the extra tight-end rub if Kelce’s snap count dips after the ankle tweak.

Coaching point Crossers must actually cross. Worthy sets the pick depth, Kelce bends under it, Johnson or Walker is the checkdown if the linebacker triggers.

Offense

Walker screen when the rush declares

Situation 2nd-and-long or 3rd-and-6 if Seattle sells out, after Mahomes took three hits and zero sacks against Los Angeles

A screen to Walker (two catches) or Johnson (two catches) punishes a rush that has to chase a 23-of-30 quarterback. It also keeps the ball in the 21-touch back’s hands without another interior plunge at the 1.

Coaching point Humphrey and the play-side tackle release to the alley. Mahomes hitch and throw. Walker catches on the move; do not dance. Johnson is the same picture from a pistol offset if Walker is gassed.

Defense

Cover-2 shell against the shot

Situation 1st-and-10 and 2nd-and-long at Seattle, taught off Quentin Johnston’s 57-yard catch that put Los Angeles on the 1

Two deep, five under takes the free vertical away and lets Bolton, coming off 11 tackles, and Conner rally. Sneed can still trigger downhill the way he did on the forced fumble, without asking Fulton to gamble through another pass-interference flag.

Coaching point Corners sink to the deep half. Conner and Bolton own the hooks. No hero ball on the hash. The Johnston clip is the why, and the tackle is the finish.

Defense

Zone blitz on a Seattle passing down

Situation 3rd-and-6 to 3rd-and-10, show pressure and drop a lineman

Herbert was sacked four times and hit nine times in a different game. The teaching point for Seattle is the picture: Karlaftis and Gillotte can present heat while a second-level rusher arrives, and a dropped lineman takes the hot throw Bolton just spent an afternoon tackling.

Coaching point Declare the simulated pressure, drop the named lineman into the hook, and rush the second level. Gillotte cannot turn the rush into another roughing penalty.

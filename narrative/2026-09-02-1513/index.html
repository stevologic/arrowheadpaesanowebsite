What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

T
Seattle Seahawks Pre Week 4 · Sat Aug 29
KC 9–9

Kansas City closed the preseason at Arrowhead in a 9–9 grind that looked like a run-game clinic and a red-zone stall. The Chiefs outgained Seattle 353–266, won first downs 21–13, and held the ball for 33:12, but the only points they put up were Harrison Butker kicks from 28, 24, and 19. Seattle answered with a Jason Myers 46-yarder and a 4-yard Jalen Milroe touchdown to Nick Vannett after a missed extra point, and the night ended in a draw that told you more about identity than about the depth chart’s ceiling.

The offensive story was stay-ahead football. Garrett Nussmeier went 22-of-34 for 198 yards and an interception — a 5.8-yard average that does not scare anyone vertically, but it kept Eric Bieniemy’s call sheet on schedule. Kansas City converted 9-of-17 third downs and posted 21 first downs because the run game was real: 155 yards on the ground, with Brashard Smith’s 16-for-72 night as the featured cut. When you run it that often and still never find the end zone, the issue is not volume. It is the last eight yards. Three Butker makes from inside the 30 are a kicker doing his job and an offense settling.

Seattle’s counterpunch was small-sample and specific. The Seahawks managed only 54 rushing yards and 13 first downs, and they were 4-of-11 on third down, which is a Spagnuolo win even in a preseason finale. The damage came in the red area: Milroe’s 4-yard toss to Vannett in the third quarter, then a missed PAT that kept the door open. Kansas City’s lone sack belonged to R. Mason Thomas. That is a useful data point for a front Veach is restocking, but it is not a finished pass-rush tape. The Chiefs forced one turnover and gave one back — Nussmeier’s interception — and the night stayed stuck in field-goal range.

Andrew Armstrong’s 5 catches for 54 yards were the chain-moving version of this offense: hitch, in-cut, stay on the sticks. That is how you post 353 yards and still score 9 points. For a club coming off 6-11, the encouraging piece is the possession profile and the run identity. The discouraging piece is that none of it cashed as a touchdown against a Seattle group that was also playing out the preseason string. Denver will treat those 19- and 24-yard field-goal drives as losses on downs.

What held up

  • Inside-zone volume and Brashard Smith’s 16-for-72 workload
  • Third-down conversion rate at 9-of-17 and 21 first downs
  • Time of possession (33:12) and a defense that held Seattle to 54 rush yards
  • Harrison Butker’s three makes from 28, 24, and 19
  • R. Mason Thomas generating the night’s sack

What broke

  • Zero offensive touchdowns despite 353 total yards
  • Red-zone trips turning into chip-shot field goals instead of six
  • Nussmeier’s interception in a one-possession preseason close
  • Explosive-pass absence: 198 net passing yards on 34 attempts
  • Failure to punish Seattle after taking a 6–3 lead into halftime

The run tape is the identity

One hundred fifty-five rushing yards, 33:12 of possession, and 21 first downs is a Bieniemy call sheet that wants to live in 2nd-and-6. Brashard Smith’s 16-carry night is the proof of concept. If that does not travel to Week 1 with the starters, the rest of the script is noise.

Finishing is the actual problem

Butker from 28, 24, and 19 means Kansas City repeatedly won the field-position fight and lost the scoring fight. A 9–9 final with 353 yards is not bad luck. It is a red-zone and goal-line issue that Denver will make you answer on the first possession.

The front won the ugly downs

Seattle’s 54 rushing yards and 4-of-11 third-down night is the Spagnuolo half of the ledger. R. Mason Thomas’s sack is a cameo, not a rotation, but it fits the post-cutdown bet Veach is making on the interior and the edge.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

6-11

The 53 is mostly written, the practice squad is up, and the only game left on the wall is Denver. Kansas City is still a 6-11 club in the standings that matter, with a preseason close that proved the run game and a cutdown week that proved Veach is betting the defensive line. From here to September 15 the work is integration, not discovery.

What we need to work on

Red-zone rules with the starters

The Seattle tape is 21 first downs and three field goals. Bieniemy has to decide which goal-line personnel actually punches it in before Mahomes takes a live red-zone snap against Denver.

Get Eason and Pyburn into Spagnuolo’s calls

The two 49ers castoffs are not camp ornaments. If they are on the 53 to be defensive-line insurance, they have to know the simulated-pressure rules before an ESPN opener, not after the first series.

Protect the run identity without abandoning the chain

One hundred fifty-five rushing yards only matter if the offensive line — the position group Arrowhead Pride readers are already asking about — can recreate those zone steps against a real AFC West front.

What to think about

Is the 53 a trench-first document or a depth-chart compromise?

Veach claimed two defensive linemen, kept a third quarterback, and left a crowded backfield plus a calculated running-back cut on the table. That is a philosophy. It is also a lot of unproven Week 1 math.

What does a -3 home favorite actually buy you after 6-11?

ESPN FPI at 58.5 percent and DraftKings at Kansas City -3 are Arrowhead taxes plus the run tape. They are not a Super Bowl number — Polymarket still has the Chiefs at 6.2 percent to win the 2027 title and 9.5 percent to win the AFC.

Who is the featured back when the games count?

Brashard Smith just earned the last-game workload, Nate Carter landed on a practice squad that already had bodies, and Kansas City took a calculated risk moving on from Emari Demercado. Somebody has to take the first-down carries against Denver.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Week 1 at Arrowhead on ESPN is the first snap that counts: a division rival, a national window, and a three-point home number that only cashes if the Seattle run identity and the new front both survive a real AFC West night.

How they match up

Kansas City wants to live in possession football — inside zone, play-action off the boot, screens when the rush overruns — because that is the only tape they just posted. Denver, as the visitor in an AFC West opener, will try to turn this into a negative-plays game: wreck early downs, force Mahomes off schedule, and make a 6-11 club prove it can finish drives it could not finish against Seattle. The leverage is the trenches. If Bieniemy’s zone game gets to 2nd-and-6, Spagnuolo can hunt. If Kansas City is in 2nd-and-11, the new waiver-wire pieces are playing from behind on national television.

Run it to call play-action, not to hide

The 155-yard night is only useful if the opening script against Denver looks like under-center zone and then a boot. Mahomes on the move is the point of the identity, not a changeup.

Keep Denver in long yardage

Seattle managed 54 rush yards and 4-of-11 on third down. That is the blueprint. Spagnuolo’s Cover-2 shell plus simulated pressure has to make the Broncos beat them underneath while the new front finds the football.

Finish the drive you already won

Three Butker kicks from inside the 30 is a scouting report Denver will pin to the board. Mesh, sneak-adjacent personnel, and a defined goal-line back have to be on the call sheet before the first red-zone snap.

Do not donate the night

Kansas City’s one interception kept a 9–9 game alive. Against a division opponent on ESPN, that is how a -3 favorite becomes a 6-11 flashback.

  1. Open 11/12 personnel under center: inside zone to the closed side, two plays in a row, and make Denver’s linebackers step downhill.
  2. Second series: play-action boot off the same zone look and get Mahomes on the perimeter with the tight end in the flat and the backside post as the shot.
  3. Third-and-medium: spacing screen to the featured back (Smith earned the last-game volume) if Denver’s edge is flying upfield.
  4. Red zone: mesh versus man with a sit/corner combo — no more 19-yard field-goal psychology.
  5. Defensively, start in a Cover-2 shell on early downs to take away the shot and rally to the run, then mix a zone blitz on 2nd-and-7-plus with a lineman dropping.
  6. If the new pieces (Eason inside, Pyburn off the edge) are active, use them as the simulated-pressure decoys so the veterans can rush clean.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The 2025 hangover is still the record — 6-11 — but the last tape Kansas City put on the field was a statement about who they want to be, not who they were. Against Seattle they won the down-and-distance math, sat on the football for 33:12, and ran for 155 yards, then settled for three Harrison Butker field goals in a 9–9 preseason close. That is an identity without a finishing kick. The 48 hours after the whistle were the other half of the story: the 53 got shaken, two 49ers castoffs landed on the defensive line, and Andy Reid’s club started pointing every snap toward a Tuesday-night ESPN opener against Denver.

What last season proved is that talent at quarterback is not a scheme. Patrick Mahomes can still tilt a Sunday, but a 6-11 club does not get fixed by hoping the deep ball comes back online in September. Eric Bieniemy’s room showed a downhill answer in the finale — Brashard Smith’s 16 carries for 72 yards were the cleanest tape of the night — and Steve Spagnuolo’s defense kept Seattle to 54 rushing yards and 4-of-11 on third down. The problem is the part that decides real games: Kansas City drove into scoring range and left with threes. Denver will not gift you that math.

The roster document now matches the tape more than the 6-11 autopsy did. Brett Veach used cutdown week as a defensive-line rewrite, then hit the waiver wire for rookie tackle Bryson Eason and edge/linebacker Jack Pyburn after San Francisco’s cut, while Garrett Nussmeier beat out Chris Oladokun for the third quarterback job and the No. 19 jersey. That is not a finished championship roster — Polymarket still has Kansas City at 6.2 percent to win the 2027 title — but it is a club choosing trenches and a run identity before it chooses nostalgia.

The next Sunday that matters is already on the wall: Broncos, Arrowhead, ESPN. ESPN FPI gives Kansas City a 58.5 percent nod and DraftKings has them -3 at home, which is a vote for the building and the ground tape, not a coronation. From here to kickoff the work is specific — install the inside-zone rules with the starters, get the new front communicating in Spagnuolo’s simulated pressures, and decide whether a crowded backfield and a still-debated offensive line can protect a script that has to finish in the red zone. Denver is the first exam, not a dress rehearsal.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Inside zone, closed side

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script vs. Denver at Arrowhead — impose the Seattle run identity before the Broncos can pin their ears back.

Kansas City just ran for 155 yards, held the ball 33:12, and featured Brashard Smith on 16 carries. That is the only offensive tape that looked like a plan. Denver will stack the box to make a 6-11 club prove it on ESPN; Bieniemy has to answer with zone steps and a one-cut rule, not a pass-first reset.

Coaching point Bieniemy’s point to the line and to Smith: lose ground on the first step and the whole night becomes 2nd-and-11 for Mahomes. Combo the play-side double, let Smith pick the first crease, and do not bounce it into an AFC West edge. Reid will want this look twice in a row so the boot later is honest.

Offense

Play-action boot off the zone look

Situation 2nd-and-6 after a live zone run vs. Denver — move Mahomes and punish linebackers who just stepped downhill.

The Seattle night was 198 net passing yards on 34 attempts because the quarterback was a camp arm staying on schedule. Week 1 is Patrick Mahomes. If Smith and the interior make Denver’s second level run-fit, the boot is the shot that turns a field-goal drive into a touchdown drive — the exact failure mode of the 9–9 close.

Coaching point Sell the same zone mesh as the run. Mahomes’s first read is the tight end in the flat, second is the crossing high-low, third is the backside post. Do not reset your feet in the tackle box; the whole point is to get the best passer in the building on the perimeter before Denver’s rush arrives.

Offense

RB spacing screen

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 7 vs. Denver when the edge is flying upfield after two zone runs.

Kansas City converted 9-of-17 third downs against Seattle by staying on schedule, not by winning one-on-ones downfield. A crowded backfield — Smith’s feature night, Nate Carter on the practice squad, the Demercado cut — only makes sense if the featured back can win in space. This call also protects Mahomes from a Broncos rush that will be coached to wreck a three-point favorite.

Coaching point Get the ball out on the third step. The play-side tackle and guard have to climb to the second level immediately; if they stall, Smith is dead in the backfield. Bieniemy should tag this to the same formation as inside zone so Denver cannot late-sub a spinner onto the running back.

Offense

Mesh in the red zone

Situation 1st/2nd-and-goal or inside the Denver 15 — the exact down Kansas City settled for Butker from 19, 24, and 28.

The 9–9 tape is a red-zone indictment: 21 first downs, 353 yards, zero touchdowns. Denver will play man and body up in that area because the preseason finale told them Kansas City will kick. Mesh with a rub and a sit/corner combo is how Reid/Bieniemy force a picker and give Mahomes a defined answer instead of another chip-shot.

Coaching point The two meshers have to rub, not politely cross. The sit player owns the hole; the corner route is the leverage shot if Denver’s outside leverage is late. Mahomes checks the running back only after the mesh window closes. No hero ball — the 6-11 version of this offense lost games by leaving points in this exact spot.

Defense

Cover-2 lid, rally to the run

Situation Early downs vs. Denver’s opening script — take away the shot and make the visitor drive the length of Arrowhead.

Spagnuolo just held Seattle to 266 yards, 13 first downs, and 54 rushing yards. That is a two-deep, five-under night. In an ESPN division opener, the worst outcome for a 6-11 club is a chunk play that turns a -3 favorite into a track meet. Cover-2 lets the new front (Eason inside, Pyburn in the rush mix) play downhill without a sabercat over the top.

Coaching point Corners squat and force. Safeties split the posts and do not cheat the run until the mesh is declared. The Mike has to fit the A-gap without popping out of the hole — that is how 54-yard nights happen. Spagnuolo’s call to the back end: we will give the underneath completion and we will not give the stadium a firework.

Defense

Simulated pressure, drop a lineman

Situation 2nd-and-7 or 3rd-and-medium vs. Denver — the down Spagnuolo just won 4-of-11 against Seattle.

Veach’s cutdown and the 49ers claims (Eason, Pyburn) only matter if they change protection pictures. R. Mason Thomas already posted a sack in the finale. A zone blitz lets Spagnuolo show heat, drop a big onto the hot throw, and rush a second-level player so Denver cannot slide to the veteran edge. That is how a patched front becomes a Week 1 plan instead of a depth chart.

Coaching point Show five or six at the line of scrimmage. The dropping lineman — Eason is the insurance piece for this exact job — has to get his hands into the passing lane, not freelance upfield. Pyburn or the second-level rusher owns the open B-gap on the snap. Secondary rolls into a three-under/three-deep or a robber; do not leave the post uncovered because you wanted a highlight.

The storylines

What we’re watching

QB room

Nussmeier earned the 53. Mahomes still has to cash Week 1.

Garrett Nussmeier beat out Chris Oladokun for the third job, made the roster, and even inherited No. 19 after a 22-of-34 night. That is a camp win. It is not the Week 1 plan. Patrick Mahomes and Bieniemy’s starter script have 16 days to look nothing like a 5.8-yard preseason average.

Front seven

Veach’s cutdown was a defensive-line document

The organizational tell is the front: a defensive-line makeover, then two waiver claims — Bryson Eason and Jack Pyburn — after the 49ers’ cut. Arrowhead Addict framed it as Veach done playing it safe. Denver is the first live test of whether that is scheme or just extra bodies.

Backfield

Smith ran like the lead. The room is still crowded.

Brashard Smith’s 16 carries were the last-game feature. Then Kansas City signed Nate Carter to a practice squad that already had runners and took a calculated risk leaving Emari Demercado available. Somebody has to take the first-down reps against Denver without a committee debate in the huddle.

Roster math

The 53 moved twice in 24 hours

Surprise roster spots for Marcus Harris and Kevin Knowles did not survive the waiver claims, and Tampa Bay spoiled the Knowles gamble. That is cutdown chaos, not panic, but it means the depth chart Denver will see is not the one that was posted on Sunday night.

Market

Home favorite is not a Super Bowl number

ESPN FPI at 58.5 percent and DraftKings at Kansas City -3, 42.5 total, is a Week 1 lean toward Arrowhead. Polymarket at 6.2 percent to win the 2027 title (9.5 percent to win the AFC) is the honest 6-11 hangover. Both can be true.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid vs. a division opener on ESPN

Andy Reid will want the first 15 calls to look like the Seattle possession tape, not like a 6-11 team hunting explosives. If Arrowhead is loud and the run game is real, he can sit on the boot later. If the first two drives are three-and-outs, the night becomes a Mahomes rescue job.

Bieniemy’s identity test

Eric Bieniemy’s room just posted 155 rushing yards and zero touchdowns. The coaching point through September 15 is not a new concept menu — it is finishing rules, two-minute red zone, and a defined feature back so the committee does not show up on 1st-and-10.

Spagnuolo integrating the new front

Steve Spagnuolo has to decide how fast Bryson Eason and Jack Pyburn are real in simulated pressure, not just on the 53. The Seattle night (54 rush yards, one sack from R. Mason Thomas) is the standard. Denver is the exam.

  • Waiver claims: Bryson Eason and Jack Pyburn

    With high waiver priority, Kansas City claimed rookie DT Bryson Eason and edge/LB Jack Pyburn after the 49ers’ cut, then reshaped the 53 around those two pieces.

  • Marcus Harris and Kevin Knowles off the 53

  • Calculated cut: Emari Demercado

    Kansas City left Demercado available on waivers in a clear roster statement. Combined with Smith’s 16-carry night, it reads as a bet on the backs they kept, not the ones they borrowed.

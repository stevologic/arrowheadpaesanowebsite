Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

W
Denver Broncos Week 1 · Mon Sep 15
KC 31–10

Kansas City opened 2026 by bludgeoning Denver 31-10 at Arrowhead, piling up 392 yards and 19 first downs while holding the Broncos to 176 yards, nine first downs, and 2-of-12 on third down. Mahomes scored on a 15-yard rush, answered Nix-to-Engram with a 13-yard strike to Rice, then watched Walker rip a 60-yard touchdown and finish the night with a 2-yard receiving score as Butker added a 28-yard field goal.

The tape is a Bieniemy tape. Kansas City lived under center, won early downs, and let Walker decide the aiming point. Twenty-three carries is a feature-back workload, not a committee split, and the 60-yard third-quarter run was the logical end of a night where Denver’s front seven got tired of fitting the same inside-zone picture. When the Broncos finally hung a hat in the backfield, Kansas City did not panic into dropback volume — it stayed on schedule, took the 28-yard field goal, and then threw Walker the football from the 2. That is identity, not improvisation.

Mahomes’ night was complementary, which is both the compliment and the warning. Fifteen of 27 for 184, two scores, one interception, plus the designed-or-broken 15-yard rushing touchdown: he won the game without winning the air. Kelce’s 71 yards on three catches were chunk plays, not a rhythm diet, and Rice’s 13-yard touchdown was the kind of red-zone finish this offense failed to find in the preseason. Arrowhead Pride’s film room is right that Walker was the explosion; Arrowhead Addict is also right that the passing tape gave you hope if you watched the answers and doubt if you watched the misses. Mahomes himself treated it like the latter.

Spagnuolo’s defense set a floor Denver never climbed off. Sixty-one rushing yards, 115 net passing, two turnovers, and a third-down night that died in the dirt. Nix’s 19-yard touchdown to Evan Engram in the first quarter was the Broncos’ only real punch; after that it was Lutz from 31 and a long evening of two-and-outs. Nohl Williams got the sack on the stat sheet, but the story was the rally-and-tackle, the collapsed pocket, and a secondary that flashed immediately when Delane intercepted Nix on the opening drive — then had to play without him after the shoulder. That is a win with a scar, not a shutout fantasy.

The hidden down was special teams, and it already cost a body. McDonald went down covering the kickoff after Kansas City’s first score, and he is on injured reserve before Week 2 practice even became a conversation. Arrowhead Addict’s warning about the same old special-teams mistakes is not vibes; it is the only unit that turned a 31-10 physical beatdown into a roster transaction. You can run for 220 and still lose the week if the coverage units keep donating players and field position.

What held up

  • Walker as a 23-carry feature back, including the 60-yard zone burst and the 2-yard receiving score
  • Time of possession (33:42) and early-down physicality that kept Mahomes out of obvious passing downs
  • Spagnuolo’s third-down clamp (Denver 2-of-12) and a 61-yard rushing lid
  • Kelce’s three-catch, 71-yard chunk profile and Rice finishing from 13 yards
  • Red-zone touchdowns after a preseason diet of field goals

What broke

  • Mahomes’ 15-of-27, 184-yard volume and the interception — functional, not sharp
  • Special teams coverage, which put Cooper McDonald on IR before the first quarter was old
  • Losing Mansoor Delane to a shoulder after the opening-drive interception
  • Net passing of 172 yards against a defense that sold out to stop the run

The run is the offense until someone stops it

Walker’s 173 on 23 and the 60-yarder were not change-of-pace. Bieniemy has a downhill call sheet again, and the 2-yard receiving touchdown says Walker stays on the field when Kansas City reaches the paint. Indianapolis will get the first real copy of that picture.

Mahomes won ugly, and he knows it

Two touchdown passes and a rushing score on 184 passing yards is a winning line, not a finished one. The interception and the 15-of-27 keep the self-criticism honest: the ground game bought him time, it did not replace the throw game.

Spagnuolo can erase a night. Coverage still has to stay intact.

Denver’s 2-of-12 third down and 176 total yards are a defensive identity win. Delane leaving after the pick is the counterweight — the floor is high, the depth chart is already being asked a Week 2 question.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

1-0

Kansas City is 1-0, in the AP top five, and still priced like a team that went 6-11 last year. The run game and the Spagnuolo floor are real; the passing efficiency, the short week, and the special-teams tax are the actual to-do list before Indianapolis walks into Arrowhead on NBC.

What we need to work on

Throw on schedule, not as a last resort

Mahomes was winning with Walker and still left a 15-of-27 tape. Indianapolis will copy Denver’s box and dare Kansas City to complete the play-action. If the second-and-6 throw does not show up, the Colts will live in the backfield on a short week.

Special teams has to stop creating roster news

McDonald is on IR because of a kickoff-coverage injury, and Arrowhead Addict already flagged the unit as the thing that will hold a new-look offense back. You cannot script Walker for 23 carries and then donate field position — or bodies — between possessions.

Secondary depth if Delane is less than full

Arrowhead Pride says there is a pretty good chance Delane plays. Pretty good is not a guarantee, and the Colts’ passing downs will test whoever is opposite the pick-six start. Spagnuolo can scheme a lid; he cannot invent a healthy corner.

Walker’s workload on a six-day turn

Twenty-three carries plus receiving work on Monday night is a lot to repeat on Sunday night. Bieniemy has to keep the identity without turning the feature back into a Week 3 cautionary tale before Miami.

What to think about

Is 15-of-27 a concern or just the tax on 220 rush yards?

Arrowhead Pride’s mailbag already asked it, and Mahomes already answered it with self-criticism. If Indianapolis sits two-high, the run still has to go. If they sit one-high, the boot and the mesh have to be ready. The staff should want both answers in the first 15 calls.

How much fullback is too much fullback?

The roster moved toward a heavier personnel package the minute McDonald hit IR. That fits the Walker tape. It also tells Indianapolis exactly which pictures are coming unless Reid and Bieniemy keep 11 personnel in the same down-and-distance.

Markets vs. the AP top five

A top-five debut after one game is a vibe. A 75.5 percent FPI nod and a 6.5-point spread are a favorite. A 7.6 percent championship price is the 6-11 ghost. Believe the run, do not cash the parade.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Indianapolis Colts

Short week, Sunday Night Football, home favorite. Indianapolis will arrive with Walker’s 173-yard film and a plan to make Mahomes beat them from the pocket. Kansas City’s assignment is to run it anyway, cash the play-action that Denver never fully paid, and refuse to let special teams become the plot.

How they match up

Styles collide in a simple way: Kansas City wants to be a downhill, under-center, time-of-possession team that uses Walker to set up Mahomes off the boot. Indianapolis, as the road underdog on NBC, should want the opposite — two-high looks, early-down disruption, and a chance to turn this into a dropback game on a six-day turn. The leverage is the line of scrimmage and third down. If the Chiefs stay on schedule like they did against Denver (9-of-17), Spagnuolo’s defense can hunt. If they fall behind the chains, Mahomes’ 15-of-27 tape becomes the whole night and the Colts’ pass rush gets to pin its ears back. Give Indianapolis this: they do not have to out-talent Arrowhead. They have to out-stubborn a team that just showed the league its new first answer.

Make Indianapolis stop Walker twice

Do not abandon inside zone because the Colts loaded the box. The second answer is play-action boot and the Walker screen, not a sudden diet of empty dropbacks. Force them to be wrong about the run and the fake.

Use Kelce as the chunk, Rice as the finisher

Kelce’s 71 yards on three catches is the play-action vertical. Rice already scored from 13. Against a defense sitting on Walker, those two have to be the defined reads, not late-in-the-down scrambles.

Spagnuolo keeps a lid, then steals a down

Cover-2 early to take away the shot Indianapolis will want on the road. Zone blitz on third-and-long, the down Denver died on (2-of-12). Nohl Williams already announced himself. Do not blitz yourself into a chunk.

Special teams is a game-plan item, not a commercial break

McDonald is gone. Coverage rules, return discipline, and not putting Mahomes on a short field are as important as the first 15 offensive calls. This is the unit that can turn a 6.5-point favorite into a miserable SNF.

  1. Open under center with Walker inside zone to the closed side — make Indianapolis fit the Denver picture on the first snap, not the tenth.
  2. Second series: same run look, play-action boot to Kelce on the leak. Mahomes’ 15-of-27 needs a defined, moving-pocket answer before the Colts start timing the drop.
  3. Install a Walker spacing screen the first time Indianapolis pins the ears back. He already scored from 2 yards as a receiver; use him as the pressure-beater, not a checkdown after the fact.
  4. Third-and-5: mesh with Rice and Kelce as the rub pair. Do not ask Mahomes to win a one-on-one deep ball to stay on the field.
  5. Early passing downs for Indianapolis: Cover-2 shell, rally, tackle. Take away the shot the underdog wants on NBC.
  6. Third-and-7-plus: simulated pressure, Williams as the add, a lineman dropping. Steal the down the way Denver’s 2-of-12 night was stolen.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The 6-11 hangover did not survive Monday night. Kansas City punched Denver in the mouth for 31-10 at Arrowhead, ran for 220 yards, sat on the football for 33:42, and handed Kenneth Walker the AFC Offensive Player of the Week after 191 yards from scrimmage. That is the new plot: Eric Bieniemy’s offense has a downhill identity again, Steve Spagnuolo’s defense squeezed Bo Nix into 115 net passing yards and a 2-of-12 third-down night, and the only unfinished business on the tape is the thing this franchise is supposed to be famous for — Patrick Mahomes throwing the league open. Indianapolis, on NBC, is the first team that gets a full week to copy Denver’s answer: load the box, dare the 15-of-27, and hope special teams keeps leaking.

Walker’s 23-carry, 173-yard, one-rushing-touchdown night — plus the 2-yard score as a receiver — was not a gadget burst. It was under-center football, zone steps, and a back who pressed the line of scrimmage until Denver’s second level broke. The 60-yarder in the third quarter was the signature, but the 2-yard pitch-and-catch to open the fourth was the tell: Bieniemy is going to use Walker as a complete down-to-down answer, not a change-of-pace souvenir. When Arrowhead Addict asked whether the passing game left something to be desired, the honest film answer is both: the run created the win, and Mahomes still left throws on the grass.

Mahomes, back from knee surgery and now 31 years old, did enough. He scrambled 15 yards for the opener, hit Rashee Rice from 13, found Travis Kelce for 71 yards on just three catches, and still walked out self-critical of a 184-yard box. That is the right temperature for a 1-0 team that just landed in the AP top five. The market has not crowned them — 7.6 percent to win it all on Polymarket is a polite nod, not a parade — and the staff knows why. Cooper McDonald is already on injured reserve after a kickoff-coverage injury, special teams is back on the problem board, and rookie corner Mansoor Delane left a pick-six-looking start with a shoulder before the building even settled.

Sunday night is not a revenge game and it is not a trap only if Kansas City treats it like one. Indianapolis comes into GEHA Field with a full week of Walker film and a simple assignment: make the quarterback beat you from the pocket on a short week after Monday Night Football. Win that, and the 220-yard sermon becomes a one-week sample. Lose the line of scrimmage again, and Bieniemy will keep calling the same play until the Colts beg for a pass rush. The three-act season has started: the run is real, the pass has to catch up, and Miami on the road in Week 3 will not care which narrative you liked better.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker inside zone, first-call identity

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script vs. Indianapolis, 21/12 personnel at Arrowhead

Walker just ran 23 times for 173 and a 60-yard touchdown against Denver, then added a 2-yard receiving score. Indianapolis will spend the week fitting that picture; Bieniemy still has to run it on first down or the entire play-action menu dies. This is how Kansas City stayed on schedule (19 first downs, 33:42) and kept Mahomes out of obvious passing downs on a short week.

Coaching point Zone steps, one cut, no east-west bounce before the line of scrimmage. The doubles have to climb to the second level the way they did on the 60-yarder; Walker presses the heels of the guards, then rips the first crease. Reid and Bieniemy should not check out of this because the Colts loaded the box — that is the point of the identity.

Offense

Boot off the Walker picture

Situation 2nd-and-6 after an inside-zone look, under center, Colts with an extra hat in the box

Mahomes was 15-of-27 for 184 with one interception — enough to win, not enough to scare a defense that just watched 220 rush yards. Kelce’s 71 yards on three catches were the chunk answers. Indianapolis will sit on the run; the boot is how Reid moves the pocket, gets Mahomes on the perimeter, and cashes the same fake Denver never fully paid.

Coaching point Sell the zone with the same first two steps as the run. Mahomes gets depth, does not fly too flat, and takes Kelce leaking or the flat if the Colts’ end crashes. After knee surgery, this is the throw he should want: defined, moving, no late-down hero ball.

Offense

Mesh to stay on the field

Situation 3rd-and-5, 11 personnel, Indianapolis in man-leaning nickel after stuffing a run

The passing game’s Week 1 doubt lives on third down if Walker gets stacked. Rice already finished from 13 yards; Kelce is the rub threat. Mesh gives Mahomes a defined answer without asking him to recapture 2022 dropback volume on a six-day turn. This is how you avoid turning 9-of-17 third down into a one-week memory.

Coaching point Rice and Kelce have to alter timing so the rub is violent, not pretty. Mahomes’ first look is the coming-open crosser, second look is the corner/sit, outlet is Walker. No scramble-first mentality — the self-criticism after Denver was about misses, not missed runs.

Offense

Walker screen when they pin their ears

Situation 2nd-and-7, Indianapolis sending an extra rusher off the play-action look

Walker already scored as a receiver from the 2, and 23 Monday-night carries plus a Sunday-night kickoff is a lot to repeat. A screen restocks the identity, punishes the Colts for selling out after the Denver tape, and takes pressure off Mahomes without abandoning Bieniemy’s ground rules. It is also the easiest way to keep Indianapolis from turning this into a dropback game.

Coaching point Linemen have to get out — this dies if it is a slow screen. Walker has to set the rush with a pass-pro hint, then get north. Rice and Kelce crack/clear the first linebacker; do not let this become a west-coast nothing play behind the sticks.

Defense

Cover-2 lid on first down

Situation 1st-and-10 vs. Indianapolis 11 personnel, early SNF drives, Delane possibly back

Denver managed 115 net passing yards and nine first downs because Kansas City took away the shot and rallied. Indianapolis, as a road underdog on NBC, will want an early explosive to flip the 6.5-point script. Spagnuolo’s two-deep shell is the adult call on a short week, especially if Mansoor Delane is less than 100 percent after the shoulder.

Coaching point Corners squat and tackle, safeties do not cheat the run until Walker proves he can gash this look too. Delane — if he goes — plays his rules, not his opening-drive hero ball. The win is a 4-yard completion and a second-and-6, not a sack on first down.

Defense

Simulated pressure on third-and-long

Situation 3rd-and-7 or more, Indianapolis in obvious pass, late in a series

Denver went 2-of-12 on third down and turned it over twice. That is the down Spagnuolo should hunt without emptying the box against a team that will try to draw him offside with max protect. Nohl Williams already has a sack on the season; a second-level add with a lineman dropping is the signature call that keeps Kansas City’s 75.5 percent FPI lean from becoming a one-score slog.

Coaching point Show heat, drop the big, rush the backer. Williams’ job is the inside seam of the protection, not a wide loop that opens a lane. The underneath droppers have to be in the throwing window — this is not a zero blitz. Steal the down, then hand the ball back to Walker.

Coaching & adjustments

Reid and the six-day turn

Monday night into Sunday night is a call-sheet problem as much as a rest problem. Reid should keep the menu small: Walker zone, boot, screen, mesh. The clever stuff can wait for Miami.

Bieniemy’s identity vs. the copycat week

Indianapolis has a full week of 23-carry film. The winning coaching point is not a new run; it is running the same run and having the boot ready when they load. Abandoning Walker early would be the tell that Week 1 was a stunt.

Spagnuolo’s down-and-distance patience

The Denver tape says the lid worked. Do not chase a second sack from Williams on first down and hand Indianapolis the explosive it needs on the road.

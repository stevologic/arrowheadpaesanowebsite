Rice walks back into a one-point Denver reply

Current state

A 5-1 team with a receiver question still open

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

5-1

Kansas City is 5-1, coming off the first loss of the live season, still the ESPN FPI lean at Denver, and still a team whose skill-position depth is a fan argument and a deadline argument at the same time.

What we need to work on

Early-down identity before the third-and-long menu

Bieniemy cannot ask Mahomes to throw Kansas City out of 3rd-and-7 as the default after 3-of-11. Walker needs a carry count that looks like a plan, not 14 rushes inside a 16-attempt team total.

Protect the quarterback without hiding the receivers

One sack and seven hits is the hit count to coach, not a slogan. Screens, meshes, and defined hot answers have to be in the call sheet before another edge, Denver's version of D. Hall, lives in the pocket.

Fit the run on first down so Spagnuolo can play coverage

Seattle's 41 attempts and 35:51 made Cover-2 and simulated pressure situational instead of structural. If Denver strings runs, the secondary never gets to the down Spagnuolo wants.

What to think about

Does Rice's return close the trade conversation?

Arrowhead Pride's Reacts poll says fans want a receiver. The same site has Rice returning and Thornton described as out for the year, with Cyrus Allen still a one-catch rookie on the Seattle sheet. The staff can welcome Rice and still decide the room is thin.

How much of the second Denver meeting is narrative?

Arrowhead Addict argues the recent second-meeting history is not the simple losing streak it gets sold as. Week 1 was 31-10 at home. This one is at Denver, with a different Kansas City offense than the one that posted that score. Treat the rematch as matchups, not folklore.

Who plays behind the veterans in the secondary?

Mansoor Delane missed Thursday's practice window. Chris Roland-Wallace is the young defensive back Arrowhead Addict wants on the field more. Spagnuolo has a real snap decision before a road division game, and the packet does not hand him a Friday clearance.

Looking ahead

The Mile High plan after a 31-10 memory

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Win the down, not the memory of 31-10. Denver is 5-2, has taken five of its last six, and hosts Kansas City on Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT on CBS in a game DraftKings prices as Chiefs minus 1.5 on 40.5.

How they match up

Kansas City's edge is still Mahomes plus a defense that, over a full season, has Spagnuolo's disguise menu, and now Rice is expected on the field with Kelce, Worthy, and Gray. Denver's edge is the building: a rebound from the Week 1 loss, emerging defenders Arrowhead Addict says cannot be overlooked, and a blueprint from Seattle that says 41 runs and 11-of-16 on third down can shrink Mahomes. Week 1's 31-10 was at Arrowhead. Mile High does not owe Kansas City that script. The FPI split, 55.7 to 44.0, is a lean, not a mismatch.

Make Denver defend Walker before it hunts Mahomes

Sixteen attempts and 50 yards is the number Denver will try to recreate. Inside zone on early downs, with Walker pressing the fit, is how play-action and boots become honest later. If the carry count stays ornamental, seven hits becomes the game.

Use Rice as a spacing player, not a highlight order

Mesh and defined crossers with Kelce give Mahomes the short answers that were missing when the Royals throw was intercepted. Four verticals is the shot once Denver squeezes the mesh, with Worthy and Rice stressing the deep half and Kelce on the seam. Do not open the game asking for the deep ball that Seattle's tape already has on incomplete deep throws to Kelce and Worthy.

Keep the chains in Cover-2 until Denver proves it can throw over it

Two deep, five under, rally to tackle. Seattle's damage was volume plus Smith-Njigba's deep left, not a requirement that Spagnuolo play single-high all day. Denver gets the shell that takes the cheap explosive away and forces a 10-play drive.

Simulated pressure as a call, timed to third down

Show heat, drop a lineman, rush the second level. That is the zone-blitz card for Denver's third downs, not a retroactive explanation of the Williams interception. Karlaftis and the second level have to be assignment-clean so a drop does not become a free run.

  1. Open with under-center inside zone to Walker and a defined Mahomes keep only if the fit spills. Earn a second early-down carry before the first empty formation.
  2. Second series: mesh with Kelce and Rice crossing, Walker as the checkdown, Worthy sitting outside the rub. Take the five-yard answer.
  3. After a run fit is on tape, boot Mahomes to the perimeter with Gray as the flat option and Rice on the deep cross. Sell the zone, do not announce a trick.
  4. If Denver spins to man on Rice, stay in mesh. If they spin to a deep shell, then call four verticals with the seams bending to the hash.
  5. Defense, first two series: Cover-2, force checkdowns, tackle. Do not chase a strip that is not on the call sheet.
  6. Third down: zone blitz with a dropped lineman and Karlaftis rushing from the second level's picture. Rally Tranquill to the back.
Last game

What 213 yards at Lumen actually showed

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

L
Seattle Seahawks Week 7 · Sun, Oct 25 · 7:20 PM CT
KC 3–20

Seattle won 20-3 at Lumen Field, scoring a 25-yard field goal at Q2 8:22, a 28-yard touchdown at Q2 1:44, a 35-yard field goal at Q3 8:09, and a 21-yard touchdown at Q4 3:47, with Kansas City's points a 50-yard field goal at Q3 1:42.

The possession split tells the story before the score does. Seattle ran 41 times for 153 yards and converted 11 of 16 third downs, so eight drives became 35:51 with the ball and 410 yards. Emanuel Wilson took 24 rushes and 25 touches. George Holani and Velus Jones Jr. added five rushes each. AJ Barner had three rushes, including direct snaps up the middle. Sam Darnold was 20-of-28 with no sacks, a 96.9 rating, and 257 net passing yards behind that run volume. Kansas City recorded two quarterback hits.

Kansas City's eight drives produced 11 first downs and 3-of-11 on third down. Walker ran up the middle, left end, and right end and still finished at 36 yards on 14 carries; the team total was 50 on 16 attempts, with Mahomes accounting for two rushes. Noah Gray's two 19-yard catches, deep right in the first quarter and deep middle in the fourth, were the clearest explosives. Worthy was targeted deep and also finished with three catches. Kelce's four catches for 44 included the three-yard grab that ended a fourth-quarter drive on downs.

The turnover ledger was one apiece, and the credits are specific. T.Okada intercepted Mahomes short left, intended for J. Royals, at Q2 6:09. N. Williams intercepted Darnold short middle, intended for Rashid Shaheed, and returned it 65 yards at Q2 0:23, with G. Karlaftis on the play. A 58-yard Butker miss followed at Q2 0:01. In the third quarter D. Hall sacked Mahomes for a two-yard loss and forced a fumble that was recovered at the Seattle 32. The fourth quarter brought a 45-yard miss, wide right, then the turnover on downs.

Drue Tranquill's 12 tackles were the volume stat on a defense that still spent too long on the field. Kingsley Suamataia's holding wiped a fourth-quarter play. Kelce's illegal shift wiped one in the first quarter. This was a control loss, not a fluke of one throw.

What held up

  • N. Williams' interception and 65-yard return gave Kansas City a late-half swing Kansas City did not finish.
  • Gray's two 19-yard catches were timing throws Mahomes can still live on when the pocket is crowded.
  • Tranquill's 12 tackles, with Bolton, Karlaftis, Tonga, and the second-level fit, kept several Seattle rushes in the 2-to-4-yard range even while the attempt count buried the defense.

What broke

  • Third down at 3-of-11 and 11 first downs.
  • A 16-carry, 50-yard rush day, with Walker at 36 on 14.
  • Seven quarterback hits and a sack-fumble forced by D. Hall.
  • Two Butker misses and a fourth-quarter turnover on downs after a three-yard Kelce catch.

The run count lost the down-and-distance

Sixteen rushes and 50 yards left Mahomes in shotgun answers against seven quarterback hits and one sack. Seattle's 41 rushes were how 11-of-16 on third down happened. Denver will have watched that down-and-distance, not just the final score.

Explosives were Gray, then empty

Gray's 19-yard catches moved the ball. The deep shots to Kelce and Worthy did not finish, and the interception was a short throw to Royals, not a hero ball down the sideline. Rice's return is a route answer, not a personality answer.

The red-zone and kick game never stacked points

Kansas City reached field-goal range and came away with a 50-yard make plus misses from 58 and 45. Seattle cashed a 25, a 35, and two touchdowns. A 40.5 total in Denver will punish empty trips the same way.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The assignment is Denver, on the road, on Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT on CBS, with Kansas City a 1.5-point favorite and Rashee Rice expected back. Week 1 was a 31-10 home win. This is the rematch, against a 5-2 Broncos team that has won five of its last six, and the market is treating it like a one-score game because the last tape was not a division-winner's tape.

Seattle won 20-3 at Lumen Field. Kansas City finished with 11 first downs, 50 rushing yards on 16 attempts, 163 net passing yards, 213 total yards, and a 24:09 possession. Seattle posted 24 first downs, 153 rushing yards on 41 attempts, 257 net passing yards, 410 total yards, and held the ball for 35:51. Both clubs had eight drives and one turnover. Third down was 3-of-11 for Kansas City and 11-of-16 for Seattle.

Patrick Mahomes was 19-of-33 for 165 yards with an interception, one sack, and seven quarterback hits, rating 58.3. Kenneth Walker III carried 14 times for 36 yards and finished with 18 touches. Travis Kelce caught four passes for 44 yards. The points on the Kansas City side were a 50-yard field goal at Q3 1:42. Harrison Butker missed from 58 and from 45. That is the film Andy Reid, Eric Bieniemy, and Steve Spagnuolo take to Mile High, not the 422-yard, 22-6 afternoon against the Chargers.

Arrowhead Pride's final Week 8 injury report has Rice returning from a hamstring, which changes the spacing Bieniemy can call. It does not erase a receiver room fans already want upgraded: the Week 8 Reacts poll wants a trade, Tyquan Thornton is described as out for the year, and the deadline pieces are still circling Kelce's supporting cast. Denver's defense, including the emerging defenders Arrowhead Addict flagged, gets a healthier Kansas City skill group and a quarterback who was self-critical after Seattle.

The number should keep everyone honest. ESPN FPI has Kansas City at 55.7 percent and Denver at 44.0 percent. DraftKings has the Chiefs minus 1.5 with a 40.5 total. A road favorite by a point and a half is not a coronation. It is a demand that the run, the third-down answers, and Spagnuolo's rush-fit show up before Denver tries to play the clock the way Seattle did.

Walker screen after a crowded pocket

Situation 2nd-and-6 or longer, after Denver shows an edge rush, anywhere on the field

Mahomes took seven quarterback hits and one sack in Seattle. A screen with the line releasing lets Walker catch in space and takes the rush's angle away without asking for a 20-yard drop.

Coaching point Sell a five-step look, let the edge up the field, then get the ball to Walker behind two releasing linemen. Gray stays in to help if the rush is too fast; do not hold the ball for a second window.

Mesh with Rice and Kelce

Coaching point Kelce and Rice cross shallow, Worthy sits outside the collision, Walker is the outlet if both crossers are jumped. Mahomes throws the first open window, not the second crosser breaking open late.

Boot off a credible zone fake

Situation 1st-and-10 or 2nd-and-3, after at least one successful inside-zone carry, boundary side

Denver will hunt Mahomes if the run fake is empty. Once Walker has moved the line, the boot puts Mahomes on the perimeter with Gray in the flat and Rice on a crossing read, away from the interior rush that produced the Hall sack.

Coaching point Under center, sell the zone all the way, boot Mahomes to the open side, and throw the flat if the force player sits. Rice's cross is the second read, not the first. This is a call for Denver, built because the Seattle rushes never made the fake honest.

Defense

Cover-2 shell on early downs

Situation 1st-and-10 and 2nd-and-medium, Denver in two-back or condensed formations

Smith-Njigba's 28-yard touchdown was a deep-left shot behind a run game. Two deep and five under takes that shot off the menu and asks Denver to drive the field, which is how Spagnuolo keeps a 40.5 game in the teens.

Coaching point Safeties stay deeper than the vertical, corners rally to the flat, and Tranquill is the hook-to-curl fitter. Tackle the checkdown. Do not bail into single-high because a back flares.

Zone blitz on Denver's third down

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 3rd-and-8, after Cover-2 has forced a longer down

Simulated pressure is the call when Denver has to throw. Show heat, drop a lineman, and rush a second-level player so the protection slides to the wrong picture. It is a disruption call for this game, coached separately from any Seattle takeaway.

Coaching point Spagnuolo declares the dropper before the snap. Karlaftis rushes the picture the protection was not taught, the dropped lineman walls the interior throw, and Tranquill closes the checkdown. If the back releases free, the call failed, regardless of whether the ball is thrown away.

Spagnuolo's down menu

Mahomes' self-critique

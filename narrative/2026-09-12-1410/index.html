Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

6-11 (2025) · 0-0 (2026)

The regular-season record is still 0-0, but the tape they carry is a 6-11 hangover plus a 9-9 reminder that yards are not points. Mahomes is back under center, the staff is Reid-Bieniemy-Spagnuolo, and the first real snap is a divisional prime-time game with a rookie at left tackle and a defensive cornerstone on a limited Friday report.

What we need to work on

Red-zone six instead of Butker threes

The Seattle tape died from 19, 24, and 28. Against Payton you will not get four trips. Bieniemy has to install a finish package — boot, mesh, tight-end leak — so Mahomes is not throwing into a loaded box on second-and-goal.

A protection plan that does not strand Kahlil Benson

Josh Simmons has been out since mid-camp with a lower back, and Reid has already pointed at Benson as the Week 1 left tackle. Help him with 12 personnel, play-action boots to the right, and screens before Payton isolates him on obvious passing downs.

A Jones-aware pressure menu

Chris Jones was a limited participant on Friday. Spagnuolo cannot pin his ears back for 60 snaps. Simulated pressure and a Cover-2 shell let the front win with disguise while Gilman learns the back end in real time.

Early-down identity that survives a loaded box

The 155-yard night only matters if it travels against a divisional front. Inside zone has to win first down so Mahomes is not in third-and-long with a rookie set and Payton sending extra men at the left edge.

What to think about

Did cutting Esa Pole leave the season thin on the island?

Pole started games down the stretch last year, hit the wire, and is already on Washington’s practice squad. The local argument is that the left tackle room is living with that decision while Simmons sits and Benson debuts against Payton.

Is the 58.9 percent FPI nod a home-field illusion?

Arrowhead plus Mahomes still gets you a short number. A 43.5 total and a 6.2 percent championship price say the league is not buying a finished product. Week 1 is the first audit of whether that discount is lazy or earned.

How much of this season is actually on Reid?

The local thesis is that 2026, starting now, sits on the head coach. That is fair only if the staff solutions — Benson, the red-zone package, a managed Jones — show up in the script, not just in the presser.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Week 1 at Arrowhead is an AFC West blood match in ESPN prime time: Mahomes versus Payton, a rookie left tackle versus a scripted edge, and a 6-11 roster trying to prove the Seattle run tape was a plan instead of a preseason mirage.

How they match up

Styles collide in the trenches. Kansas City wants to recreate the 33-minute, 155-yard night and play-action off it. Payton will test the left edge because Simmons is out and Benson is a Week 1 starter, and he will live in condensed sets if the Chiefs again stall inside the 10. Spagnuolo’s counter is disguise: Jones may not be a full-go wrecking ball, so the rush has to come from simulated looks while Gilman and the two-deep take away the shot play the 43.5 total already implies. The leverage is Arrowhead and Mahomes; the vulnerability is the blind side and the red zone.

Run it to keep Benson out of obvious pass sets

Inside zone on first down is protection, not just identity. If Kansas City stays in second-and-6, Payton cannot pin his ears back at the rookie. If they fall behind the sticks, the island becomes the game.

Move Mahomes off the left edge

Boots, nakeds, and sprint-outs to the right turn a protection problem into a throwing lane. Sell the same zone fake that gained 155 against Seattle, then get the franchise off Benson’s hip.

Finish the Seattle trips

Three field goals from inside the 30 is the scouting report Payton already has. Red-zone mesh, tight-end leak, and a designed quarterback movement have to replace the 19-yard Butker.

Pressure without emptying the Jones tank

Limited on Friday means Spagnuolo should win with extras and drops, not a 50-snap hunt. Make Denver’s protection slide the wrong way and keep explosives off the board.

  1. Open in 12 personnel, closed-side inside zone, so Benson is combo-blocking instead of isolated in an obvious pass set.
  2. On the first second-and-medium, under-center play-action boot to Mahomes’ right — away from the rookie — with the tight end leaking to the flat.
  3. If Payton shows a fifth rusher at left tackle, throw the Brashard Smith spacing screen immediately and make the extra man wrong.
  4. Red zone: mesh plus a sit route rather than another condensed iso; the Seattle 19-yarder is the look you are killing.
  5. Defensively, even fronts and a Cover-2 shell on early downs to take away Payton’s shot and force the grind a 43.5 total expects.
  6. On third-and-long, Spagnuolo simulated pressure: show five, rush four from the second level, drop a lineman, and keep Jones in a manageable snap count.
Last game

What the tape said

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

T
Seattle Seahawks Pre Week 4 · Sat Aug 29
KC 9–9

At Arrowhead, Kansas City controlled the preseason finale and still could not find the paint. Seattle took a 3-0 lead on a Jason Myers 46-yarder, Harrison Butker answered from 28 and 24, Jalen Milroe hit Nick Vannett for a 4-yard touchdown (Myers missed the PAT), and Butker’s 19-yarder in the fourth tied it 9-9. The Chiefs outgained Seattle 353-266, won first downs 21-13, converted 9 of 17 third downs, and sat on the ball for 33:12 — with Garrett Nussmeier throwing for 198, Brashard Smith running for 72 on 16 carries, and zero offensive touchdowns.

The tape is a possession clinic with a red-zone zero. Kansas City’s 155 rush yards were not empty calories: they stayed on schedule, they won early downs, and they forced Seattle to live in a 4-of-11 third-down night. That is the Bieniemy identity showing up in August. The problem is what happened once the field shrank. Butker’s three makes came from 28, 24, and 19. Those are touchdown trips that died in the shadow of the goal post, and you cannot open a real season against Payton kicking extra points you never earned.

Nussmeier’s 22-of-34 for 198 and a pick is preseason-finale football, not a Mahomes indictment, but the structure around him still matters. Andrew Armstrong’s 5-for-54 led the receivers, which tells you the ball was living underneath. When you run it 155 yards and still throw it 34 times without a touchdown, the issue is not volume, it is answers versus condensed coverage: no leak, no boot, no mesh pay-off, no tight-end finish. Seattle’s only touchdown was a 4-yard play-action dart from Milroe to Vannett. Kansas City never answered in kind.

Defensively, Steve Spagnuolo’s group did its August job. Seattle was held to 54 rush yards and 13 first downs. R. Mason Thomas got home for a sack, and the Seahawks needed a short-yardage throw to put six on the board. That is a clean complementary night if the offense cashes. It did not. A 9-9 tie with a 33-minute edge is the loudest warning this staff will get before the games count: you can win the math and still lose the narrative if you cannot punch it in.

What held up

  • Time of possession (33:12) and first-down volume (21) that kept Seattle’s offense on the sideline.
  • Inside-zone identity: 155 rush yards, Brashard Smith 72 on 16, early-down staying power.
  • Third-down conversion (9 of 17) versus Seattle’s 4 of 11.
  • Front-to-back seven: 54 rush yards allowed and a sack from R. Mason Thomas.

What broke

  • Red-zone finishing: three Butker makes from 28, 24, and 19 and zero offensive touchdowns.
  • Explosive passing: Nussmeier’s 198 yards on 34 attempts lived underneath, with Armstrong’s 54 leading the way.
  • The takeaway column: one interception in a game Kansas City otherwise controlled.
  • Complementary scoring: Seattle’s only touchdown was a 4-yard TD; Kansas City never answered with six.

The run game is real. The paint is not.

Brashard Smith’s 16-for-72 and a 155-yard team night are a Week 1 identity, not a cameo. Until those same zone steps produce a touchdown instead of a 19-yard field goal, Denver will load the box and dare Kansas City to beat them on the edges.

Third down traveled. Scoring did not.

Nine of 17 on third down and 21 first downs means the chains moved. The scoring summary is three Butker kicks. That gap is scheme and finish, not a random August variance, and it is the first thing Bieniemy has to fix in the red-zone install.

The defense bought a night the offense bounced.

Holding Seattle to 266 yards and 54 on the ground is the complementary profile Spagnuolo wants. It will not be enough against Payton if Kansas City again settles for field goals and puts the defense back on a short field after a stall.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The last tape Kansas City put on the field was a 9-9 preseason stalemate that looked like a statement until you checked the scoring summary. Twenty-one first downs. Thirty-three minutes and twelve seconds of possession. One hundred fifty-five rush yards. Three Harrison Butker field goals and an empty end zone. That is the film Andy Reid, Eric Bieniemy, and Patrick Mahomes carry into a Week 1 AFC West opener against Sean Payton on ESPN — with rookie Kahlil Benson at left tackle because Josh Simmons has not practiced through a lower-back issue, and with Chris Jones already listed as a limited participant.

Last year’s 6-11 hangover is still the record on the wall. The 2026 season, starting with this snap, is being framed as Reid’s to own, and the first stress test is not theoretical. When Kansas City cut to 53, Esa Pole — a tackle who started down the stretch a year ago — was a surprise release and landed on Washington’s practice squad. The left side is now a live wire: Simmons remains in the training room, Reid has reaffirmed confidence in Benson, and the local tape says the club is already living with the consequences of that cut rather than shopping a veteran Band-Aid like D.J. Humphries.

Denver is the wrong first opponent if you wanted a soft landing. Payton will script the left edge. The market still respects Arrowhead — ESPN FPI has the Chiefs at 58.9 percent, DraftKings posts them -2.5 on a 43.5 total — but Polymarket’s 6.2 percent championship price is not a Super Bowl hangover, it is a skeptic’s line on a roster that has to prove the Seattle run tape travels and that the red zone finally pays. Arrowhead Addict can talk fifth-best Super Bowl 61 odds; the number that matters Tuesday night is whether Kansas City can finish a drive.

The assignment is old-school AFC West, trench-first, and impatient. Bieniemy’s identity showed up in volume against Seattle and vanished at the goal line. Spagnuolo’s front has to grow in public with a new safety, Alohi Gilman, and a possibly managed Chris Jones. If the Chiefs open in 12 personnel, run inside zone, boot Mahomes off the rookie’s island, and treat the red zone like a touchdown district instead of a Butker office, the 6-11 tape gets a rewrite. If they don’t, Payton will make the left tackle the story of Week 1.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Closed-side inside zone

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script vs. Denver, 12 personnel

The Seattle tape gave you 155 rush yards and 21 first downs and still no touchdown. Against Payton, inside zone is how you keep Kahlil Benson out of obvious pass sets while Josh Simmons sits with a lower back. Brashard Smith’s 16-for-72 is the one-cut profile Bieniemy wants on the first drive so the 43.5 total stays a trench game.

Coaching point Trey Smith and the interior have to win the double and climb. Benson’s job is a tight zone step and a firm backside cutoff, not a highlight reach. Smith presses the line, one cut, no bounce into the backer Payton will hide in the cutback.

Offense

Boot Mahomes off the island

Situation 2nd-and-6 after a successful zone run, moving the pocket to the right

Reid has already pointed at Benson as the Week 1 left tackle, and Payton will test that edge. The counter is to sell the same under-center run that sat on Seattle for 33:12, then get Patrick Mahomes rolling right — away from the rookie — with an easy flat and an over. This is how you protect the franchise without pretending the blind side is solved.

Coaching point Bieniemy needs a full run fake and a tight end who chips then leaks. Mahomes’s first look is the flat, second is the deep over; do not hang in the lane hunting a shot. If the end crashes, throw it now. If he sits, keep running.

Offense

Mesh to kill the 19-yarder

Situation Red zone, 3rd-and-5 or 2nd-and-goal vs. man/condensed coverage

Butker’s 28, 24, and 19 against Seattle are the scouting report. Denver will squat on the paint and dare Kansas City to throw into bodies. Mesh puts rubs on the crossers, a sit in the hole, and Brashard Smith as the outlet so Mahomes has a rhythm throw instead of another field-goal trip.

Coaching point The crossing pair has to rub, not collide. Mahomes hits his hitch and throws; holding for the home run is how you get the pick Nussmeier took in August. If the MIKE jumps the mesh, the sit or the back is the answer. Finish.

Offense

Punish the fifth rusher

Situation 2nd-and-long or any look with an extra man walked at Benson

If Payton hunts the rookie, the ball has to come out before the island is tested. A Brashard Smith spacing screen turns that extra rusher into a minus-one on the perimeter, takes heat off Mahomes, and rhymes with the easy-offense volume that produced 72 rushing yards in the finale.

Coaching point Mahomes sells three-step. The line must get to the second level instead of blocking air. Smith catches, north-south, and Armstrong’s job is to make the first color miss. Do not peek the rush — that is how a screen becomes a sack-fumble.

Defense

Two-deep, rally, live to third down

Situation Early downs, Payton 11 personnel looking for a shot into a 43.5 total

Chris Jones was limited Friday and Alohi Gilman is playing his first game in this defense. You do not need a hero. You need to take away the explosive, force Denver to drive the length of the field, and tackle. That is how a 54-yard Seattle rushing night becomes a Week 1 plan instead of an August sample.

Coaching point Gilman and the free safety split the field and do not cross the hash hunting. Five under squeeze the intermediate. Spagnuolo’s coaching point is rally and strip the football, not a cheated look that gives Payton the one shot that flips a short home number.

Defense

Show five, rush four, save Jones

Situation 3rd-and-7, obvious pass, Jones on a managed snap count

Spagnuolo’s signature is how you pressure Payton without emptying a limited Chris Jones. Drop a lineman, send a second-level rusher, and make Denver’s protection slide toward a ghost. Gilman has talked about growing in this defense — this is the down he has to replace the dropped end and not guess.

Coaching point Show the five-man picture, bring four from a new angle, and keep Jones as the interior threat rather than the every-down hunter. The dropped end has to get depth. If the back picks up the LB, Jones still has a one-on-one; if they slide to Jones, the simulated rusher is free.

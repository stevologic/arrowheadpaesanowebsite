Mahomes is uncaged. The blind side is a rookie. The end zone is still a field goal.

Season opener against the division, Mahomes’ first real snap back, and the first look at whether a rookie left tackle can hold when Payton is on the other sideline.

The edge

ESPN FPI posts Kansas City at 57.8 percent and DraftKings hangs a short 2.5-point home number on a 43.5 total, but a 6.2 percent championship price on Polymarket is a dare: finish the Seattle run tape in the red zone and keep Mahomes clean when Payton tests the left edge.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

T
Seattle Seahawks Pre Week 4 · Sat Aug 29
KC 9–9

At Arrowhead, Kansas City controlled the night and still could not put Seattle away. The Chiefs out-gained the Seahawks 353–266, won first downs 21–13, converted 9-of-17 third downs, and sat on the ball for 33:12 — then walked off in a 9–9 tie built entirely on Harrison Butker kicks from 28, 24, and 19 yards. Seattle got a 46-yard Jason Myers field goal in the first, a 4-yard Jalen Milroe touchdown to Nick Vannett in the third (Myers missed the PAT), and just enough resistance in the paint to keep Kansas City off the board.

The film is a run-game tape, not a finishing tape. Brashard Smith’s 16 carries for 72 yards are the cleanest individual evidence that Bieniemy’s under-center identity survived cutdown week: zone steps, one cut, stay on track. One hundred fifty-five rushing yards against a defense that was also emptying the preseason roster is not a Super Bowl statement, but it is a real first-down answer. When they stayed ahead of the chains, the night looked like a possession win. When they reached scoring range, it looked like a team that had already decided three was enough.

The pass game was complementary and nothing more. Nussmeier went 22-of-34 for 198 with one interception; Andrew Armstrong led the receivers with five catches for 54. That is dink, move the chains, and hand it back to the run. There is no explosive finishing on that sheet, and the interception is the one self-inflicted wound on a night they otherwise protected the ball. For Week 1 purposes, treat this as a backup’s control game. The reads, the timing, and the red-zone answers all change when Mahomes is the one selling play-action and throwing the ball on the move.

Spagnuolo’s group won the non-scoreboard. Seattle managed only 54 rushing yards and 4-of-11 on third down. R Mason Thomas’s sack is the one named pass-rush win on the box, and the Seahawks’ only touchdown was a 4-yard toss to a tight end after Kansas City had already let a 46-yard field goal stand. That is bend-don’t-break with a short field. It also means the defense did its job in a game the offense refused to close. A 9–9 final with 353 yards is not a moral victory. It is a red-zone indictment that Payton will have on the projector before the first walkthrough.

What held up

  • Downhill run game: 155 rushing yards and Brashard Smith’s 16-for-72
  • Chain moving: 21 first downs, 9-of-17 on third down, 33:12 possession
  • Run defense: Seattle held to 54 rushing yards and 4-of-11 on third down
  • Field goals: Butker converted from 28, 24, and 19
  • Single named pass-rush win: R Mason Thomas’s sack

What broke

  • Zero offensive touchdowns despite 353 yards and trips inside field-goal range
  • Red-zone finishing: three kicks, including a 19-yarder from inside the 10
  • Explosive passing: 198 net yards and Armstrong’s 5-for-54 as the receiving high-water mark
  • The interception on Nussmeier’s 22-of-34 night
  • Closing a game they statistically controlled

The run game is real enough to script

One hundred fifty-five rushing yards, Smith at 4.5 a pop, and 21 first downs are a first-down identity, not a cameo. If that does not open the Denver script, Kansas City is asking a post-surgery quarterback and a rookie left tackle to win on obvious passing downs.

The paint is still a field-goal operation

Butker from 28, 24, and 19 with zero offensive touchdowns is the whole problem. A 19-yard kick means they were inside the 10 and still settled. That cannot be the Week 1 red-zone plan against a division rival.

The defense already has a lid

Thirteen first downs and 54 rush yards allowed is a Spagnuolo night. The only Seattle touchdown was a 4-yard throw after a missed extra point. Keep that standard and the 43.5 total starts to look like Kansas City’s friend.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

6-11

The 2026 regular season has not started, so the record on the wall is still 6-11 and the last snap is still a tie. What changed is the quarterback and what has not changed is the left tackle question. Mahomes is full go. Benson is the Week 1 plan. The red zone is unsolved.

What we need to work on

Finish the red zone

The Seattle tape is 21 first downs and three kicks. Against Payton, settling for Butker from inside the 20 is how a 2.5-point favorite becomes a 6-11 hangover. Install two-score answers — mesh, tight-red play-action, and a quarterback run threat Mahomes is allowed to use.

Build a protection plan for Kahlil Benson

Reid has reaffirmed confidence in the rookie, and Simmons has been out with a lower back issue since mid-camp. That does not mean you leave a first-year left tackle on an island against Payton for 65 snaps. Chips, tight end help, boots to the right, and screens have to be in the first 15 calls.

Re-time Mahomes without putting him in a phone booth

Reid is not holding him back, and Mahomes has already said he will not play anyone else’s game. The work is the middle path: full route tree and off-script answers, but get the ball out so a surgically repaired knee is not sitting in a collapsed pocket behind a rookie blind side.

What to think about

Is Benson actually the Week 1 answer?

Arrowhead Pride has Reid doubling down on the rookie. Arrowhead Addict is arguing it is a catastrophic way to protect Mahomes’ return. The staff has to weigh the development story against the simple fact that the first real snap is a division game on national television.

How much of the 155-yard run identity survives when defenses load the box for Mahomes?

Seattle’s preseason front is not Denver’s opener. If Payton sits an eighth in the box, Bieniemy has to decide whether to keep handing it to Smith or to let Mahomes punish the extra hat with play-action. The 9–9 tape does not answer that.

What does 6-11 still mean on a 57.8 percent home favorite?

The model and the number say Arrowhead still matters. The championship market at 6.2 percent says this roster has not proved anything. Week 1 is the first chance to pick which story is real.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Divisional opener, ESPN, Arrowhead. Kansas City is a 2.5-point home favorite on a 43.5 total with Mahomes back and a rookie at left tackle. The assignment is to run the Seattle identity, protect the blind side, and make Payton beat a Spagnuolo lid instead of hunting Benson.

How they match up

This is Payton’s schematic aggression against Reid’s full-menu offense and Spagnuolo’s simulated pressures. Kansas City’s leverage is the last-game run tape (155 yards, 21 first downs) plus Mahomes and Kelce in a short-yardage and red-zone environment they failed to cash against Seattle. Denver’s leverage is the matchup Reid cannot scheme away: a first-year left tackle, a quarterback 11 months removed from tearing an ACL and LCL, and a Chiefs roster coming off 6-11. On a 43.5 total, the team that wins early downs and avoids the obvious passing down will win the night. Do not turn this into a drop-back contest on Benson’s island.

Run it to keep Payton off the rookie

Script inside zone and play-action from the same under-center look that produced 155 yards against Seattle. If Kansas City gets into 2nd-and-11, Payton will pin his ears back at Benson. Stay in 2nd-and-5 and Mahomes can work play-action instead of a collapsed blind side.

Move the launch point off the left edge

Boot Mahomes to his right, full-slide protection the other way, and chip with a tight end when you do have to drop back. Reid has no plans to hold Mahomes back; that is not the same thing as asking him to climb a left B-gap behind a first start.

Cash the red zone

The last tape kicked from 19. Against Denver that is a loss. Use Kelce in mesh and sit concepts, give Mahomes a designed boot in the 15-to-8 window, and stop treating Butker as the closer.

Make Payton throw into a lid

Spagnuolo held Seattle to 266 yards and 13 first downs. Cover-2 on the shot downs, zone-blitz on the protection-tell downs, and do not give up the explosive that turns a 43.5 game into a track meet Mahomes does not need.

  1. Open under center with inside zone to the right hash — same look Smith ran for 72 — and force Denver’s front to respect the downhill tape before they tee off on Benson.
  2. Second series: play-action boot to Mahomes’ right, Kelce in the flat, Z on the comeback. Get the first completion of Mahomes’ 2026 on the move, away from the rookie.
  3. If Denver shows five-man heat at LT, do not hero-ball. Throw the spacing screen to Smith and let Trey Smith get to the second level.
  4. Inside the Denver 15, call mesh. The Seattle 19-yard field goal is the warning. Kelce and the X have to rub and finish.
  5. On 2nd-and-6, Spagnuolo shows overload to Benson’s side, drops the end, and brings a second-level rusher. Make Payton’s protection rules wrong.
  6. Late downs, cover-2 lid. Live with a field goal; do not live with a shot play over the top of a 6-11 secondary on a 43.5 total.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The last thing on the tape is a 9–9 possession slog that proved Eric Bieniemy’s ground game and proved nothing about finishing. Twenty-one first downs, 353 yards, 33:12 with the ball — and three Harrison Butker field goals. That is the hangover from a 6-11 year, and it is also the only recent evidence Andy Reid has before the real snaps start. Patrick Mahomes is cleared from last offseason’s ACL and LCL work, Reid has already taken the governor off, and the left tackle protecting that surgically repaired knee is rookie Kahlil Benson because Josh Simmons has been out with a lower back ailment since mid-camp. Denver and Sean Payton walk into GEHA Field at Arrowhead on ESPN. That is the whole season, compressed into one night.

Give Payton the credit he is owed. He will not wait to see if Benson can pass-set in Week 1. He will create one-on-ones, empty the backfield, and make Kansas City prove the left edge on the first drive. The market is not romantic about this roster: a 43.5 total is a trench number, a 2.5-point favorite is a polite nod to Arrowhead, and 6.2 percent to win the 2027 title (10.5 percent to win the AFC) is the 6-11 tape talking. ESPN FPI’s 57.8 / 41.8 split says the same thing in a quieter voice — home, Mahomes, slight edge, nothing like a mandate.

The offensive identity on the last tape is downhill. Brashard Smith ran 16 times for 72, the line created 155 on the ground, and third down was 9-of-17. The problem is the paint. Butker from 28, 24, and 19 means they moved it and then settled. Garrett Nussmeier’s 22-of-34 for 198 with an interception was a preseason control game, not a red-zone answer. When Mahomes takes that job back, the question is not whether he will be himself — Arrowhead Pride and Arrowhead Addict both have Reid and Mahomes saying the guardrails are already off — it is whether the supporting cast can turn 21 first downs into six instead of three.

Travis Kelce is louder about the receivers than the fan base is, and that has to show up against a division rival that just lived rent-free in last year’s tape. Steve Spagnuolo’s side of the ball actually has a clean preseason final: Seattle was held to 13 first downs, 54 rush yards, and 266 total, with R Mason Thomas getting the sack. That is the leverage. If the defense keeps the lid on and Bieniemy can run it early, Mahomes does not have to win a shootout on a 43.5 total. If Benson gets hunted and the red zone stays on a field-goal script, Payton leaves town with the first divisional scar of 2026.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-center inside zone, right hash

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script vs. Denver — same early-down look that produced 155 rush yards against Seattle

Brashard Smith went 16-for-72 in the finale, Kansas City posted 21 first downs, and a 43.5 total says this opener is won on early downs. If Bieniemy does not re-establish that downhill identity, Payton will pin his ears back at Kahlil Benson on every obvious pass.

Coaching point Zone steps, one cut, stay on track. Do not bounce Smith into Benson’s edge if the rookie is setting width. Let Trey Smith and the interior create the crease; the cutback is a last resort, not the design.

Offense

Play-action boot, launch point to the right

Situation 2nd-and-medium after a successful zone run — move Mahomes away from the rookie blind side

Reid has no plans to hold Mahomes back after the ACL/LCL recovery, and Benson is still the left tackle. Booting right sells the Seattle run look, puts Payton’s rush in chase mode, and gets Mahomes’ first 2026 completion on the perimeter instead of in a collapsed left B-gap.

Coaching point Same zone action Smith just ran. Mahomes: get depth, do not climb left. Kelce is the first look (flat to sit). If the end crashes, throw the Z comeback. This is how you let Mahomes be himself without asking Benson to win a four-second set.

Offense

Mesh in the paint

Situation Red zone, 2nd-and-7 inside the Denver 15 — the down Kansas City kicked from 19 against Seattle

Butker hit from 28, 24, and 19 and the Chiefs still tied 9–9. Kelce has more faith in this WR room than the fan base does; mesh is the Reid/Kelce answer that turns that faith into a rub and an actual six instead of another short kick.

Coaching point Kelce and the X cross with intent — rub, don’t pick-flag. Z runs the corner/sit. Mahomes stay in rhythm; this is not a scramble drill on a repaired knee if the mesh window is on time. Finish. The 19-yard field goal is the warning.

Offense

RB spacing screen vs. the heat

Situation 2nd-and-7 or 3rd-and-short when Payton shows five-man heat at Benson

Denver will test the rookie left tackle. Kansas City already showed it can throw underneath (Armstrong 5-for-54) and run it (Smith 72). A perimeter screen takes the one-on-one off Benson’s plate and punishes an aggressive edge without asking Mahomes to hold the ball.

Coaching point Smith: delay, catch, north. Trey Smith has to get to the second level and finish a linebacker. If the heat is coming off the left edge, this is the call that does not ask Benson to win the snap by himself.

Defense

Cover-2 lid on the shot down

Situation 2nd-and-10 / 3rd-and-long vs. Payton’s deep-shot menu on a 43.5 total

Spagnuolo’s group held Seattle to 13 first downs, 54 rush yards, and 266 total. The market already says this is not a shootout. Two deep takes away the explosive Payton will hunt against a secondary that just lived a 6-11 year.

Coaching point Two deep, five under, rally and tackle. Do not let a scramble become a chunk. R Mason Thomas set an edge with a sack on the last tape — keep the pocket on the quarterback and let the halves hold. Live with a field goal.

Defense

Simulated pressure, overload the rookie look

Situation 2nd-and-6, show five to Benson’s side, drop an end, bring a second-level rusher

Payton will either max-protect to hunt Benson or empty to isolate him. Spagnuolo’s signature answer is the fake: sell heat at the rookie, drop a lineman into the flat Payton likes to throw, and rush the second level. That is how you get Mahomes a short field without asking Benson to win every snap.

Coaching point Sell the overload to the left, then drop the end. Use R Mason Thomas — the named sack on the Seattle tape — as the add-on rusher, not as an every-down hero. Stay in zone behind it so there is no bust if Denver’s passer checks.

Benson is the plan

Josh Simmons has been out with a lower back ailment since mid-camp. Reid has reaffirmed confidence in rookie Kahlil Benson as the Week 1 left tackle. Arrowhead Addict is arguing that is a mistake. The tape will settle it on ESPN.

155 yards, zero touchdowns

The last game on the film is a run-game statement and a red-zone failure. Bieniemy has the first-down answer. He does not yet have the six-point answer. Denver is the first defense that will make that distinction matter.

TE/WR

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Andy Reid vs. Sean Payton

Reid is opening with a full-menu Mahomes and a rookie left tackle on national television. Payton will script the first 15 to find out if Benson can pass-set. The staff that wins the early-down call sheet — run vs. empty — wins the 43.5 game.

Eric Bieniemy’s first-down identity

The Seattle tape gave him 155 rush yards and 21 first downs. His Week 1 job is to keep that look on the field long enough that Mahomes is throwing play-action, not third-and-long from a cracked blind side.

Steve Spagnuolo’s lid

Thirteen first downs and 54 rush yards allowed is the standard. Mix cover-2 on the shot downs with zone-blitz on the protection tells and make Payton beat you underneath on a low total.

The left tackle decision

Reid has publicly reaffirmed Benson; the other side of the local split thinks it is a mistake. The coaching point is not the press conference. It is how many chips, boots, and slides are actually in the opener.

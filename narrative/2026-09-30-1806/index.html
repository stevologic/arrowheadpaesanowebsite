Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

W
Miami Dolphins Week 3 · Sun, Sep 27 · 12:00 PM CT
KC 24–10

Kansas City went in front 2:06 into the game on Kenneth Walker III’s 10-yard touchdown at Q1 12:54, with J. Moore reported in as eligible, then watched Miami tie it 7-7 on a 3-yard score at Q1 3:27. A 5-yard Chiefs touchdown at Q2 12:58 made it 14-7. Miami’s 37-yard field goal at Q3 10:10 cut it to 14-10, Harrison Butker’s 34-yard kick at Q4 11:35 pushed it to 17-10, and an 11-yard Kansas City touchdown at Q4 2:55 closed the 24-10 win. Patrick Mahomes went 20-of-24 for 246 yards, two passing touchdowns, and one interception; the Chiefs finished with 334 yards, 18 first downs, 88 rushing yards, and 25:39 of possession.

Mahomes was throwing to spots, not surviving them. At Q1 14:55 he hit Travis Kelce deep middle for 48 yards to the Miami 19, the shot that set up Walker’s 10-yard score. He later found Tyquan Thornton for 17, Xavier Worthy for 17, Noah Gray for 24 to the 8, and Rashee Rice for 34 to the Miami 15 before the 11-yard touchdown made it 24-10. Twenty completions on 24 attempts, 119.8 rating, 0 sacks. Miami still recorded 5 QB hits. That is the distinction: the pocket was touched, it was not emptied, and the ball came out on time. Arrowhead Pride’s film desk had it right — he was dealing in Miami.

The run game was Walker, and it was not explosive as a unit. He carried 18 times for 70 yards and the 10-yard touchdown and added 2 catches for 20 touches. The team managed 88 yards on 25 rushes. On Q2 6:15, Nourzad reported eligible and Walker went up the middle for no gain at the Miami 12, turnover on downs. Johnson’s 6 rushes did not change the math. When a road win is built on 246 net passing yards and 88 on the ground, the next opponent’s edge rusher becomes the entire conversation.

Spagnuolo’s group created the margin and donated the clock. L. Sneed forced and recovered a Malik Willis fumble at Q2 3:16 after Kansas City challenged the down-by-contact ruling. Karlaftis intercepted Willis short right at Q4 2:03, with Chris Jones on the play. Those two takeaways, against Mahomes’ interception to J. Rodriguez at Q2 2:00 (intended for Rice), are why a 334-329 yard game became 24-10. Everything else on that side leaked: Miami 19 first downs, 8-of-16 on third down, 31 rushes, 119 yards, 34:21 of possession. Bolton led the defense with 11 tackles and still spent the afternoon chasing. Arrowhead Pride already flagged the third-down issue. Zero sacks and 1 QB hit is the other flag.

The scoreboard never got away, and it never got comfortable. After the 7-7 tie, Kansas City led 14-7, 14-10, 17-10, then 24-10. Miami turned it over on downs at Q2 9:25 and again at Q4 6:00, and Greg Dulcich still got a 42-yard catch down the middle in the third quarter that set up the 37-yard field goal. Butker missed from 50 at Q4 0:57. Karlaftis’ illegal use of hands at Q4 0:47 wiped a C. Roland-Wallace interception. It was a two-score win with the underlying of a one-score fight, and that is the tape that goes to the Raiders.

What held up

  • Mahomes 20-of-24, 246 yards, two passing touchdowns, 119.8 rating, 0 sacks
  • Rashee Rice 7 catches, 88 yards, including the 34-yarder to the Miami 15
  • Kenneth Walker III 18 carries, 70 yards, 10-yard touchdown, 20 touches
  • L. Sneed forced and recovered Willis’ fumble at Q2 3:16
  • Karlaftis interception at Q4 2:03
  • Nick Bolton 11 tackles

What broke

  • Team rushing: 88 yards on 25 attempts
  • Third down: 3-of-7 on offense, 8-of-16 allowed on defense
  • Possession: 25:39 against Miami’s 34:21
  • Pass rush: 0 sacks, 1 QB hit
  • Mahomes interception to J. Rodriguez at Q2 2:00, intended for Rice
  • Harrison Butker 50-yard miss at Q4 0:57

Efficiency won the score. The clock did not.

Mahomes’ 20-of-24 for 246 and two passing touchdowns produced 24 points. Miami still out-gained nobody — 329 to 334 — and won first downs (19-18), third downs (8-16 vs 3-7), and possession (34:21 to 25:39). A 3-0 Raiders team at home will make you live in that second sentence.

Walker can carry it. Eighty-eight yards cannot.

Walker’s 18 carries, 70 yards, 10-yard touchdown, and 20 touches are a feature back’s line. The team’s 88 yards on 25 rushes are not a West-road line. The stuffed snap at the Miami 12 with Nourzad eligible is the one Bieniemy has to clean up before Crosby sees it.

Takeaways covered a quiet pass rush.

L. Sneed’s forced-and-recovered fumble and Karlaftis’ interception were the swing plays. Kansas City recorded 0 sacks and 1 QB hit. Willis still ran nine times. That is a win in Miami and a problem if Las Vegas keeps you on the field the way the Dolphins did.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

3-0

The Chiefs are 3-0 with a two-score road win in the books and a division road game up next, which is the good version of 3-0. The tape underneath — 88 rushing yards, 3-of-7 on third down, 25:39 of possession, 0 sacks, and a left tackle with a bulging disk — is why this still feels like a club being asked to prove the start. Las Vegas is 3-0 too. Nobody in that building is going to be impressed by 20-of-24.

What we need to work on

Get off the field on third down

Miami converted 8-of-16 and held the ball 34:21. Arrowhead Pride called it out, and a 3-0 Raiders offense at Allegiant will copy the down-and-distance plan if Spagnuolo leaves the same windows. This is the defensive install for Sun Oct 4, 3:25 PM CT.

Run it for more than 88

Walker’s 70 yards and 20 touches are real. The team’s 88 on 25 rushes will not keep Crosby from pinning his ears back. Bieniemy has to find early-down juice — Walker, Johnson’s 6-touch changeup, screens — or Mahomes will see more than 5 QB hits.

Manufacture a pass rush

Zero sacks and 1 QB hit against Willis is not a formula you take into a 3-0 West game. The takeaways were coverage and a run fumble. Pressure has to be a call this week, not a hope.

A protection plan without Simmons at 100 percent

Reid said Simmons has a bulging disk in his lower back. Crosby is the next snap. Boots, slides, extra eligible linemen, and getting the ball out on the same tempo as 20-of-24 are the answers if the left tackle is limited.

What to think about

Is 3-0 ahead of the underlying?

Kansas City has beaten Denver, Indianapolis, and Miami. The Miami box is the warning label: 18 first downs, 3-of-7, 25:39, 88 rush yards. The Raiders are 3-0. This is where the sample either thickens or starts quoting last year’s 6-11 as a ghost.

How many touches does Walker get when the edge is Crosby?

Twenty touches (18 rushes, 2 catches) is a heavy day. Johnson had 6. If the staff leans pass to protect a disk, Walker’s two catches have to become the pressure valve, not an afterthought.

Indoor Mahomes vs. a real pass rusher

Arrowhead Addict is waving the indoor record at the Raiders, and Allegiant is a roof. Crosby is still the man on the other side of Simmons’ back. The thrower can be 20-of-24 and the game can still be decided at left tackle.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Las Vegas Raiders

Week 4 at the Raiders is a 3-0 vs 3-0 AFC West game on Sun Oct 4, 3:25 PM CT (CBS). Kansas City is a 4.5-point road favorite on a 47.5 total, and the assignment is simpler than the record: run it enough to keep Crosby from hunting, get off the field on third down, and do not donate 34 minutes of clock to a division rival that has not lost yet.

How they match up

Both clubs are 3-0. Kansas City just won 24-10 while losing first downs, third downs, and possession; that is a dangerous way to walk into a division building. The Chiefs’ leverage is Mahomes’ accuracy tape (20-of-24, 246, two passing touchdowns) and an explosive-play rate Arrowhead Pride has as fourth in the NFL. Las Vegas’ leverage is home, the roof, and Crosby against a left tackle Reid has already described as a bulging disk. If the Chiefs play this like Miami — 88 rush yards, 25:39, 0 sacks — the Raiders will make 24-10 look like a generous memory. If Bieniemy can get Walker on schedule and Spagnuolo can turn third down into a call rather than a long drive, the 70.0 percent FPI number holds.

Put Walker on the field so Crosby has to honor it

Script inside zone and the screen before you ask Mahomes to drop. Walker’s 18-70-1 and 20 touches are the identity. Eighty-eight team yards is the warning. Early-down runs and easy throws keep the disk from becoming the game.

Move the launch point

Five QB hits with 0 sacks means the rush is close enough. Play-action boots and slides — the season-long play-action success Arrowhead Pride has been documenting — are how you attack Crosby without a perfect left tackle.

Cover the lid, then create the rush

Miami’s 42-yard Dulcich shot is the picture Spagnuolo cannot reprint. Cover-2 to take the deep middle, zone pressure on 3rd-and-medium to manufacture the sack that did not show up in Miami. Bolton (11 tackles) has to be the short-zone runner, not a spectator.

Win the ball again, and keep it this time

Sneed’s fumble and Karlaftis’ interception were the margin. Kansas City had the ball for 25:39. Against a 3-0 Raiders team, takeaways have to become drives that eat clock, not just scores that paper over 3-of-7.

  1. Open with Walker inside zone and a second-down play-action boot away from Crosby.
  2. Get Rice in the intermediate window the way the 7-for-88 tape did; do not wait until Q4 for the 34-yarder.
  3. Use Nourzad or Caliendo as an eligible changeup, not on back-to-back tells.
  4. Cover-2 on early downs after Miami’s deep-middle 42-yarder.
  5. Zone blitz on 3rd-and-6 to 3rd-and-8 — the down Miami converted.
  6. If Simmons is limited, slide protection and get Mahomes on the perimeter rather than a straight drop.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Kansas City is 3-0 because Patrick Mahomes just went 20-of-24 for 246 yards and two touchdowns in a 24-10 win at Miami, and because L. Sneed and George Karlaftis created the takeaways that turned a tight game into a two-score one. That is the part the power rankings will print. The part that actually travels to Las Vegas is thinner: 88 rushing yards on 25 attempts, 3-of-7 on third down, 18 first downs against 19, and 25:39 with the ball against Miami’s 34:21. Week 4 is a division road game against a 3-0 Raiders team at Allegiant, Sun Oct 4, 3:25 PM CT on CBS, and Maxx Crosby does not grade you on rating.

The Miami afternoon was a Mahomes accuracy tape, not a smash-mouth one. Kenneth Walker III still took 20 touches (18 rushes, 2 catches) and ran in the 10-yard score at Q1 12:54, but the line did not move Miami the way a 3-0 club wants to move a defense heading into a West game. Emmett Johnson added 6 rushes. The rest of the 88 yards were scraps. When the Chiefs needed a yard at the Miami 12, H. Nourzad reported eligible and Walker was stuffed. That is the snap Las Vegas will circle.

Steve Spagnuolo’s defense won the ball and lost the downs. Miami went 8-of-16 on third down, ran it 31 times for 119 yards, and kept Malik Willis on schedule with 9 rushes of his own. Kansas City answered with Nick Bolton’s 11 tackles, a fumble L. Sneed forced and recovered, and Karlaftis intercepting Willis at Q4 2:03. Zero sacks. One QB hit. That math plays differently when the opponent is 3-0 and playing at home.

Andy Reid already put the other problem on the record: Josh Simmons has a bulging disk in his lower back. Crosby is the reason that sentence matters more this week than it did in Miami. Eric Bieniemy’s desk is how to keep Walker on schedule without asking a compromised edge to hold up in a straight drop. Spagnuolo’s desk is how to get off the field on third down after Miami stayed in the game until the 11-yard score at Q4 2:55 made it 24-10.

The market is not confused. ESPN FPI posts Kansas City at 70.0 percent (Raiders 29.8). DraftKings has the Chiefs -4.5 on a 47.5 total. Polymarket still has Kansas City at 8.6 percent to win the Super Bowl and 16.0 percent to win the AFC. That is a favorite’s number with a probationary long ticket — exactly the temperature for a 3-0 team that has not yet proved it can hold the ball or protect the edge in its own division.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker downhill, early downs

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script at Allegiant vs. Las Vegas

Kansas City ran for 88 yards on 25 attempts in Miami; Walker still went 18-for-70 with the 10-yard score. Early-down zone is how you make Crosby play the run instead of Simmons’ disk.

Coaching point Bieniemy: zone steps, one cut, Walker through the first crease. Treat a Nourzad or Caliendo eligible report as a downhill snap. Finish like the 10-yard touchdown at Q1 12:54.

Offense

Boot Crosby off the edge

Situation 2nd-and-6 after a Walker zone look, right hash vs. Las Vegas

Arrowhead Pride has Kansas City using play-action as well as anyone this season, and Miami’s 5 QB hits with 0 sacks are the reason to move the launch point. The boot turns Crosby into a contain problem instead of a left-tackle problem.

Coaching point Sell Walker. Mahomes gets depth to the perimeter. Kelce or Gray in the flat, Rice on the over. Same tempo as the 20-of-24 tape — ball out before the hit arrives.

Offense

Four verts under the roof

Situation 2nd-and-7 vs. a single-high look at Allegiant

The Chiefs are the fourth-most explosive offense in the NFL through three weeks, and Mahomes’ indoor record is the tape Las Vegas has to live with. Rice (7-88), Worthy, Thornton, and Kelce can stress the deep middle the way the 48-yard Kelce shot at Q1 14:55 did.

Coaching point Seams from Kelce and Gray bend to the hash. Worthy and Thornton take the corners. Mahomes holds the safety with his eyes — 20-of-24 accuracy is built for this building.

Offense

Walker screen, easy offense

Situation 2nd-and-8 when Las Vegas pinches to Crosby

Walker’s 20 touches included 2 catches; that is the path to keep him on schedule without asking the edge to win every drop. Five QB hits and 0 sacks means the rush is close — the screen is the throw, not a deeper drop.

Coaching point Line leads in space. Rice and Worthy set the wall. Walker north-south after the catch, same decision speed as the 18-carry day. Johnson is the changeup, not the feature.

Defense

Cover-2, take the lid

Situation Early downs and 2nd-and-long vs. Las Vegas when they look deep middle

Miami hit Dulcich for 42 yards down the middle in the third quarter and went 8-of-16 on third down while holding the ball 34:21. Two deep, five under, rally to tackle — do not reprint that window for a 3-0 Raiders offense.

Coaching point Safeties split the posts. Corners squat and trail. Bolton (11 tackles in Miami) runs hook-to-curl. Jones and Karlaftis squeeze the run so Willis-style scrambles do not become first downs.

Defense

Simulated heat on third down

Situation 3rd-and-6 to 3rd-and-8, Raiders at midfield

Kansas City recorded 0 sacks and 1 QB hit in Miami while the Dolphins converted 8-of-16. This is the call to manufacture pressure against Las Vegas without emptying the coverage — show heat, drop a lineman, keep a lid.

Coaching point Spagnuolo: Jones or Karlaftis drops, a second-level rusher comes, Bolton stays in the short zone. Make the picture look like the Karlaftis interception at Q4 2:03 — a short throw into traffic — not a vacated middle.

Possession was the identity. Finishing is the problem. Denver is the exam.

Kansas City ran for 155 yards and still tied Seattle 9–9, then posted a 53 with a PUP’d interior and another imported tackle — the next Sunday that matters is Broncos, Arrowhead, ESPN.

The edge

The FPI nod and the home field-goal favorite are a vote for Arrowhead and the run tape, not for a finished roster — Norman-Lott on PUP and two mid-cutdown tackles mean Week 1 is a stress test.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

T
Seattle Seahawks Pre Week 4 · Sat Aug 29
KC 9–9

Kansas City and Seattle played a preseason finale that never found the end zone for the home side: Jason Myers opened with a 46-yarder, Butker answered from 28 and 24, Jalen Milroe hit Nick Vannett from four yards (Myers missed the extra point), and Butker drilled a 19-yarder in the fourth to freeze it at 9–9. The Chiefs outgained Seattle 353–266, ran for 155, and held the ball for 33:12, but they never converted those drives into a touchdown.

The useful film is the early-down tape, not the final score. Bieniemy’s group posted 21 first downs and went 9-of-17 on third down, which is how you sit on the ball for 33 minutes in August. That is an under-center, stay-ahead-of-the-chains operation: zone steps, one cut, and enough play-action threat to keep a defense from loading the box every snap. Smith’s 16-carry workload was not garbage-time cosmetics; it was the staff asking whether this backfield can be a real identity after last year’s 6–11 slog. One hundred fifty-five rushing yards against a Seattle group that managed only 54 of its own is the first honest ‘we can run it’ tape of the summer.

The pass game was functional and unfinished. Nussmeier’s 198 yards on 34 attempts is a checkdown-and-intermediate night, and the interception is the tax you pay when the only scoring is field goals. Armstrong’s five catches for 54 yards were the chain-moving profile this offense will need from whoever actually makes the 53 at receiver, but there was no shot down the field that changed Seattle’s shell. When the ball got inside the 20, Kansas City turned into a field-goal unit. A 19-yarder in the fourth quarter is not a two-minute drill; it is a drive that died on the doorstep. That is the red-zone install Andy Reid and Bieniemy have to clean before Denver, because division openers do not give you four possessions inside the 30 and a handshake.

Spagnuolo’s side won the complementary fight even if the scoreboard did not. Seattle’s 13 first downs and 4-of-11 third-down night are the numbers of a defense that rallied, set edges, and made the visitor one-dimensional. R. Mason Thomas’s sack was the splash; the 54 rushing yards allowed was the substance. You can live with a Milroe-to-Vannett dart from the four if the front is stuffing the run and the coverage is not springing leaks. The missed PAT is why it finished 9–9 instead of 10–9, but the coaching tape is simpler: this defense can travel in September if the interior rotation holds up without Norman-Lott.

Chiefs.com’s own recap framed it as a night for the young players, and that matches the usage. Pre Week 4 is when the 53 is still a rumor and the staff is writing the last evaluations in ink. The evaluations that stuck were the ones that move chains and set edges, not the ones that pad box scores. The evaluations that did not stick are now on the waiver wire and in the practice-squad queue.

What held up

  • Under-center run identity: 155 rushing yards, 21 first downs, 33:12 of possession
  • Third-down efficiency on offense (9-of-17) versus Seattle’s 4-of-11
  • Run defense that limited Seattle to 54 yards and kept the game in field-goal range
  • Young-player evaluation snaps that produced a real workload for Smith and a sack from Thomas

What broke

  • Zero offensive touchdowns despite multiple trips inside field-goal range
  • Nussmeier’s interception in a game that had no margin for empty possessions
  • No explosive passing answers once Seattle sat in a shell
  • Interior depth questions that the box score could not hide heading into cutdown

The run game is no longer a talking point

One hundred fifty-five yards and a 33-minute possession edge is the first full-game evidence that Bieniemy’s downhill identity is more than camp padding. If that carries into the regular season, Kansas City can keep Mahomes in manageable down-and-distance against Denver instead of living in obvious passing downs.

Red-zone offense is still a field-goal operation

Butker from 28, 24, and 19 means the Chiefs moved it and did not finish. Against a division rival on ESPN, three trips that end in kicks are how you donate a game you otherwise controlled.

Spagnuolo’s front set a September floor

Holding Seattle to 54 rushing yards and 13 first downs is the profile of a defense that can carry a still-assembling offense. The caveat is personnel: that floor has to survive without Norman-Lott’s interior push for the opening month.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

6-11

The preseason is closed, the 53 is posted, and Kansas City is a 6–11 club with a real run tape, a borrowed tackle rotation, and a national-TV division opener before the roster even feels finished. The next two weeks are not camp anymore; they are an install sprint with a practice squad to fill and a PUP to scheme around.

What we need to work on

Finish the drives the run game is creating

The Seattle tape is 155 rushing yards and three Butker kicks. Bieniemy has to script condensed-split, tight-end, and play-action answers inside the 20 so Mahomes is not handing a 9–9 profile to Denver on ESPN.

Turn two imported tackles into a protection plan

Pounds arrived Sunday from Baltimore on top of the Ezeudu deal. That is not depth; that is an admission. Week 1 protection has to be decided in walkthroughs now, not in the first series against a Broncos edge.

Replace Norman-Lott’s interior push for four-plus weeks

PUP to open the year is a long-term protection play and a short-term schematic hole. Harris earning a 53 spot is the first answer; Spagnuolo still has to manufacture pressure with simulated heat instead of a wrecking interior.

Build the 16-man practice squad on purpose

Armstrong’s five catches in the finale and the rest of the cut pile become Monday’s waiver-and-squad work. Skill-job insurance behind a three-QB 53 is how you survive the first injury wave before Miami in Week 3.

What to think about

Is a three-QB 53 a luxury or a tell?

Mahomes, Fields, and Nussmeier all made it; Oladokun did not. That is a lot of roster real estate unless Fields is a real package player or the staff simply did not trust the rest of the 90. Fans should ask what those two backup spots are costing the receiver and tackle rooms heading into Denver.

Did the 53 surprises age well past cutdown Sunday?

Local reporting framed the initial roster as shocking in spots and curious in others. Camp form got people kept. Week 1 against Denver is where those calls either look like Veach seeing something or like a club that cut the wrong kind of veteran.

How much of the run-game jump is scheme versus personnel?

The backfield is being sold as the most improved room on the roster even after Demercado’s cut. If it is scheme — Bieniemy’s zone, Reid’s under-center cadence — it should travel against Denver. If it was August looks, the -3 number gets uncomfortable fast.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Week 1 at Arrowhead on ESPN is the first night this 53 has to look like a plan: a division rival, a modest home favorite, and a Chiefs team that just spent 60 minutes proving it can run and 60 minutes proving it cannot finish. The assignment is complementary football, not fireworks.

How they match up

Kansas City wants this game in the 20s: inside zone, play-action, and Spagnuolo shells that take away the shot and rally. Denver, as an AFC West visitor, will try to wreck that math at the edges — test the Pounds/Ezeudu tackle experiment, sit on Bieniemy’s early-down run looks, and make Mahomes beat Patrick Surtain II in the half-field. The leverage is time of possession. If the Chiefs recreate the 33-minute Seattle profile, Mahomes stays on schedule and the Broncos’ pass rush never tees off. If Kansas City falls behind and the new tackles have to pass-set for 40 attempts, the 6–11 tape comes back in a hurry.

Stay ahead of the chains so the new tackles are blocking on their terms

The Seattle night was 9-of-17 on third down because first down was a run down. Script inside zone and a screen until Denver’s front has to respect the mesh point, then boot Mahomes. Do not ask Pounds and Ezeudu to survive obvious passing downs in series one.

Make Surtain the boundary and attack the other three-quarters of the field

You do not win Week 1 by throwing fades at Surtain. Four verts and crossers have to stress the post safety and the opposite corner while the run action holds the linebackers. Mahomes’ job is patience, not hero ball.

Manufacture interior pressure without Norman-Lott

Spagnuolo cannot wait on a four-man rush from a PUP’d interior. Simulated pressure, a dropping lineman, and Harris/Thomas as the chaos pieces are how you keep Denver’s quarterback from sitting on Cover-2 and picking on the tackling.

Red-zone personnel has to be decided before the national window, not during it

Three field goals inside the 30 is a coaching problem. Reid needs a two-tight, under-center package and a sneak/iso answer so Butker is the closer, not the entire offense.

  1. Open in 12/21 personnel under center: inside zone to the closed side, get the new tackles moving on zone steps instead of pass-sets.
  2. Second series, same look, play-action boot to the field — easy throw for Mahomes, punish Denver if they crash the run.
  3. Early green-zone: condensed splits, no empty, force Denver to tackle on the two-yard line instead of covering space.
  4. Second-and-medium: RB spacing screen if the Broncos pinches. Cash the 155-yard run threat without asking the tackles to hold for three seconds.
  5. Nickel downs for Spagnuolo: Cover-2 shell on early downs, zone-blitz the first long-yardage. Do not give Denver a rhythm throw over the ball.
  6. If trailing in the fourth: four verts versus a two-high look, but still keep a checkdown. Nussmeier’s INT is the cautionary tape — empty possessions kill this favorite.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

A 6–11 season does not get rewritten in one August night, but it does leave fingerprints. Against Seattle the Chiefs finally looked like an Eric Bieniemy team between the tackles — 21 first downs, 33:12 of possession, 155 rushing yards — and then they settled for Harrison Butker from 28, 24, and 19. The 9–9 result was honest: the identity showed up, the end zone did not. Sunday made the rest of it official. Brett Veach posted the initial 53, put Omarr Norman-Lott on PUP, and traded for offensive tackle Diego Pounds because the edges of this line are still being built in public. Camp is no longer about reps. It is about whether that 53 can become a Week 1 plan before Denver walks into GEHA Field at Arrowhead on ESPN.

The tape from Friday is the cleanest look we have at what Bieniemy wants this offense to be. Under-center, downhill, stay on schedule, and let the clock become a weapon. Brashard Smith’s 16 carries for 72 yards were the headline, but the complementary number is Seattle’s 54 rushing yards the other way. Steve Spagnuolo’s group squeezed the Seahawks into 13 first downs and 4-of-11 on third down. That is a defense that can carry early Sundays if the offense keeps games in the 20s. The problem is the other column: Garrett Nussmeier went 22-of-34 for 198 with a pick, Andrew Armstrong led the receivers with 54 yards, and nobody punched it in. Preseason Week 4 is not a referendum on Patrick Mahomes, but it is a referendum on complementary football, and Kansas City left points on its own grass.

Sunday’s roster was the other tape. Three quarterbacks — Mahomes, Justin Fields, Nussmeier — and a notable cut in Chris Oladokun. A running-back room that local reporting still calls the most improved unit on the roster even after Emari Demercado was waived. Two recent offensive-line imports, Joshua Ezeudu and now Pounds, which is how a club admits the tackle room was not ready to open a division season. Inside, Norman-Lott starting the year on PUP removes a second-level push Spagnuolo was counting on. Marcus Harris playing his way onto the 53 is the other side of that coin: someone has to eat those interior snaps in September.

Everything now tilts toward September 15. ESPN FPI gives Kansas City a 59.4 percent nod at home; DraftKings has the Chiefs -3 with a 42.5 total. Those are modest numbers for a Mahomes home opener, and Polymarket still prices this club at 6.3 percent to win the 2027 title and 9.5 percent to win the AFC — long-shot juice that matches a 6–11 hangover more than a finished camp. A strong two weeks of install, a practice squad that actually replenishes the skill jobs, and a run game that survives Denver’s front would make those prices look slow. A leaky tackle rotation and a red zone that keeps kicking would make them look right.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-center inside zone, closed side

Situation Series one, 1st-and-10 vs. Denver at Arrowhead — establish the Seattle run identity before the Broncos can pin their ears back at the new tackles

Kansas City just ran for 155 yards and held the ball 33:12 against Seattle. That is the entire Week 1 math: keep Mahomes on schedule so Pounds and Ezeudu are zone-stepping instead of pass-setting, and keep Spagnuolo’s defense off the field while Norman-Lott sits on PUP. Smith’s 16-carry finale is the personnel proof that Bieniemy will feed this look.

Coaching point Bieniemy’s point to the line is simple: zone steps, one cut, do not chase the bounce until the backer is wrong. Reid wants the first script to look like a run team so Denver’s front cannot tee off on a three-QB roster that just imported two tackles in a week.

Offense

Play-action boot off the zone look

Situation 2nd-and-6 after a live run, midfield vs. Denver — cash the 155-yard threat and get Mahomes on the perimeter away from a crashing edge

If Denver sells out to stop inside zone, the boot is the Reid/Bieniemy answer that does not ask the new tackles to hold a five-step drop. It also keeps Surtain out of the picture by moving the launch point and giving Mahomes an easy flat/sideline read instead of a boundary fade. Fields exists on this 53 as a package threat; even if he does not play, the boot action has to be in the script so Denver cannot key Mahomes’ drop.

Coaching point Sell the mesh point. Mahomes has to carry the fake through the linebackers’ feet, not glance at the flat early. The tight end’s job is to occupy the first contain so the boot is actually a throw, not a scramble drill for a patched tackle room.

Offense

RB spacing screen as the easy-offense valve

Situation 2nd-and-8 vs. a pinched Denver front that has started crashing to stop Smith — get the ball out before the imported tackles have to win a one-on-one pass set

The finale was complementary and slow: 198 net passing yards, no explosives, and a defense that will now game-plan the run. A screen is how Bieniemy punishes that adjustment without asking Nussmeier’s checkdown offense — or Mahomes behind two new tackles — to win a dropback down. It also protects a club that just spent roster spots on three quarterbacks instead of extra linemen.

Coaching point The line has to be a wall, not a suggestion. Pounds and Ezeudu are in this building to get out in space and finish; if they stall at the mesh, the screen is a loss of four and we are back in obvious passing downs. Backs: catch, north, do not dance.

Offense

Four verts vs. Denver two-high / Cover-3 look

Situation 3rd-and-6 or a two-minute possession vs. Denver, ball on the plus-40 — stress the post safety and the opposite corner, leave Surtain as the cone

Kansas City generated 21 first downs against Seattle and still never punched it in; part of that was a pass game that lived underneath (Armstrong’s 5-for-54 profile). Against Denver you will see a shell designed to take away the shot and make Mahomes dink. Four verts is the Reid answer that holds the safety and opens the seam without throwing the boundary fade at Surtain. It is also the look that makes a 42.5 total feel light if Kansas City ever finishes.

Coaching point Mahomes: the ball is the seam or the checkdown, not a hero ball into Surtain. Outside receivers must win on time, not win pretty. Bieniemy should tag this off run action so the linebackers cannot squat on the intermediate. No empty — keep a back in for the new tackles.

Defense

Spagnuolo Cover-2 shell on early downs

Situation 1st-and-10, Denver at midfield — take away the shot, rally to tackle, and hide a PUP’d interior on early downs

Seattle’s night was 13 first downs, 212 net pass yards, and 54 on the ground. That is a rally-and-tackle defense, not a chaos defense. Without Norman-Lott, Spagnuolo cannot live in a four-man rush that does not get home. Two-deep, five-under keeps the ESPN opener in field-goal range — the same math that made 9–9 possible — and asks Harris and the rest of the 53 to win on the two-yard tackle, not the three-yard sack.

Coaching point Corners: funnel, do not gamble. Safeties split the post and drive on the first throw. The front’s job on early downs is two-gap and squeeze, not chase a sack that is not there. Thomas can hunt on later downs; this down is structure.

Defense

Simulated pressure, drop a lineman

Situation 3rd-and-7 vs. Denver, plus territory — manufacture a hurry without Norman-Lott so Mahomes is not stuck in a track meet

The only sack on the Seattle tape belonged to Thomas; the interior depth chart just lost Norman-Lott to PUP. Spagnuolo’s signature answer is show heat, drop a lineman, and let a second-level rusher occupy the protection that just spent a week learning two new tackles on the other sideline. Harris earned the 53 in part because this front needs a snap-eater who can also drop. If this call hits, Kansas City keeps the 42.5 total honest and the -3 favorite intact.

Coaching point Sell four-down, then steal a rusher from the second level. The dropping lineman has to get depth — a two-yard drop is a seam. Secondary: play the fire-zone rules, do not freelance into Surtain’s counterpart and give up the glance. This is complementary football for a club that just kicked three field goals and called it a night.

The storylines

What we’re watching

53-man

The first 53 is a draft, not a finished product

Kansas City posted the initial roster Sunday with enough surprises that the local reaction split between winners, losers, and curious calls. Things can still move before Week 1, which is the point: this list has to survive Denver, not just the cutdown deadline.

PUP

Norman-Lott’s PUP is a September schematic problem

Parking him on PUP protects the club through the injury, but it also removes interior push for the opener and beyond. Spagnuolo’s first month is now about simulated pressure and the players who actually made the 53, starting with Harris.

OL

Two tackle trades in the cutdown window tell on the room

Ezeudu came in last week; Pounds came in from Baltimore on Sunday. That is how a front office admits the edges were not ready for a Mahomes dropback in a division game. The next two weeks are an audition, not a depth chart on paper.

QB

Mahomes headlines a three-man room that cost a roster spot

Mahomes, Fields, and Nussmeier all stuck; Oladokun is out. Nussmeier just ran the finale (22-of-34, 198, one pick), so the staff has film on the emergency option. The debate is whether Fields is a package or whether three QBs is expensive heading into a thin tackle room.

RB

The backfield is the clearest camp win — even after a cut

Demercado is gone and the room is still being described as the most improved on the roster. Smith’s 16-for-72 in the finale is the on-field argument. Denver will be the first defense that treats that tape as a scouting report instead of August noise.

PS

The practice squad is the rest of the 53

Sixteen spots, waiver claims after noon Arrowhead time, and names from the finale — Armstrong included — still in the mix. A club with $4.1 million in reported cap space and two new tackles cannot treat the squad as a parking lot.

Market

The board likes Kansas City. The board is not in love.

ESPN FPI sits at 59.4 percent and DraftKings at -3, O/U 42.5. Polymarket still has the Chiefs at 6.3 percent to win the Super Bowl. That is a bounce-back price, not a contender price, and it will not move until this 53 finishes a drive in orange.

Andy Reid vs. a division opener on ESPN

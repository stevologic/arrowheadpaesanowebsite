The story so far

Always looking ahead

Training camp at Missouri Western is not nostalgia — it is triage. A 6-11 season left the Chiefs with a clear checklist: get Patrick Mahomes healthy and timing sharp, stop living in third-and-long after 47 sacks a year ago, and turn overlooked roster questions into actual depth before the Broncos arrive on ESPN in Week 1. Rookies and select veterans have already closed the pre-camp window; full squad work now decides who is scheme-ready when the lights hit Arrowhead.

The offensive identity under Andy Reid and Eric Bieniemy has to re-center on manageable downs. Film and outside noise already flag the run game as a multi-year soft spot; Mina Kimes’ critique only echoed what Chiefs fans saw in 2025. That puts camp carries and pass-pro reps at a premium for the backfield — including rookie E.J. Smith carving his own path — and for an offensive line that must prove it can win inside zone and keep Mahomes from scrambling for survival. Play-action boots and easy-offense screens are not window dressing here; they are the bridge from a battered 2025 pocket to a functional September script.

Defensively, Steve Spagnuolo opens camp already short a body. Linebacker Ethan Downs tore an ACL and landed on IR alongside tight end John Michael Gyllenborg, creating two open camp roster spots and shrinking the margin for error on the second level. That elevates every nickel and safety rep — Jaden Hicks’ Year-3 jump included — because Spags will still want simulated pressures and Cover-2 shells ready for Denver’s skill group on opening night. Depth chart honesty now prevents desperation packages in Week 1.

Stakes bleed into the specialist and fringe-skill rooms too. Harrison Butker, the league’s third-highest-paid kicker, enters a prove-it camp after a down 2025. Jacob De Jesus arrives with a 108-catch résumé and real versatility bets on special teams and offense. Andrew Armstrong is the classic camp body who could steal snaps if the receiver room needs a climber. None of these are headline names next to Mahomes — all of them can decide whether Kansas City is a 59-percent FPI home favorite that looks the part or a fragile -3 that sweats a divisional opener.

Look past Denver and the schedule does not ease up: Colts on NBC in Week 2, then @ Miami. A strong camp — clean pocket rules, a defined RB committee, rookies who can play early without panicking — is what makes that opening stretch a launch pad instead of a 0-2 hole. The through-line from St. Joseph to September is simple: protect the quarterback, run with intent, and leave no starting job undecided when the Broncos walk into GEHA Field.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Opener Boot After Zone Look

Situation Week 1 script, early downs vs. Denver — sell run from under center, get Mahomes on the move

After 47 sacks last year and ongoing run-game skepticism, bootlegs let Mahomes throw on rhythm without a collapsing pocket and punish Broncos edge defenders who climb hard vs. zone action. Camp install tempo around Mahomes’ rehab makes moving-pocket throws a safer ramp than pure dropback max-protect.

Coaching point Bieniemy wants the TE chip-and-release tight and the playside tackle selling zone long enough to freeze the DE; Mahomes’ first read is the flat/arrow, second is the deep over — no hero ball if Denver sits under.

Offense

Early-Down Inside Zone Identity

Situation 1st-and-10 vs. Denver base personnel — establish the call that makes play-action real

Mina Kimes’ run-game critique mirrors the 2025 tape; without honest zone steps, Denver will tee off on dropbacks. E.J. Smith and the camp backs must win the first cut so Reid can keep 12/21 personnel on the field and slow Broncos pressure games.

Coaching point Offensive line zone-steps vertical, not lateral — one cut, no dancing. Smith’s coaching point is pressing the line of scrimmage before the cut; guards must win the double without lunging after a season of lost early downs.

Offense

Mesh vs. Broncos Man/Pressure Looks

Situation 2nd-and-medium or two-minute — Denver shows man or late rotation

If Mahomes’ timing is still climbing in camp, mesh gives rub free releases and an RB outlet against Spags-style pressure that Denver may mirror. Camp climbers like Andrew Armstrong and Jacob De Jesus earn roster traction by winning the short crosser and sitting in the hole.

Coaching point Reid’s detail: inside releases must brush without obvious picks; RB checks to the first green grass under the mesh. Mahomes throws hot if the MIKE walks — no holding the ball for the corner route to clear.

Offense

Easy-Offense RB Screen

Situation Long yardage or after a Broncos free runner — punish over-aggression

A year of sacks trained opposing edges to pin their ears back; the screen package is how KC turns that aggression into free yards while Mahomes’ pocket trust rebuilds. It also grades camp linemen as space athletes, not just phone-booth blockers, before the Colts and Dolphins weeks stack up.

Coaching point Guards and center must get depth and finish in space; the RB (committee winner from camp) sets the path off the first block, not before. Bieniemy will tag this off max-look protection so Denver cannot key formation alone.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 Shell Late

Situation 1st-and-10 or two-minute, Denver wanting explosives into Arrowhead air

With Ethan Downs lost for the year to a torn ACL, Kansas City needs cleaner back-end rules and fewer hero fill voids. Jaden Hicks’ Year-3 step is exactly this: hold the deep half, rally to the intermediate, and keep Denver from chunking a defense that cannot afford second-level mistakes.

Coaching point Spagnuolo’s point to Hicks and the corners: corners funnel, do not get stacked; safeties split the post and are violent downhill on the checkdown. No free crossers under the two-deep — that is where 2025 explosives lived.

Defense

Simulated Pressure on Denver’s Protection

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 3rd-and-8 — show five/six, rush four with a second-level inserter

Downs’ IR placement thins true rush depth, so Spagnuolo leans harder into look-pressure that creates free runners without emptying the coverage. Camp will crown which linebacker or nickel can insert on time; that winner becomes a Week 1 staple against Denver and a template for the Buffalo/road stretch later.

Coaching point Show blitz from the boundary, drop the end into the flat, and send the off-ball through the vacated A/B gap. Communication post-snap is the camp teach — if the inserter is late, Hicks and the back end are exposed. Spags will live in the walkthrough film on who hesitates.

The storylines

What we’re watching

QB Ramp

Mahomes’ rehab sets the install tempo

Every camp period orbits Patrick Mahomes’ availability and timing. The fantasy and roster trackers already frame the offense’s ceiling as inseparable from how fast he builds full-speed chemistry; Reid will script boots, meshes, and quick-game answers so the first team does not wait on perfection before Week 1 Denver.

OL Truth

Forty-seven sacks demand a new pocket standard

Arrowhead Pride’s 47-days countdown is also a scar count: Chiefs quarterbacks took 47 sacks last season. Camp one-on-ones and seven-man protection rules are less about depth charts than about whether Bieniemy’s early-down calls can stay on schedule against a Broncos front that will test every edge.

IR Fallout

Downs’ ACL reshapes the linebacker battle

Ethan Downs’ torn ACL and IR placement, paired with TE John Michael Gyllenborg’s IR move, opened two camp roster spots and removed a promising defender right as jobs came up for grabs. Spagnuolo now needs veterans and undrafted bodies to prove they can handle zone-blitz inserts without the developmental cushion.

RB Identity

Run-game skepticism meets E.J. Smith’s moment

Outside analysts have already voiced fans’ fear that Kansas City still lacks a true run identity in the Mahomes era. Rookie E.J. Smith’s path differs from his father’s, but camp reps on inside zone and protection will decide if he is early-down help or just a preseason headline before the Broncos series.

Specialist Heat

Butker’s career-stakes camp

Harrison Butker enters 2026 as a virtual roster lock with real pressure: high pay, uneven 2025, and no hiding in September. Kick operation consistency is a quiet camp battle that becomes loud on Monday Night Football if short fields stall.

Camp Climbers

De Jesus and Armstrong hunting real roles

The club is betting on Jacob De Jesus’ versatility after a 108-catch season elsewhere, while Andrew Armstrong works to force his way into the receiver conversation. Both are classic St. Joseph stories — win the scout-team and returns periods, and Week 1 dress-list math gets interesting.

Safety Step

Jaden Hicks, Year 3 under the radar no more

Hicks has been a role player through two seasons; camp is the audition for a larger piece of Spagnuolo’s two-deep and robber rules. With linebacker depth dinged, a Hicks jump stabilizes the back end for Denver’s vertical answers and the tough road stretch that follows.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid/Bieniemy early-down contract

Camp must marry inside zone to boot and screen tags so Mahomes is not asked to win every snap from a static pocket against Denver. The opener script is where Reid historically hides protection scars — install discipline now decides if that still works.

Spagnuolo depth after Downs IR

Losing Ethan Downs to a torn ACL forces earlier answers at off-ball linebacker and more simulated pressure from nickel/safety. Spags will use camp to stress-test who can wear the green-dot communication when the base group thins.

Special teams as roster math

With two IR spots already burning camp bodies, returner/gunner versatility from names like Jacob De Jesus is not luxury — it is how fringe skill players stick when Gyllenborg and Downs are down.

Butker’s operational standard

Andy Reid’s staff cannot treat the kicking game as autopilot after Butker’s 2025 dip; camp operation reps and protection rules need the same honesty as the OL battles before a prime-time divisional opener.

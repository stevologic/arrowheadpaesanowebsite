The story so far

Always looking ahead

Training camp at Missouri Western is not nostalgia week—it’s triage and install for a Chiefs team that finished 6-11 and now has to prove the Mahomes-Reid core still dictates AFC West terms. Through the first two full-squad acclimation practices, the tape the building wants is clear: more under-center work, a screen game designed to get skill players free in space, and defensive veterans who can teach a young secondary how Steve Spagnuolo wants the back end to communicate. Eric Bieniemy’s early absence while Mia Bieniemy recovers has been the human backdrop; Andy Reid’s update that she’s doing better steadies the room as the offense still has to own its new early-down personality before pads and before the Broncos arrive on Sept. 15.

The offensive conversation in St. Joe starts under center. Early reports flag the exact change fans have begged for—more under-center alignments that sell downhill zone and open the boot game for Patrick Mahomes—while the screen package is already drawing buzz as a way to protect the quarterback and manufacture explosives without asking the OL to win every one-on-one. Kenneth Walker’s early camp has been limited by the non-padded acclimation rules, but the design intent is obvious: get the back the ball with linemen and receivers as lead blockers, then layer play-action off the same pictures Denver will have to respect in Week 1.

Roster edges are forming in the margins that decide 53-man cuts and Week 1 packages. Undrafted and reserve receivers—Cyrus Allen standing out through two days, Andrew Armstrong earning notice, Omari Evans bet on for pure athleticism—are forcing the staff to watch the depth chart past the obvious names. On the OL, Creed Humphrey’s praise for rookie free agent Kahlil Benson has turned a quiet body into a real camp story, especially with Day 2 notes already tracking an offensive-line shift. Those battles matter because the Broncos openers will test first-down masculinity and third-down protection before Kansas City ever sees Indy in Week 2 or a road trip to Miami in Week 3.

Defensively, Spagnuolo’s priority was never mystery—it was a safety who can install the language and lead. Alohi Gilman is already validating the target: veteran communication, urgency with young DBs, and the kind of pre-snap clarity that lets simulated pressures and Cover-2 shells look identical until the snap. Chris Jones landing at No. 43 on the NFL Top 100 is a reminder the interior still tilts games when the coverage behind him is clean. Camp excellence here is not about September moral victories; it is about walking into a home opener as a legitimate -3 favorite with an identity Denver has to solve in 10 days of prep, not three weeks of excuses.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Base Under-Center Inside Zone

Situation Week 1 opener, early downs (1st-and-10 / 2nd-and-short) vs. Denver’s base front at Arrowhead

Camp reporting says the Chiefs are finally living under center after fans demanded it; that only pays if the first call is honest downhill zone with Kenneth Walker and an OL already tinkering through a Day 2 shift. Against Denver it establishes the run pictures that later sell boots and keep Mahomes out of obvious passing downs in a KC -3, 42.5 script.

Coaching point Humphrey’s unit must win first-step zone timing—Benson and whoever wins the swing reps have to finish to the second level so Walker can one-cut without Mahomes bailing early.

Offense

Under-Center Play-Action Boot

Situation 2nd-and-medium after an IZ run look has been shown to Denver’s LBs

The same under-center emphasis that is defining St. Joe install naturally feeds boot action for Mahomes—move the launch point, stress Denver’s end man, and hit the flat/intermediate window before their rush can pin the edge. Coming off 6-11, easy-rhythm answers matter more than hero scrambles on national TV.

Coaching point Reid/Bieniemy want identical zone sell from the OL and TE; Mahomes’ first read is the flat outlet, second is the crosser—don’t peek the deep cross late if Denver’s hook defender sits.

Tunnel/Perimeter RB Screen

Situation Obvious passing down or after Denver shows upfield edge pressure in the opener

Arrowhead Pride’s camp notes already flag genuine excitement for a revamped screen game while Walker waits on full pads; it’s the cleanest way to punish aggressive Broncos rushers and protect a still-settling OL. In a 42.5 total game, manufactured explosives beat dropback grinders.

Coaching point OL must get out clean—no holding in space—while the WR crack replaces the force defender; Walker’s job is catch, north-south, refuse the east-west waste that kills camp-designed chunk plays.

Offense

Four Verticals vs. Denver Cover-3 Look

Situation 3rd-and-long / two-minute, Broncos in a three-deep shell to wall off explosives

If early-down under-center work has forced Denver into softer zones late, four verts attacks the deep middle hole and the seams where camp WR risers (Allen, Armstrong, Evans) can actually earn their first real snaps. Mahomes has historically feasted on hesitant safeties—five 2026 opponents still lack a win over him, and Denver can’t sit timid if the seams are stressed.

Coaching point Outside receivers push the corners; inside seams bend to the hash on time. No freelancing—Spags’ counterpart will rotate late, so Mahomes must trigger on the first clear window, not the perfect one.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 Shell, Rally Tackle

Situation Early downs vs. Denver 12/11 personnel when KC wants to take away the deep shot and force horizontal offense

With Gilman already leading the young secondary the way Spagnuolo drafted him to, Cover-2 becomes teachable fast: two deep, five under, no cheap explosives on ESPN. Jones’ interior disruption only cashes if the back end finishes tackles and eliminates vertical shots behind the rush.

Coaching point Gilman and the opposite safety must split the field on hash rules; corners soft-pedal then disrupt; linebackers drive on the throw. Spags will flag any missed fit louder than a completion—camp habits become Week 1 leaks.

Defense

Simulated Pressure / Zone Blitz

Situation 3rd-and-medium, Denver trying to protect and push the ball to the sticks against KC’s nickel

Spagnuolo’s signature answers slow-developing protection only if the secondary speaks one language—Gilman’s early leadership is the green light to show heat, drop a lineman, and bring a second-level runner without busting voids. Jones as the featured interior piece (Top 100 nod) makes the fake rush look real.

Coaching point Sell the blitz with DB stem, then replace rushers with dropping DL into the throwing lane Mahomes’ counterpart loves. Young DBs must trust Gilman’s checks—no freelancing into the hole Spags just designed to occupy.

The storylines

What we’re watching

OL Identity

Under-center shift is the camp headline

Two practices in, the Chiefs are deliberately living under center after fans spent a year begging for it. That picture has to hold when pads come on—if the zone steps and PA boots are real, Denver’s first-down defense faces a different Chiefs offense than the one that limped to 6-11.

Screen Game

Revamped screens as easy offense

With acclimation limiting true run thump, the building is already excited about a juiced screen package to get Kenneth Walker and the skill group free. It’s complementary football: punish Broncos upfield rushers and keep Mahomes out of obvious passing downs in a prime-time opener.

WR Depth

Allen, Armstrong, Evans forcing eyes sideways

Cyrus Allen has been an early surprise through two days, Andrew Armstrong is capitalizing on every rep, and Omari Evans is the athleticism dart the club is betting translates. Somebody from this tier has to win a real package before Week 1 or the rotation stays brittle.

OL Camp Body

Humphrey’s Benson hype is loud for a reason

When Creed Humphrey publicly elevates rookie free agent Kahlil Benson, the staff listens. Pair that with Day 2 offensive-line shift notes and you have a camp battle that can decide swing-tackle and depth reliability against Denver’s front.

Secondary Glue

Gilman already doing the Spags job

Steve Spagnuolo wanted Alohi Gilman to walk in as the veteran translator—and early camp says that’s exactly what’s happening with the young secondary. Communication wins simulated pressures; silence loses them in Week 1.

Human Element

Bieniemy room steadied by Mia update

Eric Bieniemy missed the first full-squad day while with family after Mia Bieniemy was shot at home; Reid’s word that she’s doing better matters for the building’s focus as install accelerates.

Market Check

Favorite, not invincible

ESPN FPI sits KC at 59.4% for the opener and DraftKings posts -3 at 42.5 O/U—respect without a crown. Polymarket’s 6.8% title and 9.5% AFC prices match a 6-11 hangover: earn September before anyone rewrites April.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid/Bieniemy early-down identity

The staff is answering a year of fan noise with real under-center volume in St. Joe; the coaching win is keeping that picture identical between run, boot, and screen so Denver can’t late-check the boot.

Bieniemy’s availability and room focus

Reid’s update that Mia Bieniemy is doing better steadies an offense that still has to install without drama; the next step is full-speed communication once Eric is fully locked back into the daily script.

Spagnuolo’s safety translator

Gilman was brought in to lead a young secondary immediately, and early camp feedback says Spags is already seeing the communication he paid for—critical before Week 1 motion from Denver.

OL depth development

Day 2 line shifts plus Humphrey’s public push for Kahlil Benson tell you the coaches are still solving swing reliability; that depth will be tested the first time a starter goes down in September.

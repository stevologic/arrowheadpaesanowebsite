Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

T
Seattle Seahawks Pre Week 4 · Sat Aug 29
KC 9-9

Kansas City won the math and tied the game. At Arrowhead, the Chiefs posted 21 first downs, 353 yards, 155 on the ground, and 33:12 of possession, then left with three Harrison Butker field goals and a 9-9 final because they never punched a ball into the end zone. Seattle needed only 13 first downs and 266 yards, got a 46-yard Jason Myers kick, a 4-yard Jalen Milroe-to-Nick Vannett touchdown with a missed extra point, and enough red-zone resistance to turn a control game into a preseason shrug.

The identity on tape was downhill and on schedule. Nine of 17 on third down is how you sit on the ball for 33 minutes. Brashard Smith’s 16-72 was not window dressing; it was a one-cut, early-down diet that kept Kansas City in 2nd-and-medium and let the line climb to the second level. Andrew Armstrong’s 5-54 was the only receiver production that showed up in the box, which tells you this was a run-and-manage night, not a concept night. When a finale looks like that, you either have a plan for Week 1 or you have a backup quarterback taking sacks in a game that does not count.

The finishing problem is not subtle. Butker’s kicks came from 28, 24, and 19 yards. Those are red-zone possessions that died inside the 10, not explosive-play droughts. Nussmeier going 22-of-34 for 198 with an interception fits the picture: enough accuracy to move the chains, not enough threat to make a goal-line defense expand. Seattle’s only touchdown was a 4-yard throw to a tight end. That is the same area of the field Kansas City could not attack. If Bieniemy’s run game is going to be the 2026 story, it has to become a touchdown story the first time Mahomes is in the huddle.

Defensively, Spagnuolo’s group did the preseason job. Fifty-four rushing yards and 4-of-11 on third down meant Seattle never lived in the rhythm Payton will try to create on Monday. R Mason Thomas’s sack was the only name in the pressure column, which is a reminder that this tape was not a starter-versus-starter stress test. The missed Myers extra point after the Vannett score is why Kansas City got out with a tie instead of a loss, not because the red-zone defense was airtight. Take the run-stopping and the possession win. Do not take the 9-9 as proof the call sheet is ready for Denver.

What held up

  • Early-down inside run that produced 155 yards and kept Kansas City ahead of the sticks
  • Third-down efficiency at 9-of-17, which is how you win a time-of-possession fight 33:12 to 26:48
  • Spagnuolo’s front limiting Seattle to 54 rushing yards and 4-of-11 on third down
  • Butker converting from 28, 24, and 19 when the offense stalled inside the 20

What broke

  • Red-zone finishing: 21 first downs, zero offensive touchdowns
  • Nussmeier’s interception and a 198-yard passing night that never stressed the boundary
  • Goal-to-go answers after driving into Butker’s 19-yard range
  • Pass-rush depth beyond the one named sack from R Mason Thomas

Possession is the identity. The paint is the problem.

Twenty-one first downs and 33:12 of clock is a Bieniemy game. Three field goals from inside the 30 is not. Week 1 against a healthy Payton defense will not give Kansas City extra downs to figure out the last eight yards.

The run game showed a lead back even without Walker.

Smith’s 16-72 and a 155-yard team total mean the zone steps and the second-level climbs are installed. Kenneth Walker inherits that look on Monday, and the staff’s public confidence in the young room is no longer theoretical.

This was not a Mahomes tape, so do not grade it like one.

Nussmeier’s 198 and the pick are August. The transferable pieces are the first-down run, the third-down efficiency, and the red-zone stall. Those three travel. The dink-and-dunk passing game does not have to.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

6-11 (2025) · 0-0 (2026 regular season)

The regular season starts 6-11 in the rearview and 0-0 on the board, with a Monday night division game that the market only barely trusts. Kansas City has a run identity on tape, a quarterback who did not need August, and a line-and-safety combination that the local press already does not trust against Denver. The work this week is finishing and protection. The thought is whether this club is overlooked or correctly ranked.

What we need to work on

Red-zone and goal-to-go answers

The Seattle tape is three kicks from 28, 24, and 19. Against Payton on Monday, that is a 9-9 night that becomes a loss. Bieniemy has to marry Walker’s inside zone to a condensed-area package Mahomes can actually throw.

The left edge without Josh Simmons

Reid has already said there is a good chance Simmons misses Week 1 with a back issue, and the line is being billed as the five best available. If the backup LT is the plan, the call sheet has to move the pocket and get the ball out.

Safety integrity on obvious passing downs

The overlooked safety concern is a real matchup problem against a Payton script. Spagnuolo cannot play empty-the-woodwork football on early downs and expect two-high to hold up on third-and-medium if the back end is already thin.

What to think about

Overlooked or correctly parked in the AFC mud?

Local writers have the Chiefs mixed into a tight AFC group and have argued that being ignored might help. A 6.2 percent championship price says the league is not scared. Monday night is the first chance to make that look foolish or accurate.

How much Walker versus the young committee

The building is confident in the backup running backs after camp, and Smith just ran the finale. The regular season still needs a lead back who can finish. If Walker does not get the early-down diet Smith just ate, the play-action boot is a rumor.

Week 1 wonkiness versus a division grudge

Arrowhead Addict’s reminder that openers are weird is fair. This one is also Reid-Payton, Arrowhead, ESPN, and a Broncos team with a health advantage. Treat it like a trench game, not a statement script.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Monday Night Football at Arrowhead is the first snap that counts: a division rival, a coaching heavyweight in Sean Payton, a short home favorite, and a 43.5 total that is screaming trench fight. Kansas City has to run it, protect Mahomes without Simmons, and make Payton defend the boot instead of pinning his ears back at a patched left tackle.

How they match up

This is Reid versus Payton, Mahomes versus a healthy road club, and a Chiefs run tape versus a Broncos roster that has the health edge. Kansas City’s leverage is the quarterback and Arrowhead. Denver’s leverage is completeness up front and the fact that the hosts are already talking about next-man-up on the offensive line and a thin safety room. ESPN FPI’s 58.3 percent is a Mahomes-at-home number. The 2.5-point spread and the 43.5 total are a vote for field goals and first-down runs, not for a shootout. If Bieniemy can make Denver defend Walker on early downs, Mahomes wins the rest. If the left edge or the safeties leak, Payton’s script will look a lot smarter than Kansas City’s 9-9 control game.

Make Denver tackle Walker on early downs

Open in 12 and 11 with inside zone and do not get cute. The Seattle night proved the zone steps are in. Walker has to be the 16-carry version of what Smith just did, or the play-action boot never gets launched away from the backup tackle.

Move the pocket off the left edge

If Simmons is out, every obvious dropback is a gift to Payton’s rush. Boot, sprint-out, and RB screens are not gadgets this week; they are protection. Get Mahomes to the right hash and let the five best linemen work downhill instead of in a phone booth.

Finish inside the 15

Butker cannot be the red-zone plan on Monday. Condensed formations, Walker on the goal line, and one easy Mahomes throw to the boundary. A 19-yard field goal against Seattle is a loss against Denver.

Two-high first, simulated pressure later

The safety room is the quiet concern. Spagnuolo should live in Cover-2 early, take away the shot, and hunt Payton with zone blitzes on third-and-medium rather than emptying the back end on first down.

  1. Series 1: under-center inside zone to Kenneth Walker, 12 personnel, test Denver’s front seven and the second-level fits before throwing a ball.
  2. Second series: play-action boot to Mahomes’ right — away from the Simmons vacancy — with the tight end leaking and a backside post to hold the safety.
  3. Obvious passing down: RB spacing screen to Walker or the young back who just produced, linemen pulling into space so the backup LT is a lead blocker instead of a pass setter.
  4. Red zone: 13 personnel, inside zone then a glance/flat to the tight end. No more 19-yard field-goal drives.
  5. First two Denver series: Spagnuolo Cover-2 shell, rally and tackle, keep the safeties healthy and aligned.
  6. Third-and-5 to 8: simulated pressure, drop a lineman, rush a second-level player, force Payton to ID the rush after the snap.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Kansas City is walking into a real season the same way it left a lost one: able to move the ball, unable to finish, and asking the trenches to cover for everything else. The preseason closer against Seattle was a 33-minute control game that produced 21 first downs, 353 yards, and a 9-9 scoreboard because the red zone kept turning into Harrison Butker range. Now Patrick Mahomes takes the first snap that matters against Sean Payton on ESPN, on a short home number, with left tackle Josh Simmons a likely scratch and a Broncos club that is healthier than the host. The 6-11 hangover is the backdrop. Monday night is the test of whether Eric Bieniemy’s run identity is a Week 1 plan or just August padding.

The Seattle night was not empty. Brashard Smith’s 16-carry, 72-yard shift, a 9-of-17 third-down clip, and Steve Spagnuolo holding the visitors to 54 rushing yards are real things. They are also the wrong headline if you are Andy Reid. Garrett Nussmeier’s 198 yards and interception were preseason-finale football; the problem is structural. Three field goals from 28, 24, and 19 yards means the offense repeatedly won the down and then lost the series. That is the tape Payton will put on the projector.

The roster around Mahomes is still being argued in public. Brett Veach’s offensive line already looks thin with Simmons in doubt, and the local reporting has been blunt that Kansas City did not invest enough there. The other quiet leak is safety. Arrowhead Addict has flagged the depth chart and the injury report as a Week 1 problem against Denver, which is a bad place to be thin when Payton is scripting play-action and two-man games at the sticks. The counter is personnel the building actually believes in: Kenneth Walker as the lead back, a young room behind him that just ran for 155 as a group, and a secondary overhaul that now asks Nohl Williams to look like a plan instead of a hope.

Markets are not romantic about any of it. ESPN FPI posts the Chiefs at 58.3 percent. DraftKings has Kansas City -2.5 with a 43.5 total. Polymarket prices this club at 6.2 percent to win the 2027 title and 10.5 percent to win the AFC. That is a home-field, quarterback-driven nod, not a Super Bowl price. The honest read for Kingdom is simpler than the odds board: if Walker and the five best linemen can make Payton defend the run, Mahomes will have enough. If the left edge caves or the red zone stays stuck in field-goal mode, Monday night becomes the first chapter of another 6-11 winter.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker downhill, first series

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script vs. Denver, 12 personnel at Arrowhead

Kansas City just ran for 155 yards against Seattle and still kicked field goals. Kenneth Walker is the free-agent lead back who has to turn that August look into a Monday identity, especially if Josh Simmons is out and the passing game cannot risk a tight pocket. Payton will load the box until Kansas City makes him tackle.

Coaching point Bieniemy wants zone steps, a single cut, and the five best linemen climbing now — not a designed toss that exposes the vacant left edge. Walker has to finish the extra two yards Smith did not get in the red zone. If the first two runs are east-west, Denver will pin its ears back on second down.

Offense

Boot Mahomes off the run fake

Situation 2nd-and-5 after Walker’s inside zone, away from the backup LT

The Seattle tape sold the run for 33 minutes. That fake is now Mahomes’ protection plan if Simmons sits. Booting to the right hash takes the rush off a patched left edge and puts Payton’s aggressive ends in conflict. A 43.5 total says this game will be won on the perimeter, not down the middle of a crowded pocket.

Coaching point Reid has to sell the mesh point. Mahomes’ first read is the leaking tight end; the backside post holds the safety so the boot is not a scramble drill. Do not boot into the Simmons side. If the five best guys cannot pass-set, they can still climb and wall on a moving pocket.

Offense

Easy offense when Payton pins his ears

Situation 2nd-and-8 or obvious pass, Denver showing five-man heat

Arrowhead Addict’s line critique and Reid’s Simmons update both point the same direction: Kansas City cannot win a dropback fight with a healthy Broncos rush. Walker — or the young back who just posted 72 — has to become the pressure answer. A screen turns a backup tackle into a lead blocker in space instead of a beaten pass setter.

Coaching point Get the ball out on one hitch. The playside linemen have to reach the second level instead of catching a pass-rush upfield. Smith already showed he can make the first man miss; Walker has to be willing to take the 8-yard answer and keep Mahomes clean.

Offense

Make Payton’s Cover-3 defend the post

Situation 3rd-and-6 to 8, Denver in two-high look that rotates to Cover 3

Nussmeier’s 198 yards never stressed the boundary. Mahomes has to, or Payton will sit on the run and the boot all night. Four verticals against a rotated Cover 3 is the shot that turns a 43.5 total into a real drive instead of another Butker field goal. This is also how you punish a safety room the local press already thinks is thin.

Coaching point Outside receivers run the corners out of the play. The inside seam has to bend to the hash, not fade into the post safety. Mahomes throws the window, not the player — if the safety is late from the thin back end, that is the one explosive Kansas City did not have on the Seattle tape.

Defense

Two-deep, rally, survive the script

Situation 1st-and-10 vs. Payton 11 personnel, first two Denver series

The overlooked safety problem is not a third-down problem first; it is a do-not-get-shot-over-the-top problem on play-action. Spagnuolo’s Cover-2 shell takes away the deep ball Payton wants on Monday night and asks Nohl Williams and the overhauled corners to tackle in front. With Kansas City a 2.5-point favorite in a low total, missing tackles is how you donate the edge.

Coaching point Safeties split the posts and drive on the throw, not the fake. Williams has to squeeze the curl without opening the sideline go. Spagnuolo should live here until Denver proves it can run the ball — the Seattle night said this front can stop the run; the back end should not be asked to blitz itself into a shot play.

Defense

Simulated heat on Payton’s third down

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 8, Denver empty or 11, Payton trying to ID the mike

Spagnuolo’s signature is the way you pressure a veteran coach without emptying a thin safety room. Show five, drop a lineman, rush a second-level player, and make Payton’s protection wrong after the snap. That is how Kansas City gets off the field without asking the overlooked safeties to cover forever.

Coaching point The dropped end has to occupy the hook; the late rusher has to hit the open B-gap, not the occupied one. Do not send the house. The point of the look is to win with four-and-a-half rushers while two-deep still exists. If the first two zone blitzes are picked up, Spagnuolo should check back to Cover-2 rather than chase the quarterback into a shot.

Coaching & adjustments

Andy Reid vs. Sean Payton

Two Super Bowl coaches, a painted Chiefs logo on Denver’s practice field, and a Monday night that will be won in the first 15 scripted calls. Reid has to be willing to run it; Payton will be willing to rush the vacant tackle.

Eric Bieniemy’s red-zone problem

The 155-yard run game is installed. The 19-yard field goal is the tell. Bieniemy’s Week 1 job is not more first downs; it is a goal-line package that does not ask Butker to finish Mahomes’ drives.

Steve Spagnuolo’s restraint

The Seattle night showed the front can stop the run. The safety reporting says he cannot hunt every down. Cover-2 early and zone blitz later is the grown-up version of Spags, not the empty-the-woodwork version.

Brett Veach’s line bet

The five-best-guys slogan only works if the five are actually better than Payton’s rush. Local writers already think Veach underinvested. Monday night is the first public grade.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Arrowhead Boot Away from Jones

Situation Week 1 early-down, 1st-and-10 vs. Denver base—sell zone, move Mahomes

Denver will key Chris Jones’ interior disrupt; boot away from the featured rusher forces Nix’s defense to run horizontally while Kelce leaks into the vacated flat-to-intermediate window. Camp emphasis on easy offense pairs with Mahomes’ need for clean early rhythm after a 6-11 year.

Coaching point Reid and Bieniemy want a firm mesh from the backs, OL eyes upfield for one second, then Mahomes’ shoulders fully around—Kelce must flatten his stem if the SAM widens so the throw stays on time.

Offense

Seam Stress vs. Broncos Cover-3

Situation 2nd-and-medium when Denver sits in three-deep to protect against explosives

If Kohou and the corners are playing honest outside, four verticals punishes the single-high safety who has to choose between Kelce’s seam and the outside stem. It’s also a confidence snap for Mahomes without asking the OL to hold a seven-man protection forever.

Coaching point Outside receivers push the corners to nine; Kelce bends off the hash late so the MIKE can’t undercut—Mahomes’ eyes stay middle until the safety declares, then the ball is gone.

Offense

Mesh to Free Kelce vs. Man

Situation 3rd-and-5–7, Denver switching to man pressure to rattle the new-look skill group

Camp battles at receiver mean not every outside matchup will be won cleanly; mesh creates a natural rub for Kelce and a back outlet if Nix’s defense blitzes off the edge. It’s the antidote to early-season timing rust.

Coaching point Inside crossers must brush without picking—Kelce owns the low path, the Z owns the high; backs chip then sit in the vacated landmark so Mahomes always has a hot answer.

Offense

Tunnel / Perimeter Screen After the Rush

Situation Early down or obvious passing look when Denver’s edges are flying

Arrowhead Pride’s camp notes highlight real excitement for a revamped screen game while pads are still coming on—the perfect counter if Denver tees off on Mahomes. Gets the back the ball in space without asking a shifting OL to win pure dropback every snap.

Coaching point Guard and tackle must pull with flat angles, not chase; receiver stalk blocks need inside hands so the back can plant once and go north—Mahomes sells the deep drop for one full count.

Defense

Spags Two-Shell vs. Nix Rhythm

Situation Neutral early downs when Bo Nix wants to hitch-and-throw horizontally

With Mansoor Delane working through a hand issue, pure man is a risk; Cover-2 lets Kohou and the corners funnel to the sideline while safeties erase the deep shot Nix will take if you over-rotate. Buys time until the rookie is fully two-handed.

Coaching point Corners must reroute at five yards and stay on top of intermediate outs; linebackers own the hook windows—no trail technique that opens the deep hole. Jones’ push still collapses the launch point even without a blitz.

Defense

Simulated Pressure Off FAU’s First-Team Look

Situation 3rd-and-long vs. Denver, needing a drive-ending negative play without emptying the box

Felix Anudike-Uzomah earning starter reps and Gillotte’s hamstring strain mean Spagnuolo needs creative ways to generate heat. Show five or six, drop a lineman, and bring a second-level runner so Nix’s protection rules break—especially if Jones occupies two inside.

Coaching point The dropped end must get depth into the throwing lane immediately; the late freerusher times off Mahomes’ counterpart’s first hitch. Secondary plays post-snap rotation so the void isn’t real—Spags’ classic bait.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Training camp at Missouri Western is doing what a 6-11 offseason demanded: forcing competition into the open before the pads even land. Through the three-day ramp-up, Andy Reid’s club has been short, sharp, and specific—first-team looks for Felix Anudike-Uzomah, buzz around two rookies stealing snaps, and a deliberate reinstall of the screen game that can protect Patrick Mahomes while the run fits settle. The calendar is not abstract. Week 1 is Denver at Arrowhead on ESPN, and every rep in St. Joe is a vote on whether this roster opens as a finished idea or another year of searching.

The injury board is already editing the script. Rookie corner Mansoor Delane, the sixth overall pick, has been playing through a hand issue Reid described as essentially one-handed work, which puts a premium on veterans and free-agent additions in the secondary while the kid ramps. Defensive end Ashton Gillotte left Friday’s practice with a strained hamstring—the only injury Reid flagged after Day 3—tightening the rotation behind the established rushers and making Anudike-Uzomah’s first-team window more than a camp courtesy. Those are not footnotes; they are the difference between Spagnuolo playing his full call sheet in September or living in safer shells.

Offensively the through-line is rhythm and easy offense. Without full pads the run game has been muted, but the staff is clearly re-emphasizing screens and perimeter touches so Mahomes is not asked to win every early down from a clean pocket that may not exist. Travis Kelce’s presence still bends safety depth and creates the play-action leverage Reid has always loved; the tight end is also sitting on historical touchdown climbs that will matter once the red-zone package is fully installed. Depth pieces like reserve receiver Andrew Armstrong are forcing the conversation on the back end of the WR room—exactly what you want when September roster math gets cold.

Look ahead and the bracket is unforgiving in a good way: Broncos, Colts, then @ Miami in three weeks. Denver brings Bo Nix and a passing identity that can put drives on the defense’s heels if the edge doesn’t finish. A strong camp—settled OL combinations, a defined third edge, corners who can survive without rolling help every snap—turns that opener from a cautious -3 into a tone-setter. Stumble through August and you’re chasing confidence into a primetime Week 2 and a South Florida road night before the bye even appears. St. Joe is where the 2026 story stops being theoretical.

The storylines

What we’re watching

Edge Battle

Anudike-Uzomah’s first-team window

The staff is putting Felix Anudike-Uzomah with the ones early because he has to rediscover starter speed if he’s going to matter in 2026. With Ashton Gillotte dealing with a hamstring strain, those reps are no longer optional development—they’re Week 1 insurance opposite the featured rushers.

Rookie Watch

Two rookies stealing the show

Day 3 notes out of St. Joe flag a pair of rookies forcing their way onto the practice soundtrack before pads. In a 6-11 reset year, early juice from the draft class is the fastest path to roles that actually survive cutdown day.

Secondary

Delane’s one-handed ramp

Andy Reid said Mansoor Delane is playing through a hand issue that has him effectively one-handed early in camp. That slows the sixth-overall crowning and elevates veterans and newcomers who can hold the backend while the rookie gets whole.

Free Agency

Kohou’s easy yes

Kader Kohou framed Kansas City as a simple choice after a season lost to injury—scheme fit and a chance to restart opposite Spagnuolo’s pressure looks. If Delane’s hand lingers, Kohou’s camp reliability becomes a September necessity, not a depth nicety.

Skill Design

Screen game coming alive

Even in the non-padded acclimation window, the building is buzzing about a revamped screen package. That’s how you bleed pressure, get the backs touches without asking the interior to win every double, and keep Mahomes ahead of the chains against Denver’s front.

Legacy

Kelce and the history climb

Travis Kelce enters 2026 positioned to jump several all-time touchdown leaderboards if the red-zone efficiency returns. Camp install that still runs through 87 isn’t nostalgia—it’s the simplest way to punish safeties who overplay the new toys.

Market Check

Respect is conditional

Polymarket has the Chiefs around 6.8% to win the 2027 Super Bowl and 9.5% to take the AFC—humble numbers after 6-11. Camp clarity is the only thing that moves those prices before the Broncos arrive.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid’s ramp-up discipline

Three full-squad acclimation practices before pads on Monday keep the install clean and the legs fresh—Reid is prioritizing timing and screen answers over early thud collisions so Mahomes’ supporting cast learns the easy offense first.

Spagnuolo’s secondary math

Delane’s hand and Kohou’s re-acclimation after injury force Spags to mix two-shell and simulated pressures rather than living in pure man—Week 1 vs. Nix will show how much risk he’s willing to reintroduce.

Bieniemy’s early-down identity

With inside zone still limited until pads, the OC room is stacking play-action boots and screens so the OL shifts observed on Day 2 don’t turn into negative plays when Denver brings real power.

Edge development timeline

Giving Anudike-Uzomah first-team reps now is a coaching tell: they need him game-ready by September 15, not October, especially with Gillotte’s hamstring already interrupting the depth chart.

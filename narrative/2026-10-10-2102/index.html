Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

W
Las Vegas Raiders Week 4 · Sun, Oct 4 · 3:25 PM CT
KC 30–27

Kansas City answered with a 2-yard touchdown, a 28-yard field goal, then a fourth-quarter burst — an 8-yard score, a 2-yard score, and Kenneth Walker III’s 22-yard touchdown — to make it 30–19 before Brock Bowers’ 20-yard touchdown and a two-point play set the final at 30–27.

The box is a split personality. Kansas City ran for 189 yards on 26 carries and passed for 210 net, 399 total, 18 first downs, 5-of-13 on third down, 12 drives, 24:18 with the ball, no turnovers. Las Vegas ran for 72 yards on 26 carries, passed for 365 net, 437 total, 28 first downs, 5-of-14 on third down, 12 drives, 35:42 with the ball, and two turnovers. That is a defense that leaked yards and first downs and still found the two possessions that decided it: J. Bassa forced Dareke Young’s fumble on the kickoff, J. Cochrane recovered at the Las Vegas 22, and Walker walked in from the right end; later Xavier Nwankpa intercepted Cousins short left intended for Michael Mayer.

Walker was downhill identity, not a cameo. Twenty-two carries, one catch, 23 touches, 177 yards, two rushing scores. The complementary shot was Thornton: deep left for 42 yards in the second quarter, then deep left for 55 in the fourth before the ankle. Xavier Worthy’s 41-yard catch in the first quarter set up the tying score. Mahomes did not dink the Raiders into submission — 15-of-30 with two sacks and eight hits — but the completions that landed were explosives, and the two rushing scores plus two Thornton touchdowns were enough.

Steve Spagnuolo’s side lived in a long game. Ashton Jeanty handled 21 touches (15 rushes, six catches) and still the Raiders offense finished with 72 rushing yards as a team; Mike Washington Jr. added 10 touches. Michael Mayer caught eight, Bowers six, Tre Tucker four. Cousins was hit 10 times and never sacked, which is the warning clip for Justin Herbert. Nick Williams drew unnecessary roughness and defensive holding, F. Anudike-Uzomah drew roughing the passer, L. Sneed drew defensive holding, and the flag parade kept Las Vegas in a game it was losing on the ground. Matt Araiza’s 12-yard run out of punt formation was the early field-position swing; Khyiris Tonga reported eligible on separate first-quarter snaps, once with H. Nourzad and once on a Mahomes scramble.

The fourth quarter was the whole season in miniature. Kansas City trailed 17–19 after Las Vegas field goals of 44 and 48 yards, then scored twice in 19 seconds of game clock — Thornton’s 55-yarder plus a 2-yard touchdown, the Bassa-Cochrane takeaway, Walker’s 22-yarder — and still had to survive Bowers from 20 yards and a two-point play. Mansoor Delane led the defense with 10 tackles. It was a 30-point afternoon that never became comfortable, and the Chargers will see every snap of the 35:42 they were on the field.

  • 189 team rushing yards on 26 attempts versus 72 allowed
  • Chunk shots to Worthy (41) and Thornton (42, 55) that flipped field position

What broke

  • 15-of-30 passing and 5-of-13 on third down
  • 24:18 of possession against 35:42 and 28 first downs allowed
  • Ten hits on Cousins without a sack
  • The penalty list: N. Williams twice, P. Woods, N. Remigio, F. Anudike-Uzomah, L. Sneed
  • Losing Rashee Rice (hamstring) and Tyquan Thornton (dislocated ankle) from the starting receiver group

Walker is the live identity

Twenty-two carries, 177 yards, two rushing touchdowns, 23 touches. When the receiver room shrank, Bieniemy did not hunt a new passing personality — he handed the game to Walker and let Mahomes take shots off that gravity.

Explosives covered a 15-of-30 day

Mahomes’ 225 yards sat on a handful of chunk plays: Worthy for 41, Thornton for 42 and 55. That is not a volume passing tape. It is a shot-play tape that will need 12 personnel and the run game if Rice and Thornton are limited.

Yards allowed, takeaways collected

Las Vegas had 28 first downs, 437 yards, and 35:42. Kansas City had the Bassa force, the Cochrane recovery, and the Nwankpa interception. Spagnuolo can live with ugly yardage for one game. He cannot live with it against Herbert if the takeaways dry up.

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

4-0

Saturday, Oct 10: the Chiefs are on the bye, and the next opponent still has Week 5 to play. The work list is receiver roles, protection communication, and disciplined pressure. The facts below come from the linked reports; the proposed fixes are Arrowhead Paesano's analysis. A week without a Kansas City game should make the next plan more specific, not turn Monday's game recap into today's news.

What we need to work on

Give every receiver a job the play can support

Start with responsibilities rather than a demand that someone become Thornton overnight. Worthy can stretch the coverage and get movement releases. Kelce and Gray can change the formation strength, block, and release into the space a run fit leaves behind. If Rice practices and is available, restore the parts of the script that depend on his timing. If he is not, move those throws to routes the replacement group has actually rehearsed. Our preference is a smaller menu executed fast over a broad menu that forces Mahomes to wait for separation.

Make the protection call visible in the plan

Caliendo started at left guard in Las Vegas; Arrowhead Pride reported Reid declined to explain Suamataia's absence. That establishes last week's lineup, not next week's decision or the reason behind it. The bye should settle the assignment even if the staff keeps the personnel discussion private. Who handles a late interior exchange? Where does the back scan? Which receiver owns the hot route? A screen and a boot are useful changes, but neither substitutes for the linemen, back, and quarterback making the same call.

Pressure with a place for the ball to go

The film question is whether the defense can rush aggressively while keeping the quick answer contested. Put an underneath defender where the quarterback expects the easy throw, then let the front work. If Kansas City sends a second-level rusher, the replacement coverage must arrive on time. The goal is a hurried decision into a crowded window. A pressure call that merely sends Herbert toward an uncovered receiver has solved the wrong problem, even if somebody eventually reaches the quarterback.

Let special teams change the field

Araiza's award belongs in the bye-week conversation because possession quality matters. Our coaching priority is the coverage operation: release without a flag, force the returner toward help, and finish without touching the ball into the end zone. The offense can be ambitious and still accept a punt when the alternative is a desperate throw. The defense then has to earn the benefit by making the opponent use snaps to cross the field. Special teams and defense should function as one possession plan.

What to think about

What does the Denver film change?

Los Angeles has not played its Oct 11 game yet. Watch the protection structure as closely as the score. Help from a back or tight end narrows the route distribution; a quick passing plan asks Kansas City to tackle immediately; movement passes ask the edge defenders to stay home. Those are different assignments. Build answers for each, then use Sunday's film to decide which deserves the most rehearsal. The Chargers' Saturday record is a fact about today, not a forecast of the team that arrives next week.

How much two-tight-end offense is enough?

The Kelce-Gray package is a sensible starting point, but it should not become a ritual. If Los Angeles matches it with a heavy box, release a tight end and attack the space behind the linebackers. If the defense stays light, let Walker make the safety participate in the run fit. The advantage comes from forcing the defense to declare and then changing the answer. A formation only helps if Kansas City can run more than one useful play from it.

When is the wideout discussion actually news?

An announced signing or completed trade changes the depth chart. An attributed interest report changes the conversation. A projected fit is our analysis. Keep those three things clear. The Chiefs can need a receiver without every available receiver being on the way to Kansas City. Evaluate the role first: vertical speed, dependable underneath timing, blocking, and the ability to learn the calls. The next opponent still has to be prepared for with the current roster.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Los Angeles Chargers

Our Week 6 plan starts with the protection stress on both sides. Kansas City should make its own pass answers quicker and make Herbert's protection calls harder. This is a proposed script for Oct 18, not a claim about what either staff will call. Rice's practice participation and the Chargers' Denver game can change the personnel details before kickoff.

How they match up

The Chargers' Friday report ruled Joe Alt, Trevor Penning, Derwin James, and Quentin Johnston out against Denver and listed Ladd McConkey questionable. Those are Week 5 designations, not Week 6 rulings. Slater's injured-reserve move is a firmer reason to study replacement protection. Our matchup read is to force that group to communicate across the front while maintaining enough coverage to contest the immediate throw. On offense, make Los Angeles defend the width of the field before asking a depleted Chiefs receiver group to win isolated routes downfield.

Make the box declare before the handoff

Use motion and formation strength to ask the defense where the extra run fitter lives. Walker's first read should follow the blocking, not the narrative that every carry needs to be an explosive. If the safety rotates down early, the pass complement becomes available. If the safeties stay deep, take the run the numbers support. The offense needs the discipline to choose the available answer instead of proving that the original call was clever.

Give Mahomes a quick answer and a shot

Pair an immediate completion with the deeper route. The quick throw should be clear enough to use against pressure without a long scramble drill. The shot is there if the defense overcommits to the underneath answer. Movement releases can keep Worthy from fighting through contact at the line, and a tight end can change the coverage's spacing. The desired result is decisiveness. Our plan does not require the quarterback to manufacture a highlight on every passing down.

Disguise the rush without losing the edge

Crowd the front, vary the rush source, and make the protection account for someone who may drop out. That can create a useful matchup without sending an extra defender. The edge rusher still has to keep Herbert inside the pocket; the interior rush must compress the space without opening a clean lane. If the ball comes out quickly, the coverage needs to tackle it short of the sticks. A disguised pressure is a team call, not an individual race to the backfield.

Win the second answer after the first read

Herbert will have a response to the first pressure look. Kansas City should be ready to rotate the coverage, show the same front with a different rush, or play a patient shell. On offense, Los Angeles will adjust to Walker and the tight ends. The Chiefs need the counter already rehearsed: a leak, a screen, or a route into the vacated area. The adjustment is the point of the bye. Have another answer ready before a stalled possession forces the conversation.

  1. Show a balanced Walker run look and use motion to identify the extra box defender
  2. Carry an early flat or outlet throw alongside the deeper route so pressure has an answer
  3. Use Kelce and Gray together, then change which one blocks and which one releases
  4. Call a play-action boot only with an answer for the backside defender
  5. Present a crowded defensive front, vary the rush source, and keep Herbert contained
  6. Revise the personnel assignments after Chargers-Broncos and the Week 6 practice reports

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The bye has stopped the game clock, not the story. Kansas City has the undefeated record and a chance to work on the offense before the Chargers arrive. Los Angeles has Denver first, so there is another opponent game to watch before anyone declares the matchup settled. The useful Saturday question is how the Chiefs turn rest into an advantage: a cleaner receiver rotation, protection with an answer built in, and pressure that makes Herbert throw before he wants to. That is our preview, not a report from the practice field.

The biggest change since Monday is on the other sideline. The Chargers announced Friday that Rashawn Slater underwent ankle surgery Thursday and was being placed on injured reserve. Their Thursday account of the Seattle game described four rookies finishing on the offensive line. That gives Spagnuolo a protection problem to study. It does not mean the same five will start at Arrowhead. Sunday against Denver is the next piece of evidence: who plays together, who makes the calls, and how Mike McDaniel moves the launch point when the edges are under stress.

Kansas City's receiver problem also needs an honest clock. Arrowhead Pride's Monday follow-up attributed a planned surgery and a 12-to-16-week recovery estimate for Thornton to NFL Network's Ian Rapoport. That is a reported estimate, not a promised return date. Rice's hamstring exit remains a separate issue; the coming practice reports must tell us what he can do. The plan should have a branch for Rice playing and a branch for Rice sitting. It should not depend on Thornton returning next Sunday or on a trade rumor walking into the huddle.

There was actual Chiefs news in the middle of the bye. The club announced Araiza's AFC Special Teams Player of the Week award on Wednesday. All five of his punts against Las Vegas landed inside the 20, and his late kick was downed at the 1. That is how a team can rescue a possession even when the offense stalls. The next-game lesson is field position: protect the coverage lanes and give Herbert a long field. A punt that makes the opponent earn every yard can be part of the winning plan without becoming an excuse for a conservative offense.

The receiver market is moving, but reporting still has boundaries. Reuters reported Saturday, citing NFL Network and ESPN, that Arizona was finalizing a Marvin Harrison Jr. trade; the destination and compensation were unreported. Arizona's own coverage also described the timing as uncertain. That is league news. It is not a Chiefs acquisition. Kansas City's official transactions page still lists the Oct 3 standard elevation of Cole Christiansen as its latest entry at this check. Build the football discussion around the players available and update it when a move is actually announced.

Our preferred offensive answer is to give the same formation several lives. Put Kelce and Gray on the field, show Walker behind Mahomes, and make the defense choose between fitting the run and covering the release. Motion can identify who travels with a receiver; a quick flat route can punish a defender who flies downhill. The important part is timing. If the pass only becomes available after Mahomes has held the ball and the pocket has collapsed, it is not a pressure answer. The throw needs to belong to the play before the snap.

On defense, the tempting response to a disrupted line is to send everyone. Resist the temptation to make that the entire plan. Herbert can beat a free runner with a quick throw if Kansas City leaves the easy receiver uncovered. Show pressure, change the picture, and make the center and quarterback agree on who is coming. Rush lanes still matter after the disguise. A defender who runs past the pocket has offered an escape route, and an escape route can erase the advantage created by the front.

The Chiefs should spend Sunday watching Denver attack Los Angeles, then revise the preview with what survives that game. Does the replacement tackle need help? Does the offense keep a tight end in protection? Which receivers can take a full workload? The answers will change the details without changing the central idea. Kansas City has the extra preparation time. Spend it on a plan that can adjust after the first series instead of a plan that needs the first series to go perfectly.

Coaching & adjustments

Andy Reid / Eric Bieniemy — Offensive plan

Our priority is a connected menu: the run, quick throw, and play-action answer should look related before the snap. Set the receiver jobs after the practice evidence arrives, then keep enough consistency that a late change does not break the protection call.

Steve Spagnuolo — Defensive plan

Test the protection with a changing picture while making the rush and coverage work together. The defender who drops has an assignment as important as the defender who comes. Herbert's first answer should encounter somebody ready to tackle.

Dave Toub — Special teams plan

Carry the punt coverage execution into the next game. Manage the release lanes, avoid the flag that erases a useful kick, and finish the downing play. A good field-position exchange can support an aggressive offense without asking it to force a low-percentage throw.

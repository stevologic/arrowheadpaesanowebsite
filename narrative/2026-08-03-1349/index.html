The story so far

Always looking ahead

Training camp doesn’t become real until the pads go on, and that moment is here in St. Joseph: iron sharpening iron as Andy Reid’s staff installs, Steve Spagnuolo’s defense challenges Patrick Mahomes daily, and a roster still shaking off a 6-11 season hunts for an identity that can travel into September. The through-line is simple—Mahomes’ post-ACL ramp, the durability of Xavier Worthy, and which rookies and UDFA earn early snaps will decide whether Kansas City opens as a real contender or another team still searching.

Full pads mean the evaluation window is open for real. Special teams coordinator Dave Toub said it cleanly: we’ll find out about these guys. That applies to the offensive line protecting a quarterback still building trust in his knee, to the wideout room after Cyrus Allen’s collision scare, and to a defensive front that has already had to add pass-rush depth mid-camp. Josh Simmons landing on NFL.com’s All-Under 25 team after an eight-game sample last year is the brightest young OL signal; if he locks down a starting job in these padded sessions, the entire early-down identity gets cleaner for Eric Bieniemy’s run-and-play-action package.

Mahomes has every reason to believe the best years are still ahead—history with elite QBs post-ACL backs the optimism—but camp is where the timing with Worthy’s deep speed and the mesh/crosser game has to reappear under live pressure. Spagnuolo’s group has been winning its share of those early periods; that is exactly the stress test you want before a Week 1 home date with Denver on ESPN. A defense that can challenge Mahomes in July and August is the same defense that can take away Bo Nix’s rhythm in September.

Looking ahead, the schedule does not ease in: Broncos at Arrowhead, Colts on NBC, then a road trip to Miami. Oddsmakers have refused to bury Kansas City despite last year’s collapse—DraftKings has the Chiefs -3 in the opener and ESPN FPI gives them a 59.4% win probability—but markets also price the 2027 title path at just 6.8% on Polymarket. That gap between short-term favoritism and long-term skepticism is the story camp must close. Settle the OL, prove Worthy’s durability, get the UDFA and rookie contributors on the 53, and the identity question stops being philosophical and starts being schematic.

What a strong camp unlocks is simple: clarity. Clarity on who blocks, who separates, who rushes the passer when the depth chart thins, and how aggressively Bieniemy can call play-action boots and four-verts once Mahomes’ lower half is fully trusted. Everything from here points at September 15.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-Center Inside Zone Identity

Situation 1st-and-10 early script vs Denver’s base front at Arrowhead

After a 6-11 year the Chiefs need a countable early-down answer; Josh Simmons’ ascent and padded-camp emphasis on physicality give Bieniemy a chance to establish downhill zone before Denver can tee off on Mahomes.

Coaching point Simmons and the frontside must win the reach and vertical seam; Mahomes’ post-ACL trust grows when the back hits the hole on time and the play-action fake becomes real later in the drive.

Offense

Play-Action Boot After Zone Look

Situation 2nd-and-medium following a successful zone run vs Broncos overplaying the box

Mahomes’ rehab ramp benefits from moving the launch point; Denver will sit on the run once Simmons starts winning, and the boot puts the ball on the perimeter with defined reads instead of a muddy pocket.

Coaching point Sell the zone fake hard, get Mahomes outside the tackle with a tight end flat and a deep over; Worthy’s stem must hold the safety so the intermediate window opens cleanly.

Offense

Four Verts Stress vs Denver Cover-3

Situation Long yardage or 2nd-and-7+ when Broncos drop into three-deep

Xavier Worthy’s vertical threat is the camp storyline that changes geometry; if he stays healthy, four verts bends Denver’s seams and forces the single-high safety to pick a hash while Mahomes times the throw off a clean drop.

Coaching point Outside receivers push the corners; seam benders attack the hash landmarks. Mahomes must take the free-access throw if the middle-of-field safety drives early—no hero ball on a knee still building trust.

Offense

Mesh vs Man Pressure Looks

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 7 when Denver switches to man and hunts Mahomes

Camp periods have shown Spagnuolo’s own defense challenging Mahomes; expect Denver to try the same with man pressure. Mesh creates natural rubs and a back outlet so the post-ACL quarterback is not asked to hold the ball.

Coaching point Tight, decisive crossers and a clear sit/corner tag opposite; the running back must be the hot outlet if the edge freerushes. Timing over athleticism—Mahomes’ first read has to be pre-snap clean.

Defense

Spagnuolo Cover-2 Shell

Situation Early downs and obvious shot downs vs Denver’s vertical concepts with Bo Nix

With Chris Jones still a ranked interior force and camp focus on challenging the QB, a true Cover-2 shell takes away the deep shot Denver wants and forces Nix into checkdowns the linebackers can rally to tackle.

Coaching point Corners soft-pedal and funnel; safeties split the posts. Jones and the three-tech must collapse the pocket so the five-under defenders are breaking on balls, not chasing yards after catch.

Defense

Simulated Pressure / Zone Blitz

Situation 3rd-and-medium at Arrowhead when Denver’s protection slides to Jones

Camp injuries have already forced the Chiefs to add pass-rush depth; Spagnuolo’s signature answer is not always pure pin-your-ears rush but simulated heat that drops a lineman and brings a second-level runner into a vacated lane against Nix.

Coaching point Show five or six, bring four from odd angles, drop the end into the flat. Jones occupies two; the free runner must hit the opposite A or B gap on time so Nix cannot climb and locate the deep crosser.

The storylines

What we’re watching

Mahomes Ramp

Post-ACL chapter starts under pads

Patrick Mahomes is building the next phase after a devastating ACL injury, and early camp periods already show Spagnuolo’s defense forcing him to earn every throw. History says elite QBs can return stronger; the tape in St. Joe will show whether the timing and pocket trust are back before Denver.

OL Anchor

Josh Simmons as the under-25 cornerstone

Simmons impressed in eight games last season and just landed on NFL.com’s All-Under 25 team. If he wins the job cleanly in padded work, Bieniemy can lean into under-center zone and play-action without the early-down panic that defined stretches of 2025.

WR Durability

All eyes on Xavier Worthy

Worthy’s deep speed is the vertical threat that makes four-verts and play-action lethal, but camp coverage has correctly zeroed in on whether he can stay available. A healthy Worthy changes the math for Denver’s secondary in Week 1.

Rookie Scare

Cyrus Allen’s collision puts a breakout on hold

Allen went down after colliding with Kaiir Elam on a punt drill and needed help off the field. The injury scare freezes one of the more intriguing UDFA/receiver camp stories and forces the room to prove depth without him for now.

Identity Hunt

What do the 2026 Chiefs want to be?

After 6-11, the most important camp deliverable is a clear play style—run identity, explosive shots, or Spagnuolo chaos. Pads-on practices are where that answer stops being a press-conference line and becomes a call sheet.

Market Reality

Favored in Week 1, doubted for January

ESPN FPI gives Kansas City a 59.4% shot against Denver and DraftKings posts KC -3, yet Polymarket sits the Chiefs at 6.8% to win the 2027 title. Camp performance is the bridge between those two numbers.

A 9–9 finale, two borrowed tackles, and 17 days to make the 53 look like a plan

Kansas City closed the preseason in a draw with a real run game and no touchdowns — now Brett Veach’s knife, a PUP’d interior rusher, and a Broncos opener on ESPN decide whether camp actually flipped the 6-11 script.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

T
Seattle Seahawks Pre Week 4 · Sat Aug 29
KC 9–9

Kansas City and the Super Bowl champion Seahawks played to a 9–9 draw at Arrowhead in the preseason finale, a night defined by Chiefs young players, a functional rushing attack, and a complete failure to finish in the red zone. Seattle took a 3–0 lead on a Jason Myers 46-yarder, Harrison Butker answered from 28 and 24 to make it 6–3 at the half, Jalen Milroe hit Nick Vannett from four yards (Myers missed the PAT) for a 9–6 Seattle edge, and Butker’s 19-yarder in the fourth tied it for good.

The box score is a Bieniemy pamphlet. Kansas City out-gained Seattle 353–266, won first downs 21–13, converted 9-of-17 third downs against 4-of-11, and sat on the ball for 33:12. That is not garbage-time noise — that is staying on schedule. Brashard Smith’s 16 carries for 72 yards were the featured proof that the inside-zone install is more than a camp whiteboard, and 155 team rushing yards against 54 allowed is the line-of-scrimmage win Reid will actually put on the cut-up.

Nussmeier was the show, for better and worse. Twenty-two of 34 for 198 yards is a completion percentage and a yards-per-attempt that say the offense can function without asking the quarterback to create. Armstrong (5 for 54) won the underneath chain-mover role. The interception is the snap that will live in the QB room: one very bad decision in a night that was otherwise about timing, checkdowns, and living to the next down. Preseason quarterbacking is supposed to be boring. He was, until he wasn’t.

Where it broke is the paint. Three Butker makes from inside the 30 and zero offensive touchdowns means the red-zone menu — condensed space, tighter windows, no Kelce gravity with the starters out — did not exist. You can live with that on August 29. You cannot live with it on September 15. Seattle’s only touchdown was a 4-yard Milroe-to-Vannett, a goal-line personnel loss more than a schematic beating, and Myers’ missed extra point is the only reason the losing streak ended in a tie instead of 0-3.

Spagnuolo’s side did its camp job. Fifty-four rushing yards allowed, 13 first downs, 266 total, and R. Mason Thomas cashing a sack is the young-player tape Chiefs.com pointed at after the game. The defense squeezed a champion’s backups into field goals and one short throw. The question it does not answer is whether that run-stuff travels when Denver’s starters are across from a defensive tackle room that just lost Norman-Lott to the PUP list.

What held up

  • Early-down run game (155 yards) and Smith’s 16-carry feature role
  • Third-down efficiency at 9-of-17 and a 33:12 possession edge
  • Run defense holding Seattle to 54 yards and 13 first downs
  • Armstrong as a chain-mover (5 for 54) in the Nussmeier passing game
  • Mason Thomas getting home for a sack on a night the young players were the point

What broke

  • Red-zone touchdowns — three Butker field goals, zero offensive TDs
  • Nussmeier’s interception, the one snap that wrecked an otherwise clean night
  • Finishing drives inside the 20 with condensed-space answers
  • Interior defensive depth once you project this tape onto a PUP’d Norman-Lott

The run game is the only identity that showed up

One hundred fifty-five rushing yards, Smith’s 16-carry workload, and 21 first downs are the cleanest camp tape Kansas City has. Bieniemy’s under-center, downhill thesis is no longer theoretical — it is the only reason this offense moved the ball without Mahomes.

Red zone is still a field-goal operation

Butker from 28, 24, and 19 is three possessions that died in scoring range. Against Seattle’s backups that is an annoyance. Against Denver in Week 1 it is how you waste a 59.4 percent FPI nod and a -3 home number.

Young players won snaps; the 53 is still a construction site

Nussmeier, Smith, Armstrong, and Mason Thomas gave the staff reasons to keep them. The two offensive-line trades and Norman-Lott to PUP say the staff does not trust the construction as-is heading into cutdown.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Home opener, ESPN, AFC West, 17 days after a 9–9 dress rehearsal. The assignment is to make Friday’s run game travel against starters and to keep Mahomes clean behind a tackle room Veach just rebuilt on the fly. The stake is simple: a 6-11 team either looks like it used camp, or it opens the division with another field-goal night.

How they match up

Denver is the physical AFC West measuring stick — they will test the new tackles, crowd the run, and make Kelce earn every in-breaker. Kansas City’s leverage is at home, with a modest FPI/Vegas edge, a rushing identity that just posted 155 yards, and Spagnuolo’s disguise. Denver’s leverage is the Chiefs’ unfinished 53: Ezeudu and Pounds are still learning the call sheet, Norman-Lott is on PUP, and the tight-end room behind Kelce is unproven. This is a trench game dressed up as a prime-time opener.

Run it until Denver’s front climbs, then boot

Friday’s 155 yards are the script. Inside zone to Smith, stay on schedule, then play-action boot to get Mahomes on the perimeter where a shuffled tackle cannot get him killed. If Kansas City abandons the run on first down, the OL trades become the story by halftime.

Protect the quarterback with easy offense

Screens, movement throws, and 12 personnel. The Ezeudu/Pounds additions only help if you do not ask them to win 15 straight dropbacks. Spacing screens and boots are not gadget plays this week — they are the protection scheme.

Manufacture the interior rush you no longer have

Norman-Lott on PUP means Spagnuolo has to steal pressures with zone blitzes and simulated heat, not a straight four-man interior collapse. Mason Thomas already showed a sack on the last tape; the opener has to look like that on purpose.

Finish in the paint

Three field goals lost the chance to win Friday. Kelce on mesh, a leak off boot, and a commitment to not kick from the 7 are the entire red-zone plan. A 42.5 total says points will be scarce — Kansas City cannot donate three more possessions to Butker.

  1. Open under center with inside zone to Brashard Smith — make Denver defend the 155-yard tape before you throw a single empty look.
  2. Second series: play-action boot to Kelce on the leak. Sell the zone run, move Mahomes, keep Ezeudu/Pounds from isolated dropback reps.
  3. Third-and-medium: mesh with Kelce as the rub piece. Friday died in condensed space; this is the answer.
  4. If Denver’s edges climb, call the RB spacing screen and let the line lead in space — punish the aggressiveness the new tackles will invite.
  5. Defensively, start in a Cover-2 shell and steal a second-level rush on early downs. Do not let Denver settle into a five-man protection against a four-man that is missing Norman-Lott.
  6. Red zone: no more 19-yard field goals. 12 personnel, boot or mesh, Kelce as the closer. If it is 4th-and-goal from the 3, Reid has to trust the install he just spent camp teaching.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The Chiefs did not win Friday, and they did not lose it either — they played a 9–9 preseason finale that looked exactly like a roster still being built in public. Brashard Smith and the ground game posted 155 rushing yards and 21 first downs, Garrett Nussmeier moved the chains without the starters, and Steve Spagnuolo’s group stuffed Seattle to 54 rush yards. Then the red zone died on Harrison Butker’s foot, the cutdown clock started, and Brett Veach went shopping for tackles because the offensive line was not ready to open an AFC West season on a tie and a prayer.

That is the whole camp story in one night. Eric Bieniemy’s identity — downhill, on schedule, third-and-manageable — showed up in the 9-of-17 third-down tape and the 33:12 time of possession. Andy Reid did not need Patrick Mahomes to take hits in a dress rehearsal; he needed proof that the run game and the young skill players could keep a drive alive. Andrew Armstrong’s 5-for-54 night and Smith’s 16-for-72 are that proof. The three field goals from 28, 24, and 19 are the warning label.

The front office treated the warning as real. With the 53 due, Kansas City traded for Josh Ezeudu out of New York and then reached to Baltimore for Diego Pounds, a left-tackle addition meant to thicken a room that had already watched Esa Pole’s stock crater. Omarr Norman-Lott beginning the season on the PUP list punches a hole in the interior of Spagnuolo’s front at the exact moment the defense is being asked to carry early downs. Behind Travis Kelce, the tight-end room is still a vacancy sign.

Seventeen days separate that 9–9 tape from Denver at Arrowhead on ESPN. ESPN FPI has the Chiefs at 59.4 percent to win it; the total sits at 42.5. Those are modest-favorite numbers, not a coronation, and Polymarket pricing Kansas City at 6.3 percent to win the 2027 title is the market telling you last year’s 6-11 still lives in the odds. A strong close to camp — OL cohesion, a third tight end who can block, and manufactured pressure without Norman-Lott — is what turns a three-point home nod into an actual AFC West punch.

Week 1 is the first real snap of the identity. If Bieniemy can run it, if the borrowed tackles hold, and if Spagnuolo can blitz the second level instead of praying for interior push, Kansas City can look like a team that used camp. If not, Friday’s field-goal diet becomes the season’s opening act.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Inside zone, opening script

Situation 1st-and-10, first two series vs. Denver — under center, 12 or 21 personnel, Arrowhead opener

Friday’s 155 rushing yards and Brashard Smith’s 16-for-72 are the only offensive identity on the last tape. Bieniemy has to find out in real time whether Josh Ezeudu and Diego Pounds can zone-step against a Broncos front, because a dropback-first script puts a cutdown-weekend tackle room on an island. This is also how you keep Mahomes from third-and-long behind that line.

Coaching point Smith: one cut, no east-west bounce. Ezeudu and Pounds: reach and climb to the second level on the first two calls — if the combo block is late, Denver’s linebackers will fill every A-gap. Reid should keep the camera on the first four zone steps; that is the whole Week 1 thesis.

Offense

Play-action boot off the zone look

Situation 2nd-and-5 or 1st-and-10 after two inside-zone runs, hash toward the Broncos’ aggressive edge

The patched tackle rotation (Ezeudu, Pounds, Pole’s cratered stock) cannot be asked to pass-protect 40 times. Booting Mahomes off the same under-center picture that just ran for 155 yards in the finale is how Reid and Bieniemy protect the quarterback and get Kelce leaking into space Denver’s linebackers vacated to stop Smith.

Coaching point Sell the zone with the full backfield action — if the fake is lazy, Denver’s edge will not bite. Mahomes: get depth, then climb; do not drift into the unblocked end. Kelce is the first read on the leak; Armstrong is the over cut. The new tackles only have to hinge, not win a five-step drop.

Offense

Mesh in the paint

Situation Red zone, 2nd-and-7 or 3rd-and-5 inside the Broncos’ 15 — the exact down that became Butker from 19 on Friday

Kansas City kicked three field goals from 28, 24, and 19 and scored zero offensive touchdowns in the finale. Denver will play man and cluttered windows in that same space. Mesh puts Kelce on a rub with the second tight end — the job still up for grabs after an inactive TE offseason — and gives Mahomes a defined answer instead of another 19-yard kick.

Coaching point Kelce owns the mesh under; the second TE has to actually pick, not glance. Armstrong runs the corner off the top of the concept so Denver cannot sit a robber on Kelce. Bieniemy should tell Mahomes: if the rub is late, take the back out of the backfield — do not force the pick and recreate Nussmeier’s one bad interception in condensed space.

Offense

RB spacing screen, easy offense

Situation 2nd-and-8 or after Denver shows a five-man rush vs. the new tackles — early second quarter, if the edges are climbing

Veach just imported Ezeudu and Pounds because the tackle room was thin; Esa Pole’s stock has already plummeted. Denver will pin its ears back once it smells a dropback. A Smith screen lets the offensive line lead in space, takes the rush off Mahomes, and punishes the same aggressiveness that a PUP’d interior on the other side of the ball will invite from a Sean-Payton-style AFC West opponent.

Coaching point Guards and center must get out in front of Smith — this dies if Ezeudu is still attached to his edge. Armstrong and the Z have to crack the first color they see. Mahomes: catch-and-throw, no hero pump. This is the down that keeps a 42.5 total from turning into a third-and-18 disaster.

Defense

Cover-2 shell, steal the first series

Situation Denver’s opening script, 1st-and-10 through 3rd-and-medium — take away the shot and rally

Spagnuolo’s group just held Seattle to 266 yards, 13 first downs, and 54 on the ground. A 42.5 total and a Chiefs offense that kicked field goals say this opener may be a one-score game. Cover-2 lets Kansas City rally to the run without Norman-Lott collapsing the A-gap, and it keeps Denver from ending the night on one vertical over the top while the 53 is still shaking out.

Coaching point Safeties: no peeking at play-action — Denver will sell the run the same way Bieniemy will. Corners funnel and tackle; the Friday run-defense tape (54 yards allowed) only repeats if the second level actually fits. Spagnuolo should live here on early downs and save the heat for obvious passing downs, because a four-man without Norman-Lott is not a true four-man.

Defense

Simulated pressure, replace the missing three-tech

Situation 3rd-and-6 or 2nd-and-long vs. Denver, when protection slides to the edges and forgets the second level

Omarr Norman-Lott beginning the season on PUP is an interior-rush vacancy. Spagnuolo’s signature answer is not to ask four linemen to win — it is to show heat, drop a lineman, and send a linebacker or nickel into the gap Denver just left empty. R. Mason Thomas already cashed a sack in the finale; Week 1 has to look like that on purpose, or Mahomes will be in a 9–9-style field-goal game again.

Coaching point Sell the overload to the field, drop the backside end into the hook, and send the nickel through the vacated B-gap. Thomas: win your one-on-one when the slide goes the other way — do not chase a stunt into the backfield. Secondary must be in a zone behind it; a blitz into man with this 53 is how you give up the shot Cover-2 was built to steal.

The storylines

What we’re watching

PUP

Omarr Norman-Lott starts the year on ice

Arrowhead Pride reported Norman-Lott will begin 2026 on the PUP list, a second-year interior piece Kansas City cannot use in Week 1. Arrowhead Addict called the PUP move protection in more ways than one — it is also a scheme problem for Spagnuolo, who now has to manufacture the push that a three-tech is supposed to give him.

OL

Veach bought tackles because the room made him

The Chiefs traded for Josh Ezeudu from the Giants and then acquired Diego Pounds from Baltimore to thicken a tackle group that had already watched Esa Pole’s stock collapse. You do not make two of those deals on cutdown weekend if camp answered the question.

TE

The door behind Kelce is still open

Kansas City was inactive at tight end all offseason, and the last tape had no Kelce gravity in the red zone. Someone has to win a 53-man job as a blocker and a leak threat, or Bieniemy’s play-action menu is just Kelce and hope.

RB

Brashard Smith just forced the backfield conversation

Sixteen carries, 72 yards, and a team that ran for 155 in the finale. If that was more than preseason math, Smith is not a camp body — he is the downhill answer Bieniemy has been installing since St. Joseph.

QB

Nussmeier closed camp; Mahomes has to open the season

The rookie’s 22-of-34 for 198 with one killer interception is useful backup tape, not a quarterback controversy. The next 17 days are about Mahomes’ timing with an under-center identity and a line that did not exist in this form last week.

MARKET

Modest favorite, tiny title price

ESPN FPI at 59.4 percent and DraftKings at KC -3 / O/U 42.5 say Week 1 is a dogfight, not a statement. Polymarket at 6.3 percent to win the 2027 title (9.5 percent to win the AFC) is the hangover number from 6-11 — camp has to beat that price on the field, not on a whiteboard.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Andy Reid’s opener script vs. an unfinished 53

Reid has always scripted first-15 to get the quarterback comfortable. This week that script has to be run-run-boot, not empty-and-see, because the tackle room literally changed on cutdown weekend.

Eric Bieniemy’s downhill identity is on trial

The 155-yard night is Bieniemy’s exhibit A. If he abandons inside zone after two stuffed carries against Denver, the entire camp install was just preseason volume.

Steve Spagnuolo has to coach around a PUP

Norman-Lott on PUP is a scheme problem, not a depth-chart footnote. Simulated pressure and Cover-2 rally fits are how Spags has always manufactured a pass rush — this week they are mandatory.

Brett Veach is still on the clock

Arrowhead Pride had Veach prepared to add and subtract through the weekend with roughly $4.1 million in space. The Ezeudu and Pounds trades say he did not like his own tackle tape. Waiver week can still change Week 1.

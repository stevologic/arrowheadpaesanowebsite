Dress Rehearsal in the Heat: Bieniemy Is Back, the Line Is Not

After a 12-20 preseason opener and a growing list of offensive-line dings, Kansas City heads to Tampa as 5.5-point underdogs with roster cuts and the Denver opener already on the clock.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Camp at Missouri Western was supposed to be the reset after the worst Andy Reid season in Kansas City, and then Saturday happened: a 20-12 loss to the Rams, a single-digit offensive afternoon, and a red-flag challenge just to get Jared Wiley credited with a nine-yard catch. Reid put the pads back on Monday and Tuesday and started talking about details. The details, unfortunately, now include an offensive front that Arrowhead Addict pegs at roughly 40 percent compromised, with Josh Simmons’ back joining Chu Godrick’s sprained ankle on a list that will follow this team from St. Joe to Raymond James to the Denver opener on September 15. Eric Bieniemy is back from a leave no coach should have to take, and the through-line of this week is not nostalgia for the old machine — it is whether the new one can function under live rush before Brett Veach has to freeze a roster.

Treat Saturday the way Reid actually treats preseason Week 3: a dress rehearsal, not a referendum. Historically this is the last tape where starters see a real series count before the Seattle preseason finale turns into a roster-audition game. DraftKings has Tampa Bay -5.5 with a 37.5 total, which is a polite way of saying the market expects backups, clock, and another low-scoring August night. That line should not rattle anyone in this building. What should rattle them is the combination of a dinged tackle room and a wideout group already carrying enough injury concern that the local conversation has drifted toward calling an old Veach target and, separately, toward not bringing JuJu Smith-Schuster back just because he is available.

The honest edges sit in the rooms that decide whether Mahomes is playing football or survival. Creed Humphrey is still the post, Travis Kelce is still the gravity, and Wiley is the first real camp story that looks like a payoff instead of a hope. Spagnuolo will use Tampa’s preseason offense the way he always uses August: to sort which linebackers can rush, which nickels can disguise, and which safeties will tackle in a Cover-2 shell. None of that shows up in a 12-point box score. All of it shows up in September, when the Broncos walk into GEHA Field on ESPN and the regular season stops being theoretical.

Look past Tampa and the calendar gets real fast. Seattle comes to Arrowhead on August 29 for the last dress rehearsal, then the same Seahawks wait on the road in late October. Denver is Week 1 at home and Week 8 on the road. The AFC West is still the spine of this schedule, and a 6-11 team does not get to talk itself into a Super Bowl until the line holds up for four quarters against a division front. Polymarket has Kansas City at 9.5 percent to win the AFC and 6.2 percent to win the 2027 title. That is not an insult. That is the price of last year, plus an offensive line that is already asking questions in August.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Boot Mahomes off the injured edge

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script in Tampa, starters’ first live series

Josh Simmons’ back and Chu Godrick’s ankle mean you do not ask this tackle room to win a seven-step drop on the road. Bieniemy is back on the grass and Reid’s oldest answer still works: sell inside zone, move the launch point, and give Mahomes the flat/leak with Kelce or Wiley as the second look. It also keeps the 37.5-total game on schedule instead of in third-and-long.

Coaching point Humphrey’s snap-and-hat fake has to freeze the Mike or the boot is just a slow rollout. Mahomes throws the leak on time — no holding it to hunt a shot in August. If the starters sit after two series, the same call becomes a roster test for the second-team quarterback and the swing tackle.

Offense

Wiley on the stripe vs. single-high

Situation 2nd-and-6, Tampa shows one-high after sitting on the short game

Kansas City scored 12 against the Rams and is dealing with wideout injuries; the seam has to become a feature. Wiley’s challenged catch on Saturday is exactly the body-control skill four verts asks for, and Kelce’s mere alignment still pulls a hook defender. This is also the install you want on tape before Denver brings a real single-high look to Arrowhead on September 15.

Coaching point Bieniemy’s coaching point is boring and correct: inside stem to the hash, do not wander into a lazy post. Wiley stacks the hook and stays on the stripe. Mahomes throws it on rhythm or kills it — no hero ball with a banged-up line and a preseason scoreboard.

Offense

Mesh when the X room is compromised

Situation 3rd-and-5, man or match from Tampa’s second-level

The wide receiver injuries and the correct decision not to panic-sign JuJu Smith-Schuster leave Kansas City needing rubs, not isolations. Mesh puts Kelce and Wiley on collision crossers, gives the running back as the outlet, and does not require a healthy boundary X to win a 50-50. It is also the cleanest way to keep evaluating Wiley against live linebackers before Veach tapes the 53.

Coaching point Tight splits, legal pick, no lunge. Wiley has spent the week owning that he wants those contested balls — this is his down and distance. Quarterback eyes the second crosser, not the first, and takes the checkdown if Tampa switches. Reid will challenge the catch if he has to. Again.

Offense

Easy offense to keep the front honest

Situation 2nd-and-11 after a negative run, Tampa pinning its ears back

If 40 percent of the offensive front is dinged, dropback football is a tax. Reid and Bieniemy need a screen chapter that punishes an aggressive Bucs rush, gets the second-unit linemen working in space, and keeps Mahomes — or whoever is in — from eating free runners. This is also personnel tape: the guards who climb and the back who secures the ball are making the 53. The ones who dance are making the practice squad.

Coaching point Outside hand on the defensive end, now, not after the throw. Back checks the rush one beat, then gets north. Wideouts stalk, they do not look at the ball. If Simmons and Godrick are limited, this call is how you survive the series without putting the swing tackle in a phone booth.

Defense

Spags two-deep: ball in front, roster in focus

Situation 1st-and-10, early downs vs. Tampa’s preseason offense

A 37.5 total and a road dress rehearsal are not the night to hunt sacks for the highlight show. Spagnuolo’s Cover-2 shell takes away the shot, puts five under the sticks, and gives him a clean tackling tape on the safeties and linebackers who are actually fighting for jobs. That is how you use Tampa — as a sorting hat before the Seattle finale and the Denver opener.

Coaching point Corners funnel and do not get beat over the top for pride. Safeties no peek at play-action; the back-end leak is how preseason defenses get embarrassed. Spags wants the ball at the numbers and a gang tackle. If you cannot do that in August, you will not do it against Bo Nix in September.

Defense

Simulated heat to sort the 53

Situation 3rd-and-6, late in the starters’ stint or early in the second unit

This is Spagnuolo’s signature and the last useful live look before cuts. Show five, send four, drop a lineman, and find out which Chiefs linebackers and nickel defenders can actually rush while a big stays in the passing lane. Tampa’s preseason protection rules will tell you the truth. The winners of this snap are in the Week 1 dime package against Denver. The losers are watching it from the stands.

Coaching point Disguise until the snap — no early flinch from the second level. The dropping end has to land in the hook, not wander into a void Kelce would eat in a real game. Rush lanes stay wide; a clean A-gap in August becomes a Mahomes problem in September if you are teaching bad habits to your own scout-team look.

The storylines

What we’re watching

OC returns

Bieniemy is back on the grass, not just on a laptop

Eric Bieniemy has been in St. Joe for a week after a leave of absence for a family emergency, and Arrowhead Pride reports he did more than watch practice remotely while he was away. For a 6-11 offense that just scored 12 in the opener, the value is simple: the play-caller who has to script Mahomes’ first live series in Tampa is standing on the field again. Saturday is the first road test of that reunion.

OL alarm

Simmons’ back makes the tackle room a Week 1 problem

Josh Simmons’ back injury, stacked on Chu Godrick’s sprained ankle, is the first real threat to the September plan. Arrowhead Addict frames it as roughly 40 percent of the offensive front compromised, which is how you end up as a 5.5-point underdog in a preseason game and how you start talking about boots, screens, and tight-end gravity instead of seven-step shots. Tampa is a live rush. Denver will be a live rush that counts.

TE payoff

The Wiley patience project finally has tape

Jared Wiley’s nine-yard grab against the Rams needed Andy Reid’s challenge flag to become official, and he has spent the week talking about making those catches on purpose. Arrowhead Addict says the building’s patience may finally be ready to pay off. With the wideout room banged up, that is not a cute camp story — it is a usage plan for Tampa, Seattle, and Week 1.

WR market

Do not confuse availability with a Veach phone call

The wide receiver injuries have already spawned the annual outside-the-building shopping list, including the idea of circling back to an old Brett Veach target. JuJu Smith-Schuster hitting the waiver wire is the other magnet, and Arrowhead Addict’s read is the correct one: do not bite just because the jersey used to be red. Depth is a real problem. Nostalgia is not a scheme.

Top 100

Mahomes at 18 is a ranking, not a scouting report

Patrick Mahomes landed at No. 18 on the NFL Top 100, with Travis Kelce at 79 and Creed Humphrey at 94. After a 6-11 year the drop was inevitable, and Arrowhead Addict is right that lists like this cannot price greatness after a forgettable season. The only ranking that matters in this building is whether the pocket in Tampa, then against Denver, looks like a place a franchise quarterback can work.

Kingdom

Gansz, the noise, and the long game at Arrowhead

Former special teams coordinator Frank Gansz Jr. passed away at 64, a hard note in a camp week already heavy enough. The Kingdom still ranks second in Nielsen’s fandom index behind only Buffalo, and the Bills’ stadium saga is being read locally as an Arrowhead 2.0 cautionary tale. The noise will be there in September. The roster still has to earn it.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid’s August script vs. a real September problem

Andy Reid will not let a 12-20 preseason loss rewrite the opener, but Monday’s padded practice was already about honing details after the Rams tape. The coaching win in Tampa is a clean first-down menu that does not expose the tackle injuries, then a fast hook for the starters.

Bieniemy’s first road headset since the leave

Eric Bieniemy’s return from a family emergency is the most important staff note of camp week. The offense that scored 12 on Saturday now has its coordinator on the grass and on the headset; Tampa is the first chance to see whether the timing layer snaps back before Seattle and Denver.

Spagnuolo using Tampa as a personnel lab

Steve Spagnuolo does not need a preseason win. He needs to know which simulated-pressure pieces travel. Cover-2 and zone-blitz answers against Tampa’s backups are how he builds the first-down and third-down menus for the Broncos on September 15.

Special teams memory in a cut week

The passing of former special teams coordinator Frank Gansz Jr. is a reminder that this franchise has always treated the fourth phase as a coaching craft, not an afterthought. The bubble players who cover kicks in Tampa are playing for Gansz’s old standard as much as for a roster spot.

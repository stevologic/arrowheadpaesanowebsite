The story so far

Always looking ahead

After a 6-11 2025, the through-line in St. Joseph is construction, not coronation. Andy Reid and offensive coordinator Eric Bieniemy are hanging a new early-down identity on Kenneth Walker — and Travis Kelce is, per Arrowhead Addict, downright ecstatic about it — while Steve Spagnuolo is still trying to get a reshaped defense on the same page. The first live bullets arrive Saturday at Arrowhead against the Rams, Dave Toub has already sketched the preseason plan, and every roster decision from right tackle to the second corner is now a Week 1 problem, not a July hypothetical.

Walker is the clearest offensive swing. Kansas City needed a lead back who can win early downs and make the play-action catalog honest again; Kelce’s reaction tells you the locker room sees a complementary piece, not a camp body. That matters because the pass game is still being re-stitched. Rashee Rice is back on the grass after a parole violation wiped out most of his offseason, talking about the group around him, while the wideout competition has taken another injury hit heading into the opener. Hollywood Brown’s 2024 flier is already a footnote in Philadelphia. The ball is going to live with Mahomes, Kelce, Walker, and whoever is actually healthy outside.

The other addition is a reunion. Emmanuel Ogbah is back on the 90-man, a decade in, the same rusher Spagnuolo has always had an affection for from the 2019 Super Bowl year. The film says there is still a veteran edge who can set a compact and rush in defined games; the scouting grapevine is more mixed on what Year 11 actually is. That is a classic Spags signing: not a savior, a chess piece, especially useful while the coordinator himself admits the defensive sync has been a little slow-moving and spent Day 12 talking tackling.

Camp is also a job board. Jaylon Moore is welcoming a surprise challenge at right tackle. Mansoor Delane, the LSU corner Kansas City traded up three spots to draft, just produced the kind of update that lets the building exhale. Jake Briningstool, the Clemson UDFA with real guarantee money, is healthy again and back in the tight-end mix behind Kelce. The first unofficial depth chart is out, and Saturday against a Rams staff that already got joint-practice reps is the first chance to see which of those lines were courtesy and which were a tell.

Look past the dress rehearsal and the market is honest. A 6-11 team sitting at 6.5 percent to hoist the 2027 trophy is the Mahomes tax, not a Super Bowl projection. The path to making that number look cheap is boring and specific: Walker has to be a down-to-down answer, the right tackle has to stop being a camp plot, Rice has to be available and in rhythm, and Spagnuolo’s front has to stop looking like it is still introducing itself. Arrowhead gets its name back for 2026. The product still has to earn the noise.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker downhill, first-and-10 identity

Situation 1st-and-10, 12/21 personnel, opening script vs. the Rams at Arrowhead — first live evaluation of the new lead back

Kenneth Walker is the offensive swing of the offseason and Kelce is already selling it. After a 6-11 year that lost its early-down teeth, Bieniemy needs a one-cut answer that makes the rest of the call sheet honest. Saturday is the first padded defense that did not share a cafeteria with them.

Coaching point Walker has to press the line of scrimmage and pick, not bounce every run into a camp highlight. Jaylon Moore and whoever is winning the surprise RT job must lock the backside B-gap or the whole identity play dies in the backfield. Kelce’s combo to the play-side linebacker is the difference between a two-yard thud and a crease.

Offense

Sell Walker, boot Mahomes

Situation 2nd-and-medium after Walker has hit the A-gap once or twice; move the pocket vs. a Rams front that will climb to the run fake

This is still a Reid-Mahomes offense. If Walker is real, the boot becomes the easiest completion on the card and the cleanest way to protect an offensive line that is auditioning a right tackle. Kelce leaking off the fake is the read Mahomes has thrown in his sleep for seven years.

Coaching point Mahomes holds the mesh one extra count so the Rams end bites. Moore cannot get beaten up the field — if the contain player stays home, the boot is a sack drill. Kelce sells the block for two steps, then leaks; Rice or the healthy boundary receiver runs the over to hold the safety.

Offense

Easy offense: Walker in space

Situation 2nd-and-long or any down the Rams show five-man heat; keep Mahomes clean while the WR room is dinged up

Arrowhead Addict flagged another hit to the wideout competition heading into the opener. You do not need four healthy separators to throw a screen. Walker plus an offensive line climbing to the second level is how Bieniemy manufactures explosive plays without asking a thinned room to win vertically on Saturday night.

Coaching point Walker sets the landmark and goes — no extra dance in the backfield. The playside tackle and guard have to get to the linebackers, not admire the toss. Kelce chips and leads; the remaining healthy receiver cracks the force player. This is also a live test of whether the new staff and the line are speaking the same language.

Offense

Rice-Kelce rub, third down

Situation 3rd-and-5, Rams nickel/man, intermediate or red-area — first chance to see Rice in a real chain-moving concept after the offseason he had

Rice is back saying he has the right group around him, and the outside room is taking on water. Mesh puts the two guys you actually trust — Kelce and Rice — on colliding crossers and lets Jake Briningstool, now healthy, sit in the hole as the zone-beater. It is the most Kansas City answer to a thin wideout board.

Coaching point Rice has to run a legal rub, not a pick, after the year he just had — officials will be looking. Kelce throttles vs. zone and rips vs. man. Briningstool, who already flashed with the starters before the injury timeout, sits at six yards and becomes Mahomes’ answer if the Rams pass off the mesh. No hero ball from Rice on the first live series.

Defense

Two-deep while the defense catches up

Situation Early-down Rams 11 personnel, first-team period Saturday — protect the young corner and coach the tackling Spagnuolo just spent a day on

Spagnuolo said the defensive sync has been a little slow-moving and used Day 12 to talk tackling. Delane just produced a positive update, but he is still a drafted bet, not a finished shutdown. A two-deep shell takes away the shot, lets Delane play with help, and forces the rally-and-tackle that this unit has not proven it can do yet.

Coaching point Safeties stay on top of the numbers — no curiosity steps. Delane funnels inside and breaks on the throw, he does not hunt a highlight. Linebackers fit the alley and finish; Spags made missed arm tackles the talking point for a reason. Vanilla is a feature this week, not a bug.

Defense

Ogbah as the fake heat

Situation 3rd-and-medium, show five, let the new-old edge drop and rush the second level — first live look at why Spags wanted him back

The market on Ogbah as a straight rusher is mixed. The market on Ogbah as a Spagnuolo translator is why he is here. A slow-syncing front needs a veteran who has already played this call in this building. Simulated pressure lets Kansas City manufacture a rush without asking Year-11 Ogbah to beat a Rams tackle clean, and it tests whether the second level is finally lining up on time.

Coaching point Ogbah sells two hard steps, then drops to the hook — that fake is the entire play. The Mike has to be late enough to miss the slide and early enough to hit the backfoot. If the Rams hot the ball to the vacated flat, the dropped end has to be there. This is an installation snap as much as a pressure snap.

The storylines

What we’re watching

New hammer

Kelce is selling Walker as the missing piece

The biggest offensive change of the offseason is a lead back, and Travis Kelce is not doing polite veteran enthusiasm. If Bieniemy can hang inside zone and the boot game on Kenneth Walker, Mahomes gets the play-action leverage this offense lost when 2025 went sideways.

Year 11

Ogbah is a Spags comfort signing, not a savior

Emmanuel Ogbah returns to the building that used him in 2019, when he posted 5.5 sacks on the Super Bowl LIV team. The film still shows a long, compact setter who can rush in defined games; the recent reviews are mixed. Treat him as a veteran organizer for a front that is still late to sync.

Rice

Year 4, finally on the grass

Rashee Rice missed most of the offseason program after a parole violation accelerated a jail sentence, then stood in front of the cameras this week and talked about the group around him. Kansas City needs the 2023 version of Rice, not the controversy, and a thinned wideout room makes that urgency louder.

Right tackle

Moore has company he did not expect

Jaylon Moore opened camp as the presumed right tackle and is now publicly welcoming a surprise challenge. That is not a feel-good quote. That is an offensive staff telling you the job is live heading into the first padded game.

Secondary

Delane update lets the building breathe

The Chiefs moved up three spots for LSU corner Mansoor Delane because they want a shutdown piece next to the veterans. The latest report is the first one Kingdom can exhale over — which only matters if he is actually taking the preseason reps that turn a draft card into a Week 1 plan.

Tight end

Briningstool is back in the fight

Jake Briningstool landed $264,000 guaranteed as a Clemson UDFA, flashed early in camp with the starters, then had to get healthy. A two-tight-end package behind Kelce is how Reid and Bieniemy can protect a banged-up wideout room.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Bieniemy hanging an identity on Walker

The new offensive staff’s first job is not a cute formation. It is making Kenneth Walker a down-to-down answer so Reid’s play-action catalog stops being theory. Saturday’s script should tell you whether that install is real or still a walkthrough.

Spagnuolo coaching sync and tackling, not exotic

When the coordinator says the defense has been a little slow-moving and then spends a practice on tackling, believe him. Expect two-deep, defined rush lanes, and Ogbah used as a known quantity while the new pieces catch up.

Dave Toub’s preseason plan is a roster tool

Toub has already mapped how he wants to use the Rams game. In a 6-11 hangover, those teams snaps are not exhibition — they are the last honest evaluator for the bottom of the 53.

Reid vs. a Rams staff that already went live

The Rams took joint-practice reps; the Chiefs have not. Give Sean McVay’s group credit for being further along in look-on-look work. Kansas City’s edge is the quarterback and the red-and-gold script, not the prep calendar.

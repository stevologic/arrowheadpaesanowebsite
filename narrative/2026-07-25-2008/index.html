The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Mahomes Boot Answer off Zone Look

Situation Week 1 script, early-down 1st-and-10 vs. Denver contain ends who are coached to spy the knee brace

Mahomes is cleared as a full participant and has said he plans to do everything he did before; the boot reintroduces perimeter throws without demanding a hard plant in the middle of the pocket against Denver’s first-drive pass rush.

Coaching point Reid/Bieniemy: sell inside zone with Humphrey and the guards, get Mahomes to the edge on time, and give him a high-low on the boot leg so he never has to force a layered in-breaking throw on the first series.

Offense

Seam Stress vs. Denver Cover-3 Shell

Situation 2nd-and-medium in the red-zone approach when Denver drops to protect the sticks on MNF

A healthy Mahomes deep-ball calendar is the fastest way to punish Broncos safeties who will sit on intermediate crossers after watching the 2025 tape; four verts forces the middle-of-field defender to pick a seam and leaves a one-on-one outside.

Coaching point Bieniemy point: outside receivers must win release stem versus press, seams bend to the hash on time, and Mahomes’ first read is the single-high collision—no holding the ball to admire the brace holding up.

Offense

Mesh Chaos vs. Broncos Man Heat

Situation 3rd-and-5, empty or 11 personnel when Denver shows two-high then locks man and late-adds a slot blitzer

If Denver tests Mahomes’ post-injury processing with pure man, mesh creates free access without requiring a deep drop; it also gives Fields package reps in camp a template if he’s needed as a scrambler off the same look later in the year.

Coaching point Teach the rub as legal traffic, not a pick—inside receiver must flatten, outside sits or corners based on leverage, and the running back’s check path is Mahomes’ safety valve if the first crosser gets trailed.

Offense

Easy Button Screen When Denver Over-Rushes

Situation 2nd-and-long after a first-down incompletion; Broncos pin their ears back at the edge

Protecting Mahomes’ knee early in games means stealing free yards when Denver’s ends ignore the back; camp needs this timing crisp so Week 1 does not become a dropback marathon at 2nd-and-11.

Coaching point OL must get to the second level immediately—no holding at the line of scrimmage—while the RB shows pass set for one count. Mahomes’ hitch and throw have to be on rhythm; Fields can mirror the same play in scout-team lookalikes to keep the look honest.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 Rally vs. Denver Vertical Pairing

Situation Long-yardage or two-minute before half when Denver tries to take the top off with twins verticals

With fringe corners still battling for nickel snaps, a true Cover-2 shell protects rookies and bubble veterans by putting a safety over the top and asking the front five to finish—exactly the kind of structure you want if Delane or Williams is forced into extended MNF snaps.

Coaching point Spagnuolo: corners soft-pedal and funnel inside, linebackers own the hook windows, and the rush must keep Mahomes’ counterpart from stepping up. No freelancing from the fourth corner—play the technique that got you the roster spot.

Defense

Simulated Pressure Fire vs. Broncos Max Protect

Situation 3rd-and-7 or longer; Denver keeps in an extra back/TE to account for KC’s edge rotation

The open defensive-end snaps—and VJ Anthony’s camp audition—are built for Spagnuolo look-alike pressures that show six and bring five while dropping a lineman into the throwing lane Mahomes’ counterpart wants on MNF.

Coaching point Show heavy to the field, late-walk a linebacker, drop the end into the flat, and make the hot throw go into traffic. If Anthony is active, use him as the looper who wins with timing, not just pure bull rush—teach the exchange until it’s invisible on the practice film.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Training camp at Missouri Western is not nostalgia; it’s triage after a 6-11 year that finally put real doubt on the dynasty narrative. Patrick Mahomes is cleared to practice as a full participant, brace and all, with his eyes locked on starting the Monday night opener against Denver at Arrowhead—and the rest of the building has about six weeks to prove the supporting cast can keep up when the pads come on.

Andy Reid’s first camp remarks framed the medical board cleanly enough: Mahomes is up, the football is moving, and the quarterbacks-rookies-veterans wave has already hit St. Joseph. That matters because Eric Bieniemy’s install cannot be theoretical. With Justin Fields in the building carrying real career stakes as a change-of-pace and emergency option, every 7-on-7 rep is a referendum on whether Kansas City still dictates leverage with 11-personnel spacing or has to manufacture easy buttons—screens, boots, and early-down zone—to protect a surgically repaired knee.

The roster edges are where camp will get loud. Kansas City never made that last veteran defensive-end signing, which puts UDFA VJ Anthony squarely in the ‘earn a hat’ conversation opposite the established rushers. On the back end, the top of the corner depth chart looks settled enough, but the fringe battle—first-rounder Mansoor Delane, Nohl Williams, and the rest of the bubble—will decide whether Steve Spagnuolo can stay multiple without nickeling himself into soft coverage on early downs.

Look ahead and the schedule does not ease anyone in. Denver in Week 1 on ESPN, Indy in a primetime home rematch feel, then a road swing to Miami. ESPN FPI has the Chiefs around 59 percent to take care of the Broncos; DraftKings sits Kansas City at -3 with a 42.5 total—favorite, not juggernaut. Polymarket’s 6 percent title price and 9.5 percent AFC flag are the market’s polite way of saying the league has moved on until this team re-proves it. A strong camp—Mahomes throwing without hesitation, an identity run game, and two surprise defenders sticking—turns that skepticism into September oxygen.

The through-line is simple: this is not about rewriting 2025. It’s about stacking clean practices so Week 1 against Denver is an extension of camp rules, not a scavenger hunt for timing. If the brace becomes background noise and the rookies who must play early actually play early, Arrowhead gets its edge back before the AFC West starts keeping score in October.

The storylines

What we’re watching

Mahomes Ramp

Full participant, brace on, eyes on MNF

Reid confirmed Mahomes will be a full participant to open camp, and the quarterback said he plans to do everything he did before with Week 1 against Denver as the checkpoint. The first presser also put the brace and the mental side of returning from a serious knee injury on the record—timing and trust will be the real camp stats.

UDFA Watch

VJ Anthony’s clean path to a hat

Kansas City skipped a late veteran defensive-end signing after the draft, leaving a real runway for UDFA VJ Anthony. In a Spagnuolo front that lives on rotation and simulated pressure, a rookie who can win third-down snaps in August forces his way onto the Week 1 active list.

Corner Bubble

Fringe CBs will decide nickel math

The top of the corner room is stable, but the bottom is one of camp’s most competitive fights—Mansoor Delane, Nohl Williams, and the veterans on the cut line. Spagnuolo’s package variety only works if four or five corners can play clean coverage without lighting up the red cross.

QB Room Stakes

Justin Fields’ career hinge year

Fields is a virtual lock to make the roster, but 2026 is about proving he can be a reliable Mahomes complement and situational weapon. Clean camp command and a few explosive package reps would reframe him from reclamation story to chess piece for Bieniemy.

Media Temperature

Doubt is real—and useful

After 6-11, national skepticism is no longer a straw man; it’s the default. That outside noise is fuel if camp answers are decisive, especially with a Week 1 division opponent already circling Mahomes’ mobility on the chalkboard.

AFC West Lens

Division camps are hunting the same thing

Denver, Las Vegas, and the Chargers all enter camp with their own quarterback and identity questions. Kansas City’s advantage is still coaching continuity—Reid, Bieniemy, Spagnuolo—if the health and depth questions don’t erase it by mid-August.

Bieniemy identity under a repaired knee

Expect an early-down menu that marries inside zone to boot and screens—calls that move the launch point and keep Denver from teeing off on a stationary Mahomes.

Spagnuolo’s depth math at corner and end

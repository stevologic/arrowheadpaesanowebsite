The story so far

Always looking ahead

The 6–11 hangover is still in the building. Kansas City opened camp's live portion with a 20–12 loss to the Rams, 12 accepted penalties, and a Monday padded practice spent sanding down the details Andy Reid did not like on tape. Preseason Week 3 in Tampa is the dress rehearsal — starters get a real script, the bubble gets a last long look, and the only number that matters is whether the protection plan around Patrick Mahomes, returning from the most serious injury of his career, can survive a live rush before the September 15 home opener against Denver.

Reid's Monday update was two-toned. Trey Smith's hip drew encouraging language that still left the bigger picture unresolved, and tackle Chu Godrick, who sprained an ankle in the Rams game, did not work. That is a problem of sequencing, not just depth: Eric Bieniemy's offense cannot install a September identity if the interior cannot certify inside zone and the edges cannot certify a max-protect boot package. Arrowhead Addict put it bluntly — the Chiefs cannot afford to get Mahomes' protection plan wrong in 2026 — and Tampa is the first place that plan gets stress-tested against a home crowd and a defense that will not be in preseason pajamas for the first quarter.

The tape from Saturday was not empty. Fifth-round back Emmett Johnson wasted no time looking like a steal, rookie quarterback Garrett Nussmeier handled his debut cleanly, wideout Cyrus Allen hit a highlight-reel catch, and three undrafted players forced harder roster math. Jaden Hicks ate extended defensive snaps; Mansoor Delane, back in a full practice, immediately made his mark on the corner depth chart. Those are the August stories that actually move a 53. Veach is still working with roughly $4.2 million in cap space and a public conversation about trades before the roster is finalized — the Seattle preseason finale on August 29 is the last exhibition, not the last decision.

The market is not buying a bounce-back on reputation. Tampa Bay is a 5.5-point home favorite with a 37.5 total, a preseason number that reads like a fade of Kansas City's starter availability and of last year's 6–11 finish, the worst of the Reid era. Polymarket has the Chiefs at 9.5 percent to win the AFC and 6.2 percent to win the 2027 Super Bowl. Treat those as a temperature check, not a prophecy — but do not pretend the room is still assuming February. The through-line from Tampa to Seattle to the ESPN opener against Denver is simple: clean up the flags, certify the line combinations, give Mahomes a defined, low-risk script, and decide which bubble players are actually on the plane in September.

Arrowhead will be loud when it counts. Chiefs fans just ranked second in Nielsen's fandom index, behind only Buffalo, and the first chance to cash that energy is Week 1 against the Broncos on September 15. Nothing that happens in Tampa changes the standings. Everything that happens in Tampa tells you whether this staff is ready to stop the slide or still arguing with last year's tape.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Open the script downhill

Situation 1st-and-10, opening series in Tampa, first-team 11/12 personnel — the dress-rehearsal identity snap.

Trey Smith's hip is the unresolved variable on the interior, and Bieniemy cannot install a September run game if the ones do not certify zone steps against a live Bucs front. Emmett Johnson already showed the one-cut answer against the Rams; this is the snap that tells you whether that look survives when the offensive line is not at full health.

Coaching point Reid's Monday note was about details after a sloppy opener — first step, hat placement, no wasted climb. If Smith is managed, the combo to the play-side linebacker has to be called tighter and the backside cut has to be automatic. Johnson: one foot in the ground, no dancing, get the call on schedule so the boot off this picture is real later in the drive.

Offense

Move the launch point

Situation 2nd-and-6 after a zone look, Mahomes in the game, Bucs edge setting a hard upfield rush.

The entire 2026 conversation is about not getting Mahomes' protection plan wrong as he returns from the most serious injury of his career. Booting him off the inside-zone fake takes the edge rusher out of a straight-line sack and gives Reid a defined, low-risk throw in a game where Tampa is laying 5.5 and will play the first quarter live.

Coaching point Sell the zone with the guard and the tight end, do not peek the rush, get the shoulders around. The first read is the boot flat, the second is the over. If Godrick's ankle is still keeping a tackle out, this is how you protect the franchise without asking a backup to win a one-on-one set. Catch, throw, get down — this is a dress rehearsal, not a hero ball tape.

Offense

Punish the upfield rush

Situation 2nd-and-9 or 3rd-and-medium, Bucs pinching or twisting, Mahomes or Nussmeier under center/gun.

Johnson already won in space against the Rams, and a screen package is the cleanest way to keep the quarterback from holding the ball while Smith's hip and Godrick's ankle leave the protection combinations unsettled. It is also a live eval for the undrafted linemen who just made the roster math harder — can they get out and finish in space?

Coaching point Ball is gone on the third step. Linemen release now, do not linger in pass sets. Johnson: catch, north, one cut off the first color. Bieniemy wants this to look like easy offense, not a broken play. If Tampa's home crowd is juicing the rush in the first quarter, this is the answer that also feeds the bubble tape Veach still has to grade.

Offense

Give the rookie defined reads

Situation 3rd-and-5, second quarter or two-minute, Nussmeier in, Tampa nickel likely in man after the ones sit.

Nussmeier made a clean first impression against the Rams and will own the middle of this game once Mahomes' script is done. Mesh gives a rookie quarterback two rub crossers, a sit/corner answer, and a checkdown — the opposite of a hero throw. Cyrus Allen already hit a highlight catch; this is how you find out if that was a play or a role.

Coaching point Timing on a string: inside foot through, do not climb. If Tampa switches the crossers, go to the sit or the back immediately — no late balls into traffic in a game that does not count. Reid and Bieniemy are still honing details after the opener; this call is the detail. Complete it, stay on schedule, do not donate a short field to a home preseason defense.

Defense

Take the shot off the board

Situation 1st-and-10, Bucs first- or second-team trying a scripted vertical, Chiefs ones or twos in the game.

Jaden Hicks just got extended snaps in the opener and Mansoor Delane just returned to a full practice and won his reps. Spagnuolo's two-deep shell is how you keep a dress rehearsal from becoming a track meet and how you get clean communication tape on the young secondary before Seattle and the Denver opener.

Coaching point Corners squat, funnel, and do not get stacked. Hicks is the alley/robber trigger — downhill on anything in front, no free explosives over the top. The Rams tape already had too many mental mistakes; this look only works if the back end is on the same page for three seconds. Tackle, peel, next snap. That is the whole coaching point.

Defense

Show Denver the simulated look

Situation 3rd-and-6, late first half, Spagnuolo sending a dress-rehearsal pressure vs. Tampa's ones or twos.

This is Spagnuolo's signature, and Week 3 is when you put the simulated-pressure package on tape so it is not a foreign language on September 15 against Denver. It also grades the bubble edge and the young linebackers: can they rush and drop on command? After 12 penalties against the Rams, the communication has to travel.

Coaching point Show five or six, drop a lineman into the hook, bring the WILL. Hicks and the nickel have to announce the hot and the hole. No free runners, no coverage bust behind the pressure — that is how preseason third downs become September problems. If the look is clean in Tampa, you can spend the Seattle finale on the last roster bodies instead of reteaching the call.

The storylines

What we’re watching

Protection

The Mahomes plan cannot be theoretical

Mahomes is coming back from the most serious injury of his career, and the entire 2026 offense is downstream of whether the pocket is real. Tampa is where Reid and Bieniemy have to show the boot, screen, and max-protect answers — not just talk about them — before Denver arrives at Arrowhead.

Interior

Trey Smith's hip is the quiet crisis

Reid's language was encouraging and still incomplete. If Smith is managed or limited, the inside-zone identity and the play-action boot off it both wobble, and the Week 1 plan versus Denver has to be rewritten in a hurry. This is the talking point that follows Kansas City to Tampa whether or not his name is in the starting lineup.

Rookie RB

Emmett Johnson already looks like a Veach hit

The fifth-round back did not wait for a depth-chart invitation. His first NFL snaps against the Rams were decisive enough that the staff now has a live complement to the veteran room and a built-in screen-and-zone answer if the pass protection stays shaky.

QB2

Nussmeier's debut bought him the middle of this game

The rookie handled his first live series with poise, which is exactly what you want heading into a dress rehearsal where the starter's snap count will be managed. Tampa is his real eval: can he operate mesh, screens, and a two-minute board without putting the defense in a short field?

Secondary

Delane is back, Hicks already ate snaps

Mansoor Delane's first full practice of the summer turned heads, and Jaden Hicks' extended playing time against the Rams was not an accident. Spagnuolo needs both to look like rotation pieces in Tampa so the Seattle finale can be about the last bubble bodies, not the starting nickel.

Bubble

Three UDFAs just made cutdown week harder

Preseason openers exist for the nameless, and three undrafted Chiefs used the Rams game to force a real decision. With $4.2 million in space and trade chatter still live, Veach can still add — but he also has to stop cutting people who just won their snaps.

Bieniemy's install vs. an incomplete line

The OC has to certify inside zone and the boot/screen answers off it while Trey Smith's hip and Chu Godrick's ankle sit in the background. If the pictures are ugly in Tampa, the Denver call sheet gets rewritten in a week.

Spagnuolo's dress-rehearsal package

Cover-2 to take away the shot, simulated pressure to stress protection and communication. Hicks' extended snaps and Delane's practice return only matter if those two looks look like rotation football, not a camp drill.

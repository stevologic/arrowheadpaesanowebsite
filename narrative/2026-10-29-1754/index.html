Current state

A 5-1 that still has to fix the rush, the kick, and the hits

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

5-1

The live record is 5-1, the 2025 finish of 6-11 is history, and the football problem is current: an offense that posted 213 yards on the road and a kicking operation Toub is trying to steady before Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT.

What we need to work on

Early-down carries that move the sticks

Sixteen rushes for 50 yards left Mahomes in shotgun on third-and-long too often. Walker’s 14-for-36 line has to be a floor Denver is forced to respect, not a ceiling.

Get the ball out before the seventh hit

Seattle recorded seven quarterback hits and one sack. Bieniemy does not need a speech about toughness. He needs screens, mesh, and boots so 19-of-33 is not the script again.

Two hits and zero sacks of Darnold

Darnold took zero sacks. Kansas City had two quarterback hits. Arrowhead Pride flags Chris Jones and the pass rush as a pressing concern. Spagnuolo’s answer in Denver is coverage that rallies and a called simulated pressure.

Butker’s operation, with Tucker in the building

Toub said the club is trying to get Butker right and that Tucker’s practice-squad signing is not a replacement. The misses from 58 and 45 are on the tape. A 40.5 total means those kicks can decide a one-score game.

What to think about

How much of Week 1 still applies

Do not treat the September score as a scouting report.

Kelce’s standard versus the touch count

Kelce told New Heights the team cannot be okay with being mediocre, via Arrowhead Pride. He had four catches for 44 and an illegal shift that wiped a play. Usage and detail both have to rise.

Whether the market is ahead of the tape

FPI 55.7 percent and minus 1.5 say contender. Eleven first downs and 50 rush yards say the contender label is rented until Denver. Arrowhead Addict still has the club among Super Bowl favorites. Fans can hold both ideas.

Looking ahead

The Denver rematch: clock, width, and a one-score road plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Denver is the assignment: complete the season series on Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT on CBS, lay 1.5, and play like a team that remembers 31-10 without pretending 213 yards was a one-off weather story. A win, Arrowhead Pride notes, can open a three-game division cushion; a loss can square the race.

How they match up

Kansas City’s edge is still Mahomes, Kelce, Walker, and a defense coordinated by Spagnuolo that can rally when the shell is right. Denver’s edge, if it copies the last opponent’s formula, is early-down runs, third-down stays, and field position if Butker is unsteady. Denver does not need Seattle’s skill names to steal the same down-and-distance. Kansas City’s counter is width and easy offense before any vertical shot. Worthy and Gray can stress the sideline and the intermediate; Walker has to be more than a 36-yard complement. The pass rush, Jones included, has to affect the quarterback without abandoning the run fits that Wilson-type volume exposes. Special teams is a live unit, not a footnote: Toub, Butker, and a practice-squad Tucker.

Own first and second down

If Walker and the line cannot create third-and-manageable, Denver will sit in the same third-down bind Seattle used, 3-of-11. Inside zone and a screen on early downs are calls, not decorations.

Two-deep against the chunk

Smith-Njigba’s 28-yard touchdown and the 28-yard catch after it are the warning. Cover-2, with Delane and Williams, takes the deep shot away and asks the linebackers to tackle. Do not ask a single corner to win every vertical.

Simulated pressure as a call, not a trophy

Zero sacks of Darnold and two hits is the problem. A zone blitz — show heat, drop a lineman, rush from the second level — is how Spagnuolo can bother Denver’s quarterback.

Kick the ones that fit the game

A 50-yard make is in the scoring list. A 58-yard miss and a 45-yard miss are too. In a 40.5 game, Reid and Toub should not chase distance that the operation has not shown.

  1. Open with under-center or pistol-looking inside zone for Walker so Denver cannot sit in a pass-only call sheet after the 16-carry tape.
  2. On the first third-and-medium, throw mesh to Kelce with Worthy on the sit or corner and Gray as the rub partner, Mahomes’s checkdown to Walker available.
  3. After any seven-man pressure look, call the screen to Walker or a quick throw to Gray and let the line get in space.
  4. Once Denver flows to Walker, boot Mahomes to the perimeter with Kelce on the flat and Worthy clearing.
  5. On Denver’s early downs, play Cover-2 and rally. Tranquill, Bolton if available from the injury note on the Seattle tape, and the safeties fit the run before chasing a sack.
  6. Call one zone blitz on a Denver third-and-medium with Jones rushing and a second-level player replacing him in the zone.
Last game

What 11 first downs actually looked like at Lumen

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

L
Seattle Seahawks Week 7 · Sun, Oct 25 · 7:20 PM CT
KC 3–20

At Lumen Field, with kickoff Sun Oct 25, 7:20 PM CT, Seattle beat Kansas City 20-3. The Chiefs’ eight drives produced 213 yards and 11 first downs; Seattle’s eight drives produced 410 yards, and the Seahawks held the ball for 35:51 to Kansas City’s 24:09.

The run game never became an identity. Walker had 14 carries for 36 yards and four catches, 18 touches, and the team rushed 16 times for 50 yards, with Mahomes accounting for the other two carries. The listed carries were modest: up the middle for 2, 4, 3, and 1, left end for 4 and 2, right end for 4. Creed Humphrey’s shotgun snap at Q1 13:11 was fumbled and recovered by Mahomes, and it still stole a down from a drive that already had no margin.

The pass tape was volume without explosives that stuck. Mahomes was 19-of-33 for 165, rating 58.3, with one sack and seven quarterback hits. Gray caught a 19-yarder to the Seattle 48 in the first quarter and another 19-yarder in the fourth. Worthy was targeted on incompletions deep right, including one with Derick Hall in coverage, and had three catches on the touch list.

Seattle’s explosives and its run clock were the game. Smith-Njigba caught the 28-yard touchdown on the reversed deep-left throw at Q2 1:44, with Mansoor Delane the defender on the original out-of-bounds ruling, then a 28-yard catch to the Kansas City 2 at Q2 0:35. Arrowhead Pride notes Delane, the sixth overall pick, is working through those snaps. Wilson’s 24 carries, plus Jones and Holani, built the 153-yard rushing total. Darnold was not sacked. Kansas City managed two quarterback hits. Nick Bolton was injured on a Wilson carry at Q3 11:19 and still shows up on later tackles in the play list; Drue Tranquill finished with 12 tackles.

The one defensive takeaway did not flip the score. At Q2 0:23, Nohl Williams intercepted Darnold’s short-middle throw intended for Rashid Shaheed and returned it 65 yards, with George Karlaftis credited on the play. Kansas City then missed a 58-yard Butker attempt at Q2 0:01. In the fourth, Butker missed from 45, wide right, at Q4 8:38, and the next Chiefs drive ended on downs at Q4 3:19 on a 3-yard completion to Kelce. Kingsley Suamataia’s holding at Q4 11:44 wiped a play on a drive that was already behind. Seattle closed it with the 21-yard touchdown at Q4 3:47.

What held up

  • Williams’s interception of Darnold and the 65-yard return
  • Gray’s two 19-yard catches and Kelce’s 4 for 44
  • Tranquill’s 12 tackles in a game Seattle ran it 41 times
  • A 50-yard field goal that finally put Kansas City on the board at 3-13

What broke

  • 50 rushing yards on 16 attempts and Walker at 14 for 36
  • 3-of-11 on third down and 11 first downs
  • Seven quarterback hits on Mahomes and one sack
  • Zero sacks of Darnold and two quarterback hits by Kansas City
  • Smith-Njigba’s 28-yard touchdown and the later 28-yard catch, with Delane in the picture

Eight drives, 11 first downs

Kansas City and Seattle each had eight drives. The difference was conversion and clock: 3-of-11 and 24:09 against 11-of-16 and 35:51. Denver will try to live in that same early-down run picture.

Okada, not the intended receiver

The Chiefs’ turnover was Okada’s interception of a Mahomes throw intended for Royals. Williams’s 65-yard interception return was the Chiefs’ takeaway. Neither changed the 3-20 final.

The 50-yard make did not cover the misses

Kansas City’s only points were a 50-yard field goal at Q3 1:42. Butker was short from 58 and wide right from 45. That is the file Toub is working before Denver.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The next kickoff is at Denver on Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT on CBS, a rematch of the Week 1 win at Arrowhead, 31-10, with Andy Reid’s team laying 1.5 and carrying a 3-20 loss that never let Patrick Mahomes own the ball. Arrowhead Pride frames the AFC West math cleanly: a sweep could put Kansas City three games up in the division, and a loss could even the standings into the second half.

Seattle is the supporting tape, not the lead. Kansas City finished with 11 first downs, 50 rushing yards on 16 attempts, 163 net passing yards, 213 total yards, a 3-of-11 third-down line, one turnover, eight drives, and 24:09 of possession. Seattle had 24 first downs, 153 rushing yards on 41 attempts, 257 net passing yards, 410 total yards, an 11-of-16 third-down line, one turnover, eight drives, and 35:51. Sam Darnold was 20-of-28 with a 96.9 rating and zero sacks. Mahomes was 19-of-33 for 165 yards, one interception, one sack, and a 58.3 rating, with seven quarterback hits charged to Seattle and two hits recorded by Kansas City.

The scoring order belongs to Seattle until a 50-yard Kansas City field goal. Seattle’s 25-yard field goal at Q2 8:22 made it 0-3. Seattle’s 28-yard touchdown at Q2 1:44 made it 0-10, the play-by-play showing Darnold to Jaxon Smith-Njigba. Seattle’s 35-yard field goal at Q3 8:09 made it 0-13. Kansas City’s 50-yard field goal at Q3 1:42 made it 3-13. Seattle’s 21-yard touchdown at Q4 3:47 made it 3-20. Harrison Butker missed from 58 and from 45. That is the special-teams file Dave Toub is trying to repair, with Justin Tucker now on the practice squad and Toub saying the move is not a replacement of the starter.

Denver does not have to be exotic to make this uncomfortable. If the Broncos pile up carries the way Seattle did — Emanuel Wilson 24 rushes, George Holani and Velus Jones Jr. mixing in, direct snaps to AJ Barner — Steve Spagnuolo’s front has to tackle on early downs. If Denver sits in man and heat, Eric Bieniemy has answers that do not require a 33-attempt hero script: inside zone for Kenneth Walker III, mesh and a screen for Travis Kelce, Noah Gray, and Xavier Worthy, then a boot once the run look is honest. Week 1’s 31-10 is useful memory, not a blueprint for a 40.5 total on the road.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid and Bieniemy, down-and-distance first

The head coach and offensive coordinator cannot ask Mahomes to throw them out of 3rd-and-long again. Walker’s carries and the easy concepts have to show up before the verticals that were incomplete to Kelce and Worthy.

Spagnuolo’s two jobs

Cover-2 to limit explosives, and a zone blitz he actually calls, because two quarterback hits and zero sacks of Darnold will not win a division rematch if Denver runs it 30 times.

Toub and the kickers

Toub’s public line is that the staff is trying to get Butker right and that Tucker on the practice squad is not a replacement. The operation still has to be trustworthy from 45 in a one-score game.

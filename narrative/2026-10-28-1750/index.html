Current state

A 5-1 room with a kicking question and a short reset

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

5-1

The Chiefs are 5-1, a one-score road favorite at Denver, and a week removed from their worst offensive yardage line of this season. The staff's job is to keep the Chargers identity — 422 yards, 22 first downs, a 22-6 win — from being treated as ancient history, and to stop Seattle's 20-3 tape from becoming the league's template.

What we need to work on

Early-down carries for Walker

Fourteen carries and 36 yards will not stress a defense that can rush four and hit the quarterback seven times. Bieniemy needs Walker on schedule before third down, not as a checkdown after the pocket is already noisy.

Third-down answers under seven hits

Mahomes was 19-of-33 with one sack and seven hits. Quick width to Worthy, Kelce, and Gray has to be the answer before another deep incompletion to Worthy becomes the series.

The kicking competition, in public

Denver's 40.5 total means one kick can swing the 1.5.

What to think about

Does Denver copy the clock game?

Arrowhead Pride's mailbag asks whether other teams follow Seattle. A 35:51 possession game with 41 rushes is copyable even if the opponent quarterback is not Darnold. Spagnuolo has to tackle the run on first down or the same third-down math returns.

Does one loss rewrite the scouting report?

ESPN FPI still hangs 55.7 percent on the Denver trip and DraftKings still lists Kansas City minus 1.5. That is a lean after one loss, not a scouting report. The scouting report is 213 yards and 3-of-11.

What is real about the tackle depth?

Suamataia's holding penalty wiped a fourth-quarter play. Arrowhead Addict reports Ian Rapoport raising doubts about Josh Simmons playing for the Chiefs again. Reid cannot treat tackle stability as settled heading into a division road game.

Looking ahead

How Kansas City attacks a one-point road number in Denver

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Denver is the assignment: a division road game on Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT, CBS, with Kansas City laying 1.5 on a 40.5 total and FPI at 55.7 percent. The Broncos get a rested look at a 3-20 tape. Kansas City has to steal the script back before Denver sits on early-down runs and third-and-long.

How they match up

Seattle won with volume rushing, third-down efficiency, and explosives to Smith-Njigba. Denver does not need that personnel to borrow the idea. Kansas City's leverage is still Mahomes, Kelce (four catches, 44 yards), Gray's 19-yard answers, Worthy on the perimeter, and Walker if the carries come before the down-and-distance gets ugly. Spagnuolo's leverage is fitting the run with the tacklers ESPN logged: Tranquill (12), Nick Bolton (11), Nohl Williams (10), Jack Cochrane, Khyiris Tonga, and George Karlaftis. Give Denver credit: home field, a one-score number, and a clear film study of how to keep Mahomes at 11 first downs.

Win first down so third down is not 3-of-11 again

Inside zone and a defined screen keep Walker, who had 18 touches, from becoming a 14-carry, 36-yard afterthought. If Denver plays the Seattle rush-four plan, the ball has to come out before the seventh hit.

Take the deep middle without living there

Worthy had three catches and three charted incompletions deep. Gray was the completion. Mesh and a boot give Kelce and Gray the easy answers; four verticals is a shot, not the identity.

Rally fits on early-down runs

Cover 2 takes away the shot Seattle hit for 28 yards to Smith-Njigba. A zone blitz is a call to change the picture on third down, not a claim that pressure created either interception.

Treat the kick as a possession weapon

A 40.5 total and a 1.5 spread mean Butker's 50-yard make and the two misses are game-plan facts. Do not ask for 58 if the drive can steal four.

  1. Open with under-center inside zone to Walker and a shotgun check to Kelce so Denver cannot sit in the Seattle third-and-long look.
  2. Call the screen to Walker on an early second-and-medium if the rush shows the same heat that produced seven hits.
  3. Use mesh versus man on third-and-4 to 6, with Worthy on the corner route and Gray as the sit.
  4. Save four verticals for a clean pocket look after a run fake has moved a safety, not as the answer to pressure.
  5. Play Cover 2 on early downs against a shot team that just watched Smith-Njigba win deep left.
  6. Call the zone blitz on a Denver third-and-medium, dropping a defensive lineman and rushing the second level, with Karlaftis and Tranquill as the fitters.
Last game

What 3-20 at Lumen actually showed

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

L
Seattle Seahawks Week 7 · Sun, Oct 25 · 7:20 PM CT
KC 3–20

At Lumen Field on Sun Oct 25, 7:20 PM CT, Seattle beat Kansas City 20-3. The Seahawks opened the scoring with a 25-yard field goal with 8:22 left in the second quarter, added a 28-yard touchdown with 1:44 left in the half, kicked a 35-yard field goal with 8:09 left in the third, and closed with a 21-yard touchdown with 3:47 left. Kansas City's points were a 50-yard field goal with 1:42 left in the third.

The box is the story. Kansas City had 11 first downs, 213 total yards, eight drives, one turnover, and the ball for 24:09. Seattle had 24 first downs, 410 yards, eight drives, one turnover, and 35:51. Third down was 3-of-11 for the Chiefs and 11-of-16 for Seattle. Arrowhead Pride's third-down piece is right to hang the offensive failure there: drives never became a rhythm.

Mahomes finished 19-of-33 for 165 yards with one interception and a 58.3 rating, and the team net passing line is 163. He was sacked once and absorbed seven quarterback hits. Kansas City recorded two quarterback hits and no sacks on Darnold, who went 20-of-28 with a 96.9 rating. Kenneth Walker III carried 14 times for 36 yards and finished with 18 touches. Travis Kelce tied Kenneth Walker III at four catches and tied Noah Gray at 44 receiving yards. The team rush total was 50 yards on 16 attempts. That is not a run game Denver has to fear until the early-down calls change.

Seattle's explosives were real. With 1:44 left in the second quarter, Darnold hit Jaxon Smith-Njigba on a deep left throw that became a 28-yard touchdown, and the replay reversal confirmed the score. Smith-Njigba finished with 11 touches. Emanuel Wilson carried the grind: 24 rushes and one catch, 25 touches, inside a 41-carry, 153-yard team rush. AJ Barner had seven touches, including a direct-snap rush up the middle on the snap where O. Oluwatimi reported eligible. George Holani added six touches. Steve Spagnuolo's front spent the game fitting a committee, not a single back.

The takeaway ledger is narrow and it belongs to the players on the play list. Mahomes threw short left for J. Royals and T. Okada intercepted, with N. Emmanwori in on the play. N. Williams intercepted Darnold short middle, intended for Rashid Shaheed, and returned it 65 yards, with G. Karlaftis credited on the play. Creed Humphrey's fumble was recovered by Mahomes. Derick Hall forced a fumble on the sack, and Mahomes recovered it. Harrison Butker missed from 58, short, and from 45, wide right, around the 50 he made. A late 4th-and-10 to Kelce for three yards ended in a turnover on downs. Drue Tranquill's 12 tackles and Mansoor Delane's trail work, which Arrowhead Pride flagged as the clean defensive note, were bright spots on a night the scoreboard did not reward.

What held up

  • N. Williams' interception of Darnold, returned 65 yards, with Karlaftis on the play.
  • Noah Gray's two 19-yard catches, one deep right and one deep middle, gave Mahomes a middle-of-the-field answer when the vertical shots to Xavier Worthy were incomplete.
  • Tranquill's 12 tackles and Delane's trail position, noted by Arrowhead Pride, were the defensive details that held up while Seattle still controlled the clock.

What broke

  • Third-and-long offense: 3-of-11, 11 first downs, eight drives, 213 yards.
  • The run fit against Wilson's 25 touches and a 41-carry Seattle rush plan. Kansas City had two quarterback hits and no sacks.
  • Kicking: misses from 58 and 45 around one make from 50, which is why the Tucker practice-squad report is now on the week.

Third down decided the yardage gap

Kansas City was 3-of-11. Seattle was 11-of-16. That is how eight drives apiece became 213 yards against 410 and 24:09 against 35:51. Denver will try to live in that down-and-distance window.

The run game never became a down-and-distance weapon

Walker had 14 carries for 36 yards and 18 touches. The team rushed 16 times for 50. Seattle rushed 41 times for 153. Early downs have to look different before third-and-long becomes the Denver plan.

The scoreboard was field goals until Seattle's two touchdowns

Seattle scored on a 25-yard field goal, a 28-yard touchdown, a 35-yard field goal, and a 21-yard touchdown. Kansas City's answer was the 50-yard field goal. Butker also missed from 58 and from 45.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The next game is Denver, not a replay of Lumen Field. Kansas City walks into Empower Field as a 1.5-point favorite on Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT, CBS, at 5-1, with ESPN FPI at 55.7 percent. Seattle already cashed the blueprint other staffs will study: 24 first downs, 41 rushes for 153 yards, 35:51 of possession, and a 20-3 final.

Arrowhead Pride's mailbag is the right question for this week. Will other teams follow Seattle's plan? Denver does not have to copy Sam Darnold's 20-of-28, 96.9-rated day to make Kansas City uncomfortable. It only has to keep Patrick Mahomes off schedule the way Seattle did: seven quarterback hits, one sack, 19-of-33, a 58.3 rating, and an interception by T. Okada. Eric Bieniemy and Andy Reid have six days to put answers on the call sheet before a division road kickoff.

The season is not the 6-11 finish from 2025, and it is not a closed case after one loss. Arrowhead Pride's power-rankings roundup has ESPN sliding the Chiefs from 2 to 4 after the first defeat. That is a media correction, not a roster collapse. The football correction is simpler: 50 rushing yards on 16 attempts and 163 net passing yards will not travel against a team that can sit on a lead. The prior afternoon against the Chargers — 150 rushing yards, 272 net passing yards, 22 first downs, 30:26 of possession in a 22-6 win — is the identity Reid still wants. It is not the tape Denver will defend.

Arrowhead Pride and Arrowhead Addict both report Justin Tucker signed to the practice squad as competition. Special teams is now part of the Denver plan, not a side note. So is the offensive line story around Kingsley Suamataia, whose holding penalty wiped a play in the fourth quarter, and the open question Ian Rapoport has raised about Josh Simmons. None of that changes the number. Kansas City is laying 1.5. It has to play like it.

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker downhill before the down gets long

Situation 1st-and-10 early, at Denver, before the defense can replay Seattle's third-and-long rush

Kansas City rushed for 50 yards on 16 attempts, and Walker had 14 of those carries for 36 yards. Denver will expect Mahomes to throw into seven-hit math. An under-center zone carry gives Walker a one-cut downhill look and gives Bieniemy a down that is not already third-and-7.

Coaching point Reid and Bieniemy: call it under center, zone steps, one cut. Walker presses the point of attack and bends off the first free hat. Do not wait for shotgun on every early down the way the Lumen tape lived in shotgun.

Offense

Screen the rush that produced seven hits

Situation 2nd-and-6 to 8 after a modest gain, if Denver shows four heat

Mahomes took seven quarterback hits and one sack. Walker already has the hands for a four-catch game. A screen lets the line get out in space and turns pressure into a catch-and-run instead of another incompletion deep to Worthy.

Coaching point Bieniemy: sell the five-step, then let the guards and the back work the same side. Walker catches on the move. Kelce stays in to help if the end crashes. This is an answer to hits, not a shot play.

Mesh for Kelce on third-and-medium

Situation 3rd-and-4 to 6, the down Seattle won 11 times in 16 tries

Kansas City was 3-of-11 on third down and Kelce tied Gray at 44 receiving yards, with Walker also catching four. A rub gives him a window against man, with Worthy on the corner and Gray sitting if the linebacker chases the crosser.

Coaching point Kelce and Gray cross at depth with timing, not a scramble drill. Worthy bends the corner. Walker is the checkdown if both crossers are squeezed. Mahomes throws the first open window, the way the three-yard Kelce ball on the late drive needed more than three yards of separation.

Boot after the run has been honored

Situation 2nd-and-5 on the opening drive, once Walker has a carry on the sheet

This is a call for Denver, not a label on the Seattle throws. The Lumen tape did not give Denver a boot to study. Selling Walker, then moving Mahomes to the perimeter, is how Reid changes the picture after a pocket that absorbed seven hits.

Coaching point Under center, sell the zone, boot Mahomes away from the rush. Kelce leaks to the flat. Gray runs the over. Worthy stretches the corner. Mahomes takes the flat if the second level sits.

Defense

Cover 2 against the shot Seattle just hit

Situation 1st-and-10 after a Denver completion, protecting the deep left that became a 28-yard touchdown

Smith-Njigba's 28-yard touchdown and his 11 touches are the film Denver's offense will enjoy. Two deep safeties take the go-ball off the table and let Tranquill, who had 12 tackles, rally with Bolton and Cochrane.

Coaching point Spagnuolo: two deep, five under. Corners jam and sink. Safeties stay on top of the vertical. Linebackers fit the run so a 41-carry plan cannot be repeated. This shell is about rally tackles, not a gambling single-high look.

Defense

Zone blitz on third-and-medium

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 7, Denver in shotgun, Kansas City needing a stop that does not give up the shot

Seattle converted 11 of 16 third downs while Kansas City recorded two quarterback hits and no sacks. The zone blitz is a call to change the rush picture in Denver. It is not a claim that a blitz created Williams' interception or Okada's.

Coaching point Show heat, drop a defensive lineman into the hook, and rush a second-level player. Karlaftis sets an edge. Tranquill triggers only if the call sends him. The dropper has to carry the crosser so a checkdown does not become a first down the way Seattle's short game did.

21 first downs, three kicks, and a Monday night that resets the 6-11 tape

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

T
Seattle Seahawks Pre Week 4 · Sat Aug 29
KC 9-9

Kansas City closed the preseason at Arrowhead in a 9-9 draw that looked like a win everywhere except the scoreboard. The Chiefs out-gained Seattle 353-266, ran it 155-54, converted 9-of-17 third downs, and held the ball for 33:12, then walked off with three Harrison Butker field goals and one turnover. Seattle’s only touchdown was a 4-yard Jalen Milroe toss to Nick Vannett in the third; the PAT failed, which is the only reason this game stayed tied.

The tape is a Bieniemy tape. First downs (21 to 13) and rushing yards (155 to 54) are not accidents; they are under-center zone, extra hats in the box, and a backfield that kept the chains moving. Brashard Smith’s 16 carries for 72 yards were the featured look, not a changeup, and the offense never had to abandon it. When you sit on the ball for 33 minutes against a preseason defense, you are telling the building what next month is supposed to look like.

The finishing is the indictment. Butker from 28, 24, and 19 means Kansas City repeatedly got into scoring range and could not punch it. That is not a special-teams story. That is a red-zone sequencing story: too many early downs that did not threaten the goal line, too little play-action leverage once Seattle sat on the run, and a passing game that topped out at Garrett Nussmeier’s 22-of-34 for 198 with an interception. Andrew Armstrong’s 5-for-54 was the only receiver who consistently won, which is not a Week 1 answer against Payton.

Defensively, Steve Spagnuolo’s group did what a preseason close is supposed to do. Seattle managed 13 first downs and 4-of-11 on third down, the run game was dead at 54 yards, and R. Mason Thomas came off the edge for a sack. The Milroe-to-Vannett score is the clip that will bother the room — a short-yardage leak in the red area, not a structural collapse. Give Seattle credit for the 46-yard Jason Myers opener and for stealing a touchdown when Kansas City could not. The PAT miss is luck. The 9-9 is earned on both sides of the finishing problem.

Context matters: this was the fourth preseason game, Nussmeier was the quarterback of record, and you do not overfit a finale to Mahomes. What you can take is the identity. If Bieniemy is going to be the coordinator again, this is the contract — run it, convert third down, own the clock — and the fine print is that none of it counts until somebody scores a touchdown.

What held up

  • Under-center run game: 155 rushing yards, Smith as the featured back
  • Third-down offense: 9-of-17 kept the chains and the clock
  • Time of possession: 33:12 to 26:48, 21 first downs to 13
  • Spagnuolo’s run fit: Seattle stuck on 54 rushing yards and 4-of-11 third downs
  • R. Mason Thomas as an edge answer (1 sack)

What broke

  • Red-zone finishing: three trips, three field goals, no touchdowns
  • Explosive passing: Nussmeier’s 198 yards came without a score and with an interception
  • Receiver depth behind Armstrong’s 5-for-54
  • The one short-yardage leak that became Milroe-to-Vannett
  • Turning a 353-yard night into more than 9 points

The run game is a real install, not a camp slogan

155 rushing yards and 21 first downs against Seattle are the cleanest evidence yet that Bieniemy’s downhill identity survived the spring. Smith’s 16-for-72 was volume, not a cameo, and the 33:12 possession time is what Reid will want to live in against Denver.

Red-zone offense is still a field-goal factory

Three Butker makes from inside 30 and zero touchdowns is the whole problem. Payton’s Broncos will not miss the extra point and will not let Kansas City keep settling. If the same sequencing shows up Monday, a 9-9 tape becomes a loss.

The defense can travel if the offense stops leaking points

Seattle’s 266 yards, 54 on the ground, and 4-of-11 on third down is a Spagnuolo close. Thomas’s sack is a nice clip. The assignment now is to do it against a Payton script, not a preseason backup drive.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

6-11

Kansas City is a 6-11 team with a new-old offensive coordinator, a quarterback coming off leg surgery, a tackle room that still makes people nervous, and a preseason close that proved the clock can be won without the game being won. Week 1 is not a referendum on the next decade of Arrowhead. It is a test of whether Bieniemy’s identity actually scores.

What we need to work on

Red-zone sequencing, not just red-zone volume

The Seattle tape got them to the 10-yard line and then went small. Against Payton, early-down play-action, tight end leverage, and a designed quarterback movement have to replace the three-kicks plan or the -3 number is a trap.

Tackle protection that does not need Mahomes to scramble for his life

Josh Simmons is still a cloud, and the Veach tackle bet is the offseason argument that will not die. Screens, boots, and a committed run game are the schematic answers if the edges are still a work in progress Monday.

A defined wideout hierarchy that is not a free-agent rumor

Darius Slayton is not the answer to the receiver problem, and Armstrong’s 54-yard preseason night is a clip, not a WR1. Kelce and the run game have to carry the open until somebody else wins.

Spagnuolo’s first-15 answers to a Payton script

Two waiver additions on defense are depth, not a scheme change. The staff has to decide which simulated pressures they will actually call on early downs versus a coordinator who lives to punish the first look.

What to think about

How limited is Mahomes, really?

Sean Payton is on record that he does not expect Mahomes to be limited after the offseason leg surgery. That is an opponent’s scouting report, not a medical. Reid’s call sheet should still protect the first 20 snaps with under-center runs and movement throws.

Is the offensive line actually 23rd, or is that tape from last year?

ESPN’s ranking of the line as 23rd is the national view of a 6-11 season. The Seattle run tape and the local pushback say the interior can move people. Monday night is the first snap that settles the argument.

Which side of the ball is allowed to rank higher in 2026?

The building is already debating whether the Bieniemy offense or the Spagnuolo defense will be the better unit. The honest answer after 9-9 is that the defense is closer to finished and the offense is the one with the scoring debt.

How much of Arrowhead’s edge is still real?

The countdown to the last games in this building is on, with roughly 40 remaining before the Kansas move. That is atmosphere, not a coverage. It still matters on ESPN in Week 1, and it will not cover a red-zone drought.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Monday night at Arrowhead is an AFC West opener on ESPN, a 7:15 window, and the first real snap of the Bieniemy-Mahomes pairing against Sean Payton. Kansas City is a short home favorite. The assignment is to make the Seattle clock look like a plan instead of a preseason curiosity.

How they match up

This is a coordinator game before it is a star game. Payton will script the first 15 and hunt the first coverage he does not like. Reid and Bieniemy will try to take the air out of that script with under-center zone, play-action boots, and Kelce on early downs. Both quarterbacks are coming off leg surgery; Payton does not expect Mahomes to be limited, which means Denver will not play him as a statue. Kansas City’s leverage is the run tape (155 yards, 33 minutes) and home field. Denver’s leverage is Kansas City’s red-zone finishing and a tackle room that is still a live question. Do not give Payton cheap early-down explosives and do not let this become a track meet at 43.5.

Establish inside zone on first down so the boot is a threat, not a tell

If Denver can sit on play-action because the run is a suggestion, Mahomes is throwing from a well. Smith and the under-center looks have to win the first two series the way they won the Seattle clock.

Make Kelce the red-zone answer, not Butker

Three field goals against Seattle is the clip Payton will show. Mesh, glance, and tight end leverage inside the 15 have to replace the kick. If Kelce is covered, the back has to be a checkdown that still scores.

Do not let Payton’s first script get the crowd out of it

Spagnuolo should open in a two-deep shell, then mix the simulated pressure on second-and-medium. The waiver additions are extra bodies. The plan is still disguise, not blitzing the building empty.

Protect the edges without asking Mahomes to be 2018

Simmons uncertainty and the tackle bet mean the call sheet needs screens, sprints, and a running back who can pick up the first rusher. If the pocket collapses, the 3-point number evaporates.

  1. Open in 12 personnel, under-center inside zone to the closed side — test Denver’s first-down fit and get Smith the same volume look he had against Seattle.
  2. Second series: play-action boot off the zone look, Mahomes on the move, Kelce as the first read in the flat-to-intermediate window.
  3. Third-and-5 or shorter: mesh with Kelce as the primary rub, Armstrong or the Z as the corner/sit, back as the outlet. Do not live on isolations.
  4. If Denver pins its ears back, spacing screen to the running back — let the line get to the second level instead of asking the tackles to hold a four-man rush for 3.2 seconds.
  5. Defensively, first series in a Cover-2 shell. Take away the deep shot Payton wants to script and rally to the checkdown.
  6. On the first second-and-medium, zone-blitz: show five, drop a lineman, bring a linebacker. Make Payton’s protection slide the wrong way before he can get to the shot play.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The 2025 hangover is still the number on the wall — 6-11 — but the last tape Kansas City put on the field was a possession identity that refused to pay off. Against Seattle they stacked 21 first downs, 353 yards, and 33:12 of clock, then settled for Harrison Butker from 28, 24, and 19. That box score is the entire offseason argument in one night: Eric Bieniemy’s downhill stuff is back on the grass, and the finishing is not. Monday night against Denver is the first snap that will actually be remembered.

Andy Reid did not spend the spring reuniting with Bieniemy so the offense could live on field goals. Travis Kelce has already made the point that the coordinator reunion matters to how this room works, and the Seattle finale was the first clean look at the identity: under-center runs, third-down conversion (9-of-17), and a clock that never came back to the visitors. The problem is the last 20 yards. Three red-zone trips, three Butker makes, zero touchdowns. Sean Payton will not leave the extra point on the board the way Seattle did.

The market is treating Arrowhead like Arrowhead. ESPN FPI has Kansas City at 58.5 percent, DraftKings has them -3 with a 43.5 total, and the building still does the rest on a national ESPN window. That is not a Super Bowl price — Polymarket has the Chiefs at 6.2 percent to win the 2027 title and 10.5 percent to take the AFC — and it should not be read as one. National power rankings have them in the middle of the pack, NFL.com at 14, which is exactly where a 6-11 tape plus a run-heavy preseason close should live.

What has to change by kickoff is not the clock. It is the paint. Brett Veach’s tackle room is still a live argument, Josh Simmons is still a cloud, and the wideout market is not about to be solved by a name that does not fit. Spagnuolo’s defense, freshly topped up with two waiver claims, has to be the adult in the room while Mahomes ramps off leg surgery Payton himself does not expect to limit him. Win the trenches, finish the possessions, and Week 1 is a statement. Kick another three field goals, and the 6-11 hangover still owns the night.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-center inside zone, closed side

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script vs. Denver — 12 personnel, Arrowhead, ESPN

The Seattle finale was 155 rushing yards, 21 first downs, and 33:12 of clock with Brashard Smith on 16 carries. That is the identity Bieniemy brought back, and it is the only way to make Payton defend the boot later. Kansas City cannot open in empty and ask a cloudy tackle room to pass-protect Mahomes off leg surgery.

Coaching point Reid and Bieniemy: zone steps, one cut, do not bounce it into Simmons’ edge if that side is the question. Smith has to press the line of scrimmage and trust the double. If the first two plays gain 4 and 5, the entire Payton script slows down.

Offense

Play-action boot off the zone look

Situation 2nd-and-6 after a real run, early series vs. Payton’s contain

Payton has already said he does not expect Mahomes to be limited, which means Denver will respect the keep. Selling the same under-center zone that just gained yards puts Mahomes on the perimeter with easy reads and keeps the tackles from having to hold a four-man rush. Kelce in the flat is the answer to the red-zone debt from the 9-9 tape.

Coaching point Mahomes: sell the handoff, do not peek the rush, throw on time to Kelce or the crossing intermediate. Bieniemy has to call this off a gain, not a 2nd-and-11, or Payton’s backside end will sit on it.

Offense

Mesh vs. man in the scoring area

Situation 3rd-and-5 or red-zone 2nd-and-7 — the down Kansas City kicked from against Seattle

Three Butker field goals from 28, 24, and 19 are the clip Denver will pin up. Kansas City does not have a solved wideout room — Slayton is the wrong answer, Armstrong’s 5-for-54 is a preseason clip — so the rub game with Kelce as the primary is the grown-up way to score. Man coverage is where mesh pays; if Denver zones it, the back is the outlet.

Coaching point Kelce sets the rub, does not hunt contact for its own sake. The Z has to finish the corner/sit so Mahomes has a high-low. Nussmeier’s finale interception is the warning: do not force the first crosser if the backer sits. Check it down and live.

Offense

RB spacing screen, easy offense

Situation 2nd-and-long or obvious passing down if Denver pins its ears back

Veach’s tackle gamble and the Simmons cloud are the protection problem of the summer. If Kansas City has to drop back and pick up Payton’s timed rush, the -3 number is a lie. The Seattle night already showed a backfield that can win in space; get the line into the second level and take the pressure off Mahomes’ legs.

Coaching point Smith (or the listed back): hitch, show the pass, do not leave early. Guards have to get to the second level, not dance with the first rusher. Reid should have this in the first 12 calls so Denver cannot tee off on the edges.

Defense

Cover-2 shell, first series

Situation Denver’s opening script, 1st-and-10, take away the shot Payton wants on ESPN

Payton will script a play-action shot if he smells a single-high look. Spagnuolo’s two-deep, five-under opener matches the Seattle close — 266 yards allowed, 54 on the ground, 4-of-11 on third down — and keeps the crowd in it. The two waiver additions are extra bodies to rally and tackle, not a reason to get cute on snap one.

Coaching point Spagnuolo to the safeties: split the post, no one gets over the top on the first series. Corners sink, linebackers rally to the checkdown. R. Mason Thomas and the edge set the run first; do not let a Payton boot become a 20-yard gain because somebody chased the quarterback.

Defense

Simulated pressure, second-and-medium

Situation 2nd-and-7 vs. Payton after the first-series shell — steal a negative down

Payton lives to punish the first look he has already seen. After a Cover-2 opener, Spagnuolo’s signature is the fake heat: show five, drop a lineman, bring a second-level rusher. That is how you make a post-surgery quarterback throw hot without emptying the building and giving up the shot that wrecked the 6-11 tape last year.

Coaching point Show the five-man picture, drop the end into the hook, bring the backer off the slot. Thomas can be the pictured rusher who actually peels. Secondary must play the throw on time — this call dies if the hole shot is late getting filled.

Coordinator

Bieniemy is back. The red zone has to prove it.

QB

Payton does not expect Mahomes to be limited. Plan like he might be anyway.

Both quarterbacks are coming off offseason leg surgery. Payton’s public read is that Mahomes will not be limited. Reid should still script movement throws and under-center runs until the first drive proves the legs are live.

O-line

WR

Slayton is not the fix

The receiver need is real and the free-agent board had a name. The marriage with Darius Slayton does not fit what this offense wants. Until somebody else wins, Kelce, the backs, and play-action have to be the passing game.

Front office

Two waiver bodies and a still-open tackle argument

Kansas City claimed two defensive players on waivers and quietly restored some of the roster margin it lost this offseason. That is depth for Spagnuolo. It does not answer the edges on offense.

Building

The clock on Arrowhead is already running

There are roughly 40 games left in this stadium before the Kansas move. Monday night is one of them. Atmosphere is not a coverage, but it is still a real edge on ESPN if the offense does not waste it.

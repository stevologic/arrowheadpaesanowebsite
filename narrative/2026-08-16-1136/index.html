The story so far

Always looking ahead

A 6-11 hangover does not vanish because training camp in St. Joseph sounded optimistic. The Chiefs opened the 2026 preseason with a 20-12 loss to the Rams at Arrowhead, Justin Fields under center, Patrick Mahomes warming up on the sideline, and an Andy Reid first-script that looked painfully familiar to anyone who watched last year. They had spent August talking about revitalizing the run game and lining up under center. Then they played a game that did not look like that conversation. Preseason Week 3 in Tampa is the only night left that functions as a dress rehearsal: starter snaps, bubble answers, and a schematic tell before the Seattle finale becomes a roster-cut tape and Denver arrives on ESPN.

The encouraging film is young and cheap, which is both the point of August and a warning about September. Rookie running back Emmett Johnson led the night with 59 yards on 12 carries. Seventh-round quarterback Garrett Nussmeier went 13-of-19 for 98. Reid singled out rookie Cyrus Allen for knowing how to set routes up. Kahlil Benson and a rebuilt secondary were the other green flags coming out of a sloppy debut. That is a development camp, not a contender’s camp, and the market is not confused about it: Kansas City sits at 5.9 percent to win the 2027 championship and 9.5 percent to come out of the AFC. Those are bounce-back numbers, not dynasty numbers.

The problems are the ones that travel. Chris Jones has already said he does not want to play in the preseason, so Steve Spagnuolo will have to manufacture a pass rush without his best interior wrecker. Felix Anudike-Uzomah is still trying to make the first-round capital look like something other than a sunk cost. The tight end room was thin enough that the local press spent the week scouting the Rams’ TEs as possible help. Harrison Butker is no longer an automatic answer on the ranking sheets. And the play-calling question is schematic, not cosmetic: if Reid and Eric Bieniemy do not actually live under center and run inside zone in Tampa, the revitalized run game is a camp slogan when the Broncos show up.

Tampa in late August is heat, humidity, and a franchise that put Cover-2 on the league map. Most staffs play the ones for a quarter in Week 3. The Chiefs may not. Mahomes’ knee sets the snap count, not the calendar — he was on the field throwing before the Rams game and still did not take a snap, and Reid’s only injury note after that night was that the roster came out clean with one exception. What you should watch is not the scoreboard. Watch whether Fields or Mahomes operate from under center. Watch whether Johnson and the line win early downs. Watch whether Spags’ young DBs can play a two-deep shell without exploding. Watch which bubble players survive the cut to 53 before Seattle. Everything after that is about September 15.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-Center Inside Zone — The Identity Snap

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script in Tampa, Fields group (or Mahomes if the knee is cleared for a series)

Reid’s Rams script abandoned the under-center/run emphasis they preached in St. Joseph, and Arrowhead Addict called the play-calling painfully familiar. Emmett Johnson already averaged nearly five yards a carry. This is the last tape Denver will take seriously. If Benson and the interior do not win the zone double in the humidity, the identity talk is dead.

Coaching point Zone steps, one cut, do not bounce. Johnson has to press the line of scrimmage and trust the combo block instead of hunting the edge. Bieniemy needs this as the first call on the card, not the fifth, or the Broncos will not respect the fake.

Offense

Play-Action Boot off the Zone Look

Situation 2nd-and-medium after a live zone run, Fields or a pitch-counted Mahomes series

The boot only exists if they actually run under center first — the thing they failed to do against the Rams. Fields’ legs make the keep real; if Mahomes takes a series, moving the pocket is the responsible way to ease a December knee back into live rush lanes against a front that will climb to the run fake.

Coaching point Sell the zone mesh, lose the mesh point, get the quarterback on the perimeter. TE leaks to the flat, X runs the over. Do not hold the ball in the well. Reid will be grading whether the fake is honored, not whether the throw is pretty.

Offense

Mesh — Allen Sets the Rub

Situation 2nd-and-7 vs Bucs nickel, man or pattern-match, rookie WRs on the field

Reid’s specific praise after the Rams game was that Cyrus Allen knows how to set routes up. Mesh is a route-setting concept, not a highlight concept. A Tampa-2 heritage defense will still play man in preseason nickel, which is exactly the look Allen has to prove he can break.

Coaching point Allen sets a legal, physical rub without picking. Crossers have to be on time or the rub is a penalty. Running back is the outlet if Tampa switches. Reid and Bieniemy are watching the stem, not the catch.

Offense

Johnson Space Screen vs the Heat

Situation 3rd-and-short or 3rd-and-medium when Tampa shows pressure, backup-led KC offense

The Rams debut was sloppy and the quarterback room is Fields and Nussmeier with Mahomes still ramping. Easy offense is the responsible call. Johnson already proved he can create in space, and a late screen is the one thing a knee-conscious staff cannot have a quarterback holding.

Coaching point The line has to get to the second level, not just release into air. Johnson’s first three steps set the wall. Throw it on time. Nussmeier’s 13-of-19 night was efficient because he did not hunt heroes — this is that same rule, live.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 Shell — Evaluate the New DBs

Situation Early downs vs the Bucs, base and nickel, rebuilt secondary on the field

The rebuilt secondary was flagged as a green flag in a sloppy Rams debut, and Tampa is the spiritual home of two-deep football. This is the cleanest teaching tape Spagnuolo will get before Denver. Take away the shot, rally, tackle — the exact things that looked uneven in Week 1.

Coaching point Corners funnel, safeties stay deep as the next town over, five underneath fit the run. This is a tackling drill disguised as a coverage call. Spags is grading explosion points and missed fits, not interceptions.

Defense

Simulated Pressure Without Chris Jones

Situation 2nd-and-long or 3rd-and-medium in Tampa, Jones on the sideline by preference

Jones said he does not want to play in the preseason, which strips the interior wrecker off the evaluation tape. Felix Anudike-Uzomah still has to prove the first-round capital is not a sunk cost. Spagnuolo’s signature — show heat, drop a lineman, bring a second-level rusher — is how you grade this front without your best player.

Coaching point The dropped lineman has to land in the hook or the simulation is just a busted coverage. FAU’s job on the actual rush is win with effort and contain, not hunt sacks that wreck the fit. Spags is grading discipline as much as pressure, and Tampa will throw hot if the disguise is late.

The storylines

What we’re watching

QB timeline

Mahomes is throwing. He is not playing. That is the whole story.

Patrick Mahomes was on the field throwing before the Rams game and still did not take a snap, the latest data point in a ramp-up from the serious December knee injury. Justin Fields started. Until the knee dictates otherwise, Tampa is a Fields-and-kids tape with a Hall of Fame shadow on the sideline.

Identity

The under-center run game has to show up or stop being advertised.

Reid and Bieniemy spent camp talking about lining up under center and revitalizing the run. The Rams script was more of the same old look. Preseason Week 3 is the last honest chance to put that identity on tape before Denver’s staff starts clipping it for real.

Rookie tape

Emmett Johnson ran like he wants the job.

Twelve carries, 59 yards, and the only consistent juice on a 20-12 night. If the Chiefs are serious about early-down identity, Johnson just forced the staff to keep feeding him in Tampa — not as a camp body, as a plan.

Route skill

Reid’s Allen comment was not a courtesy.

Andy Reid does not waste postgame air on empty compliments. Saying Cyrus Allen knows how to set routes up is a specific skill endorsement — rubs, stems, the stuff that shows up in mesh and man-beater packages. Tampa is where that camp praise has to survive a live nickel.

OL green flag

Kahlil Benson was one of the few winners in a sloppy debut.

The local tape graders came away encouraged by Benson and by a rebuilt secondary. Those are the two rooms that have to look functional in Tampa if this roster is going to pretend the 6-11 season was an interruption instead of a new baseline.

Pass rush

Spags is working without his best player on purpose.

Chris Jones does not want to play in the preseason. That is his right and it is a problem for evaluation. The simulated pressures, the FAU snaps, and the second-level rushers are the entire pass-rush grade on Saturday.

Bubble

The 53 is being built in Tampa, not Seattle.

Week 4 against the Seahawks is a cutdown tape. Week 3 is the last night bubble players get starter-adjacent looks. Tight end, edge, and the back end of the secondary are the rooms that cannot afford a quiet night.

Market check

5.9 percent is not a narrative. It is a price.

Polymarket has the Chiefs at 5.9 percent to win the 2027 title and 9.5 percent to win the AFC. After 6-11 and a quarterback coming off a serious knee, that is honest. The Mahomes-is-back chorus will get louder than the number if he looks himself. Until he takes a snap, the number is the adult in the room.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid’s script vs Reid’s rhetoric

The Rams opener was advertised as the start of an under-center, run-first reset and then played like last year’s offense. Week 3 is when Andy Reid either puts that identity on tape or admits it is still a camp talking point.

Bieniemy’s early-down mandate

Eric Bieniemy’s job in Tampa is not creativity. It is to live in inside zone and play-action off it long enough that Denver has to prepare for it. If the second series is empty gun again, the coordinator-head coach conversation is the story.

Spagnuolo without Jones

Steve Spagnuolo has built a career on simulated pressure. He will need every bit of it Saturday. Jones sitting is a preference, not an injury, which means the staff already knows the Week 1 defensive identity has to travel without its best player.

Snap-count philosophy vs the calendar

League-wide, preseason Week 3 is starter night. The Chiefs’ calendar is different: Mahomes’ December knee, a long layoff into a September 15 opener, and a Week 4 Seattle game that will be a cutdown tape. The smart staff plays the ones just enough to get clean pictures, then gets out.

Roster moves

Personnel

  • Justin Fields as the preseason starter

    Fields started the Rams game with Mahomes held out. Until the knee is cleared for live snaps, Fields is the first-team quarterback and the entire easy-offense plan has to be built around his legs and his limitations in the well.

  • Garrett Nussmeier’s third-QB push

    The seventh-rounder went 13-of-19 for 98 against the Rams. That is not a starting tape, but it is a clean enough preseason tape to keep the QB3 job from becoming a camp afterthought heading into cutdowns.

  • Emmett Johnson seizing early-down work

    Fifty-nine yards on 12 carries is how a rookie running back forces the staff to keep him in the game plan. Tampa should be a referendum on whether Johnson is a camp story or the lead back in an identity offense.

  • Cyrus Allen’s route-setting audition

    Reid’s postgame praise was specific. Allen now has to show the same stems and rubs against a live nickel in Tampa or the compliment stays in the press-conference transcript.

  • Felix Anudike-Uzomah, last honest look

    The first-round edge has not lived up to the capital, and the local press is already arguing the staff cannot give up on him yet. A Jones-less pass rush in Tampa is the look. Quiet production here makes the 53 conversation brutal.

  • Tight end room still a problem

    The Chiefs spent the week of the Rams game being told they might need to raid someone else’s tight end room. Week 3 is the last night an in-house TE can steal a roster spot on tape before Seattle becomes paperwork.

  • Rebuilt secondary vs the cutdown

    The new back end was one of the few encouraging rooms against the Rams. Spags will play them in two-deep in Tampa to decide who is a starter, who is a nickel, and who is a practice-squad call in two weeks.

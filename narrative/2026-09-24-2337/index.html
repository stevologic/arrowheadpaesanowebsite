Overtime paid the bill. Miami collects the first road invoice.

The edge

A 10.5-point road favorite on a 45.5 total (DraftKings) with an 85.3 percent ESPN FPI nod still sits at 7.6 percent to win the Super Bowl on Polymarket — the market likes Sunday in Miami more than it likes the long-term ticket, and the 30-point Colts tape is why.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

W
Indianapolis Colts Week 2 · Sun, Sep 20 · 7:20 PM CT
KC 33–30

On NBC at Arrowhead, Kansas City outgained Indianapolis 523-329, ran 37:00, and did not turn it over, yet still needed overtime to close a 33-30 win. Daniel Jones hit Tyler Warren from a yard out to open the night, Jonathan Taylor ripped a 24-yard touchdown just before half, and the Colts kicked two second-quarter field goals; Mahomes answered with touchdowns to Kelce, Worthy, and Royals, Walker carried 24 times for 117, and Harrison Butker's 46-yarder kept the first quarter from getting away.

The offensive story is complementarity, not a box-score spike. After Denver ate 220 rush yards, Indianapolis had to honor the inside zone, and Bieniemy cashed the other side of that respect: Mahomes at 382 yards on 47 attempts, Kelce as the chain-mover (9-101-1), and two different wideouts finishing from the 1. That is how you post 29 first downs. It is also how you survive a 5-of-13 night on third down — volume and explosives covering a conversion rate that will not travel if it stays there.

The run was not a decoy. Walker's 24-117 kept the game in under-center and play-action language even while the pass stacked 371 net yards. When the Colts loaded for the downhill game they had just watched on the Denver tape, Mahomes had the answers in the intermediate middle and on the goal line. The 13-yard Kelce score and the two 1-yard throws are the red-zone correction from a preseason that kept settling for kicks.

The defensive story is the one Spagnuolo will put on the projector. Thirty points, 24 first downs, 119 rush yards, and a 24-yard Taylor touchdown are not a fluke of finishing; they are a unit that let Indianapolis stay on schedule for 33:00. Bolton's 13 tackles mean the second level was busy. Busy is not the same as tight. A 5-of-12 Colts third-down night kept it from becoming a track meet, but the explosives and the goal-line leak to Warren are the clips that do not belong on a 2-0 tape if this defense wants to look like itself in October.

Overtime does not make it a lucky win so much as a loud one. Kansas City never trailed the turnover margin, won the yardage by almost 200, and still had to finish extra football because the points allowed outran the points earned for too long. That is the difference between a complete offense and a complete team. Miami is the first place that difference shows up on the road.

What held up

  • Walker remaining a 24-carry feature after the Denver tape, not a change-of-pace afterthought
  • Mahomes-to-Kelce as the intermediate answer once Indianapolis loaded the box
  • Red-zone throws to Worthy and Royals from the 1 instead of another field-goal night
  • Zero turnovers and 37:00 of possession as the floor, even in overtime
  • Harrison Butker's 46-yarder answering the opening Warren touchdown so the game never tilted early

What broke

  • Third-down offense at 5-of-13 despite 523 yards
  • The defense allowing 30 points, 24 first downs, and a 24-yard Taylor touchdown
  • Too many tackles for Bolton, a sign the second level was reacting instead of dictating
  • Letting Indianapolis hold the ball for 33:00 in a game Kansas City otherwise dominated on paper

The pass finally paid the run's tab

Walker still got 24 carries and 117 yards, but Mahomes' 32-of-47, 382, and three scores are the new problem for coordinators. Kelce at 9-101-1 is the coverage magnet that makes Worthy's and Royals' 1-yard finishes possible.

Third down is the hidden leak on a 523-yard night

Five of 13 is not how a 29-first-down offense should live. Explosives and red-zone throws covered it against Indianapolis. A noon road kick will not, not if Miami can force the Chiefs into obvious passing downs.

Spagnuolo already graded the defense honestly

Thirty points, 329 yards, and 24 first downs are not a footnote to 2-0. Bolton leading the team in tackles is the second-level alarm. The self-scout is the actual Week 3 install.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

2-0

The Chiefs are 2-0 with a complete offensive menu and an incomplete defensive standard. They can lean on Walker or let Mahomes throw for 380. They cannot take a winless Miami team lightly on the first road Sunday of the year, not after Spagnuolo's own unit admitted Week 2 was too loud, and not with Chamarri Conner still easing back into the secondary.

What we need to work on

Third-down offense

Five of 13 against Indianapolis is the first number Bieniemy has to fix before a noon kick. Mesh, boots, and easy throws have to replace hoping the next explosive covers a stalled series.

Points allowed, not just yards allowed

Indianapolis left with 30 and 24 first downs. Miami does not have to be good to make that a problem if Kansas City's defense again plays on its heels for 33 minutes.

Road situational football

This is the first true road environment of 2026. Early-down runs, clock, and a clean pocket for Mahomes matter more at noon in South Florida than they did under the lights at Arrowhead.

Secondary communication with Conner ramping up

Conner is easing back at the right time, but a returning safety cannot be asked to erase a 30-point tape by himself. Nohl Williams and the corners need a simpler Sunday than isolated man all afternoon.

What to think about

Is Miami actually a trap?

The 2-0 club is a double-digit favorite against a winless team widely treated as the league's floor. That is exactly how noon road games get sloppy if the Colts film session stays in the building.

How much run is enough now that the pass cashed

Mahomes can see defenses respecting Walker. The temptation is to throw it 47 times again. The smarter 3-0 path is still 20-plus Walker carries that buy the boots and play-action.

What 3-0 is worth in September

Kansas City is one of eight 2-0 teams and one of two unbeaten in the AFC West with Las Vegas. A 3-0 start is a mark, not a coronation — Polymarket still has the Chiefs at 7.6 percent to win it all.

Noise versus next snap

Tyreek Hill reunion talk will follow this roster until it doesn't. Sunday is about Conner's snap count, Felix Anudike-Uzomah's quad, and whether Spagnuolo's calls look like the self-scout.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Miami Dolphins

First road trip of 2026, noon CBS, a winless Miami team Kansas City has beaten in five straight meetings — and a Chiefs defense that just allowed 30. The assignment is to play the Dolphins like the Colts tape, not like the spread.

How they match up

Kansas City brings a two-headed offense (Walker downhill, Mahomes and Kelce when the box loads) into a building that will try to make this a speed-and-explosives problem. Miami's path is chaos and cheap points; Kansas City's path is 37-minute football, early-down runs, and a Spagnuolo plan that takes away the deep shot instead of chasing 24 first downs. Conner easing back and FAU popping up with a quad tilt the Chiefs toward simulated pressure and two-deep structure rather than a pin-the-ears-back road game.

Run it until Miami's safeties step up, then boot

Walker at 24-117 is the lever. Inside zone on early downs, then under-center play-action boots for Mahomes and Kelce once the box is heavy. That is how you keep a noon road game from turning into a track meet.

Third down cannot be a 5-of-13 sequel

Script mesh and easy throws for Kelce against man, and do not let 47 dropbacks become the plan. If Kansas City stays on schedule, Miami's best hope (chaos, explosives, a short field) never arrives.

Spagnuolo's self-scout is the real install

Two-deep shells to take away the shot, rally with Bolton, and mix zone blitz so a banged-up edge does not have to win every snap. Thirty points against Indianapolis is the look you take away, not the look you repeat.

Get Conner on the field without asking him to be the entire answer

His return is timed for the perimeter stress of a road secondary. Pair him with Nohl Williams in a simpler coverage menu. Do not isolate a ramping safety and then wonder why the Colts tape came with you on the plane.

  1. Open 11/12 personnel under center: Walker inside zone, two-high look be damned, and make Miami's linebackers play downhill on snap one.
  2. Second series: same run fake, Mahomes boot, Kelce leaking — cash the respect the Colts already showed after Denver's 220.
  3. Keep Rice and Worthy on vertical stems off play-action so the first 20-plus of the day is scheduled, not hoped for.
  4. Third-and-medium: mesh with Kelce as the rub/sit and Walker as the outlet; do not live on 47 dropbacks again.
  5. Defensively, start in a Cover-2 shell and make Miami drive the field; Bolton and the second level have to tackle in space instead of posting another 13-tackle chase.
  6. On third-and-long, simulated pressure — show heat, drop an end, green-dog Bolton — rather than pinning FAU's ears back through a quad issue.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Kansas City is 2-0 because Eric Bieniemy's offense finally has two answers, not one. Week 1 was Walker and the clock against Denver. Week 2 was Mahomes, Travis Kelce, and 523 yards in a 33-30 overtime win over Indianapolis at Arrowhead. That is a complete enough identity to travel. It is not clean enough to treat a winless Miami team like a scheduled bye on the first road Sunday of 2026.

The Colts tape is the first real self-scout of the Andy Reid-Bieniemy-Spagnuolo year. Kansas City owned the math — 29 first downs, 37:00 of possession, zero turnovers, 152 on the ground after 220 in Week 1 — and still needed overtime because Indianapolis posted 24 first downs, 329 yards, and 30 points. Nick Bolton leading the club with 13 tackles is production; it is also a tell that the defense spent too much of Sunday night in a chase. Spagnuolo has already said that part out loud.

What changed from the opener is the pass. Mahomes went 32-of-47 for 382 yards and three scores, Kelce put up 9-101-1, and the red-zone finishes were Xavier Worthy and Jalen Royals from the 1 after a 13-yard Kelce strike. Rashee Rice's explosive was not a random dart; it was the payoff of a look Indianapolis had already seen in Week 1. Defenses are loading for Walker, and Mahomes can see it. That is the leverage for Miami, not a reason to shelve the run.

Now the calendar gets honest. This is the first road trip, a noon CBS kick, and the front of a three-game AFC stretch that goes to Las Vegas in Week 4 and back home against the Chargers in Week 6. Kansas City is chasing 3-0 with the Raiders as the other unbeaten in the AFC West. The books have the Chiefs as a double-digit favorite. The prediction markets have not crowned anybody. The assignment is to play the Dolphins like a team that just allowed 30, not like a team that just found 523.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker downhill, first snap in Miami

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script, 12 personnel under center on the road vs. Miami

Walker just carried 24 times for 117 after 220 in Week 1, and Mahomes has already seen defenses step down to stop that tape. A noon road game against a winless Miami team is won by staying on schedule, not by throwing it 47 times again. Bieniemy's identity is the clock; 37:00 against Indianapolis is the standard you take on the plane.

Coaching point Zone steps, one cut, no bounce. Walker has to press the line of scrimmage so Miami's linebackers cannot cheat the boot. Tight end seals the play-side end; if the safety is walking down, that is information for the next call, not a reason to abort this one.

Offense

Cash the loaded box

Situation 2nd-and-6 after a Walker inside-zone look, same personnel, hash toward Kelce

Indianapolis had already gotten the message from the Denver run tape, and Mahomes could see it. Miami will too. Booting him off the fake turns Kelce into the easy read and keeps a 382-yard passer off a stationary spot in a road pocket. This is the complement that makes 20 Walker carries sustainable instead of stubborn.

Coaching point Sell the run at the mesh point. Blind-side tackle and the pulling path have to wall the edge so Mahomes can play full-speed to the perimeter. Kelce leaks; Rice holds the deep safety with a post. Do not peek the rush — play the first open window.

Offense

Punish the Cover-3 sit

Situation 2nd-and-7, 11 personnel, Miami two-high rotating to three-deep after eating two runs

Rice's first explosive against Indianapolis was set up on the Week 1 tape; Worthy is the vertical closer who already finished from the 1. A winless Miami team that tries to keep a lid on Mahomes will live in three-deep. Four verticals is how you make the hole in the middle the story instead of another 5-of-13 third-down night.

Coaching point Outside receivers run through the corners, no hitch. Kelce or the inside stem bends to the hash and sits in the seam if the Mike drops. Mahomes' eyes go deep middle first — that is the 20-plus, not the checkdown.

Offense

Fix 5-of-13 without another 47 dropbacks

Situation 3rd-and-5, 11 personnel, Miami showing man from the slot

Kelce at 9-101-1 is the man-beater, and the Colts night died on third down even with 523 yards. On the road you cannot wait for the next explosive. Mesh puts two crossers on a collision course, gives Worthy or Royals a rub, and leaves Walker as the outlet if Miami's rush follows the Colts' heat.

Coaching point Timing is the rub, not the win-your-1-on-1 speech. Kelce has to come tight off the mesh and sit if the linebacker trails. Mahomes hits the first separation; if both crossers are covered, the back is the throw. No hero ball on 3rd-and-5 in Miami.

Defense

Take away the shot, live with the drive

Situation 1st-and-10, Miami 11 personnel, Chiefs protecting a lead or protecting a noon road start

Thirty points and 24 first downs against Indianapolis is what happens when the defense chases. Conner is easing back, so this is not an island-man Sunday. Two deep, five under, Nohl Williams squeezing the boundary, Bolton rallying — that is how you make Miami drive the field instead of exploding it.

Coaching point Safeties split the posts, no late rotation into a one-high look unless the motion forces it. Williams and the corners have to funnel, not grab. Bolton's 13 tackles last week were a chase; this week they have to be downhill after the catch, not 15 yards past the sticks.

Defense

Heat without pinning FAU's ears back

Situation 3rd-and-7, Miami empty or 11, Chiefs need a stop after the Colts' 5-of-12 kept them alive

Felix Anudike-Uzomah popping up with a quad is not the week to live on a four-man edge rush. Spagnuolo's signature is show heat, drop a lineman, send the second level. Interior pressure from Chris Jones and a reminder that Omarr Norman-Lott exists, plus Bolton as the green-dog, is how you get off the field without asking a banged-up edge to win the down.

Coaching point Show five at the snap, drop the end into the hook, and let Jones wreck the protection from inside. Bolton only comes if the back stays in; if the back releases, he is the flat defender. Do not give Miami the same clean third-down looks Daniel Jones had in the red zone.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Andy Reid vs. the noon trap

Reid's job this week is tempo and script, not speechmaking. First road kick, double-digit favorite, winless opponent — the veteran answer is to stay in the run-boot language that produced 37:00 against Indianapolis.

Eric Bieniemy has both answers. He should still pick the run first.

The pass cashed (382, three scores). The run is what made it cash. Twenty-plus Walker carries is how Miami's safeties get caught in-between, and how a 5-of-13 third-down night does not repeat.

Steve Spagnuolo's self-scout is the actual game plan

He is not pretending 30 points, 329 yards, and 24 first downs were a finishing-luck problem. Two-deep structure and simulated pressure are the grown-up calls with Conner ramping and FAU on the quad report.

Lumen Field film: 3-20 and no red-zone cash

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

L
Seattle Seahawks Week 7 · Sun, Oct 25 · 7:20 PM CT
KC 3–20

At Lumen Field, Seattle controlled both lines. Kansas City managed 213 yards and 24:09 of possession. Seattle managed 410 yards and 35:51 of possession. The visitors scored on Butker’s 50-yard field goal. Seattle scored 20.

The rushing gap is the film. Kansas City ran 16 times for 50 yards. Walker’s 14 carries gained 36, with Mahomes adding two scrambles. Seattle ran 41 times for 153. Wilson’s volume, George Holani’s six touches, Velus Jones Jr.’s five rushes, and three AJ Barner rushes — including direct snaps — kept Darnold in manageable down-and-distance. A second-quarter Wilson carry lost three to K. Tonga, and another lost a yard to George Karlaftis and Chris Roland-Wallace. Those wins did not change the down-and-distance trend.

Mahomes’ 19-of-33, 165-yard line included two 19-yard completions to Noah Gray, one deep right in the first quarter and one deep middle in the fourth, plus four Kelce catches for 44 yards and six Xavier Worthy targets that produced three catches for 11 yards, including a deep right with pressure from D. Hall and a deep left with pressure from M. Morris. The interception was a short left throw intended for J. Royals, taken by T. Okada with N. Emmanwori in on the play. Creed Humphrey’s first-quarter snap was fumbled and recovered by Mahomes. Hall later sacked Mahomes for a two-yard loss and forced a fumble that Mahomes also recovered. That is one sack and seven quarterback hits, not a clean pocket.

Seattle’s explosive answers were specific. Darnold’s deep left to Smith-Njigba was ruled a 27-yard catch to the 1, then reversed to a 28-yard touchdown. He had already scrambled left end for seven, and Jones had a right-end carry of 11. Later Darnold scrambled right end for 10. N. Williams intercepted a short middle throw intended for Rashid Shaheed and returned it 65 yards, with Karlaftis credited on the play. That takeaway did not produce points. Butker’s 58-yard try was short, and the 45-yard try was wide right.

Special teams and penalties framed empty drives. Kelce’s illegal shift wiped a first-quarter play. Kingsley Suamataia’s holding call wiped a fourth-quarter play. Seattle had three false starts, by G. Zabel, A. Bradford, and J. Sundell. On one first-quarter snap, J. Jones and O. Oluwatimi reported eligible before a direct snap to Barner up the middle. Later Barner direct snaps are on the log without that same report. Nick Bolton was injured on a third-quarter Emanuel Wilson carry at Q3 11:19 and returned at Q3 10:03. He finished with 11 tackles.

What held up

  • Gray’s two 19-yard catches gave Mahomes an intermediate answer when the deep shots to Worthy and Kelce were incomplete.
  • Williams’ interception and 65-yard return, with Karlaftis involved, was the defense’s cleanest event.
  • Tonga, Karlaftis, Bolton, and Tranquill each showed up on Seattle runs for negative or short yardage, and Tranquill finished with 12 tackles.

What broke

  • Sixteen rushes, 50 yards, and 3-of-11 on third down left Mahomes in long-yardage shotgun too often.
  • Seven quarterback hits and one sack, plus Hall’s forced fumble on the sack, kept the timing offense late.
  • Two missed Butker tries and a 3-yard Kelce catch on 4th-and-10 that turned the ball over on downs kept the only Kansas City points on the 50-yard make from changing the game.

Third down decided the clock

Seattle converted 11 of 16 third downs. Kansas City converted 3 of 11. Eight drives apiece, and Seattle held the ball for 35:51 to Kansas City’s 24:09. Spagnuolo’s group kept the score at 20 and still never stole a series.

Walker never got a down-and-distance friend

Eighteen touches, 14 of them carries, 36 rushing yards. Seattle’s front, not a mystery gap, is what the coaches have on film.

The takeaways did not cash

Williams’ interception was a real defensive play. It did not become points. Butker made the 50-yarder and missed from 58 and 45. A three-yard Kelce catch on fourth down turned the ball over on downs.

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

5-1

What we need to work on

Early-down carries that are not heroic

Walker needs a carry count that is not stuck at 14 for 36. Denver will take the same invitation Seattle took if the first two downs stay behind the sticks.

Protect the 19-of-33 version of Mahomes

One sack and seven hits is the count. Suamataia’s holding flag and the Humphrey bobble are operational, not theoretical. Help rules and defined answers to Gray and Kelce are the install.

Third-down stops without living on the takeaway

Seattle was 11-of-16. Kansas City had two quarterback hits. Cover-2 rally tackles and a called zone blitz are the install, not a claim that pressure created Williams’ interception.

Looking ahead

Denver at 3:25: take the early downs back

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Week 8 is at Denver on Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT, CBS. Kansas City is a 1.5-point favorite with a 41.5 total. ESPN FPI has the Chiefs at 55.7 percent. The assignment is to look like the club that won the first meeting 31-10, not the club that just posted 50 rushing yards and 3-of-11 on third down.

How they match up

Kansas City beat Denver 31-10 in Week 1 at Arrowhead. Week 8 is at Empower Field at Mile High, a different building, with Denver at 5-2. Kansas City’s defensive points rate is still 17.2 per game. The mismatch risk is offensive: if Walker is stuck in second-and-long, Mahomes will see the same seven-hit profile Seattle just produced. Spagnuolo can live in Cover 2 and sprinkle simulated pressure. Bieniemy has to give him a lead, or at least a tied game, with inside zone and defined throws to Kelce and Gray.

Make Denver defend 16-plus honest runs

Sixteen attempts were not a run game. Script Walker under center and in shotgun on early downs, with Mahomes’ legs as a constraint, not the identity.

Answer before the vertical

Mesh and the screen are the answers to a rush that hunts. Four verticals can wait until Denver is light in the box. Worthy remains the outside vertical; he does not have to be the first read.

Rally, then rush on your terms

Two deep, five under takes away the shot that Smith-Njigba just cashed from another quarterback. The zone blitz is a call for a known third-and-medium, with a lineman dropping and a second-level rusher.

  1. Open with under-center inside zone to Walker, then a Mahomes keep only if the edge widens.
  2. Second series: mesh to Kelce and Gray, Worthy on the corner route, Walker as the checkdown.
  3. If Denver shows a heavy rush on second-and-long, throw the screen and let the line get downhill in space.
  4. Play-action boot off a successful inside-zone look, Mahomes to the perimeter, Gray as the flat answer.
  5. Defensively, Cover 2 on early downs so Tranquill and Bolton can rally.
  6. Call the zone blitz once on third-and-5 to third-and-7, drop a defensive lineman, and do not tell that story as a takeaway.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The 5-0 start met a Seattle team that kept the ball, ran it, and never let Patrick Mahomes find a rhythm. Kansas City finished with 11 first downs, 50 rushing yards on 16 attempts, 163 net passing yards, 213 total yards, and 24:09 of possession. Seattle finished with 24 first downs, 153 rushing yards on 41 attempts, 257 net passing yards, 410 total yards, and 35:51 of possession. Both clubs had eight drives and one turnover. The final was 3-20.

Seattle scored on a 25-yard field goal, a 28-yard touchdown, a 35-yard field goal, and a 21-yard touchdown. Kansas City’s scoring play was Harrison Butker’s 50-yard field goal. Butker also missed from 58 and from 45, and a late 4th-and-10, 3-yard catch by Travis Kelce ended in a turnover on downs. Mahomes was 19-of-33 for 165 yards with one interception, one sack, and a 58.3 rating. Seattle recorded seven quarterback hits. Kansas City recorded two. Sam Darnold was 20-of-28 with no sacks and a 96.9 rating.

Kenneth Walker III had 14 carries for 36 yards and four catches, 18 touches in a game Seattle clearly wanted to shrink. Emanuel Wilson had 24 rushes and one catch. Jaxon Smith-Njigba had nine catches and two rushes, including the 28-yard touchdown throw from Darnold. Drue Tranquill’s 12 tackles were the loudest Kansas City defender on the sheet, and the unit still could not get off the field: Seattle was 11-of-16 on third down, Kansas City 3-of-11.

The defense has allowed 17.2 points per game through six contests (10+30+10+27+6+20). This tape was 20 allowed, 11-of-16 third downs surrendered, and 35:51 of Seattle possession. Denver is next. Kansas City won the Week 1 meeting 31-10 at Arrowhead. Week 8 is on the road at Empower Field, Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT, CBS. Denver is 5-2. The market still leans Kansas City. Boot off the zone look

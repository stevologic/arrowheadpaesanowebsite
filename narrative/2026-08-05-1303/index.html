The story so far

Always looking ahead

Training camp at Missouri Western is doing what a 6-11 offseason is supposed to do: sort the real from the roster filler. The pads came on, and the loudest early truth is on the interior defensive line—rookie tackle Peter Woods, the 29th pick out of Clemson, has looked like a problem in the first two padded practices, with Khyiris Tonga branding him “a little pitbull.” That kind of early juice matters because Steve Spagnuolo’s defense is shorter on familiar faces than it has been in years, and George Karlaftis is already saying the group can take the unit wherever it wants if the young pieces hit. On the other side of the ball, the story is less romance and more accountability: Rashee Rice once led his draft class in production buzz and is now the Associated Press’ Chiefs player with the most to prove in a contract year while watching peers cash in. Add Josh Simmons drawing national praise after a rocky rookie year, Joe Bleymaier running point with Eric Bieniemy absent, and a secondary that just got younger by force, and you’ve got a camp about roles, not vibes.

The through-line for Andy Reid’s building is simple: install clean, identify who can play in September, and don’t pretend last year’s record was a blip you ignore. Mahomes’ rehab ramp and timing work sit over everything—every mesh rep, every boot, every easy screen is about getting the quarterback to full-speed decision-making without asking him to be Superman behind a line still sorting itself. Simmons landing on NFL.com’s All-Under 25 team is the kind of external noise that only matters if the tape in St. Joe matches it; Kansas City spent a first-round pick on him for a reason, and a clean camp from the young tackle is one of the few things that actually changes Week 1 math.

Spagnuolo’s side is where the personality is showing up early. Karlaftis talking ownership with Chris Jones as the elder pillar, Woods winning the “who’s that?” segment of padded work, and Louis Riddick already flagging rookie Jadon Canady for a huge defensive role tells you the staff is open to playing youth if it earns it. The secondary piece is just as real—Chamarri Conner and the second-year corners are being asked to lead, not shadow. That is how you turn a reset year into a defense-first identity: trust the pitbulls and make the veterans set the standard.

Offense still has to answer the skill-room depth questions the injury scares keep exposing. Signing Jeff Weimer off a minicamp tryout is a small move with a loud subtext—bodies and competition at receiver matter when one ding reshuffles the rotation. Cooper McDonald saying he’s ready for the opportunity he’s been chasing fits the same theme on defense/special teams: second-year guys who know the callsheet now have to win jobs. EJ Smith fighting to be more than a famous last name is camp color until he forces a roster math conversation.

Look ahead, not sideways. Polymarket sitting the Chiefs around 7.4% to win the 2027 title and 9.5% to take the AFC is a cold read on a 6-11 roster in transition—not dead, not favored. What a strong camp unlocks for Week 1 is boring and decisive: a defined OL hierarchy, Rice playing like a WR1 on a mission, Woods and Canady in packages Spagnuolo trusts on early downs and third-and-long, and Mahomes operating an install that already has answers for man, single-high, and pressure. That is the September product. Everything else is noise from the practice fields in St. Joe.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Mahomes Boot After Zone Look

Situation Camp install / early-down Week 1 script when defenses sit on inside zone

With Mahomes ramping back to full timing and Simmons still proving anchor reliability after a rocky rookie year, moving the launch point protects the rehab ramp and creates easy throws for Rice on the move. Bleymaier’s pass-game emphasis in Bieniemy’s absence thrives on defined reads, not pure dropback hero ball.

Coaching point Sell the zone with tight zone steps from the interior, get Simmons and the backside seal locked, and let Mahomes come off the boot with Rice as the primary flat-to-sideline progression—no staring down the deep cross against camp-scout eyes.

Offense

Seam Stress vs. Single-High Camp Looks

Situation 3rd-and-medium team periods when Spagnuolo’s scout defense shows Cover-3 shells

Rice needs high-leverage targets to rewrite the contract-year narrative, and four verts forces the middle-of-field safety to pick a seam. It also tests whether the young OL—Simmons included—can give Mahomes a clean five-step without the pocket collapsing into the rehab timeline.

Coaching point Outside receivers push the corners; the inside seam (Rice or TE) bends to the hash on time. Mahomes’ eyes fix the single-high safety before the throw—Bleymaier will flag any late hitch that turns a answer concept into a sack period.

Offense

Mesh to Free Rice vs. Man

Situation Red-zone and 3rd-and-short camp periods vs. press-man scout looks

If Rice is truly the player with the most to prove, mesh puts him on a rub crosser where separation is scheme-assisted. It also gives Mahomes a quick answer while the OL and Simmons settle protection rules without holding the ball forever.

Coaching point Tight, purposeful rub—no freelancing. Rice must win through the traffic and finish; the back is the outlet if both crossers get trailed. Reid/Bleymaier want ball out on rhythm so camp ‘sacks’ don’t become bad habits.

Offense

Easy Button Screen

Situation After a coverage sack period or when scout edges are pinching upfield hard

Receiver injury scares already exposed how thin the skill room can feel; screens manufacture yards without asking Mahomes to live in a muddy pocket. They also let camp bodies—including newcomer Jeff Weimer—show up as blockers and perimeter finishers in real rules work.

Coaching point OL must get to space on time; the back’s path is decisive, not decorative. Mahomes throws it with touch and gets the defense punished for over-aggression so the next dropback breathes.

Defense

Spag’s Two-Deep Answer

Situation Obvious passing downs in camp when the offense wants the deep shot off play-action

With Conner and young corners being forced into bigger roles, Cover-2 is the structure that protects them while the front—Jones, Karlaftis, Woods—rallies and finishes. It lets the youth play fast without getting roasted over the top during the evaluation grind.

Coaching point Safeties split the field; corners squat and disrupt. Woods and the interior must close the pocket so ‘two-deep’ doesn’t become ‘two-deep and a scramble drill.’ Spagnuolo will coach the hell out of the five-under fits.

Defense

Simulated Heat With Woods Drop/Rush Mix

Situation 3rd-and-long install periods; dress rehearsal for Week 1 money downs

Karlaftis says this defense can go wherever it wants; Spagnuolo’s signature simulated pressures are how you hide a younger secondary and still generate hits. Woods’ early ‘pitbull’ reputation only matters if he can rush and, on selected calls, alter surfaces so linebackers free-release.

Coaching point Show four or five at the line, drop the designated lineman, and bring the second-level runner into a vacated gap. Canady or Conner must be flawless on the back-end replacement so the gamble doesn’t become a camp explosive.

The storylines

What we’re watching

Rookie Juice

Peter Woods, the ‘little pitbull’

Through the first two padded practices in St. Joe, the Clemson product and 29th overall pick has already drawn vet praise from Khyiris Tonga for his style and disruptiveness. If Woods keeps winning early downs, Spagnuolo can build packages around him beside Chris Jones instead of sheltering him. That is how a draft pick becomes a Week 1 plan, not a developmental footnote.

Contract Year

Rashee Rice has the most to prove

The Associated Press tabbed Rice as the Chiefs player with the most to prove, and the broader narrative is blunt—he once led his draft class’s buzz and is now watching peers get paid. Camp is his stage to reassert WR1 gravity for Mahomes and reset the financial conversation with tape, not quotes.

OL Continuity

Josh Simmons’ national bump

Simmons landed on NFL.com’s All-Under 25 team after a rocky rookie year and eight-game sample that still showed why KC spent a first-round pick. A clean, healthy camp from Simmons is one of the few offensive developments that directly stabilizes Mahomes’ dropback world for Week 1.

Staff Note

Bleymaier holding the callsheet

Pass-game coordinator Joe Bleymaier has stepped up in Eric Bieniemy’s absence and already faced the local media after Day 6. How clean the pass install looks under that setup is a real camp storyline—timing, protection rules, and route depth all still have to travel when the lights come on.

Secondary Youth

Conner and the next wave

With veterans gone, Chamarri Conner and the young corner group are being asked to lead the back end, not finish plays behind stars. Spagnuolo’s trust in that room will dictate how often KC can afford single-high pressure looks in September.

Market Check

7.4% is not a superteam number

Polymarket pricing the Chiefs at 7.4% for the 2027 title and 9.5% for the AFC crown matches a post-6-11 reality: path exists, presumption does not. Camp winners have to become September roles or those numbers stay charity.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Bleymaier running the pass game in Bieniemy’s absence

Joe Bleymaier’s Day 6 media session underscored that the pass install still has a clear voice even with the OC unavailable. Watch how quickly protections and route depths standardize—that is the real camp grade for the staff bridge.

Spagnuolo empowering a younger defense

Karlaftis’ ownership tone plus early love for Woods and national buzz on Canady fit Spagnuolo’s history: if you can rush and tackle, you play. The coaching task is defining packages before Week 1 so the youth isn’t guessing on money downs.

Reid’s camp priorities after 6-11

Reid’s building doesn’t reinvent football in August; it sorts who can execute the staples. Mahomes’ timing, Simmons’ set point, and Rice’s detail work are the Reid-facing report cards that actually travel into September.

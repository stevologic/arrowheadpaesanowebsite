Current state

Seed math with a backfield and a trench question

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

7-2

Kansas City is 7-2 and back at Arrowhead for Arizona, tied with Denver in the AFC West and one game behind Buffalo in the race for the No. 1 seed. Two straight games have ended 35-13 and 35-12. The next one is priced like a get-ahead week. The roster says it is a manage-the-bodies week.

What we need to work on

Third down before the price makes it optional

A 16.5 favorite that keeps handing the ball back lets a 3-7 team stay in the script longer than the number suggests, and Buffalo is on the calendar four days after this kickoff.

Johnson's touches without burning him for Thursday

Walker is out. Johnson just played 28 touches. Carter has a two-touch line to grow into a spell, not a 28-touch job. Bieniemy needs early-down efficiency so the closer is the score, not another 22-carry afternoon stacked onto a short week.

Protection with the trench report incomplete in public

Eight hits and one sack on Mahomes in Atlanta is the verified pressure line. Three starters are ruled out and the wire has not named the offensive linemen. Reid should plan as if the pocket is shorter, not as if the Falcons rush was a one-off.

What to think about

How seriously to treat 88.5 percent

FPI and the 16.5 say Arizona is the lesser side, and a 3-7 team that has moved on from Marvin Harrison Jr. deserves that read. The same model does not play third down or replace Walker. The edge is real. The margin for a sloppy series is not 16.5 points of comfort.

Whether Justin Reid is a this-week topic or a later one

Looking ahead

Arizona at Arrowhead: finish a 3-7 club without getting cute

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Arizona Cardinals

Host a 3-7 Arizona team on Sun Nov 22, 12:00 PM CT on CBS, lay 16.5, and leave Arrowhead healthy enough for Buffalo on Thu Nov 26, 7:20 PM CT. The Cardinals have lost consecutive games and no longer have Marvin Harrison Jr. They are also described in the wire as injury-depleted. Credit the record. Do not gift them hidden explosives by playing loose on third down.

How they match up

Kansas City's offense just produced 461 yards, five touchdowns, and a 135.5 passer rating, with the run game concentrated in Johnson. Arizona's season has been losses, a departed No. 1 receiver, and a depleted injury sheet. The mismatch is real: FPI 88.5 percent, DraftKings minus 16.5. The leverage Arizona can actually hold is pace and Kansas City's own 4-of-13, plus whatever pass rush shows up against an offensive line the club has not fully clarified. Spagnuolo should still expect a full game script. Atlanta gained 370 yards and held a 31:24 possession edge in a 22-point loss.

Score on early downs so third down is not the offense

Johnson on inside zone and Mahomes's easy answers — screens and mesh — should turn 1st-and-10 into 2nd-and-short. The Atlanta miss was finishing drives without an 18-, 39-, or 43-yard throw. Arizona should not be allowed to live on Kansas City's 4-of-13 habit.

Keep the deep menu, change who has to win it

Downs, Allen, Armstrong, and Rice all produced explosives in Atlanta. Worthy was targeted deep without a completion on the listed throws. With Harrison gone, Arizona's coverage picture is thinner on paper. Bieniemy can still stress it vertically without asking Mahomes to hold the ball into eight hits.

Rush the passer, then tackle the checkdown

Rally tackles matter because a 3-7 offense will try to live underneath once the deep shot is capped.

Win the game without a 28-touch sequel if the score allows

Walker is unavailable. Johnson is the starter. If Kansas City is comfortably ahead, Carter's two-touch role can grow on inside zone and screens so Thursday in Buffalo is not a recovery week for the lead back.

  1. Open with Johnson on early-down inside zone and a Mahomes keep or scramble rule, the same family as the 9-yard up-the-middle scramble, so Arizona cannot sit on one back.
  2. On 2nd-and-long, throw the spacing screen to Johnson — six catches last game — and let the line get to space instead of asking Mahomes to absorb another hit cluster.
  3. Use mesh for Kelce and Rice against man looks, with Downs as the sit/corner piece that scored from 18.
  4. Take a four-verticals shot once the box commits to Johnson, aiming the seams at Rice and Allen, who scored from 39.
  5. Spagnuolo in Cover-2 on early Arizona downs to cap the deep middle Penix found with London, Dotson, and Pitts.
  6. Call the zone blitz on obvious passing downs, with a lineman dropping and Karlaftis as a possible second-level rusher — a call, tied to no takeaway from last week.
Last game

Atlanta's 35-13, read as context not the assignment

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

W
Atlanta Falcons Week 10 · Sun, Nov 15 · 12:00 PM CT
KC 35–13

Kansas City beat Atlanta 35-13 at Mercedes-Benz Stadium, scoring on a 12-yard touchdown at Q1 8:53 — 6:07 into the game — and finishing with five touchdowns to Atlanta's one. The visitors piled up 461 yards and zero turnovers. They also went 4-of-13 on third down and gave the ball back often enough that Atlanta ran 15 drives to Kansas City's 13.

The scoreboard ran away late more than it ran away early. Atlanta's 3-yard touchdown at Q4 8:12 and 38-yard field goal at Q4 6:45 cut it to 28-13 before Andrew Armstrong's 43-yard touchdown at Q4 4:18 made it 35-13.

Xavier Worthy was targeted deep and those listed throws were incomplete. Travis Kelce, who returned in the second quarter, finished with two catches. This was a shot-play passing day, not a third-down machine. Kansas City was 4-of-13; Atlanta was 5-of-16.

Johnson was the run game. He carried 22 times for 101 of the team 122 rushing yards on 27 attempts, with listed gains up the middle and at both ends, including an 11-yard right-end run and an 18-yard left-tackle run that A.J. Terrell forced out of bounds. That fumble did not become a turnover — the box lists zero for Kansas City. Justin Fields's direct-snap rush went for no gain. Carter had two rushes. Mahomes added two rushes, including a 9-yard scramble up the middle.

Atlanta's 100 rushing yards on 20 attempts were mostly Bijan Robinson (16 rushes and four catches, 20 touches), with a 17-yard run up the middle and an 8-yard left-end carry. Penix was 23-of-43 for a 64.7 rating, took three sacks, and was hit as part of Kansas City's five quarterback hits. Drake London caught six passes, including 38 yards. Jahan Dotson had a 37-yard catch. Kyle Pitts Sr. had a 28-yard catch and was in on the strip: N. Bolton forced the fumble, C. Conner recovered at the Atlanta 44. N. Williams intercepted Penix on a deep-middle throw intended for Chris Blair and returned it 20 yards. An earlier Penix fumble was aborted and recovered by Penix. Kansas City also failed on downs at Q4 3:39.

What held up

  • Mahomes at 22-of-35, 348 yards, five touchdowns, 135.5, one sack, zero team turnovers.

What broke

  • Third down at 4-of-13, and a turnover on downs on an incomplete to Allen.
  • Eight quarterback hits on Mahomes against one sack — pressure without a collapse, still a protection note.
  • Johnson's fumble, forced by A.J. Terrell and out of bounds, plus Atlanta holding the clock at 31:24 and gaining 370 yards.

Explosives covered a bad down-and-distance day

Five touchdowns and 461 yards will travel in the highlights. The 4-of-13 third-down line is what Bieniemy has to answer before a 16.5 price makes fans assume every series will finish. Atlanta still possessed the ball for 31:24.

The backfield load is already Johnson's

Johnson's 28 touches — 22 rushes and six catches — produced 101 rushing yards inside a 122-yard team total. Carter's line was two touches. With Walker out, that split is the roster fact, not a one-week curiosity.

The defense closed with two credited takeaways

N. Bolton forced and C. Conner recovered the Pitts fumble.

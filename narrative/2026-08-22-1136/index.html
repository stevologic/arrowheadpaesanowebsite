The story so far

Always looking ahead

A 6-11 season does not get washed off in one August night, and Kansas City just spent the first dress rehearsal losing 12-20 to the Rams with the same two problems staring back at them: an offensive line that looks thinner than the depth chart advertised, and a pass rush that just lost another body. Saturday at Raymond James is the second of three preseason games and the last one on the road — Patrick Mahomes is ruled out with seven others, Ashton Gillotte’s ruptured plantar fascia has already punched a hole in Steve Spagnuolo’s front, and DraftKings has Tampa Bay -5.5 with a 36.5 total. That is the market telling you this is evaluation tape, not a statement game. Treat it like one. The only scores that matter in two weeks are the ones that decide who is still here when the Broncos come to Arrowhead on September 15.

Andy Reid will not put the franchise quarterback out there to prove a point, and he should not. Eric Bieniemy’s job this week is to script a backup-friendly menu — inside zone, boot, mesh, a couple of verticals for the kid who has actually separated — and come home knowing which linemen can zone-step and which ones are about to force a phone call. Arrowhead Addict flagged a bigger offensive-line depth problem than expected, and Arrowhead Pride has the club at roughly $4.1 million in cap space. That is not “go buy a starter” money. That is “be right about the guys already in the building, then nick a veteran if you must.” Saturday is the live interview.

The other side of the ball is a Spagnuolo problem with a familiar patch. Emmanuel Ogbah is back because he always planned to be, and because a thin edge room just got thinner. Gillotte going down in Tuesday’s practice is the kind of quiet camp injury that becomes a September problem if you pretend it is not. Simulated pressure, two-deep shells, and defined rules for Kaiir Elam — a former first-rounder on his last clean look — are how you survive a night when you cannot just pin your ears back. Give Tampa credit: they are at home, they are getting the Chiefs without Mahomes, and the number says they should control this. If Kansas City’s backups cannot finish drives or get off the field on third down, that is information, not bad luck.

Zoom out and the calendar is the real opponent. Nine games this year are against 2025 playoff teams, and the soft-looking pockets on the slate are the exception, not the plan. After Tampa it is Seattle at Arrowhead — last cuts, last looks, Backyard Sports on the concourse — then a long wait for Denver in prime time. Polymarket has the Chiefs at 6.3 percent to win the 2027 title and 9.5 percent to come out of the AFC. That is a proving-it price for a Mahomes team, and it matches a year that ended 6-11 and a Top 100 that just parked him at No. 18. Saturday does not change the odds. It tells you whether the edges that got them there are being fixed or just being managed until Week 1.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Downhill identity: inside zone as a live job interview

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script at Raymond James, backups in, Bucs loading the box on a no-Mahomes Chiefs offense

The offensive-line depth problem is no longer a camp whisper — it is the reason Kansas City is expected to make roster moves this week with only about $4.1 million in space. Bieniemy needs to know, before Seattle and long before Denver, which second-unit linemen can zone-step, combo to the second level, and finish. A 12-20 Rams tape already said the first look was not good enough.

Coaching point One cut, no bounce. The center and play-side guard have to win the double and climb; if they lose it, the backup QB is going to eat the same collapse the Rams already served. This is evaluation tape for the line, not a chance to get cute with the back.

Offense

Move the pocket: play-action boot without Mahomes

Situation 2nd-and-medium off the zone look, backup QB, Bucs end crashing the run fake

Mahomes is ruled out with seven others, so the Reid/Bieniemy answer is the same one they have always trusted with a second-team quarterback: sell zone, get the launch point outside, and give him an easy flat/leak/over read instead of a five-man pocket he may not have. It also keeps a suspect OL from having to hold a rush for three seconds.

Coaching point Same zone steps as first down or the fake is a lie. TE leaks, backside receiver sits in the flat, and the backup throws on time — Tampa will have a contain end or a spy if he drifts. Do not climb. Do not hunt the deep over unless it is wide open.

Offense

Let Allen run: four verts vs. a Cover-3 lid

Situation 2nd-and-7 or 3rd-and-medium, Bucs in a two-high look that rotates to Cover 3

Cyrus Allen is the one camp riser the local film room has stopped hedging on after St. Joseph and the Rams night. A backup does not need to paint; he needs one defined shot down the hash. Four verts stress the deep middle, bend a seam to the post safety, and tell you whether Allen is a roster lock or just a July story.

Coaching point Outside receivers run the corners out of the play. Allen (or the Z) bends the seam to the hash and sits in the hole if the safety hangs. The backup takes the first window — no late post, no hero ball into the lid. If Tampa rotates late, the hole shot is the throw.

Offense

Third-down oxygen: mesh for the bubble and the backup

Situation 3rd-and-5, Tampa showing man or a late mug, last WR/TE jobs still live

Without Mahomes, third down cannot be a backyard scramble. Mesh is Bieniemy’s friendliest answer: rub-heavy crossers, a corner/sit combo, and a back as the outlet when the OL is leaking. It also puts the bubble receivers and tight ends in traffic, which is exactly the tape you want before the Seattle cut-down.

Coaching point The two crossers have to rub through the same window — no polite traffic. If Tampa switches, the back is hot. The backup takes the first open break and lives to punt; holding it for the corner route is how you donate a sack-fumble on the road.

Defense

Spags’ lid: Cover-2 to protect a thin front and Elam

Situation Early downs, Bucs trying to chunk a Chiefs front missing Gillotte

Gillotte’s ruptured plantar fascia makes four-man heat a wish, not a plan. Two deep and five under lets Spagnuolo take away the shot, rally to tackle, and give Kaiir Elam a defined half-field with help over the top on a night he may be playing for his roster life. You cannot ask a short-handed edge room to pin its ears back for four quarters in Tampa.

Coaching point Corners squat and funnel; do not get stacked. Elam’s rule is simple: lose the first five yards and the Cover-2 hole shot is dead. Safeties are the lid, linebackers rally, and nobody hunts a strip when the front cannot finish the rush. Tackle and get off the field.

Defense

Manufacture a fourth: zone blitz with Ogbah in the menu

Situation 3rd-and-long, show zero or an overload, Bucs backup protection

Pass-rush concerns are already public, Gillotte is down, and Ogbah just got here because this room needed a grown-up. Simulated pressure is how Spagnuolo creates a fourth rusher without asking a short-handed line to win four one-on-ones. Ogbah’s 2019 tape in Kansas City was as a rusher who can also drop — that is the entire point of bringing him back before Denver.

Coaching point Show the overload, drop the end or a backer into the hook, rush a second-level player. Ogbah has to know the call on the first snap: if he rushes when he is the dropper, the hole is a walk-in. Hold the disguise until the ball is snapped and make the Bucs’ backup count the wrong man.

The storylines

What we’re watching

QB room

Mahomes sits. The script still has to look like him.

Reid ruled Patrick Mahomes and seven others out for Tampa, which is the correct September decision and a stressful August one. Bieniemy has to get boot, play-action, and a clean run-action look on tape so the next man is not asked to win from a collapsing pocket the way the Rams night drifted. Mahomes landing at No. 18 on the NFL Top 100 is the offseason’s honest ranking: still the engine, no longer treated like the default title favorite.

Edge

Gillotte’s plantar fascia is a front-office problem, not a footnote.

Ashton Gillotte ruptured his plantar fascia in Tuesday’s practice, and a defensive line that was already thin just lost a second-year end. Reid has downplayed it; the depth chart cannot. This is why Emmanuel Ogbah is in the building and why Spagnuolo will live in simulated pressure instead of four-man hope.

Veteran add

Ogbah’s return is a plan, not a reunion tour.

Ogbah played here in 2019, said he always planned to come back, and signed last week in time to be on the field Saturday. He is not a scheme-wrecking edge anymore. He is a grown-up who can rush, drop, and keep Spagnuolo’s zone-blitz menu from becoming a six-man scramble when Gillotte is in a boot.

Rookie WR

Cyrus Allen has separated. Now make the Bucs pay for it.

Camp noise is camp noise until it survives a live secondary. Allen did enough against the Rams — and enough in St. Joseph — that the local tape-watchers have stopped hedging. Saturday is the go-ball and the seam. If the backup hits him once down the hash, the WR room’s bubble math changes before Seattle.

Bubble

Kaiir Elam is out of clean looks.

The Chiefs gave a former first-round corner the kind of runway most retreads never get. Tampa is a bad place to play scared: if Elam gets stacked, grabbed, and ridden in a Cover-2 look with help over the top, there is no schematic excuse left. Three other jobs are on the same clock. Cuts do not wait for Week 1.

O-line

The depth problem is bigger than the next man up.

Kansas City is expected to make roster moves in the next week to patch problem spots, and the offensive line is the one that keeps showing up in the reporting. With about $4.1 million in cap space, they cannot miss on the combinations they run Saturday. Inside zone and screens will tell them who can play and who is about to get replaced.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid’s August conservatism vs. a 6-11 hangover

Sitting Mahomes and seven others is the right long view toward September 15. It also means Reid and Bieniemy cannot hide a sloppy script behind the franchise quarterback — Tampa will tell them, live, whether the second unit can execute zone, boot, and third-down mesh.

Bieniemy’s install window is two games long

After Saturday there is one home night against Seattle and then a long wait for Denver. The offensive coordinator has to leave Florida with a short list of OL combinations and a backup-proof third-down menu, not a pile of empty calories.

Spagnuolo has to coach the disguise, not the win-rush

Gillotte’s injury and a thin edge room force the coordinator into his signature look: show heat, drop a lineman, rush the second level. Ogbah only helps if the assignments are clean on the first series, not after a preseason sack-fumble.

Tampa gets the situational edge and should use it

Home crowd, Chiefs without Mahomes, a -5.5 number. A competent Buccaneers staff will pin Kansas City in long yards and make the backup win from the pocket. If KC’s coaches do not win first down on the ground, the night becomes a coverage-and-punt tape.

New York Jets Week 9 · Sun, Nov 8 · 12:00 PM CT
KC 35–12

At Arrowhead on Sun Nov 8, 12:00 PM CT, Kansas City beat the Jets 35-12 after trailing 6-0 and going to halftime up 14-12. The second half was three Kansas City touchdowns and a defense that kept Geno Smith off the scoreboard.

The opening stretch belonged to New York's kicker and Kansas City's ball security. A 23-yard Jets field goal at Q1 6:47 made it 0-3. A 48-yarder at Q1 4:35 made it 0-6. In between, a kickoff return fumble was forced by Q. Stiggers and recovered by J. Bassa, so Kansas City kept that ball. On the next Chiefs drive, H. Nourzad reported as eligible and Justin Fields was stopped up the middle; the Jets won a challenge and D. Davis recovered at the Kansas City 34. That is one of the two Kansas City turnovers.

Eric Bieniemy's answer was the run game once the score flipped. Kenneth Walker III finished with 18 carries for 102 yards and a rushing touchdown, plus four catches for 41 yards and two receiving touchdowns, 22 touches in all. The scoring table shows a 7-yard Walker catch from Mahomes at Q2 14:56 and a 25-yard Walker catch from Mahomes at Q2 5:37, then 1 and 2 yards in the third and 6 yards in the fourth. The play-by-play tags the 2-yard score to Walker up the middle at Q3 0:11 and the 6-yard score to Emmett Johnson up the middle at Q4 3:35, with K. Tonga among the eligible reports on that snap. Johnson had six rushes and no catches. Jaydn Ott added three late carries.

Mahomes' line is efficient and scarred. He was 23-of-31 for 242 yards and two touchdowns, rating 117.9, with two rushes and two sacks. D. Bailey forced a sack-fumble that J. Moore recovered for Kansas City, then forced another sack-fumble that W. McDonald recovered for the Jets at the Kansas City 30. The play-by-play notes Mahomes was injured on that second sack. He still scrambled for 7 yards before halftime and for 1 yard on the goal-line series. Travis Kelce led the receivers with four catches for 72 yards. Xavier Worthy had six catches, Rashee Rice five, Jalen Royals two, Noah Gray one, Jake Briningstool one. Kelce also had one rush. Harrison Butker missed a 51-yard field goal wide right; the Jets missed a 53-yarder wide left.

Spagnuolo's second-half leverage showed up in down and distance, not in a shutdown of the run early. Hall had 15 rushes and three catches, 18 touches. Allen had eight rushes and two catches. Omar Cooper Jr. caught eight passes. Kansas City still held New York to 2 of 12 on third down and 229 yards. Nick Bolton forced Allen's fumble, and A. Gilman recovered it. George Karlaftis and Chris Jones were in the tackle and sack picture late: Smith was sacked at the Kansas City 26 on a fourth-quarter drive that ended on downs, and another Jets series died on downs after an incompletion to A. Mitchell. Kansas City recorded 7 quarterback hits. Penalties cut both ways — Briningstool and J. Canady were flagged for offensive holding, M. Smith for illegal touch on a kick — but New York's flags, including holds, false starts, illegal contact, a face mask on K. Sadiq, and an unsportsmanlike on A. Membou, stalled drives that already lacked a touchdown.

What held up

  • Walker at 22 touches, 18 carries for 102 yards and a rushing touchdown, plus four catches for 41 yards and two receiving touchdowns
  • Mahomes at 23-of-31, 242 yards, two touchdowns, 117.9
  • Kelce at four catches for 72 yards in the all-time QB-tight end yardage pairing the CBS broadcast flagged
  • Three Kansas City touchdowns in the second half, including Johnson’s 6-yard score

What broke

  • A 0-6 hole on two Jets field goals before the offense scored
  • Two turnovers, including a sack-fumble with Mahomes hurt on the play
  • Eight quarterback hits allowed, with two sacks
  • Butker’s 51-yard miss wide right, on a week the kicking conversation is already loud
  • Early rushing room for Hall and Allen before the down-and-distance squeeze

The run closed the game

Walker finished with 18 carries for 102 yards and a rushing touchdown, plus four catches for 41 yards and two receiving touchdowns. After a 14-12 halftime the Chiefs scored on 1-, 2-, and 6-yard touchdowns — Kelce, Walker, and Johnson — and closed 35-12. Kansas City finished with 163 rushing yards on 32 attempts. That is the identity Bieniemy can pack for Atlanta.

Two giveaways in a 23-point win

Fields’ fumble, recovered by D. Davis, and Mahomes’ sack-fumble, forced by D. Bailey and recovered by W. McDonald, are the two Kansas City turnovers. A 6-of-11 third-down day does not erase either one.

The rush won the down chart

New York was 2 of 12 on third down and scored on four field goals.

Current state

6-2, deadline Tuesday, and the fixes

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

6-2

The Chiefs are 6-2 after 35-12, back in the win column, and still a team Reid had to discuss on Monday because Mahomes, Butker, and the injury list are live topics. The football standard for Atlanta is finishing drives without the two giveaways and without living on the long field goal.

What we need to work on

Protect the second sack

Mahomes took two sacks and eight quarterback hits. One of those sacks became a fumble W. McDonald recovered, and the play-by-play says he was injured on it. Atlanta does not need a strip if Kansas City hands the ball over on the edge of field-goal range again.

Keep the eligible-report snaps clean

Nourzad’s eligible report accompanied the Fields fumble that D. Davis recovered. Tonga’s eligible report accompanied Johnson’s 6-yard touchdown. Those are different snaps with different results. Bieniemy can use the heavy look; he cannot treat every jumbo report as the same play.

Kicking inside a 45.5 total

Butker missed from 51, wide right, while the Jets made from 23, 48, 49, and 54 and missed from 53. A three-and-a-half point road number dies on a miss the offense already drove into range to avoid.

What to think about

Does Veach buy a receiver before 3:00 p.m. Tuesday?

Reid’s Monday line was that Veach always keeps his eyes open, with receiver smoke around the deadline. Worthy (six catches), Rice (five), and Kelce (four for 72) just produced. The question is depth, not a blank receiving room.

How much of the Jets rush carries to a 3-6 Falcons team?

Five sacks of Smith and seven Kansas City quarterback hits are a get-right for Spagnuolo. Atlanta just traded Jessie Bates, so the back end Kansas City sees is not the one that opened the season. Do not grade the Falcons as the Jets.

Looking ahead

Mercedes-Benz, minus Bates, minus 3.5

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Atlanta Falcons

Week 10 is at Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Sun Nov 15, 12:00 PM CT on CBS. Kansas City is a 3.5-point favorite on a 45.5 total, and ESPN FPI has the Chiefs at 76.3 percent. Atlanta is 3-6. The assignment is to play like a road favorite that already knows what a 14-12 halftime feels like.

How they match up

Kansas City brings a 163-yard rushing afternoon and a quarterback who just completed 23 of 31. Atlanta brings a 3-6 record and, per the wire, a secondary without Jessie Bates after the trade. The leverage is on the ground and in the middle of the field: Walker and Johnson on early downs, Kelce and Rice as the chain-movers, Spagnuolo rushing from a shell that does not need a hero shot. The risk is ball security and eight hits traveling. A 3.5 spread is a one-score game if the Falcons steal a short field the way D. Davis and W. McDonald did.

Hand Walker the early down

Walker’s 18 carries for 102 yards and a rushing touchdown, plus four catches for 41 yards and two receiving touchdowns, are the whole-game line. Atlanta should see inside zone before it sees a seven-step drop. Johnson’s six touches are the changeup, not the starter.

Throw where Bates is not

The Falcons traded Jessie Bates ahead of this week. Kelce’s 72 yards on four catches and Rice’s five catches are the answers in the voids a traded safety leaves. Worthy’s six catches stretch the sideline so those in-breakers are not crowded.

Rush on schedule, drop the show

Cover-2 takes the deep shot. The zone blitz is a call when Atlanta sits in third-and-medium, not a story about last week’s fumble.

  1. Open under center with Walker inside zone and live with a second-and-6 instead of a second-and-10 drop.
  2. On the first third-and-medium, mesh Kelce and Rice and leave Worthy on the sit if the corner chases.
  3. If the Falcons rush heat, throw the Walker screen behind it — he just caught four balls for 41 yards and two receiving touchdowns.
  4. In the red zone, use the heavy report only on the snap you intend to run, the way Tonga’s report sat on Johnson’s 6-yard score, not as a costume on every snap.
  5. Spagnuolo: two-deep on early downs, then a zone blitz on third-and-6 or longer so a dropped lineman is the spy on a scramble.
  6. Butker’s range is a conversation, not a personality. Steal the long field goal with a fourth-and-short run if the spot is outside the 30s.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Kansas City closed a two-game losing streak with a 35-12 win over the New York Jets at Arrowhead, then turned the page to a road favorite's job: the Atlanta Falcons on Sun Nov 15, 12:00 PM CT on CBS. Andy Reid's team is 6-2. The tape that got them there was a second-half finish, not a clean opening.

The Jets led 6-0 on field goals at the game clocks Q1 6:47 and Q1 4:35, then stayed within a score into halftime at 14-12. Kansas City scored on a 7-yard Walker catch from Mahomes at Q2 14:56 and a 25-yard Walker catch from Mahomes at Q2 5:37, and New York answered with 49- and 54-yard field goals. After halftime the Chiefs added touchdowns of 1, 2, and 6 yards and won by 23. Five Kansas City touchdowns, four New York field goals, no Jets touchdown.

The box is a real offensive afternoon and a leaky one. Kansas City posted 27 first downs, 163 rushing yards on 32 attempts, 228 net passing yards, and 391 total yards, converting 6 of 11 third downs across 12 drives while holding the ball 28:54. Patrick Mahomes was 23-of-31 for 242 yards and two touchdowns with a 117.9 rating, and he was sacked twice. The Jets recorded 8 quarterback hits. Kansas City also turned it over twice.

New York held the ball 31:06 on 11 drives and still managed 15 first downs, 106 rushing yards, 123 net passing yards, and 229 total yards, going 2 of 12 on third down with one turnover. Geno Smith was 18-of-32 with a 70.4 rating and was sacked five times. Chris Jones had two of those sacks. Steve Spagnuolo's front bent on early runs by Breece Hall and Braelon Allen, then squeezed the down-and-distance chart.

Arrowhead Pride has Kansas City keeping pace in the AFC picture after 35-12, with Buffalo still the conference marker. The trade deadline is Tuesday, November 10, at 3:00 p.m. Arrowhead time, and Reid said Brett Veach always keeps his eyes open. The next football problem is Atlanta, 3-6, at Mercedes-Benz Stadium, with Jessie Bates traded before the trip.

Zone blitz on third-and-6

Situation 3rd-and-6 or longer, Atlanta in the shotgun, Kansas City ahead or tied in the second half

Use it. Show heat, drop a lineman, rush the second level. Smith was sacked five times when he could not stay on schedule, and Jones already has the two-sack tape. This call is for Atlanta’s third downs, where a dropped big can take the scramble the Jets used when Smith ran up the middle for 8.

Week 3 2026 Week 3 Preview Updated Friday, Sep 25, 2026

Mahomes got loud. The pass rush did not. Miami is the first road exam.

A 33-30 overtime win over Indianapolis put Kansas City at 2-0 with 382 passing yards and 117 on the ground; Sunday noon in Miami is where Steve Spagnuolo’s front has to prove the Colts tape was an outlier, not a trend.

The edge

DraftKings makes Kansas City a 10.5-point road favorite on a 45.5 total and ESPN FPI sits at 85.4 percent, yet Polymarket’s 9.4 percent Super Bowl price and 16.5 percent AFC ticket still treat 2-0 as a sample — and a trap-game noon in Miami is exactly how samples get ruined.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

W
Indianapolis Colts Week 2 · Sun, Sep 20 · 7:20 PM CT
KC 33–30

Indianapolis struck first on a Tyler Warren one-yard touchdown from Daniel Jones, and for a long stretch Sunday night it felt like Kansas City was going to win the yardage battle and still lose the scoreboard. Harrison Butker answered with a 46-yard field goal, Mahomes hit Kelce from 13, then the Colts stacked Spencer Shrader field goals from 42 and 49 before Xavier Worthy’s one-yard score. Jonathan Taylor ripped a 24-yard touchdown just before the half, Jalen Royals answered from a yard out in the third, and a 523-yard Chiefs offense still needed overtime to finish a 33-30 win at Arrowhead.

The telling number is not 382 passing yards. It is 29 first downs on 5-of-13 third down. Bieniemy won early downs so thoroughly — Walker on 24 carries, play-action off that look, Mahomes working the intermediate field to Kelce — that Kansas City did not have to be a great third-down team to hang 523 yards. That is a feature when it works. It is also a tell: when the explosives calm down on the road, you need a cleaner conversion tape than 5-of-13, or Miami will get the ball back in a game the market has already decided.

Mahomes’ 32-of-47 night was the correction the Week 1 box score asked for. He was not hunting hero ball; he was taking what Indianapolis gave him after Walker forced the box to count to eight. Kelce’s 9-for-101-and-a-score was the old safety valve, and the two one-yard touchdowns to Worthy and Royals were red-zone answers even if they were not vertical answers. Worthy is on the board. He is not yet the field-stretcher the first-round tape promised, and Indianapolis was content to make Kansas City drive the long way.

The defensive tape is why this did not feel like 2-0. Nick Bolton leading the club with 13 tackles is a MIKE playing winning football and a warning light at the same time: the ball was getting to the second level. Taylor’s 24-yard touchdown before half is the explosive run Spagnuolo’s front is not supposed to post. After a four-sack opener against Bo Nix, the pass rush versus Jones was quiet enough that Arrowhead Pride treated it as the week’s actual problem, and Spagnuolo did not hide from 30 points, 329 yards, and 24 first downs. When the coordinator says it was not good enough, believe him.

Give Indianapolis its due. Jones lived in the pocket, Warren scored, Shrader kept stacking threes, and Taylor forced overtime conversations that a 194-yard yardage gap should never create. Kansas City did not turn it over and still needed extra time. That is the film they take on the plane: an offense that can hang 500, a defense that has not yet decided whether Denver was the identity or the Colts were.

What held up

  • Walker’s 24-carry volume and 117 yards keeping Kansas City on schedule and on the clock
  • Mahomes’ 382-yard, three-score night with Kelce as the 9-for-101 intermediate answer
  • Zero turnovers and 29 first downs, the Reid floor that still wins ugly
  • Red-zone payoffs to Worthy, Royals, and Kelce after a preseason that settled for kicks

What broke

  • Third-down offense at 5-of-13 despite 523 yards — too many drives that needed explosives instead of conversions
  • A quiet pass rush after the four-sack Denver opener, leaving Jones comfortable enough to hang 30
  • Explosive run defense, specifically Taylor’s 24-yard touchdown before half
  • A secondary that asked Bolton to post 13 tackles and still needed overtime to protect a 194-yard yardage gap

Early downs are the identity

Twenty-nine first downs and 37:00 of possession with Walker at 24-for-117 means Bieniemy can still call the game downhill. If that look travels, Mahomes does not have to be 32-of-47 every week. If it does not, Miami’s noon kick becomes a drop-back game in the heat.

Red zone scored. It did not stretch.

Worthy and Royals both punched from the 1, Kelce from 13. That is better than the preseason’s field-goal diet, but it is not the vertical pay-off the Worthy pick was sold as. Indianapolis sat on the run and made Kansas City methodical. Miami will copy that until Worthy or Rice forces a safety out of the box.

Thirteen tackles is not a pass-rush stat

Bolton cleaning up 13 times means the edges and interior did not wreck the party. Spagnuolo already owned the 30-point night. The first road game is where a quiet rush either gets a simulated-pressure answer or becomes the thing every remaining opponent writes in Sharpie.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

2-0

Kansas City is 2-0 with an offense that just posted 523 yards and a defense that just admitted 30 points was too loud. The 6-11 hangover from 2025 is no longer the headline; the first road trip is. Miami is the first time this roster has to travel with a lead in the standings and a target on the tape.

What we need to work on

Get the four-man rush home — or fake it

Arrowhead Pride’s pass-rush note after Indianapolis is the week’s real install. If Chris Jones and the edge group cannot bother a struggling Miami offense with four, Spagnuolo has to bring the simulated pressure that has always been his signature, especially with Chamarri Conner still easing back.

Third down on the road

Five-of-13 against a Colts defense you just hung 523 on is a luxury you do not take into September heat. Bieniemy needs a cleaner early-down menu so Mahomes is not 32-of-47 again just to stay on schedule.

Force the vertical pay-off

Worthy’s touchdown was a yard. Rice’s chunk from Week 2 was set up in Week 1. Miami will sit on Walker until someone wins over the top. This is the week the first-round speed has to be more than a goal-line package.

What to think about

Is noon in Miami actually a trap?

Arrowhead Pride asked it directly, and the 10.5-point road number is how Vegas says no. Trap games are not about talent; they are about energy after an overtime Sunday night and a visitor’s locker room that has already read its own press clippings.

How much secondary can you spend while Conner eases back?

The Thursday report had Conner working his way in, not sitting out the conversation. Miami’s whole existence is speed in space. If the nickel is not whole, Spagnuolo’s answer is two-deep and a clock that never leaves Walker’s hands.

What is 2-0 actually worth?

ESPN FPI is at 85.4 percent for Sunday. Polymarket is at 9.4 percent for February. Those two numbers can both be true if the defense that just allowed 30 is the version that travels.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Miami Dolphins

First road trip, noon CBS, a winless Miami team, and a 10.5-point number that will get this locker room in trouble if they believe it. The assignment is to play the Colts offensive tape and the anti-Colts defensive tape in the same afternoon.

How they match up

Kansas City wants to be a downhill, play-action, clock-eating visitor. Miami, even at 0-2, is still a speed-in-space home team that will test a secondary easing Chamarri Conner back and a pass rush that just took a quiet night off. The leverage is simple: if Walker and the offensive line can post another 150-yard rushing afternoon, Mahomes can pick his spots and Spagnuolo can live in two-deep. If the Chiefs get impatient and drop back 47 times again, the trap-game script writes itself in the heat.

Run the ball to take Miami’s crowd out of it

Walker at 24-for-117 is the road plan. Inside zone, play-action boot, and a 37-minute target keep a desperate home offense on the sideline and protect a secondary that is not fully whole.

Pressure without emptying the coverage

The Colts night said the four-man rush is not automatic. Spagnuolo has to mix zone blitz and simulated heat so Jones-level pocket comfort does not show up in a Dolphins quarterback. Do not blitz Conner’s absence into a busted coverage.

Make Worthy and Rice the reason Miami cannot sit on Kelce

Indianapolis made Kansas City methodical. Four verticals and a real go-ball are how you stop a winless team from loading the box on Walker. Rice already showed the chunk; Worthy still owes the one that changes a safety’s alignment.

Do not donate the short field

Zero turnovers beat Indianapolis. One cheap giveaway in Miami, with this defense coming off 30 allowed, is how 10.5-point favorites become the clip show.

  1. Open in 12/21 personnel inside zone to Walker — punch the first series downhill and make Miami’s front declare.
  2. First play-action boot to Kelce off that look; Mahomes’ knee is a non-story, so move the pocket and take the easy flat/dig.
  3. Early four-verticals shot to Worthy once the safety walks down on Walker; Rice runs the opposite seam so it is not a one-man vertical.
  4. If Miami’s rush gets loud, spacing screen to Walker or the back with the line leading — cash the aggressive upfield takeoff.
  5. Defensively, start in a Cover-2 shell and rally. Do not give Miami a one-on-one vertical while Conner is still easing back.
  6. On second-and-long, zone-blitz with a dropped lineman and a second-level rusher — the Colts tape said four-man is not enough.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Two weeks into 2026, the Chiefs finally look like an Andy Reid offense again — and they still needed overtime to beat a Colts team they outgained by 194 yards. Kenneth Walker III’s 24-carry, 117-yard hammer and Patrick Mahomes’ 32-of-47, 382-yard, three-touchdown night finally shared the same box score at Arrowhead, with Travis Kelce going 9-for-101 and the red zone answering on one-yard throws to Xavier Worthy and Jalen Royals. That is the good tape. The other tape is Steve Spagnuolo’s: 30 points, 24 first downs, 329 yards, and a pass rush that went quiet after a four-sack opener against Denver. Now the 2-0 Chiefs leave Missouri for the first time, a noon CBS kick in Miami against a winless Dolphins club the internet has already filed under “trap,” and the only honest question is whether Kansas City treats 30 allowed as a footnote or as the assignment.

Eric Bieniemy’s downhill identity survived the first real pushback. Walker’s volume, 152 team rush yards, 29 first downs, 37:00 of clock, and zero turnovers are how Reid wants to travel. The hitch is third down (5-of-13) and the fact that Daniel Jones and Jonathan Taylor were still living in the game in the fourth quarter. When you post 523 yards and still go to overtime, you did not lose because of talent. You leaked because the front never put the visitor on the floor.

Miami is where that leak either gets patched or becomes a personality. Arrowhead Pride has already asked the trap-game question out loud, and the market has answered it with a 10.5-point road number. That number is a compliment and a dare. The Dolphins are 0-2 and widely treated as the league’s floor, but they are also the first road locker room, the first heat game, and the first opponent that will see Kansas City as a Super Bowl seed instead of a peer. Spagnuolo has already said the Colts night was not good enough. The noon kick is the quiz.

The long-term ticket is still cautious, and it should be. Polymarket has the Chiefs at 9.4 percent to win the Super Bowl and 16.5 percent to win the AFC — a 2-0 price, not a coronation. Mahomes’ knee has left the conversation, Worthy is on the board, Rashee Rice is creating the chunk plays that Week 1’s ground game set up, and Chamarri Conner is easing back onto the injury report as the secondary tries to get whole before South Florida’s speed. The story of this week is not 2-0. It is whether the defense that just allowed 30 can travel with an offense that just found its voice.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker downhill to steal the noon crowd

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script and any early-down series @ Miami

Walker just carried 24 times for 117 and Kansas City sat on the ball for 37:00 against Indianapolis. Miami is 0-2 at home, the market has this at -10.5, and the only way a trap game starts is if the visitor gets impatient. Inside zone is how Bieniemy keeps Mahomes out of a 47-dropback heat game and how Spagnuolo keeps a Conner-easing-back secondary off the field.

Coaching point Reid and Bieniemy: stay in 12/21, make the first cut downhill, and do not check to empty just because Miami is winless. Walker has to finish runs through arm tackles; the Colts night died when the offense started hunting explosives instead of staying on schedule.

Offense

Boot Kelce off the Walker look

Situation Play-action, 1st-and-10 or 2nd-and-medium after a Walker run has pinned a safety

Mahomes’ knee is no longer the conversation, which means Reid can move him again. Indianapolis had to count Walker in the box; Miami will copy that until the boot hits Kelce in the flat or the coming-back dig. This is also how you protect a quiet pass rush on the other side — by keeping Miami’s offense watching, not playing.

Coaching point Mahomes: sell the run, get width, and take the first easy answer. Kelce works the boot-side drag-to-sit; do not hold it looking for Worthy 40 yards downfield on the first boot of the day. The Colts tape was 32-of-47 because the easy ones were there.

Offense

Make Worthy the reason they cannot sit on Walker

Situation 2nd-and-5 or 1st-and-10 vs. a single-high look after Miami walks a safety down

Worthy’s touchdown against Indianapolis was a yard, not a statement. Arrowhead Pride is still asking when the Chiefs will see the best of him, and Rashee Rice already showed the chunk that Week 1’s ground game set up. Four verticals is the call that punishes a winless team for loading the box on Walker and answers the 5-of-13 third-down problem with a shot instead of another methodical stall.

Coaching point Worthy has to win the stem and not flatten; Rice runs the opposite seam so the free safety cannot cheat. Mahomes: if the middle of the field is closed, the checkdown is alive — do not force the 382-yard hero ball in the first road game.

Offense

Mesh to cash man and get Thornton involved

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 3rd-and-8, two-minute or any down where Miami shows man

Kansas City was only 5-of-13 on third down despite 523 yards. Kelce just caught nine balls; Tyquan Thornton’s value, per Arrowhead Addict, is as the extra winner in a receiver room that still has questions. Mesh is how Bieniemy converts without asking the pass rush on the other side to keep bailing the offense out.

Coaching point Kelce and the slot run the rub with intent — contact is the point. Thornton has to finish the crosser; Walker is the outlet if Miami switches and the mesh gets muddy. Mahomes: hitch and throw, no late-over-the-middle heroics after a zero-turnover night.

Defense

Two-deep until Conner is whole

Situation Early downs and any obvious Miami shot down, especially with Conner easing back

Spagnuolo already owned the 30-point, 24-first-down Colts tape. Chamarri Conner is easing back on the Thursday report, and Miami’s entire home identity is speed in space. Cover-2 takes away the vertical you cannot afford to give a desperate 0-2 team and lets Nohl Williams and the safeties rally instead of asking a half-whole nickel to win an island.

Coaching point Corners: squat and funnel, do not get stacked. Bolton has to play the hook and not chase 13 more tackles 10 yards downfield. Spagnuolo’s point to the group is simple — 329 yards was too many, and the first road game is not where you gamble man coverage to look aggressive.

Defense

Fake the heat that did not arrive vs. Jones

Situation 2nd-and-long / 3rd-and-medium, when Miami’s protection slides to Jones

The four-man rush was quiet enough after Denver’s four-sack opener that Arrowhead Pride treated it as the week’s problem. R Mason Thomas is turning flashes into production, Omarr Norman-Lott is the interior piece fans were told not to forget, and Spagnuolo’s signature answer has always been to show heat, drop a lineman, and rush the second level. Do this enough and Miami cannot max-protect and still throw the shot that beats Cover-2.

Coaching point Sell the six-man look, drop the end into the hook, and let Bolton or the nickel come late. Jones still has to win the one-on-one that the slide leaves behind — the Colts night failed because nobody won that rep. Do not blitz Conner into a busted coverage while he is still easing back.

Coaching & adjustments

Reid vs. the trap-game clock

Andy Reid’s road opener is a noon CBS kick as a double-digit favorite. The Colts overtime tape is his best teaching tape: you can hang 523 and still make it a one-score game if you get cute. Run it, boot it, take the points.

Bieniemy’s identity has to travel

Eric Bieniemy finally got the pass game to cash what Walker started. The install this week is not a new concept menu; it is the same downhill game in heat, with third-down answers that do not require 32 completions.

Spagnuolo’s self-scout

Steve Spagnuolo already said the defense did not play well. That is the green light to play two-deep, mix zone blitz, and stop asking Nick Bolton to post 13 tackles as a lifestyle.

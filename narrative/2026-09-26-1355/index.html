Week 3 2026 Week 3 Preview Updated Saturday, Sep 26, 2026

The offense found its voice. Miami will ask if the front has one.

A 33-30 overtime win over Indianapolis produced 29 first downs, 382 from Patrick Mahomes, and 117 from Kenneth Walker III; Sunday noon in Miami is the 3-0 chase against a Dolphins defense starting rookies, and the pass rush that went quiet against Daniel Jones is the open item on Steve Spagnuolo's desk.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

W
Indianapolis Colts Week 2 · Sun, Sep 20 · 7:20 PM CT
KC 33–30

Kansas City survived Indianapolis 33-30 in overtime at Arrowhead after a 523-yard night that never felt safe. Daniel Jones hit Tyler Warren from a yard out to open the scoring, Harrison Butker answered from 46, and Mahomes found Kelce from 13 to take the first lead; the Colts then stacked field goals from Spencer Shrader (42 and 49) before Mahomes snuck Xavier Worthy in from a yard. Jonathan Taylor's 24-yard sprint put Indianapolis back in front 20-17 late in the half, Jalen Royals' 1-yard catch in the third made it 24-20, and the Chiefs still had to finish it in overtime after 37:00 of possession and zero turnovers.

The box score says dominance. The down-to-down tape says a team that moved the chains and then leaked. Twenty-nine first downs and 523 yards with Mahomes at 382 should bury a visitor; 5-of-13 on third down is why they were still playing after dark. Bieniemy got the look he wanted on early downs — Walker 24-for-117 as the identity back, 152 as a team — but the drive-ending snaps kept arriving, which is how a 37-minute possession night becomes a one-score overtime instead of a statement.

Mahomes' 32-of-47 was not garbage time. It was the offense admitting the run game, good as it was, could not close a game that the defense would not close. Kelce (9-101-1) was the adult in the room: the 13-yard score, the middle-of-the-field answers, the reason Indianapolis could not just load the box on Walker. Worthy and Royals both finishing from the 1-yard line is the other tell — Reid is using speed and the young receivers as finishers, not just as vertical window dressing. That is progress from a Week 1 night that asked whether the pass game could cash Walker's checks.

The defensive problem is schematic, not effort. Bolton's 13 tackles are a MIKE playing a lot of football because the front did not wreck the timing. Indianapolis still put up 24 first downs and 329 yards, Taylor popped the 24-yard touchdown, and Jones had enough clean drops to hit Warren in the red zone. Arrowhead Pride put the contrast on the board: four sacks of Bo Nix in the opener, then a quiet prime-time pass rush against the Colts. Spagnuolo can live with 5-of-12 on third down from the opponent. He cannot live with 30 points allowed if this is supposed to be a Super Bowl roster.

The hidden number is the turnover margin (KC 0, IND 1) plus the time of possession. Kansas City did not beat itself. Indianapolis needed a long, physical night and got one. That is credit to Shane Steichen's group as much as it is a warning for Arrowhead: when you hand a visitor 33 minutes and a 119-yard ground game, you are one missed tackle from overtime even with 382 from Mahomes.

What held up

  • Walker as a 24-carry feature back (117 yards) that forced Indianapolis to defend the run on early downs
  • Mahomes' volume night (32/47, 382, 3 TD) with zero turnovers
  • Kelce as the chain-mover and red-zone adult (9-101-1)
  • Worthy and Royals finishing from the 1-yard line instead of settling for field goals
  • Time of possession (37:00) and 29 first downs that kept the Colts' offense on the sideline longer than it wanted

What broke

  • Third-down offense at 5-of-13 despite 523 total yards
  • A pass rush that went quiet after a four-sack opener against Denver
  • Thirty points allowed, including Taylor's 24-yard touchdown run
  • A defense that put Bolton in a 13-tackle tracking contest instead of a hunt

Early-down identity is real

Walker at 24 carries and 117 yards, plus 152 as a team, is not a cameo. Bieniemy is building the offense through inside zone and play-action, and Kelce is the play-action tax Indianapolis kept paying. Miami's rookie linebacker will see that tape immediately.

Third down is the leak in a 523-yard night

Five of 13 on third down with 29 first downs means the explosives and the volume are there, and the money downs are not. That is how you possess the ball for 37 minutes and still need overtime. Cleaner protection and tighter route spacing on 3rd-and-5 will decide whether Miami becomes a romp or another grind.

The rush was a no-show when it mattered

Bolton leading the team with 13 tackles is the second-level alarm bell. If the four-man rush does not move the quarterback, Spagnuolo has to manufacture it with simulated pressure, because a noon road kick will not gift him the same Arrowhead noise he had against Indianapolis.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

2-0

The Chiefs are 2-0 with a smash-mouth opener and a high-volume overtime escape, which is a good start and a messy tape. The next Sunday is not another home night: it is a noon road kick in Miami against a defense built on rookies, with Chamarri Conner doubtful and a pass rush that has already been told the Colts game was too quiet.

What we need to work on

Manufacture a pass rush, do not wait for it

The Denver tape (four sacks of Nix) and the Colts tape do not match. Miami at noon will not create pressure for Kansas City; Spagnuolo has to bring simulated heat and second-level rushers so the young Dolphins offensive line is playing ghosts, not a static four-man.

Fix 3rd-and-medium without abandoning Walker

Five of 13 on third down is how 523 yards become overtime. Mesh, boots off inside zone, and Kelce on the money down should be the answers against a rookie-heavy Miami secondary, not another 47-attempt grind that asks Mahomes to be the entire plan.

Protect the deep middle if Conner sits

Chamarri Conner is doubtful for the first road trip. That is a Cover-2 and match-zone week, not a week to get isolated over the top in a noon stadium that will try to steal one explosive and hang around against a double-digit favorite.

What to think about

Is 2-0 identity or just two home games?

Walker and Kelce have been real. The defense has been a split tape. Miami is the first data point away from Arrowhead, and the market's 85.4 percent FPI number will look silly if the front repeats the Colts night.

How fast do you unleash Worthy as more than a 1-yard finisher?

He scored from a yard out, and Arrowhead Pride is already asking when the offense will see the best of him. Against rookie corners in Miami, the vertical and the mesh speed cut are the reasons you drafted him — not just the goal-line package.

How much simulated pressure is too much with a young Miami offense?

Spagnuolo's signature is showing heat and dropping a lineman. If he over-blitzes a team that will take the cheap screen and the checkdown, Bolton ends up with another 13-tackle night. The balance is the whole game.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Miami Dolphins

First road Sunday of 2026, noon on CBS, Kansas City -10, and a Dolphins defense starting rookies at linebacker, safety, and corner. The assignment is to make Miami's youth play NFL speed for 60 minutes and to stop this from becoming another 30-point track meet because the rush took the week off.

How they match up

Styles collide in Kansas City's favor on paper: Bieniemy's downhill inside zone and Mahomes' play-action boot game against a defense that Arrowhead Pride describes as inexperienced at the money spots, with one of Miami's only veterans a former Chiefs favorite. The leverage flips if Spagnuolo cannot get off the field. Kansas City just allowed 30 and 119 on the ground; a quiet four-man rush on the road is how a 10-point favorite wanders into a 45.5-total slog. Conner being doubtful means the secondary cannot play hero ball. The Chiefs should win with Walker, Kelce, and patience — and they can lose with explosives given up and third downs left on the field.

Run Walker at the rookie MIKE until Miami's safeties creep

Twenty-four carries against Indianapolis is the tell. Inside zone, then play-action boot, then four verts when the young safety cheats the run. Do not get bored in the first quarter just because you are a 10-point road favorite.

Attack man coverage with mesh and Kelce

Rookie corners and a rookie safety are mesh food. Kelce as the rub and the sit, Worthy as the speed crosser, checkdown as the adult outlet. This is the 3rd-and-5 fix from the Colts tape.

Create pressure without emptying the coverage

Zone-blitz and simulated looks, not all-out hero hunts. The Colts night proved the four-man rush can vanish. Drop a lineman, bring a second-level rusher, keep two deep behind it so a Conner-less secondary is not the story of the noon window.

Take the cheap yards and leave with 3-0

RB screens, boots, and first-down zone runs keep Mahomes off 47-dropback volume. Miami's youth will take bad angles; Kansas City should make them tackle. A 45.5 total and a -10 number want a professional, slightly boring win, not another overtime special.

  1. Open in under-center inside zone to Walker and make the rookie middle linebacker play downhill for three straight first-down snaps.
  2. Second series: play-action boot to Kelce in the flat/over combination off the same zone action — the Colts tape already sold it.
  3. On the first 3rd-and-5, mesh with Worthy as the speed crosser and Kelce as the rub; do not live in empty if Miami shows man.
  4. If Miami's safety sits on Walker, hit four verts and make the rookie choose between the seam and Worthy over the top.
  5. Defensively, start in a Cover-2 shell on early downs with Conner doubtful, then sprinkle zone blitz on 2nd-and-8 rather than waiting until 3rd-and-long.
  6. Keep Bolton as the tackle-and-spy piece, not the entire pass rush — use him as the add rusher, not the cleanup crew.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Kansas City is 2-0 and finally looks like an Andy Reid offense again — Kenneth Walker III leaning downhill, Travis Kelce eating in the middle, Patrick Mahomes throwing it 47 times when the game asked him to — but the tape from Arrowhead is not a coronation. The Colts hung 30, the third-down offense stalled at 5-of-13, and Steve Spagnuolo's front spent a prime-time night tackling more than it hunted. Sunday at noon in Miami is the first time this roster has left GEHA Field at Arrowhead Stadium, and it is the first time a young Dolphins defense full of rookies at linebacker, safety, and corner gets to decide whether 523 yards was identity or just a home-field sample.

Week 1 was a statement run game. Week 2 was a volume passing night that still needed overtime. That is the real 2-0 story: Eric Bieniemy can feature Walker for 24 carries and still need Mahomes at 32-of-47 because the defense would not put the game away. Indianapolis ran 119 and scored on a 24-yard Jonathan Taylor burst; Nick Bolton led the Chiefs with 13 tackles, which is another way of saying the football kept finding the second level. Arrowhead Pride already framed the problem in plain language — the pass rush must improve after a quiet Colts game, especially after the Denver opener had looked like a different unit.

Miami is the wrong place to drag that problem. It is a noon CBS kick, a first road environment, and a Dolphins defense that Arrowhead Pride notes is starting rookies at the money positions. That is a leverage gift for Kelce, Xavier Worthy, and a play-action boot game off Walker's inside zone — if Kansas City does not get cute and if Spagnuolo actually manufactures pressure instead of hoping a four-man rush suddenly appears. Chamarri Conner being doubtful only raises the cost of a coverage bust on the road.

The market already has an opinion. ESPN FPI hangs an 85.4 percent win probability on this trip and DraftKings lays Kansas City -10 on a 45.5 total, but Polymarket still prices the Chiefs at 9.4 percent to win the Super Bowl and 16.5 percent to win the AFC. That is the honest split: Sunday in Miami is supposed to be a professional beating of a young defense, and the long ticket will not move until this team proves the Colts' 30 points were an outlier. The 3-0 chase starts at noon, not in the power rankings.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker downhill at the rookie MIKE

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script in Miami, 21/12 personnel

Walker just ran 24 for 117 against Indianapolis and the team posted 152 on the ground; Arrowhead Pride's Miami preview is built on a rookie middle linebacker. This is the snap that forces the young MIKE to play downhill and pulls the rookie safety up, which is the entire Bieniemy identity after two home wins.

Coaching point Bieniemy wants zone steps and one cut — Walker presses the play-side A gap and does not bounce into a rookie who is hunting the edge. Kelce and the play-side linemen have to win first contact so Mahomes is handing it, not guessing.

Offense

Boot it when the safety creeps

Situation 2nd-and-6 after a Walker inside-zone gain, under center

One hundred fifty-two rushing yards against the Colts sold this fake; Miami's rookie safety and corner will be the first to bite the run. Mahomes on the perimeter with Kelce leaking is how you punish youth without asking the quarterback for another 47-dropback night on the road.

Coaching point Reid/Bieniemy: sell the mesh point, get Mahomes's shoulders downhill, first read is Kelce in the flat, second is the deep over. Do not hold and invite a scramble that a noon road crowd will turn into a strip.

Offense

Mesh the rookies on 3rd-and-5

Situation 3rd-and-5, 11 personnel, Miami showing man from rookie DBs

Kansas City was 5-of-13 on third down while piling up 523 yards — that is the Colts leak. Miami starting rookies at corner and safety is the week you fix it with rubs. Kelce (9-101-1) is the adult rubber; Worthy is the speed crosser who just scored from a yard out and is the reason Arrowhead Pride is asking when we see his best.

Coaching point Kelce must create the contact without a pick flag; Worthy runs through the window, does not dance. Mahomes takes the first opening and lives with the checkdown. This is not a hero-ball down on the first road trip.

Offense

Four verts when Miami loads the box

Situation 2nd-and-7, safety walked down toward Walker, Cover-3 look from a rookie deep middle

If the inside zone works, Miami's young safety will cheat. Worthy over the top and seams at the hashes are how a 382-yard Mahomes night becomes a 10-point road win instead of another overtime. A rookie safety is the exact Cover-3 stress this concept is built for.

Coaching point Seams bend to the hash, outsides run through the corner's cushion. Kelce occupies the hook so the rookie cannot drive the seam. Mahomes throws the window, not the highlight — Conner is doubtful, so do not turn this into a track meet the other way.

Defense

Two-deep because Conner is doubtful

Situation Early downs and 3rd-and-long on the road, first series in Miami

Chamarri Conner is doubtful on the first road trip, and the Colts just hung 30. Spagnuolo cannot play isolated man and hope. Two deep, five under, take away the shot, and let Bolton rally — the 13-tackle night against Indianapolis is the reminder that this unit has to tackle when it does not sack.

Coaching point Corners squat and break on the throw; safeties split the field and do not get nosy on play-action. Bolton is the hook-to-curl fitter, not a free rusher on every snap. The noon game is won by eliminating explosives, not by gambling for a strip-sack graphic.

Defense

Simulated heat after a quiet Colts rush

Situation 2nd-and-8, first half in Miami, four-man rush having won nothing

Arrowhead Pride's own headline is the scouting report: the pass rush must improve after a quiet Colts game, following a four-sack opener against Denver. Spagnuolo's signature — show heat, drop a lineman, rush the second level — is how you pressure a young Miami offense without leaving a Conner-less secondary on an island.

Coaching point Show five, bring Bolton or a nickel off the edge, drop a defensive lineman into the throwing lane Jones-style hot. Do not send the house on 2nd-and-8 and hand Miami the cheap screen. The point is confusion, not a minus-one box that recreates Taylor's 24-yard touchdown.

Worthy

Conner

Doubtful in the secondary on the first road trip

Chamarri Conner is doubtful, per the final Week 3 injury report. That is not a reason to panic-blitz. It is a reason to play two-deep, tackle well, and refuse to let one missed assignment become the noon story.

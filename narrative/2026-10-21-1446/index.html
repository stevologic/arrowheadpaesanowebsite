Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

5-0

Kansas City is 5-0, ranked No. 2 in the Arrowhead Pride Week 7 sampling, and still priced as a road underdog for Sun Oct 25, 7:20 PM CT. The offense can move the ball. It is not yet turning that movement into a touchdown rate that travels.

What we need to work on

Third down has to cash more than 3-of-10

Eleven drives and 422 yards produced 22 points. Against a Seattle team laying 2.5, another 3-of-10 afternoon on the road will hand the game to field position and the 42.5 total.

Finish red-zone and kicking details

A missed extra point and a miss from 52 turned touchdown and long-field chances into a thinner margin. Reid and Bieniemy need a call at the 1 that is not a stuffed left-guard run, and Butker needs the routine points.

Protect the takeaway math

Kansas City’s box lists two turnovers, including Mahomes to Hart. Los Angeles also had two, via Cochrane and Tranquill. A wiped interception from a roughing foul is a discipline fix for Gillotte, not a scheme trophy.

Sort the receiver snaps before the road

Armstrong’s 42 snaps and 1 catch, Worthy’s 7-for-73, and Kelce’s 4 catches are the usage facts. The room has to be a plan, not a snap-count experiment, once Seattle crowds the box against Walker.

What to think about

Is the ugly win the identity or the ceiling?

Arrowhead Pride’s mailbag asks if the current approach is sustainable. Arrowhead Addict says the club has remastered winning ugly after last year’s 6-11. Both can be true for five games. Week 7 is the test.

Model versus the number

ESPN FPI has Kansas City at 52.8 percent. DraftKings has Seattle -2.5. That split is the honest edge: respect the building, do not pretend the market has already conceded the trip.

How much Gilman, and how much Bolton?

Arrowhead Addict tracked Alohi Gilman from 90 percent of the defensive snaps in Week 4 to 49 percent in Week 6. Bolton just posted an 11-tackle game. Spagnuolo’s sub package on the road should be a choice, not a drift.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Seattle Seahawks

Win the early-down run, steal a third down, and keep the score ahead of a Seattle team the market still favors by 2.5. Kickoff is Sun Oct 25, 7:20 PM CT on NBC at Seattle. No result exists yet.

How they match up

Kansas City brings a 5-0 run-leaning offense — 150 rushing yards and a 23-of-30 quarterback with 0 sacks last time out — into a loud road building where Seattle is laying the number. The Chiefs’ defense just held a division offense to 73 on the ground and 6 points, with a leak on one 57-yard shot. The leverage is Kansas City’s downhill carries versus a home favorite that will want to squeeze third down, where Kansas City was 3-of-10.

Make Walker the first-down answer

Inside zone and the occasional Mahomes keep keep Seattle’s second level fit-conscious. Team rush has to stay a team number, with Johnson’s 5-touch changeup available so the closer is not asked to be the whole plan.

Manufacture third-down completions

Mesh and a running-back screen give Mahomes answers that do not require a 40-yard shot. Worthy and Kelce are the separators. Armstrong has to be a route, not just a snap.

Two-deep, then a called zone blitz

Cover 2 takes away the Johnston-style shot that broke the Chargers tape. A simulated pressure is a call Spagnuolo can dial — show heat, drop a lineman — not a retroactive label on Cochrane’s interception.

Bank the points Butker can make

A 42.5 total does not require a track meet. It does require the extra point and the makeable field goal. Leave the 52-yard hero kick for a last resort.

  1. Open under center with Walker on inside zone and force a light box before any boot.
  2. On a confirmed run look, boot Mahomes to the perimeter with Kelce as the flat option and Worthy clearing.
  3. Third-and-medium: mesh Kelce and a wide receiver, Johnson as the checkdown.
  4. Second-and-long after a sack or a penalty: spacing screen to Walker so the 3 hits from last game do not become a dropback habit.
  5. Defensively, start in Cover 2 on standard downs and rally Bolton to the ball.
  6. Call a zone blitz on a Seattle second-and-medium, with a defensive lineman dropping and the second level rushing — a call, not a story about last week’s takeaways.
Last game

What the tape said

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

W
Los Angeles Chargers Week 6 · Sun, Oct 18 · 3:25 PM CT
KC 22–6

At Arrowhead, Kansas City led 3-0 on a 44-yard field goal at Q1 11:20, then Kenneth Walker III ran up the middle 33 yards for a touchdown at Q1 7:21. Harrison Butker’s extra point was no good, wide left, so the score was 9-0. Los Angeles answered with a 23-yard field goal at Q1 1:33. Los Angeles kicked a 30-yard field goal at Q3 10:44. Butker’s 30-yard field goal at Q4 3:52 made the final 22-6. Los Angeles did not score a touchdown.

The rush tape is the cleanest part of the offense. Walker’s 33-yard score was an up-the-middle cut with the extra point missed, so a touchdown drive produced six. His other listed rushing scores sit on the leader line — 19 carries, 128 yards, two touchdowns — inside a team rush of 150 on 27 attempts. Emmett Johnson’s line is separate: 3 rushes and 2 catches, 5 touches. Patrick Mahomes added 5 rushes, including a 10-yard gain off the left end in the second quarter and an 8-yard scramble off the right end in the fourth. That is quarterback movement, not a claim that any throw was play-action.

Mahomes’ dropbacks were clean in the pocket count and messy in the finishing. He was 23-of-30, 0 sacks, with 3 quarterback hits recorded against him, 272 yards, and a rating of 89.9. The interception was a short-right throw intended for Andrew Armstrong, taken by C. Hart for a touchback. Xavier Worthy led the receivers with 7 catches for 73 yards. Travis Kelce had 4 catches. Cyrus Allen had 2 catches, including a 23-yard gain in the fourth quarter. Armstrong played a heavy snap count — Arrowhead Addict has the figure at 42 — and finished with 1 catch. The passing game moved the ball. It did not finish enough drives with touchdowns.

Steve Spagnuolo’s unit held Los Angeles to 259 yards and 73 on the ground. Nick Bolton led the tackle sheet with 11, a total Arrowhead Pride ties to a franchise record for games with 10 or more tackles. Jack Cochrane intercepted Herbert on a short-right throw intended for KeAndre Lambert-Smith and returned it 48 yards. L. Sneed forced a fumble on a short-right completion to Lambert-Smith; Drue Tranquill recovered. Chamarri Conner was credited with 1 sack. Kansas City recorded 9 quarterback hits. A separate second-quarter interception by Christian Roland-Wallace was wiped by Ashton Gillotte’s roughing-the-passer penalty and is not a takeaway. Quentin Johnston’s 57-yard catch to the 1 shows the shell can still lose a deep ball; Los Angeles then kicked, they did not punch it in.

The hidden scoreboard is penalties and kicks. Travis Kelce was flagged for an illegal block above the waist on the opening series. Trent Smith’s holding wiped a play. Kingsley Suamataia’s holding wiped another; the play-by-play notes he had returned. Gillotte’s roughing gave Herbert a drive back. Chris Jones was flagged for roughing late. Butker missed the extra point and later missed a 52-yard field goal wide right. A fourth-quarter Kansas City series with K. Tonga reported eligible ended on downs when Walker went left guard to the 1 for no gain. Special teams also had a punt loose ball forced by T. Dye that was reversed and recovered by the Chargers’ C. Murphy. Kansas City’s box still lists two turnovers. One of them is the Hart interception. The other is not a play this wire lets us rename.

What held up

  • Walker’s up-the-middle and perimeter carries, especially the 33-yard touchdown and the 16-yard run in the fourth quarter
  • Mahomes at 23-of-30 with 0 sacks and 272 net yards, matching the team passing total
  • Run defense: Los Angeles 73 rushing yards on 22 attempts, with Bolton at 11 tackles
  • Cochrane’s interception and the Sneed-forced, Tranquill-recovered fumble

What broke

  • 3-of-10 on third down and a fourth-quarter snap that died at the 1
  • The missed extra point and the missed 52-yard field goal
  • Mahomes’ interception intended for Armstrong
  • Gillotte’s roughing penalty, which erased a Roland-Wallace interception
  • The 57-yard completion to Johnston, which put Los Angeles on the doorstep before a field goal

Walker was the points

Team rush was 150. Walker was 19 carries for 128 and two touchdowns, with 21 touches once his 2 catches are included. The 33-yard score at Q1 7:21 was the explosive, and the missed extra point kept a touchdown drive at six.

Third down and the tee lagged the yardage

Kansas City was 3-of-10 on third down and scored 22 on 422 yards. Butker made kicks of 44, 50, and 30, missed the extra point, and missed from 52. That is how a yardage win becomes a one-score-looking cushion that was actually 16 at the end.

The takeaways have names

Cochrane is the interceptor on the live Herbert throw. Sneed forced the Lambert-Smith fumble and Tranquill recovered it. Do not move those credits.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The 2026 Chiefs are 5-0 because the identity run and the complementary defense keep cashing games the scoreboard does not dress up. Against the Chargers, Kansas City posted 22 first downs, 150 rushing yards on 27 attempts, and 272 net passing yards for 422 total, then won 22-6 while converting 3 of 10 third downs and giving the ball away twice.

Kenneth Walker III carried the rush line: 19 carries, 128 yards, and two touchdowns inside a team total of 150. Patrick Mahomes was 23-of-30 for 272 yards with a rating of 89.9, zero sacks, and one interception. Los Angeles managed 15 first downs, 73 rushing yards, and 186 net passing yards. Justin Herbert was 22-of-37 with a 63.3 rating. The clock was close — Kansas City 30:26 on 11 drives, Los Angeles 29:34 on 12 — so the separation was explosives and the end zone, not a long possession monopoly.

Do not blend those clocks. The Chargers afternoon was the higher-yardage, lower-scoring version of the same idea Arrowhead Addict has been calling winning ugly, and Arrowhead Pride’s mailbag is right to ask whether 22 points on 422 is a sustainable weekly diet.

The schedule argument starts now. Arrowhead Pride notes the slate gets tougher beginning in Week 7, and the number agrees in a split way: Seattle is laying 2.5 on a 42.5 total while ESPN FPI still has Kansas City at 52.8 percent. National rankings have the club at No. 2. The film says the staff must turn third down and the kicking game into points before Lumen Field asks them to do it on the road.

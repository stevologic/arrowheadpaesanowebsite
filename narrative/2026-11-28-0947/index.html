Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

8-3

Kansas City is 8-3 with a Thursday trip to Los Angeles and a Buffalo tape that does not look like the Arizona win. The defense can still wreck a quarterback. The offense and special teams just failed to protect a 10-7 lead.

What we need to work on

Third down before the Rams front

2-of-11 will not survive a one-score road price. Bieniemy has to steal first downs with Johnson's inside zone and Kelce's intermediate catches so Mahomes is not dropping back into six-hit volume again.

The kicking operation

Arrowhead Pride and Arrowhead Addict both put the Buffalo loss on special teams.

What to think about

Which offensive identity is real

Against Arizona, Kansas City had 435 yards, 27 first downs, 7-of-13 on third down, and 32:26 of possession. Reid and Bieniemy have to decide which script they trust on Thursday, without pretending the weather erases the down-and-distance misses.

How hard to lean on Johnson

He had 21 touches and the touchdown. That is a carry load, not a cameo. The question is whether Los Angeles sees the same inside runs Buffalo saw, and whether the screen game has to take some of the six hits off Mahomes.

Snap share after a messy game

Arrowhead Addict noted back-to-back games that make snap share hard to read, and Arrowhead Pride's count still had Xavier Worthy leading the receivers. The catch sheet that traveled was Kelce, Johnson, Gray, Rice, Downs, Royals, and Cyrus Allen.

Looking ahead

How Bieniemy and Spagnuolo attack a one-score Rams price

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Los Angeles Rams

Thursday is the Rams, at Los Angeles, on Prime Video, Thu Dec 3, 7:15 PM CT. Kansas City is a 1.5-point underdog on a 44.5 total. FPI says 45.0 to 54.7. The plan is to steal the clock Buffalo just stole, and to keep Spagnuolo's shell from having to play 37 minutes again.

How they match up

McVay's offense is built to stress shells with timing and motion; Spagnuolo just showed he can take the deep ball away from a star quarterback and live with a run-heavy opponent if the tackling is sound. Bieniemy's problem is the other way: Mahomes was efficient enough to post 174 yards on 29 throws, but 2-of-11 and six hits say the structure around him was late. Johnson is the early-down answer. Kelce is the proven catch-point answer from the Buffalo tape. The Rams get the venue, the slight number, and a Chiefs special-teams unit that just lost a tied game on kicks. Kansas City gets the better recent defensive performance against a mobile quarterback and a zero-giveaway offense that can still hit Kelce in the middle of the field.

Win first down so third down is not 2-of-11 again

Johnson up the middle was the listed run. Stay on that on early downs, then use mesh and the screen when the Rams declare a rush. A 23:05 possession line cannot be the travel plan.

Cover 2 as the base answer, zone pressure as the change-up

Buffalo's 64 net passing yards came with two interceptions and one Bolton sack.

Protect the kicks

A 44.5 total says field goals matter. Butker's miss and Buffalo's 54- and 57-yard makes are the warning. Do not spot Los Angeles a short field with a punt operation or a penalty on the kick unit.

  1. Open with under-center inside zone to Johnson and force the Rams to declare a front before Mahomes throws.
  2. On 2nd-and-medium, mesh Kelce and Rice with Gray as the sit, Johnson as the checkdown.
  3. If the rush shows six-hit intent again, throw the screen to Johnson and let the line get downhill.
  4. One four-verticals shot off a clean pocket to Kelce or Cyrus Allen, not a habit.
  5. Spagnuolo in Cover 2 on early downs, rally fits to the run.
  6. Zone blitz on 2nd-and-6 to 2nd-and-8: drop a lineman, rush the second level, and do not treat it as the reason Gilman or Williams intercepted in Buffalo.
Last game

Highmark held the ball, Kansas City held the lead once

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

L
Buffalo Bills Week 12 · Thu, Nov 26 · 7:20 PM CT
KC 10–13

Kansas City lost 13-10 at Highmark Stadium after Buffalo's 5-yard touchdown at Q2 14:32, a Kansas City 26-yard field goal at Q2 0:02, Emmett Johnson's 1-yard touchdown at Q3 11:28, and Buffalo field goals of 54 and 57 yards. The Bills possessed the ball for 36:55; Kansas City had it for 23:05.

The score sequence is the game. Buffalo led 7-0 on Cook's 5-yard run up the middle. Kansas City closed the half with a 26-yard field goal. Johnson's 1-yard score put Kansas City ahead 10-7. Buffalo tied it at 10 with a 54-yard field goal at Q4 11:58 and won it with a 57-yard kick at Q4 0:00. Butker's 46-yard attempt at Q4 6:01, with the score 10-10, was no good, wide right. Arrowhead Addict framed Tyler Bass's makes against that miss; the yardage on the made kicks belongs to Buffalo's scoring lines.

Spagnuolo won the passing matchup and lost the clock. Allen was 10-of-21 with an 18.3 rating and 64 net passing yards. Gilman intercepted the short left throw intended for Knox, with Karlaftis on the play, and the result was a touchback. Williams intercepted the short right throw intended for Shakir at the Kansas City 13 and returned it to the 20. Buffalo still ran 43 times for 166 yards. Cook had 32 rushes and one catch. Allen added seven rushes, including a 9-yard scramble to the right end. That is how a team with two turnovers can still own 17 first downs, go 9-of-16 on third down, and run 11 drives to Kansas City's 10.

Bieniemy's night was a grind, not a yardage night. Johnson had 21 touches — 17 rushes and four catches — for 72 rushing yards and the touchdown, with several listed runs up the middle, including a 6-yarder, a 3-yarder, a loss of 2, and a 7-yarder after Nourzad reported eligible. Mahomes was 16-of-29 for 174 yards, took one sack, absorbed six quarterback hits, scrambled once to the right end for 2 yards on an eligible-Nourzad snap, and recovered two aborted fumbles himself. Kelce caught four passes for 71 yards, including 24 and 21 on the late first-half drive. Cyrus Allen's 19-yard catch was the other chunk. Rice had two catches; a late third-down catch was overturned, and Reid told Arrowhead Pride it was not clear and obvious. Net passing was 167. Team rushing was 74 on 20 attempts. Total offense was 241. Third down was 2-of-11. Turnovers were zero.

The flags and the kicking game decided a one-score game more than the two interceptions did. Suamataia was flagged for holding and for unnecessary roughness. Nourzad and Kelce had plays wiped. Cochrane's ineligible-downfield kick accompanied an Araiza fumble that Araiza recovered. Kansas City also had a false start on Johnson and an offside on Jones. Arrowhead Pride called the special-teams cost real. That is the tape Reid takes to Los Angeles, not the 435-yard Arizona afternoon.

What held up

  • Allen held to 10-of-21, 64 net passing yards, and an 18.3 rating, with interceptions by Gilman and N. Williams.
  • Zero giveaways, and Mahomes recovered both of his aborted fumbles.
  • Johnson's 17-carry, 72-yard line and the 1-yard touchdown that produced the 10-7 lead.
  • Kelce's 24- and 21-yard catches on the drive that ended in the 26-yard field goal.

What broke

  • 2-of-11 on third down, 13 first downs, 241 total yards, and 23:05 of possession.
  • Six quarterback hits on Mahomes and a sack, with holding and roughness flags on the line.
  • A late Rice catch overturned, which Reid said was not clear and obvious.

The clock was the Bills' weapon

Buffalo held the ball for 36:55 to Kansas City's 23:05 and ran 43 times for 166 yards. Cook's 32 carries were the game script. Kansas City's two takeaways did not flip the possession math.

Johnson was the offense

Twenty-one touches, 72 rushing yards, and the 1-yard touchdown. Kelce's four catches for 71 yards were the passing answer. Everyone else on the listed catch sheet was at one to three touches.

The miss and the two long kicks

Butker's 46-yard attempt was wide right at 10-10. Special teams, not a giveaway, closed the margin.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The Los Angeles Rams are the assignment, and the number is thin: DraftKings has them minus 1.5 on a 44.5 total, Prime Video has Thu Dec 3, 7:15 PM CT, and ESPN FPI gives Kansas City only a 45.0 percent chance against 54.7 for Los Angeles. Andy Reid's club is 8-3 because it has won the games it was supposed to win, then lost a 13-10 rock fight at Buffalo in which the Bills kept the ball for 36:55 and Kansas City managed 241 yards.

That Highmark result is context, not the lead. Buffalo rushed 43 times for 166 yards, James Cook carried 32 times, and Josh Allen finished 10-of-21 for an 18.3 rating with 64 net passing yards. Steve Spagnuolo's group forced two interceptions — A. Gilman on a short left throw intended for Knox, N. Williams on a short right throw intended for Shakir — and Nick Bolton was credited with the sack. The offense did not match that control: Patrick Mahomes was 16-of-29 for 174 yards and a 73.1 rating, one sack, six quarterback hits, 167 net passing yards, and Emmett Johnson's 17 carries produced 72 yards and the only Kansas City touchdown.

The fixes that travel to Los Angeles are specific. Kansas City was 2-of-11 on third down, posted 13 first downs on 10 drives, and lived with 23:05 of possession. Eric Bieniemy does not need a philosophy speech. He needs early-down efficiency so Mahomes is not living on 3rd-and-long behind a line that just drew holding and false-start flags from Suamataia, Nourzad, and Johnson.

Kansas City's edge is the quarterback and a defense that just smothered a mobile starter. Los Angeles's edge is the price, the venue, and a Chiefs offense that has to prove the Arizona yardage — 317 net passing, 118 rushing, 7-of-13 on third down — was the identity, and Buffalo was the weather, not the regression.

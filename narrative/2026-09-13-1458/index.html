Jones is cleared. Simmons is not. The red zone still owes a touchdown.

The preseason finale produced 21 first downs, 155 rush yards, and zero touchdowns; Week 1 is Denver on ESPN with Patrick Mahomes back, Josh Simmons and Chamarri Conner already ruled out, and Chris Jones cleared after a calf scare.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

T
Seattle Seahawks Pre Week 4 · Sat Aug 29
KC 9–9

Kansas City controlled the preseason finale at Arrowhead and still could not score a touchdown. The Chiefs out-gained Seattle 353–266, won first downs 21–13, converted 9-of-17 third downs, ran for 155 yards, and held the ball for 33:12, then left with three Harrison Butker field goals (28, 24, 19) and a 9–9 tie after Jalen Milroe hit Nick Vannett from 4 yards in the third quarter and Jason Myers missed the extra point.

The box score is a possession clinic that never got paid. Twenty-one first downs and a 155–54 rush edge mean Kansas City won early downs and the line of scrimmage. Brashard Smith’s 16 carries for 72 yards were the clearest identity snap of the night: inside-zone, one cut, stay on schedule. That is how you sit on a team for 33 minutes. It is also how you get greedy in the red zone and then settle, because the scoring sequence never changed — Butker from 28, Butker from 24, Butker from 19. A 19-yard field goal is not a field-position win. It is a failed trip to the doorstep.

Garrett Nussmeier’s 22-of-34 for 198 yards and an interception is preseason tape, not a Mahomes verdict, but it still tells you what this offense looked like without a closer. Andrew Armstrong led the receivers (5 for 54) on a night with no explosive finishing play. The passing game moved the chains and then died on the paint. When the only opponent touchdown is a 4-yard dump to a tight end and the PAT fails, you should win the game. Kansas City instead kicked a 19-yarder in the fourth quarter and shook hands at 9–9.

Defensively the finale was complementary, not dominant. Seattle managed 266 yards and 4-of-11 on third down with only 54 rush yards, and R Mason Thomas chipped in a sack. That is a Spagnuolo-looking skeleton: take away the run, live with some net passing yards (212), and trust the offense to finish. The offense did not finish. Carry that into a real Monday night against Payton and the 43.5 total starts to look like a warning, not a coincidence.

What held up

  • Early-down run identity: 155 rush yards, Brashard Smith’s 16-for-72, and a 21-first-down clock drain.
  • Third-down efficiency (9-of-17) that kept Seattle’s offense on the sideline.
  • Run defense that held Seattle to 54 rushing yards and 13 first downs.
  • Complementary field position that produced three Butker makes, including from 19.

What broke

  • Red-zone finishing: 353 yards, zero offensive touchdowns, a 19-yard field goal as the last score.
  • Explosive passing: Nussmeier’s 198 yards came without a closer and with an interception.
  • Payoff on 33:12 of possession — a control game that still tied 9–9.

The run game is real enough to build a week around

One hundred fifty-five rush yards and 33:12 of possession are not a mirage, even in Week 4 of the preseason. If Bieniemy is going to protect a replacement left tackle in Week 1, this is the tape he wants: early-down zone, Smith as a volume back, and third downs that stay manageable.

The red zone is still a kicking operation

Three field goals, including a 19-yarder, against a preseason Seattle defense is the loudest problem on the 2026 tape. Payton will take that bet every time. Touchdowns, not first downs, are the only way a 2.5-point home favorite cashes.

The closer was not on the field

Nussmeier’s 198 yards and pick are a control-game stat line. Monday night is Mahomes, and the bar on that comeback should stay honest: the structure from this tape can travel, the finishing from this tape cannot.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

0-0 (coming off 6-11)

Kansas City is 0-0 with a 6-11 hangover, a functional run tape, and a Week 1 injury report that already removed the left tackle and a starting safety. The opener is a home favorite on ESPN, not a referendum on January, but the market’s 5.7 percent championship price will not move if Monday night looks like another three-kick night.

What we need to work on

Finish the Seattle red-zone tape

Twenty-one first downs and a 19-yard field goal is the install that matters this week. Payton will trade field goals all night. Bieniemy has to have a goal-line and 5-yard-line answer that is not Butker, or the 43.5 total becomes a trap.

Protect Mahomes without Josh Simmons

Reid ruled Simmons out. The preseason run game is now a protection plan, not just an identity. If this turns into obvious dropback on second-and-long, Denver will hunt the vacant blind side and the Mahomes comeback becomes a survival drill.

Replace Chamarri Conner without giving up the top

A starting safety is out for a Monday nighter against a Payton script that loves play-action and the deep over. Spagnuolo has to live in two-deep answers and rally-to-tackle football, not hero coverage from a body short.

Use a cleared Chris Jones, don’t empty him

The calf scare was real enough to limit him Friday; the final report cleared him. That is not a license to play him like a 70-snap every-down piece in Week 1. Win the interior, then get him off the field.

What to think about

How low is the Mahomes bar on night one?

History on similar quarterback returns says the expectation should be structure, not fireworks. If the offense looks like the Seattle tape — first downs, no paint — that is a problem. If Mahomes is merely clean and on time, that is a win the market has not priced.

Did cutting Esa Pole leave them with no left-tackle floor?

Pole started down the stretch last season, hit the Washington practice squad, and the left-tackle room is now a Week 1 nightmare with Simmons already out. Signing a veteran just to sign one (D.J. Humphries is the name in the rumor mill) is not automatically the fix. The fix might be the run game and the boot.

Is a Wild Card the honest 2026 ceiling until this roster proves otherwise?

Chiefs fans in the Week 1 Reacts poll already see a Wild Card seed. That matches a 5.7 percent Super Bowl ticket better than any glory narrative. Beat Denver at home and the conversation can wait a week. Lose the trenches on Monday night and the 6-11 hangover is still the story.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Monday night at Arrowhead is an AFC West blood match on a 2.5-point home number and a 43.5 total: Kansas City is a modest favorite that has to prove the Seattle run tape travels and that Mahomes can finish drives with the left tackle and a starting safety already scratched.

How they match up

Styles collide in the trenches, not in space. Kansas City just ran for 155 and sat on the ball for 33 minutes; Denver, with Sean Payton scripting, will try to make this a dropback and play-action game that hunts a replacement left tackle and a secondary missing Chamarri Conner. The Chiefs’ leverage is early-down zone, a cleared Chris Jones collapsing the middle, and Spagnuolo’s simulated pressure. Denver’s leverage is the vacant blind side, the missing safety, and any series that becomes second-and-long. The low total says both staffs already know this.

Stay in early-down run so the backup tackle is blocking downhill

The Seattle tape is the script: inside zone, Smith as a volume piece, 21 first downs. Every designed dropback on first down is a gift to Payton. Make Denver defend the run, then boot Mahomes off the left edge instead of asking a replacement to pass-set against a wide-9 look.

Move the pocket and take the easy throws

Mahomes is back, but the comeback bar is structure. Play-action boots, mesh, and screens are how you keep him clean without Simmons. If Bieniemy gets stubborn about seven-step shots, the 9–9 red zone will look generous.

Two-deep answers without Conner, Jones as the closer

Do not play hero coverage a safety short. Cover-2 shells take away Payton’s deep over, and a cleared Jones plus zone-blitz looks can wreck max-protect. R Mason Thomas already showed a pass-rush chip in the finale; use it in spots so Jones is not the entire plan.

Red zone is the only stat that cashes -2.5

A 19-yard field goal lost the Seattle game as a statement. Against Denver, Butker can still win a 9–6 slog, but the market’s 43.5 and FPI’s 58.0 percent both assume Kansas City is the more functional offense. Function without touchdowns is how 6-11 starts over.

  1. Open in under-center 12/21 personnel and run inside zone to the closed side so the replacement left tackle is covered up, not isolated.
  2. Second-series play-action boot to Mahomes’ right — away from the vacant blind side — with a leaking tight end and a deep over as the alert.
  3. On obvious passing downs, get to an RB screen before Denver can pin the edge rusher on the backup tackle.
  4. Red-zone first down: run it again. Do not throw a 19-yard field goal on the first trip.
  5. Defensively, first two series in a Cover-2 shell; do not give Payton a one-high look while Conner is in street clothes.
  6. Third-and-medium: show double-A gap, drop a lineman, send a second-level rusher — Jones as the interior threat, not the only rusher.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The 6-11 tape is closed. What Kansas City actually put on film before the real snaps start is a 9–9 possession game that won first downs and lost the paint: 353 yards, 33:12 of clock, three Harrison Butker field goals, and a red-zone offense that treated the 5-yard line like a wall. Week 1 does not care about that control. It cares whether Patrick Mahomes, returning on a short home number against Sean Payton, can turn the same downhill identity into touchdowns while Andy Reid is already without his starting left tackle and a starting safety.

That is the whole opener in one sentence. ESPN FPI still leans Kansas City at 58.0 percent at GEHA Field at Arrowhead Stadium, and DraftKings has them -2.5 at a 43.5 total, which is a trench number, not a shootout number. Polymarket is colder: 5.7 percent to win Super Bowl 61 and 10.0 percent to win the AFC, a market that has not forgotten last year’s 6-11 and will not move on a preseason tie that never reached the end zone.

The injury report is the plot twist the 9–9 tape did not have to survive. Andy Reid has declared Josh Simmons and Chamarri Conner out for the Broncos, which means the first real dropback of 2026 is coming with a replacement on the blind side and a secondary that is already a body short. Chris Jones’s calf put a scare into the week — he was limited Friday — but the final report cleared him, so Steve Spagnuolo at least gets his interior closer for an old-school AFC West blood match.

Eric Bieniemy’s job this week is not clever. It is to keep Mahomes on schedule so the vacant left tackle is blocking downhill, not pass-setting in obvious third-and-long against a Payton script. Spagnuolo’s job is the mirror image: two-deep answers without Conner, Jones as the collapse piece, and enough simulated pressure to keep Denver from turning this into a 40-pass night that hunts the backup tackle and the missing safety. The Colts and a primetime home date sit in Week 2. None of that matters if Monday night becomes another field-goal slog.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Early-down inside zone, closed side

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script vs. Denver — keep the replacement left tackle covered up

Kansas City just ran for 155 yards and held the ball 33:12 against Seattle with Brashard Smith as the volume back. Josh Simmons is out, so every first-down dropback is a gift to Sean Payton. Under-center zone to the closed side lets Eric Bieniemy put Mahomes on schedule and hides the vacant blind side inside a downhill run instead of a pass set.

Coaching point Bieniemy: zone steps, one cut, do not bounce it to the left edge. Smith has to press the LOS and finish north-south so the backup tackle is down-blocking, not chasing. Reid should stay in 12/21 until Denver proves it can stop the Seattle tape.

Offense

Play-action boot away from the vacant side

Situation 2nd-and-6 after a zone run, first two series vs. Payton

Simmons is scratched and Mahomes is on a comeback clock. Booting him to his right — away from the left tackle — turns the Seattle run fake into protection and gives him a simple high-low on the perimeter instead of a seven-step drop behind a replacement. Payton will have the edge set for dropback; he is slower to the naked boot if the zone fake is real.

Coaching point Mahomes: sell the mesh point, get depth, and take the leak in the flat if the end crashes. Do not reset and hunt a hero ball into two-deep. The deep over is the alert, not the first read, until the blind side proves it can survive.

Offense

Mesh vs. the man look Payton will test

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 7, middle of the field, Mahomes’ first real money down

A returning Mahomes does not need a 2022 shot chart on night one, and a vacant left tackle does not need a five-man protection that loses. Mesh gives him rubs, a sit, and Brashard Smith as the outlet — the same easy-offense philosophy as the 9-of-17 third-down tape, except now the closer is actually in the game. Andrew Armstrong’s 5-for-54 in the finale is the underneath target this concept was built for.

Coaching point Bieniemy: put the mesh on the hashes and tell Mahomes the checkdown is a win. No late-in-the-progressions heroics into Conner’s vacated deep middle. If Denver sits the rub, Smith is the throw, not the seam.

Offense

RB screen when they pin the backup tackle

Situation 2nd-and-long or 3rd-and-medium after Denver wins a pass-rush down

Payton will hunt the left edge the first time Kansas City is behind the chains. The answer is not to max-protect a replacement tackle; it is to throw the ball to Smith in space and make Denver’s rushers run to the boundary. That also keeps Mahomes from holding it, which is the entire point of a comeback opener with Simmons and Conner already out.

Coaching point Get the left side out in front of Smith — this only works if the linemen treat it like a run. Mahomes: catch, fake, and dump. Do not pump yourself into a hit. One explosive screen is worth more than another 19-yard field goal.

Defense

Cover-2 shell without Conner

Situation Early downs and any Payton play-action look, first half

Chamarri Conner is out. One-high against Sean Payton on Monday night is how you donate the deep over the Seattle tape never had to cover. Two deep, five under takes away the shot, lives with the underneath completion, and asks the Chiefs to tackle — the same complementary skeleton that held Seattle to 13 first downs and 54 rush yards.

Coaching point Spagnuolo: no hero post safety. Corners squat, safeties split the hashes, and the Mike has to be right on the play-action fake or Payton will sit the tight end in the hole. Jones can still rush; the coverage does not have to.

Defense

Simulated pressure with Jones as the interior lie

Situation 3rd-and-4 to 8, second half, once Payton is in obvious protection

Chris Jones is cleared after the calf scare, which means Spagnuolo finally has the player who makes the fake look real. Show double-A, drop a lineman, send a second-level rusher, and force Payton to account for Jones while the replacement pieces (including R Mason Thomas as a rotational rusher off the Seattle sack) eat a one-on-one. Do this after the Cover-2 has taken away the shot, not instead of it.

Coaching point Spags: Jones is the collapse, not the every-down stunt piece — the calf is cleared, not forgotten. The dropped lineman has to occupy the throwing lane Mahomes’ counterpart wants on the hot. If the adders are late, this becomes a bust with Conner already in street clothes.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Andy Reid vs. Sean Payton

Reid called it an old-school AFC West blood match, and the 43.5 total agrees. This is not a gadget night. Reid’s edge is the run-and-boot script; Payton’s edge is the vacant tackle and the missing safety. Whoever wins early downs wins the 2.5.

Eric Bieniemy’s first real script

The OC inherits a 155-yard run tape and a left tackle already ruled out. His Week 1 grade is simple: keep Mahomes on schedule, finish the red zone the Seattle finale refused to finish, and do not ask a replacement to pass-set 40 times.

Steve Spagnuolo without Chamarri Conner

The signature zone blitz still plays with Jones cleared. The coverage behind it cannot. Two-deep first, pressure second, or Payton will throw the shot the preseason finale never tested.

Harrison Butker is not the red-zone plan

Three makes, including from 19, kept them in a 9–9 tie. If he is the leading scorer again on Monday night, Kansas City did not solve the only problem on the tape.

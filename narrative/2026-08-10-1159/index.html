The story so far

Always looking ahead

A 6-11 hangover does not vanish because camp opens at Missouri Western. Andy Reid’s staff is running a rebuild-in-place: Patrick Mahomes is cleared after the December knee injury and detailing the last pre-camp steps, but the head coach is already leaning toward holding him out of the preseason opener against the Rams at Arrowhead. That puts the spotlight where it belongs—on the roster’s soft spots and the kids who have to fill them before the real Sundays arrive.

The receiver room is the loudest problem. Xavier Worthy has missed a week with a shoulder issue and Rashee Rice is still working his way back, which leaves Eric Bieniemy’s offense short on proven separators just as preseason reps become currency. Noah Gray is finally in line for a clear answer on his role in the passing game, and the staff will have to manufacture easy buttons—screens, mesh, play-action boots—until the perimeter stabilizes. You cannot scheme around a thin WR group for 17 games; you can only bridge it for a month.

On the other side of the ball, Steve Spagnuolo’s front is the actual bet on 2026. First-round defensive tackle Peter Woods carries a ceiling-floor gap that will define early-down stops, and fourth-year end Felix Anudike-Uzomah is drawing real praise from coaches and teammates for his work. If Woods wins snaps and Anudike-Uzomah finally turns the corner, Spags can play his simulated-pressure menu without selling out. If not, the back end will live in third-and-long too often.

Interior line continuity is one of the few quiet wins: every new guard contract around the league makes the Trey Smith extension look smarter, and camp notes already have Kahlil Benson taking right-guard reps in full pads. That is how you protect a rehabbing MVP and install downhill zone without rewriting the call sheet. Rookie Garrett Nussmeier, for his part, has said he will be ready for preseason work—exactly the insurance you want when Reid is managing Mahomes’ pitch count.

Look ahead, not sideways. The Polymarket board has Kansas City at 9.5% to win the AFC and 6.6% to win the Super Bowl in 2027’s cycle—underdog numbers that match a 6-11 roster still hunting identity. The exhibition against the Rams is not about scoreboard; it is about which young linemen, receivers, and backups seize the depth chart before the opener. Win the battles in St. Joe, and the market moves. Lose them, and 6-11 becomes a trend instead of a floor.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Downhill starter: Inside Zone base

Situation Preseason 1st-and-10 vs. Rams base look — establish identity while ones mix in

With Trey Smith locked in and Kahlil Benson taking right-guard reps in full pads, Kansas City can live in under-center zone even if the WR room is banged up. Worthy’s shoulder and Rice’s ramp make perimeter explosives less reliable; winning early downs on the ground protects a managed Mahomes and feeds play-action later.

Coaching point Bieniemy wants zone steps and a single cut—Smith must own the double to the backer, Benson finish the combo, and the RB press the heel-line without bouncing. No hero cuts in August; film the landmark.

Offense

Boot off zone action

Situation Play-action on early downs vs. Rams when they squat on the run after zone looks

Mahomes thrives moving the pocket, and if Reid limits his dropback volume, boot gives him perimeter answers without max protection every snap. Thin WR health (Worthy shoulder, Rice returning) means the TE and RB become the primary options on the move while Nussmeier can run the same menu in team periods.

Coaching point Sell the zone fake with the OL; Mahomes (or Nussmeier) deep drop then climb. TE hinge then release to the flat/intermediate; backside WR carry the corner. Spit the ball on the first open window—no holding it to impress camp.

Offense

Mesh to stress man

Situation 2nd-and-medium vs. Rams nickel man — create free access without needing contested fades

Noah Gray is due a long-awaited clarity snap count; mesh puts the TE and a slot on rub crossers so Kansas City does not need Worthy at full speed to win. Against preseason man lookers, the rub plus RB outlet is the definition of easy offense while Rice works back.

Coaching point Timing over athleticism—inside release, flatten the mesh at five yards, outside guy win leverage. Gray must finish through contact on the rub without peaking early; RB checkdown is live if both crossers get trailed.

Offense

RB screen pressure-punisher

Situation Obvious pass / 3rd-and-long vs. Rams when they pin their ears back on backups

A depleted perimeter and a QB pitch count scream for free-yardage calls. Screens let the OL get to space, take heat off Mahomes or Nussmeier, and keep aggressive Rams camp rushers honest without asking injured or ramping WRs to win vertically every snap.

Coaching point OL sprint to landmarks—don’t dance. RB show pass set then sink; Nussmeier if in must throw on rhythm before the mock pressure arrives. Wideouts crack/seal the first flier; no freelancing.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 shell

Situation Long-yardage and two-minute vs. Rams preseason pass game — take away the shot

With a young front still sorting roles (Woods’ snaps, Anudike-Uzomah’s push), Spagnuolo can play two-deep five-under to erase explosives while the rush finds its timing. Camp tempers and returns from injury make assignment football the priority over gambling.

Coaching point Corners soft-pedal and funnel; safeties split the posts and drive on anything under. Linebackers wall crossers—no trail technique freelancing. Trust the front to collapse the pocket without a sixth rusher every down.

Defense

Simulated pressure with DL drop

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 7 vs. Rams backup protection — create free runners without emptying the box

Peter Woods’ importance is early-down disruption and interior stunt integrity; pairing him with Anudike-Uzomah on a Spagnuolo zone blitz lets Kansas City show heat, drop a lineman, and still bracket the easy throw. That is how a rebuilding front manufactures sacks while the secondary stays sound.

Coaching point Pre-snap mugged look, post-snap exchange—Woods crash or drop on the call, Anudike-Uzomah win the edge hinge. Second-level rusher must be dirty through the A/B gap on time; secondary plays the throw window, not the rush celebration.

The storylines

What we’re watching

QB ramp

Mahomes cleared, carefully managed

Patrick Mahomes detailed the final steps in his pre-camp clearance after the season-ending knee injury and is practicing in St. Joe. Reid’s lean toward sitting him against the Rams is standard pitch-count discipline, not a red flag—Nussmeier’s readiness is the planned bridge.

WR room

Worthy’s shoulder and Rice’s return

Xavier Worthy missing a week with a shoulder injury and Rashee Rice still ramping create a real wide-receiver problem with few clean external fixes. Camp and preseason will force Bieniemy to lean on TEs, stack releases, and easy-offense concepts until the perimeter stabilizes.

DL bet

Peter Woods’ ceiling is the season’s hinge

The 2026 draft class—and especially first-round DT Peter Woods—will decide whether Spagnuolo can control early downs. His ceiling-floor range is the difference between a disruptive front and a unit that lives behind the chains.

Edge growth

Anudike-Uzomah’s fourth-year push

Coaches and teammates are praising Felix Anudike-Uzomah’s work; at 24 he is still in the youth cohort next to Woods and the other young linemen. Production now turns a former first-round stub into a real piece of the 2026 pass rush.

OL value

Trey Smith and the guard market

As interior linemen cash new extensions league-wide, Kansas City’s decision to lock up Trey Smith keeps looking sharper. Pair that with Kahlil Benson’s right-guard work in pads and the run-game identity has a foundation.

Last game

What the tape said

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

W
Denver Broncos Week 1 · Mon Sep 14
KC 31–10

Kansas City opened the Andy Reid–Eric Bieniemy 2026 season with a complete-game beating of the Broncos on ESPN, 31-10, at Arrowhead. Mahomes scored on a 15-yard scramble in the first quarter, answered Bo Nix-to-Evan Engram with a 13-yard strike to Rashee Rice before half, then watched Kenneth Walker rip a 60-yard touchdown in the third and finish a 2-yard touchdown catch early in the fourth. Denver never recovered the line of scrimmage: 176 total yards, 61 on the ground, two turnovers, and a defense that spent the night chasing jumbo and explosive cuts.

The film is a downhill identity tape, not a Mahomes fireworks tape. Walker’s 23 carries were not garbage-time padding — they were the design. Kansas City committed to extra-linemen and two-back looks, even sprinkling defensive tackle Khyiris Tonga and offensive lineman Josh Ezeudu into short-yardage jumbo, and Denver’s front never reset the line of scrimmage. The 60-yarder in the third quarter was the tell: once Walker got a crease, there was no second-level tackle. That is why he ended up with 173 rushing yards and nearly half of Kansas City’s 392-yard night.

Bieniemy’s effect showed up in early-down leverage more than in the passing sheet. Mahomes went 15-of-27 for 184 yards, two scores, and an interception — efficient enough to finish drives, not voluminous enough to pretend the dropback game was the star. Kelce’s 71 yards on three catches were the chain-moving exception; Rice’s 13-yard touchdown was the red-zone answer the preseason never found. The rest of the passing game was play-action window dressing off a run that did not need help. That is complementary football when you have 33:42 of possession. It is also a look Indianapolis can copy by crowding the box.

Spagnuolo’s side was just as physical. Denver converted 2-of-12 on third down and managed nine first downs. Jaylen Waddle’s Broncos debut was a non-event; the Chiefs squeezed the perimeter and made Nix beat a covered field. Nohl Williams posted the sack on the stat sheet, and Felix Anudike-Uzomah played like a different edge setter up front. When you hold a division rival under 180 yards and still get a takeaway margin, you are not winning on scheme tricks — you are winning on first contact.

The blemishes are real and small, which is how 31-10 tapes hide them. Mahomes’ interception is the one possession Kansas City donated. Harrison Butker’s 28-yard field goal in the third is the one trip that did not finish in the paint. Mansoor Delane left with a shoulder that tests later suggested should cost him minimal time, but it is still a Week 1 scar on a secondary that otherwise erased Waddle. None of that undoes 220 rush yards. All of it is on the Colts’ Wednesday install.

What held up

  • Kenneth Walker’s one-cut explosiveness (23-173-1 rushing, plus the 2-yard TD catch)
  • Jumbo and extra-linemen packages (Tonga and Ezeudu in short yardage) that produced 220 team rush yards
  • Third-down leverage: KC 9-of-17, Denver 2-of-12
  • Kahlil Benson holding the left edge so Mahomes could scramble for a 15-yard score and live in play-action
  • Spagnuolo’s physical front and the erasure of Jaylen Waddle
  • Travis Kelce as the chain-mover (3-71) and Rashee Rice finishing a 13-yard touchdown

What broke

  • Dropback volume and rhythm: 15-of-27, 184 yards, and a passing game that needed the run to stay on schedule
  • Mahomes’ interception — the only donated possession on a night Kansas City otherwise controlled
  • The 28-yard Butker field goal as the one red-zone trip that settled for three
  • Wide-receiver production beyond Rice’s touchdown; the perimeter did not create the offense
  • Mansoor Delane’s early shoulder scare, even if tests point to minimal time missed

Walker is the offense until someone loads the box

Twenty-three carries, 173 yards, a 60-yard touchdown run, and a two-yard touchdown reception is not a committee night. Bieniemy built the game around one-cut zone and jumbo, and Walker repaid it by becoming 49 percent of the yardage. That identity travels only if the next opponent respects it — or if Mahomes punishes the respect.

The defense won third down, not the highlight reel

Denver’s 2-of-12 third-down night and 176 total yards are the real number, not the 31-10 score. Spagnuolo kept Nix in long-yardage, erased Waddle, and let the front (Anudike-Uzomah, Williams) set the point of attack. That is how you sit on a division game for 33:42.

The passing game was complementary, not solved

Fifteen completions, 184 yards, and one interception is a winning line when you run for 220. It is not proof the intermediate game is fixed. Rice and Kelce made the throws that mattered; everyone else was window dressing. Sunday night is the first time that mix gets stress-tested.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

1-0

Kansas City is 1-0 with a top-five power-ranking debut, a restored downhill identity, and a market that already makes them a touchdown favorite at home. The job this week is not to celebrate 220 rush yards. It is to decide how much of that tape was Denver and how much is the 2026 offense — and to get the passing game and the secondary healthy enough that Indianapolis cannot simply load the box on NBC.

What we need to work on

Intermediate passing off eight-man looks

Mahomes did not need to, so he did not. Indianapolis will not grant Walker 23 uncontested creases on Sunday night. Bieniemy has to script play-action, mesh, and the deep-over answers now, or the 15-of-27 night becomes the scouting report.

Keep jumbo as a weapon, not a tell

Tonga and Ezeudu in short yardage paid off against Denver. If those packages become obvious on second-and-short against the Colts, Kansas City will be in third-and-long dropbacks it did not have to win on Monday.

Secondary depth behind Delane’s shoulder

Tests suggest the rookie corner misses minimal time, but Week 2 on NBC is a short turn to certify that. Spagnuolo needs a plan if Delane is limited, because the Waddle erasure does not automatically transfer to Indianapolis’ perimeter.

What to think about

Was 184 passing yards game script or a ceiling?

The mailbag already asked how concerning the passing game was. Honest answer: it was not concerning in a 31-10 run game. It becomes concerning if Walker is held under 80 and Mahomes has to live in third-and-7. That is the Week 2 debate, not whether Bieniemy should have thrown more on Monday.

How many more 23-carry nights can Walker handle?

The major difference in this offense is the back. That is the point of the acquisition. It is also a September workload question. Reid and Bieniemy have to decide if Walker is a hammer every week or the lead in a rotation that still needs a change-of-pace to keep him explosive in December.

How real is Anudike-Uzomah’s Week 1 jump?

One film-room night against Denver is not a new identity for the edge. If he repeats the point-of-attack work against Indianapolis, Spagnuolo’s simulated pressures become a lot more violent. If he does not, the front is back to being Chris Jones and whoever wins the down.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Indianapolis Colts

Sunday night at Arrowhead is the first time Kansas City has to prove the Denver tape was an identity, not a matchup. ESPN FPI sits the Chiefs at 75.0 percent and DraftKings lays 6.5 at a 46.5 total — a home favorite that still has to answer a loaded box. The assignment is simple and not easy: keep feeding Walker until Indianapolis overcommits, then make Mahomes punish the extra hat, while Spagnuolo turns the Colts into the same third-down drought Denver just lived through.

How they match up

Kansas City wants to be a physical, jumbo, inside-zone team that uses Mahomes as the closer. Indianapolis, coming into Arrowhead on NBC, will have a full week of Walker film and every incentive to pack the box, dare the 15-of-27 passing game to beat them, and test a rookie left tackle and a corner coming off a shoulder scare. The leverage is early downs. If KC stays on schedule (the 9-of-17 third-down version), Benson and the jumbo packages can wear a visiting front. If Indianapolis wins first down, Mahomes is in the dropback game the Broncos never forced, and the 46.5 total starts to look like a track meet Kansas City did not practice on Monday.

Make Indianapolis defend Walker on early downs

Do not get cute because it is Sunday Night Football. Script inside zone and jumbo the way Bieniemy did against Denver. Force the Colts to pull a safety down. The moment they do, Mahomes’ play-action boot and Kelce’s over routes become the real passing game — not empty 11-personnel dropbacks.

Punish the extra hat with easy offense

If Indianapolis pins its ears back or loads the box, get Walker the ball on screens and get Rice/Kelce on mesh. The Denver tape showed the perimeter receivers were not the engine; this week they have to be the answer to the engine. That is how you keep 220-yard math from becoming 2nd-and-11 math.

Third-down defense before highlight defense

Spagnuolo’s win Monday was 2-of-12, not sacks. Mix Cover-2 to take away the shot and simulated pressure to rattle a visiting quarterback into the same long-yardage soup. Anudike-Uzomah and Nohl Williams set the edge; the back end rallies. Do not hunt strip-sacks on first down and gift Indianapolis free plays.

Protect the blind side you just trusted

Benson proved Reid right for one night. Indianapolis will test that tape. Keep a back or tight end as a chip on obvious passing downs until the rookie has shown he can live without help for a second straight week. A clean Mahomes is a 75-percent favorite. A chased Mahomes is the 6-11 hangover.

  1. Open in 13/jumbo and inside zone to Walker — announce the Denver identity before Indianapolis can settle into a light box.
  2. Second series: under-center play-action boot to Kelce after the run fake, with Rice as the backside comeback if the Colts safety sits on the tight end.
  3. If Indianapolis jumps the run, hit Walker on a spacing screen the other way and make their linebackers tackle in space.
  4. Third-and-medium: mesh with Kelce and Rice, Walker as the checkdown — do not live on isolations against a stacked box.
  5. Defensively, start in a Cover-2 shell on early downs to take away the shot, then sprinkle Spagnuolo simulated pressure on second-and-long with Anudike-Uzomah as the rusher who is not always rushing.
  6. Red zone: keep Walker on the goal line (the 2-yard TD catch was the design) and do not settle for another 28-yard Butker kick if you have already won first-and-goal.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The 6-11 hangover did not survive Monday night. Kansas City opened 2026 by sitting on Denver for 33:42, running for 220 yards, and walking out of Arrowhead with a 31-10 win that looked nothing like last year's offense. Kenneth Walker III was the hammer — 173 yards on 23 carries, a 60-yard sprint, and a two-yard touchdown catch — while Steve Spagnuolo's defense held the Broncos to nine first downs and 2-of-12 on third down. The evolving story is not whether this roster can punch. It is whether the punch is a real Bieniemy identity or a one-week Broncos problem, because Patrick Mahomes threw for only 184 yards and the next Sunday night will not gift Kansas City the same light box.

What changed from the preseason tape was finishing. The Seahawks dress rehearsal never found the end zone; this one produced four touchdowns, jumbo personnel that actually moved people, and a left tackle in Kahlil Benson who did not get Mahomes killed. Rashee Rice's 13-yard score and Travis Kelce's 71 yards were complementary, not the engine. Walker accounted for nearly half the offense, which is a feature when you are bludgeoning Denver and a vulnerability the moment Indianapolis loads eight in the box on NBC.

National rankings already shoved Kansas City into the top five after one night, which is exactly the kind of sugar that gets a 1-0 team sloppy. The honest read of the market is more useful than the applause: a 75 percent FPI nod and a 6.5-point home number say Arrowhead still matters, but a 7.8 percent championship price says the league has not forgotten 6-11. One 220-yard ground beating does not rewire a Super Bowl board.

Week 2 is the first real test of whether Eric Bieniemy can keep the downhill identity without becoming predictable, and whether Spagnuolo's front — Felix Anudike-Uzomah included — can travel against a different offensive line on a short week of film. Indianapolis comes to GEHA Field at Arrowhead as a Sunday-night problem, not a parade. If the Colts make Kansas City one-dimensional, the 15-of-27 passing night stops being game script and starts being a tell.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker downhill, extra hats on the move

Situation 1st-and-10 / 2nd-and-short vs. Indianapolis, opening script and after any Colts three-and-out

Kansas City just ran for 220 yards and Walker posted 173 on 23 against Denver by winning early downs in zone. Indianapolis will have that film; the answer is not to abandon it on NBC, it is to re-announce it until a safety comes down. Jumbo with Tonga and Ezeudu already paid off in Week 1 short yardage — that is the personnel that makes inside zone a physical problem instead of a light-box gift.

Coaching point Bieniemy’s point to Walker is one cut and go — do not bounce the Denver 60-yarder into a lost edge against a front that is sitting on it. Benson and the left side have to win first contact so Mahomes never has to dummy a handoff from a compromised mesh. If the Mike steps over the top, Walker presses and hits the back-side crease; if he sits, it is a north-south tackle for a gain of four and another 2nd-and-6 Kansas City already proved it can convert.

Offense

Sell Walker, move Mahomes, throw Kelce

Situation 2nd-and-5 after a Walker gain, under center, Colts safety creeping

Indianapolis’ entire week is loading the box to stop the back who just took 49 percent of Kansas City’s yards. That is when Reid and Bieniemy have always wanted Mahomes on the move. Kelce’s 71 yards on three catches against Denver were the chain-moving exception in a run game; against a creeping safety they become the shot. Rice’s 13-yard touchdown showed he can finish if the boot holds the flat defender.

Coaching point Mahomes has to ride the fake long enough that the Colts linebackers step to Walker — the Denver tape already trained them to. Benson’s job on the boot is to cut off the backside edge so the pocket actually moves, not collapses. Kelce sits in the first window if the safety stays high and bends to the sideline if he drives. If both are covered, Mahomes keeps it; he already scored from 15 yards out when Denver vacated.

Offense

Easy offense when they pin their ears back

Situation 2nd-and-7 or after KC play-action, Colts edge rushing upfield to stop Mahomes

If Indianapolis treats Walker as a 23-carry problem and Mahomes as a scrambler, the rush will outrun the screen. That is the exact look that makes a spacing screen the complement to 220 rush yards. Walker already caught a two-yard touchdown; getting him the ball in space on early downs keeps the Colts from teeing off on Benson and keeps the 15-of-27 passing night from turning into third-and-long.

Coaching point The offensive line has to sell pass for one count, then get out in front of Walker — this is not a bubble, it is a lead-blocking screen. Rice and Kelce’s job is to wall the first linebacker, not hunt yards after the catch themselves. Walker’s coaching point from the Denver film: hit the crease north-south. Do not dance behind the line and donate the down Indianapolis is trying to get you into.

Offense

Rub the box, find Rice and Kelce

Situation 3rd-and-6 vs. man, Indianapolis having taken away Walker on first down

The Arrowhead Pride mailbag already asked how concerning the passing game was after 184 yards. The honest answer against man is mesh: Kelce and Rice crossing, Walker as the outlet. Denver never forced this down because Kansas City stayed on schedule (9-of-17). Indianapolis will. This is the call that keeps a loaded-box plan from winning the game on third down.

Coaching point Reid’s mesh only works if the rub is violent enough to free the second crosser — Rice emphasized physicality after Monday night, and this is where that quote has to show up. Kelce cannot settle for the short sit if the linebacker trails; he has to run through the contact. Mahomes’ first look is the free access throw, not a hero ball downfield. If both crossers are trailed, Walker’s checkdown is the conversion, not a consolation.

Defense

Take away the shot, rally to tackle

Situation 1st-and-10 and long-yardage vs. Indianapolis, early possessions on NBC

Spagnuolo’s Week 1 win was not a blitz party — it was nine first downs and 176 yards allowed, with Waddle erased. A Cover-2 shell on early downs keeps Indianapolis from answering Walker’s 60-yarder with a shot of their own and forces the Colts into the same 2-of-12 third-down math. Delane’s shoulder makes this even more useful: two-deep with five under protects a corner who may not be 100 percent and still lets the front set the edge.

Coaching point Safeties split the field and do not bite the play-action that Kansas City itself just used. Corners funnel, they do not trail verticals over the top. The front — Anudike-Uzomah included — has to squeeze the run so Cover-2 does not become a light box for Indianapolis’ back. Williams and the second level rally; missed tackles are how a two-deep shell becomes a 46.5-total track meet.

Defense

Show heat, drop a lineman, rattle the visitor

Situation 2nd-and-8 / 3rd-and-7, Indianapolis trying to throw out of a hole Walker created

Spagnuolo’s signature is the simulated pressure, and Week 1 gave him a front that finally looked like it could rush and drop. Anudike-Uzomah’s jump and Nohl Williams’ sack are the personnel that make a zone blitz more than window dressing. Against a visiting quarterback on NBC, showing heat and bringing a second-level rusher is how you recreate Denver’s 115 net passing yards without selling out the explosive that Cover-2 is designed to take away.

Coaching point The fake is the point: Anudike-Uzomah cannot always be the rusher, or Indianapolis will slide to him. Drop him into the hook on one snap and bring Williams or a linebacker off the other edge on the next. Secondary must play the hole behind the blitz — Delane, if active, cannot open his hips for a shot over the top the way a true all-out would. The coaching point from the Waddle tape: squeeze, do not chase. Let the quarterback throw into a covered window.

Three catches, 71 yards, and a move into second place on the all-time tight-end receiving-yardage list. Against a loaded box, he is the play-action answer. Against man, he is the mesh rub. He is not the volume star of this offense until Walker gets tackled.

Coaching & adjustments

Reid/Bieniemy vs. a loaded box on NBC

The staff already won the identity argument against Denver. The Week 2 coaching test is restraint: keep the jumbo and inside zone on the call sheet even when the national broadcast wants Mahomes to cook, then have the boot and mesh ready the snap Indianapolis overplays Walker.

Spagnuolo’s third-down math

The Broncos went 2-of-12; that is the standard, not the sack total. Mix Cover-2 and simulated pressure so Indianapolis cannot chart a single tell, and do not let Delane’s shoulder turn early-down coverage into a scramble drill.

Reid’s left-tackle trust, week two

Benson earned the job on Monday. The coaching adjustment is help early (chip from Walker or Kelce) and a plan to take the help off if the rookie keeps winning. A second straight clean night turns the gamble into a setting.

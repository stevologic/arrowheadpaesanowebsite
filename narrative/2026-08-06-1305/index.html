The story so far

Always looking ahead

Kansas City is back in St. Joseph with something it has not carried into camp in over a decade: a losing record and no participation trophies. The 6-11 collapse was not a blip the locker room pretends was fake—Trent McDuffie’s comments captured the shock—but Andy Reid’s staff is treating Missouri Western like a reset lab. Installs matter more than vibes. The offensive line must give Patrick Mahomes a clean pocket and a downhill run game; Steve Spagnuolo needs linebacker speed he has chased for years; and the skill room has to prove Brett Veach was right to largely run it back instead of chasing shiny wideout trades.

The clearest structural win so far is on the left edge. Josh Simmons is up a weight class and already penciled in as the Week 1 left tackle after an eight-game audition that put him on NFL.com’s All-Under 25 team. That stability is huge for Eric Bieniemy’s early-down menu—inside zone, play-action boots, and max-protect shots all start with a LT who can stone the edge. The problem is the other side: the right tackle competition is forcing a real Week 1 decision, and every rep in the wet grass at MWSU is audition tape, not depth-chart theater.

Defensively, the headline is dual-track. Rookie DT Peter Woods—the 29th pick out of Clemson—has veterans like Khyiris Tonga calling him a “little pitbull” through the first padded practices, which is exactly the interior disruption Spagnuolo needs beside Chris Jones. But the long-running linebacker speed question has not vanished; if camp does not produce a second-level answer, Cover-2 shells and simulated pressures will keep springing leaks in space the way they did during the 2025 freefall.

Rashee Rice is the skill player with the most to prove and the most encouraging early noise, while Kristian Fulton has missed again—another reminder that the secondary cannot assume full health into September. Emari Demarcado is carving the classic undrafted niche (protection, scouts, finish), and Cooper McDonald is talking like a second-year defender who finally sees a real snap path. None of that is chrome; it is roster math.

Look ahead, not backward. Polymarket has the Chiefs at 9.5% to win the AFC and 6.7% to hoist the Lombardi in 2027 pricing—honest underdog numbers after a lost year, not a dynasty default. What a strong camp unlocks is simple: a defined OL, a DL that can win early downs without blitzing every snap, and enough LB athleticism for Spagnuolo to call his full menu in Week 1. Fail those battles in St. Joe and the schedule will not care about the Arrowhead name going back on the building.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-Center Inside Zone — Simmons Seals the Edge

Situation 1st-and-10, early script, Week 1 identity snaps when defenses load the box to test the new OL

With Josh Simmons locked at LT and up a weight class, Bieniemy can finally call true downhill zone without the left edge collapsing. A settled (or at least decided) RT lets the center/guards work double-to-linebacker while Emari Demarcado or the lead back press the landmark—exactly the early-down tax the 6-11 offense stopped collecting.

Coaching point Simmons’ first step and anchor have to finish the reach; backs one-cut on the play-side hip—no east-west freelancing that recreated last year’s second-and-long death spiral.

Offense

Play-Action Boot After Zone Look

Situation 2nd-and-medium following a successful inside-zone series; move Mahomes off the spot vs. aggressive Week 1 ends

Simmons’ LT stability sells the fake; boot gets Patrick Mahomes on the perimeter with easy high-low reads and protects whatever RT wins the camp job from pure speed rushes. Rice working back across the formation is the prove-it touch Mahomes needs if the static WR room is truly enough.

Coaching point Mahomes holds the fake one full count—Spagnuolo’s own camp scout looks will jump run. TE hinge must wall the DE; if the RT is still green, tight end chip rules are non-negotiable.

Offense

Mesh vs. Man — Rice as the Rub Winner

Situation 3rd-and-5 to 3rd-and-7, two-minute or early third-down sets against press-man looks Week 1

AP flagged Rashee Rice as the Chiefs skill player with the most to prove; mesh puts him on a natural rub crosser where YAC and toughness show up without needing a new vertical burner. Avoiding a Calvin Ridley distraction only works if this room wins man-coverage downs in September.

Coaching point Rice must attack the contact and finish through the tangle—no polite paths. Back checkdown (Demarcado niche) is live if both crossers get trailed; Mahomes throws on time or the mesh dies.

Offense

RB Screen — Easy Offense When the Pass Rush Climbs

Situation Early downs or empty protections when opposing fronts pin their ears back at Mahomes after 2025 tape study

A rebuilt interior with Woods and Jones will draw extra attention; the screen punishes that aggression and keeps Mahomes from living in collapsed pockets while the RT battle is still settling. Demarcado’s “niche” value is exactly this: set up, release clean, finish in space.

Coaching point Guards and center must get to the second level flat—no wasted vertical steps. Back sells pass pro for a full beat; Mahomes’ eyes stay downfield until the throw window, not a panic dump.

Defense

Spagnuolo Cover-2 Shell — Protect the LB Speed Gap

Situation Long-yardage and obvious passing downs Week 1 while the linebacker athleticism question is still live

If camp does not fully solve the multi-year LB speed issue, Cover-2 is the honest answer: two deep take away the shot, five under rally, and Jones/Woods win the interior so the second level is tackling, not chasing vertically. McDuffie’s standard on the outside only works if the shell is disciplined behind him—especially with Fulton missing practice time.

Coaching point Corners squat and funnel; safeties split the posts, no freelancing. LBs must expand then collapse—Cooper McDonald’s Year-2 opportunity shows up here as a run-fit and shallow-cross robber, not a highlight blitzer.

Defense

Simulated Pressure — Woods as the Pitbull Piece

Situation 2nd-and-7 to 3rd-and-5, neutral script when Spagnuolo wants a negative play without true all-out heat

Peter Woods’ early padded-practice violence lets Spagnuolo show five or six and still drop a lineman into the hole—signature simulated pressure that stressed QBs when healthy. Pairing Woods’ interior burst with Jones means the “blitz” can be a mirage that still wins the A-gap while covering the LB speed limitation in behind zones.

Coaching point Pre-snap crowd the LOS, post-snap exchange clean—Woods either wins the one-on-one or occupies two so the second-level rusher is free. Dropping end must replace the hook; missed exchanges are how 2025 turned pressures into chunk plays.

The storylines

What we’re watching

LT Locked

Josh Simmons is the camp constant

Simmons has jumped a weight class and is treated as the Week 1 left tackle after flashing across eight games a year ago. That lets Bieniemy script under-center zone and boots without protecting a soft edge first. His All-Under 25 nod is nice; finishing camp clean is the real deliverable.

RT Battle

Right tackle is the Week 1 decision nobody can fake

With Simmons set on the left, the right side is a live competition creating a difficult Week 1 call. Whichever body wins must hold up in both inside zone and seven-man protections or Mahomes will be back to backyard rules by October.

Rookie Juice

Peter Woods is already moving grown men

Through the first padded days, Woods has drawn “little pitbull” praise from Khyiris Tonga and looked like a star-in-the-making interior piece. Pairing that with Chris Jones is how Spagnuolo gets negative plays without selling out every down.

LB Speed

The same linebacker question still shapes the defense

Kansas City has chased second-level speed for years; camp is the latest referendum. If the LBs cannot run, Cover-2 turns into catch-and-run football and zone blitzes leave voids that good QBs will punish in Week 1.

WR Reality

Rice has the most to prove—and the noise is positive

The Associated Press tabbed Rashee Rice as the Chiefs player with the most to prove, and early camp reports have been encouraging even as the room largely ran it back. Chasing a Calvin Ridley rebound is a distraction Veach should keep ignoring.

Niche Work

Demarcado and McDonald fighting for real roles

Emari Demarcado is embracing the undrafted niche—protection, scout-team detail, finish—while Cooper McDonald says he is ready for the opportunity he has chased into Year 2. Those are the edges that decide 53-man cuts and early-down packages.

Stadium

Just Arrowhead again

The club and G.E.H.A. are returning the plain Arrowhead Stadium branding for 2026—a small cultural reset that matches the on-field strip-down after 6-11. The building gets its name back; the roster still has to earn the noise.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid / Bieniemy early-down identity

Camp must reinstall under-center zone and boot as the spine so Mahomes is not stuck in empty shotgun guesswork by Week 1. Simmons at LT is the green light; the RT winner is the red light if he cannot finish zone steps.

Spagnuolo second-level math

The linebacker speed question still shapes call-sheet aggression. Until that room proves it in pads, expect more Cover-2 shells and simulated pressures that hide athletic gaps behind Jones and Woods.

Veach roster philosophy under fire

Running back the WR room and ignoring Ridley-trade noise only looks smart if Rice’s prove-it camp turns into September separation. Niche contributors like Demarcado are how a 6-11 roster finds hidden snaps without splash signings.

Camp conditions as evaluator

Weather-impacted days moving indoors then back to wet grass at Missouri Western are lengthening practices and stress-testing conditioning—Reid’s injury updates after those sessions are as important as the depth chart graphics.

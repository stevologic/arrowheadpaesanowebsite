Current state

Rice's return and a thin receiver room

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

5-1

Kansas City is 5-1, still the team that beat Denver 31-10 in Week 1, and also the team that just posted 213 yards in a 20-3 loss. The standings can hold both facts. The next game will not.

What we need to work on

Early-down carries that change third down

Sixteen attempts and 50 yards made 3-of-11 predictable. Walker needs a real early-down share so Mahomes is not living on 19-of-33 and seven hits again.

Get the ball out before the second hit

Seattle recorded seven quarterback hits and one sack. Mesh, screens, and defined intermediate throws to Kelce and Gray are the protection plan as much as the tackle plan is.

Finish kicks and red-zone trips

Against a 40.5 total, those empty trips are the game.

What to think about

Buy a receiver or play the room you have

Arrowhead Pride's Reacts poll wants a trade, and Arrowhead Addict is tracking multiple paths to help. Rice is expected back. Thornton is described as more or less done for the year. The staff has to decide whether Sunday's plan is Rice-Kelce-Worthy or a deadline swing.

How much Week 1 still means

A 31-10 home win over Denver is real. So is a 5-2 Broncos team that has won five of six since. Arrowhead Addict's look at second meetings is a reminder not to treat the rematch as a replay.

Tackle stability without overreacting to one holding call

Suamataia's holding wiped a fourth-quarter play, and the tackle depth chart is already a long-term Arrowhead Addict topic. This week the question is communication, not a 2027 mock draft.

Looking ahead

How to play Denver as a short favorite

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Win the rematch on Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT at Denver by playing like a 1.5-point favorite on a 40.5 total: own early downs, keep Mahomes off a seven-hit script, and make Denver drive the length of the field against Cover-2 and selective simulated pressure.

How they match up

Kansas City's offense just failed to stay on schedule; Denver's defense, including the emerging contributors Arrowhead Addict flagged, will try to keep that version on the field. Kansas City's defense just faced 41 rushes and an efficient Darnold day. Denver at 5-2 does not have to be Seattle to steal a short-priced road game. The Chiefs' leverage is Rice's expected return, Kelce's chain-moving, Walker's receiving chops, and a Spagnuolo plan that rallies to the ball the way Tranquill did. The Broncos' leverage is any repeat of 3-of-11 and 50 rushing yards.

Make Denver defend Walker before it hunts Mahomes

Call inside zone and the screen on early downs so the rush does not get a free seven-hit plan. Walker's four catches in Seattle are the outlet if the box loads up.

Use Rice as a new first-read, not a hero ball

Rice is expected back from the hamstring. Pair him with Kelce on mesh and with Worthy on vertical stress so Mahomes is not forcing the Royals window that Okada jumped.

Take the deep crosser away and rally

Smith-Njigba's 28-yard touchdown was a deep ball to the boundary. Cover-2 is the shell that keeps that shot from becoming Denver's answer, with the second level running to tackle.

Pressure on your terms

Zone pressure is a call for obvious passing downs. Show heat, drop a lineman, and keep Karlaftis and the second level from being static.

  1. Open with under-center inside zone to Walker and a quick Kelce sit so the first third down is not 3rd-and-8.
  2. If Denver shows a heavy rush, throw the spacing screen to Walker behind the line's release.
  3. On 2nd-and-medium, mesh Kelce and Rice and keep Gray as the sit/corner piece that already won twice for 19.
  4. Save four verticals for a called shot once Denver squeezes the crossers, with Worthy on the outside vertical.
  5. Defend first down in Cover-2 so a chunk throw does not flip field position the way Seattle's explosives did.
  6. On 3rd-and-6 or longer, call the zone blitz, rush the second level, and rally the dropper to the checkdown.
Last game

What 213 yards at Lumen actually showed

The previous result, with the details that matter to the next assignment.

L
Seattle Seahawks Week 7 · Sun, Oct 25 · 7:20 PM CT
KC 3–20

At Lumen Field on Sun Oct 25, 7:20 PM CT, Seattle beat Kansas City 20-3. The Seahawks scored a 25-yard field goal, a 28-yard touchdown, a 35-yard field goal, and a 21-yard touchdown; Kansas City's points came on a 50-yard field goal.

The night loss was a possession game Seattle controlled without needing a trick scoreboard. Kansas City had eight drives and 24:09; Seattle had eight drives and 35:51. Seattle was 11-of-16 on third down. Kansas City was 3-of-11. When the chains move that cleanly, Spagnuolo never gets the down-and-distance he wants, and Bieniemy never gets a two-minute board that matters.

Mahomes' line was 19-of-33 for 165 yards, a 58.3 rating, one interception, and one sack, with seven quarterback hits charged to Seattle. The interception was a short left throw intended for J. Royals, taken by T. Okada with N. Emmanwori in on the play. Deep shots to Kelce and Worthy were incomplete on the listed play-by-play. Noah Gray did connect on two 19-yard catches, one deep right and one deep middle, so the intermediate window was there when the ball came out on time.

The run game never became a counter. Walker had 14 carries for 36 yards and four catches, 18 touches, with carries charted up the middle, off left end, and off right end. Team rushing was 50 yards on 16 attempts, so this was not a volume problem disguised as a big night for someone else. Emanuel Wilson, by contrast, had 24 rushes and a catch. Seattle's 153 rushing yards on 41 attempts, plus Jaxon Smith-Njigba's 11 touches and the 28-yard touchdown from Darnold, kept Drue Tranquill busy. Tranquill finished with 12 tackles.

Two non-scoring kicks and a late empty trip framed the frustration. Harrison Butker missed from 58 and from 45. Kansas City also turned it over on downs on a 3-yard completion to Kelce. Nick Bolton was injured on a third-quarter Seattle run. On the other side of the ball, N. Williams intercepted Darnold, intended for Rashid Shaheed, and returned it 65 yards, with George Karlaftis in on the play. A Creed Humphrey fumble was recovered by Mahomes, and a later Mahomes fumble was forced by D. Hall.

Seattle's own mistakes did not flip the game. False starts on G. Zabel, A. Bradford, and J. Sundell wiped plays, and Darnold took zero sacks on 20-of-28. Kansas City's penalties that wiped plays were Kelce's illegal shift and Kingsley Suamataia's holding. Direct snaps to AJ Barner showed up more than once; the eligible report on the list is O. Oluwatimi with J. Jones on the first-quarter snap, not a blanket look for every Barner carry.

What held up

  • N. Williams' interception of Darnold and the 65-yard return gave the defense its cleanest play.
  • Tranquill's 12 tackles were the rally point in a game Seattle ran it 41 times.

What broke

  • 11 first downs, 213 yards, and 3-of-11 left the offense without a sustained drive.
  • One sack and seven quarterback hits on Mahomes, plus the Okada interception, stalled the dropback plan.
  • Missed kicks from 58 and 45, and a fourth-quarter turnover on downs, kept the only Kansas City score at a 50-yard field goal.

Third down decided the clock

Seattle converted 11 of 16 third downs and held the ball 35:51. Kansas City converted 3 of 11 and held it 24:09. Eight drives apiece, and the yardage still landed 410-213.

The rush answer was too small

Walker’s 14 carries produced 36 yards, and the team finished at 50 yards on 16 attempts. End runs and inside runs were both on the tape. Neither created a down-and-distance change.

Explosives were Seattle's, not Kansas City's

Darnold's 28-yard touchdown to Smith-Njigba, plus a later 21-yard Seattle touchdown, did the scoring damage. Kansas City's big connections on the list were Gray's 19-yard catches, not touchdowns.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The next kickoff is the one that counts: Kansas City goes to Denver on Sun Nov 1, 3:25 PM CT on CBS, laying 1.5 in an AFC West rematch after opening the year with a 31-10 win over these Broncos. Denver is 5-2 and has won five of its last six, so Week 1 is context, not a script.

Andy Reid and Eric Bieniemy get Patrick Mahomes a cleaner operation than the one Seattle just filmed. The Chiefs finished with 11 first downs, 213 total yards, 50 rushing yards on 16 attempts, and a 3-of-11 third-down line. Mahomes was 19-of-33 for 165 yards with one interception, one sack, and seven quarterback hits. That is the tape Denver will study, and it is the tape Kansas City has to leave in Seattle.

Rashee Rice is expected back from a hamstring injury, which changes the receiver math even if the trade wire stays loud. Arrowhead Pride notes fans want a receiver before the deadline, with Tyquan Thornton described as more or less out for the year. Rice, Travis Kelce, Xavier Worthy, and Noah Gray are the verified answers on this roster until a deal actually happens.

Steve Spagnuolo's group has a different problem than the offense. Seattle posted 24 first downs, 410 yards, 153 rushing yards on 41 attempts, and 11-of-16 on third down while holding the ball for 35:51 to Kansas City's 24:09. Both teams had eight drives. Denver does not need to copy Sam Darnold's 20-of-28, 96.9-rated day to win a 40.5-total game. It needs to keep Kansas City in the same down-and-distance jail.

ESPN FPI still has Kansas City at 55.7 percent and Denver at 44.0 percent. That is a lean, not a coronation. The edge is real only if Kenneth Walker III gets more than a 36-yard rushing day and the protection plan answers seven hits without asking Mahomes to invent explosives on third-and-long.

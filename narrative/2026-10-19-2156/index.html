Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

5-0

Kansas City is 5-0 and a road underdog at Seattle. The Chargers afternoon proved the defense can erase a division rival. It also proved 422 yards can stall at 22 if third down, the goal line, and the right tackle are unsettled before Seattle.

What we need to work on

Third-down answers

3-of-10 will not travel. ESPN FPI now favors Kansas City at 52.2 percent at Seattle, with Seattle at 47.4. Eric Bieniemy needs a third-and-medium menu that is not another hero ball after the sticks move late.

Goal-line identity without a pile-up

The Q3 6:34 snap, Tonga reported eligible, died at the 1. That is a separate look from Mike Caliendo’s Q2 9:26 eligible report. Script a call that does not require stacking every heavy name on one down.

Right-tackle plan

Moore left in the second quarter with a concussion. Suamataia was hurt on a fourth-quarter holding snap and returned to the game. The staff still has to decide who protects Mahomes' right side on the road.

Kelce’s ankle volume

He left briefly in the first half with an ankle injury and still finished with four catches. Four catches on five targets is useful. Ankle swelling before Seattle would cut that volume.

What to think about

Is 5-0 hiding a finishing leak?

Los Angeles was held to six points and zero touchdowns. Kansas City still needed field goals at Q2 0:02 and Q4 3:52 and failed at the 1. Style points are optional. Red-area math is not, once the opponent is Seattle.

How much of the Vegas run game is this offense?

Against Las Vegas, Kansas City rushed 26 times for 189 yards, passed for 210 net, and possessed the ball 24:18, while Las Vegas held it 35:42. Against Los Angeles the club ran 27 times for 150, passed for 272 net, and possessed the ball 30:26. Walker is the connector.

Underdog or trap narrative?

DraftKings is Seattle -2.5, total 42.5. FPI is 52.2 percent. Believe the number, then beat it with a plan.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Seattle Seahawks

Week 7 is at Seattle on Sun Oct 25, 7:20 PM CT, NBC, with Kansas City a short underdog to the defending champions. The assignment is to keep Walker’s downhill identity, give Mahomes answers if the right tackle is a question, and make Spagnuolo’s shell the reason Seattle cannot steal explosives.

How they match up

Seattle gets Walker back in its building and gets the better market number. Kansas City gets the hotter record, a quarterback who just completed 23 of 30 without a sack, and a defense that just held a division rival to 6 points, 73 rushing yards, and 5-of-16 on third down. The leverage is early-down runs into play-action movement, then a two-deep shell so the road crowd cannot turn one shot into a swing.

Feed Walker without announcing the down

Nineteen carries and two touchdowns just happened. Early downs should stay on inside zone and end runs he already showed — up the middle, left end, right end — so Seattle cannot sit on one path.

Move the launch point

Moore left with a concussion, so the boot is a protection answer. Kelce and Worthy are the easy perimeter reads if the ankle and the route volume cooperate.

Steal third down with rubs and screens

3-of-10 cannot repeat. Mesh against man and a running-back screen give Mahomes the checkdown he did not need often against the Chargers and will need if Seattle heats the right side.

Two deep, then simulated heat

Johnston’s 57-yarder reached the 1 and still became a field goal. On the road, Cover 2 takes the shot away first. The zone blitz is a later call.

  1. Open with under-center inside zone to Walker, then a boot away from the questioned right edge once Seattle flows.
  2. On 2nd-and-medium, mesh Kelce and Worthy with Johnson as the outlet.
  3. If the rush declares, throw the screen to Walker, who already caught two balls on 21 touches.
  4. Third-and-long: four verticals only if the shell is single-high; otherwise stay in Cover 2 rules on defense and check to a sit route on offense.
  5. After a sack or a negative play, show simulated pressure with Jones or Karlaftis dropping, and rally Bolton and Conner to the checkdown.
  6. In the red area, keep Caliendo’s eligible snap and Tonga’s eligible snap as separate looks. Call one heavy picture and a Mahomes run off left end, where he already gained 10, or off right end, where he already scrambled for 8.

A 44-yard field goal at Q1 11:20 made it 3-0, Walker's 33-yard touchdown at Q1 7:21 made it 9-0 after Harrison Butker's extra point sailed wide left, and Los Angeles answered with a 23-yard field goal at Q1 1:33. The run tape is specific. Walker gained 13 up the middle, 2 off left end, 4 up the middle, and 3 off right end before the 33-yard touchdown up the middle. Later he ripped 11 and 13 off left end and, on the Q3 6:34 snap with K. Tonga reported eligible, was stopped at the 1 on a left-guard carry for no gain — a turnover on downs. Emmett Johnson added five touches. Kansas City's team line is 27 carries for 150. Walker had 128 of those yards. Against Las Vegas the club rushed for 189.

Mahomes' throws included the deep-middle 23-yarder to Cyrus Allen and the short-right throw Cam Hart intercepted, which became a touchback. Creed Humphrey's fumble on a snap was recovered by Mahomes, who found Travis Kelce for 16 on the continuation. The 23-of-30, zero-sack line still came with three hits. The miss that stings is finishing — 3-of-10 on third down, a 52-yard Butker miss wide right, the failed fourth-down snap at the 1, and two giveaways on a day the offense reached 422 yards.

Spagnuolo's group kept a winless offense in field-goal range. Herbert scrambled for gains, including 6 and 7 up the middle and 7 off left end, but Chamarri Conner was credited with a sack and the rush finished with four sacks and nine hits. Cochrane intercepted a short-right throw intended for KeAndre Lambert-Smith and returned it 48 yards; a Chris Roland-Wallace interception was wiped by Ashton Gillotte's roughing penalty and does not count. Penalties leaked into the story. Kelce was flagged for an illegal block above the waist on the opening series, Trey Smith holding wiped a play, L'Jarius Sneed was called for unnecessary roughness, and Kingsley Suamataia holding wiped another. Gillotte's roughing and a later Chris Jones roughing-the-passer flag gave Herbert extra downs. The defense still won the down.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

Andy Reid's club is 5-0 because the complementary pieces keep cashing wins that the scoreboard does not dress up. Against Los Angeles, Kansas City put up 22 first downs, 150 rushing yards, and 272 net passing yards, finished with 422 total yards and 30:26 of possession, and still left Arrowhead with a 22-6 final that felt like a finishing problem wearing a division win.

The last afternoon was a Steve Spagnuolo game as much as a Patrick Mahomes game. Justin Herbert went 22-of-37 with four sacks, a 63.3 rating, and nine quarterback hits from Kansas City; Los Angeles managed 15 first downs, 73 rushing yards, 186 net passing yards, 259 total yards, and 29:34 of possession, and the only Los Angeles points were field goals. Jack Cochrane intercepted Herbert, and L'Jarius Sneed forced the fumble Drue Tranquill recovered — the giveaways that matched the Chargers' two-turnover line.

Mahomes was 23-of-30 for 272 yards with an 89.9 rating, zero sacks, and three hits. That is an efficient day. Kenneth Walker III carried 19 times for 128 yards and both Kansas City touchdowns — the 33-yard score up the middle at Q1 7:21, extra point no good, and the 2-yard score at Q2 1:49 — on 21 touches with two catches mixed in. Xavier Worthy caught seven passes for 73 yards in a receiver room that did not have Rashee Rice.

The season ask changes on Sun Oct 25, 7:20 PM CT. DraftKings lists Seattle -2.5. The 5-0 record travels. The benefit of the doubt on third down and in the red area does not.

Mesh on third-and-medium

Situation 3rd-and-4 to 3rd-and-6, the down Kansas City converted only 3 of 10 against the Chargers.

Seattle can play man and dare a hero ball. Rubs between Worthy and Kelce, with Johnson as the checkdown, are how a 23-of-30 quarterback stays efficient when the crowd is not Arrowhead’s.

Coaching point Worthy stems inside, Kelce crosses under, Andrew Armstrong — one catch, and the target on the Hart interception — sits the corner only if the rub is clean. Mahomes hits the first open window, not the second read into a trap.

Screen the aggressive front

Situation 2nd-and-8 or after a second-level rush shows, with three hits already the recent tax on Mahomes.

Zero sacks does not mean the right tackle question is solved. A screen to Walker, who caught two passes, or to Johnson lets the line lead and keeps Seattle from teeing off on a backup tackle.

Coaching point Mahomes: catch, set, throw the screen before the third hit accumulates. Offensive line releases to the perimeter. Walker catches on the move. On the screen he is the receiver. Leave a 20th touch off the script if the screen is the call.

Defense

Cover 2 on any explosive down

Situation 1st-and-10 after a Seattle chunk, or 2nd-and-long when the shot is the obvious call.

Johnston reached the 1 on a 57-yarder and the drive still became a field goal. On the road, Spagnuolo cannot donate that throw. Two deep, five under, rally — Sneed and the corners on top, Bolton and Conner underneath.

Coaching point Spagnuolo: no freelance bail. Sneed stays square, Conner is the hook robber, Bolton fits the checkdown the way he fit Herbert’s scramble. This shell is about taking away the 57-yard shot.

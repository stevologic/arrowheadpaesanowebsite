The story so far

Always looking ahead

Training camp at Missouri Western opened with the Chiefs treating recovery like a competitive advantage rather than a crisis. Rashee Rice and L’Jarius Sneed stepped off before 7-on-7 for planned rehab work, Patrick Mahomes’ availability remains the gravitational center of every install, and Eric Bieniemy was away from the first full-squad practice as Mia Bieniemy continues to improve after the shooting at their home—Andy Reid’s update was steady and human, not football spin. The football story underneath all of that is simple: Kansas City went 6-11, took 47 sacks, and spent the offseason buying explosive help in the backfield with Super Bowl 60 MVP Kenneth Walker. Camp is where Reid and Spagnuolo decide whether 2026 is a reclamation year or another slog.

The install identity should not be mysterious. With Walker signed to a three-year deal, the Chiefs finally have a back who forces safeties to creep and linebackers to fit downhill—exactly the look Reid needs to re-center play-action boot and inside zone after a season spent surviving negative plays. That only works if the offensive line wins early downs and Mahomes’ rehab ramp stays on schedule; the fantasy trackers and camp notebooks are already orbiting that single variable because everything else—mesh timing, four-verts spacing, screen game—hangs on whether No. 15 is full-speed by mid-September.

On the edges of the roster, the Chiefs are doing what they always do in St. Joe: betting on traits. Omari Evans is the undrafted athleticism dart they’re hoping translates on Sundays, and early camp chatter already has a rookie wideout earning trust in the first full-team period. Cool the jets—camp hype misleads more than it reveals—but the depth chart reality is real: if Rice’s rehab lingers, somebody has to win third-down and red-zone snaps before Denver arrives on MNF.

Defensively, Spagnuolo again has a veteran safety to lean on. Alohi Gilman is making a point of leading a young secondary while Sneed’s rehab path plays out, and that matters because the schedule does not ease anyone in. Week 1 is Denver at Arrowhead, then Indianapolis at home, then Miami on the road—three different offensive identities in 13 days. Spags will live in simulated pressures and Cover-2 shells until the backend proves it can handle man losses without a finished corner room.

Zoom out and the markets are polite, not euphoric: ESPN FPI sits Kansas City around 59 percent against Denver, DraftKings has the Chiefs -3 with a 42.5 total, and the long-range boards still price them as a single-digit AFC contender. A strong camp—Mahomes throwing in rhythm, Walker dictating box counts, Gilman organizing the backend, and at least one rookie WR sticking—turns Week 1 from a vibes game into a statement. A messy camp puts 6-11 energy right back on the table before the leaves turn.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker Sell → Boot Flood

Situation 1st-and-10, opening script vs. Denver at Arrowhead

Kenneth Walker’s early-down gravity is the offseason acquisition built for this look; after 47 sacks in 2025, moving Mahomes off the spot on a sold run fake is the cleanest protection upgrade Reid can call without changing personnel.

Coaching point Walker must finish the fake through the line—no bounce—so Denver’s second-level pursuers bite. Mahomes’ first read is the flat/outlet; don’t hunt the deep cross until the edge defender crashes.

Offense

Inside Zone, Heavy Close

Situation 2nd-and-short / four-minute lean vs. Broncos nickel

Walker was brought here to win the A-gap early downs Denver will try to steal with light boxes. Establishing zone before the MNF crowd gets antsy keeps Mahomes out of obvious passing downs while the OL is still proving it fixed last year’s damage.

Coaching point Covered linemen take zone steps and climb; uncovered work double-to-linebacker. Walker’s one-cut discipline matters more than hero jumps—win the first shoulder and finish.

Offense

Mesh vs. Broncos Man-Pressure

Situation 3rd-and-5, Denver showing two-high then late man

With Rashee Rice on a planned rehab track and a rookie WR already earning trust reps, mesh creates free access throws if Mahomes is medically cleared to drive short-to-intermediate balls. It’s also Bieniemy’s classic answer when an opponent wants to muddy protections.

Coaching point Inside crossers must rub without picking; the sit/corner combo holds the robber. RB is live on the checkdown the second both meshers are trailed—no hero ball from Mahomes early in the ramp.

Offense

Easy Button Screen

Situation 2nd-and-long after a first-down incompletion; Broncos pinching the edges

If Denver’s rush wins early like opponents did during the 47-sack season, Reid needs a pressure release that also features Walker in space. Camp is the time to clean up the timing so Week 1 isn’t a live experiment.

Coaching point Guard and tackle must get to the second level without holding; Walker’s first three steps are catch, set, then press the alley. Mahomes sells the drop—eyes downfield one beat longer than comfortable.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 Latch

Situation 1st-and-10, Denver play-action shot period in the red-to-red intermediate

With L’Jarius Sneed in planned rehab and a young secondary still organizing behind Alohi Gilman, soft two-deep with five under protects against the vertical shot while Gilman coaches alignments in real time.

Coaching point Corners funnel and jam; Gilman and the opposite safety split the post hash with no curiosity steps. Linebackers must wall the crossers—tackle integrity over highlight INTs while the room is still young.

Defense

Fire Zobos: Simulated Pressure

Situation 3rd-and-6 midfield, Denver max-protect tell vs. empty or 11

Kansas City has shown no interest in adding Jadeveon Clowney, so Spagnuolo has to manufacture heat with disguise. Simulated pressure lets a second-level rusher crown the protection while a lineman drops into the throwing lane Mahomes’ counterpart wants on MNF.

Coaching point Show five or six at the line, bring four from odd seats. The dropping end must own the flat/hook immediately—no ball-watching. Gilman communicates the shell late so Denver’s protection slide guesses wrong.

The storylines

What we’re watching

QB Ramp

Mahomes’ rehab is the camp

Every install, 7-on-7 period, and red-zone period orbits Patrick Mahomes’ progression. The offense’s ceiling in Weeks 1–3 is less about scheme novelty and more about whether he can drive the ball on time without the offense protecting him from himself.

Backfield

Kenneth Walker changes the math

Signing Super Bowl 60 MVP Kenneth Walker is the clearest admission that Mahomes needed a real early-down threat. If Walker wins first-and-10, Reid’s play-action catalog comes back online and the sack tally should plummet from last year’s 47.

Secondary

Gilman as the grown-up

Alohi Gilman is doing the unglamorous work of leading a young backend while L’Jarius Sneed handles planned rehab. Spagnuolo always needs one safety who can align the room—Gilman is that player in St. Joe.

WR Battle

Rookie trust reps matter now

Day 1 already flagged a rookie wideout gaining trust, and the building is openly betting Omari Evans’ athleticism translates. With Rice in a rehab plan, those reps are not depth-chart trivia—they’re Week 1 contingency planning.

Edge Depth

Still no Clowney path

The club continues to show no interest in Jadeveon Clowney despite the obvious need for proven edge help. That tells you they are committed to internal answers—and that camp battles up front are not optional.

Special Teams

Butker’s career stakes

Harrison Butker enters 2026 with real performance pressure after a down 2025. In a division game scripted around field position and sevens vs. threes, camp consistency for the kicker is a quiet season-shaper.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-center inside zone — re-establish first down

Situation 1st-and-10, early script in 2026 — camp full-pads identity reps carrying into September

Day 9 went back to pads with heavy run-game team work and Benson getting right-guard snaps; if that interior locks, inside zone becomes the foundation that keeps Mahomes out of obvious passing downs after a 6-11 year defined by losing leverage early.

Coaching point Bieniemy’s install (even remote) wants zone steps, one cut, and no lateral drift—RG must win the first step so the back can press the heel of the guard without Mahomes holding a collapsing edge.

Offense

Play-action boot after zone sell

Situation 2nd-and-7 or success run look — move Mahomes when the interior is still settling

With OL combinations still auditioning and Reid managing a lengthy camp injury list, bootlegs reduce pure dropback stress and let Mahomes work half-field while the staff evaluates who can actually pull and replace in space.

Coaching point Sell the zone handoff hard, TE chip-then-release, and Mahomes must throttle the ball on the move—no hero ball into the boundary until the new guard-tackle pairing proves it can wall off the chase defender.

Offense

Mesh to answer man-heavy pressure

Situation 3rd-and-5 — expected man or mixed pressure when opponents test the new OL

If 2025 free-agency misses left the skill and line groups uneven, mesh creates free access with rubs and gives Mahomes a defined progression that does not require perfect protection depth.

Coaching point Inside crossers must flatten timing for the rub; the RB outlet is live if Spags-style simulated look from the opponent leaves a free runner—Mahomes eyes the first clear window, not the highlight.

Offense

RB screen as pressure punishment

Situation 2nd-and-long or empty-protection tell — slow down aggressive edges while OL gels

Camp notes stress run periods and team work while the defense elsewhere is still climbing; offensively, screens buy the interior time and punish overeager rush lanes created when opponents smell a vulnerable guard spot.

Coaching point Vertical set to invite upfield rush, then climb to linebackers—back must stutter to let Benson/guard and tackle get to space; Mahomes throws on rhythm, not late, or the play dies in the backfield.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 shell — take away the shot

Situation 1st-and-10 / obvious shot downs while the defense is still finding itself in camp

Camp reporting after Day 8 was blunt that the defense has a long way to go; starting in a two-deep, five-under shell protects young or unsettled back-seven pieces and forces opponents to drive the field while Spags sorts personnel.

Coaching point Corners squat and funnel, safeties split the posts, and linebackers must run the alley—no free vertical seams while the unit rebuilds confidence and tackling standards in pads.

Defense

Simulated pressure / zone blitz triggers

Situation 3rd-and-4 to 3rd-and-7 — create a free runner without emptying the coverage

Spagnuolo’s signature answers the ‘long way to go’ problem by manufacturing numbers at the protection’s weak point instead of asking every one-on-one to win pure; that is how a still-forming defense steals drives in September.

Coaching point Show five or six, drop a lineman into the hot seat, and bring a second-level runner opposite the slide—safeties must be clean on the exchange so Mahomes’ counterparts cannot answer with the easy glance seam.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

A 6-11 season stripped the varnish off this roster, and training camp at Missouri Western is functioning like an audit. Full pads are back, the run game is getting real volume, and the offensive line is being stress-tested in public—including looks at Kahlil Benson at right guard—while Steve Spagnuolo’s defense is still being described as having a long way to go. Andy Reid is managing a longer injury list than anyone wants in August, Patrick Mahomes’ preseason workload is already a talking point, and Eric Bieniemy is steering the install from afar while live-streaming practice as he tends to family. The through-line is blunt: free-agency swings from 2025 are under the microscope, the 53-man picture is still mushy after one week-plus of camp, and every rep from here is about building a team that can climb out of the AFC pack rather than assume the old hierarchy returns on brand alone.

Roster construction is the real camp battle. When a shop misses in free agency, camp becomes the correction mechanism—draft picks, swing linemen, and depth pieces get accelerated reps because the paid solutions did not land. Benson seeing work at right guard is not a trivia note; it is the staff hunting for a combination that can protect Mahomes and re-establish early-down leverage after a year that went sideways. Arrowhead Pride’s early 53-man projection work underscores how unsettled the margins still are on both sides of the ball.

Defensively, Spagnuolo does not do panic, but ‘long way to go’ after padded practices is a flashing yellow. The schematic answers—Cover-2 shells to take away explosives, simulated pressures that free second-level rushers—only matter if the personnel fits the triggers. That is the camp homework: who can win a one-on-one enough to let Spags call his full menu when the lights rise in September.

Context check on expectations: Polymarket has the Chiefs around 9.5% to win the 2027 AFC and 6.7% to take the Super Bowl. That is not dynasty pricing; that is a market pricing a bounce-back path with real variance. Harrison Butker tweaking his routine after a nine-miss year fits the same theme—established names re-earning trust. Meanwhile the building itself is getting its name back: Chiefs and G.E.H.A. are restoring Arrowhead Stadium branding for 2026. Sentiment helps sell posters and Red Thursday runs. It does not create a clean pocket or a third-down stop.

What it means for the Sundays ahead is simple. Identity first: inside-zone credibility, play-action boots that put Mahomes on the edge, and Spags pressures that do not break the back end. If the OL sorts right guard and the defense shortens that ‘long way,’ the market number is soft. If camp battles stall and the 2025 free-agency miss calcifies, 6-11 becomes a trend line instead of a floor.

The storylines

What we’re watching

OL Battle

Kahlil Benson getting right-guard run

Day 9 went back to full pads with heavy run-game and team work, and Benson’s snaps at right guard are a live audition for early-down stability. If he sticks, it reshapes how aggressive Bieniemy’s zone and boot menu can be without putting Mahomes in perpetual second-and-long.

Coordinator

Bieniemy present from a distance

Bieniemy remains away caring for his wife and family, but he has been live-streaming camp practice to stay on the install. That keeps the offensive language consistent even if the voice on the grass is not his—critical when the roster still needs a defined identity after 6-11.

Defense

Spags’ unit still climbing

After a lightened Day 8 following three straight padded practices, the honest read from camp was that the defense has a long way to go. Scheme will be fine; the question is whether the front and back end are finishing plays enough to justify Spagnuolo’s pressure package in September.

Special Teams

Butker rewriting the process

After nine misses a year ago, Harrison Butker is openly tweaking his routine with his career leverage in play. In a rebuild year, reliable field position and finishing drives are not luxuries—they are how you steal games while the roster hardens.

Front Office

2025 free agency under review

Camp is doubling as a verdict on last year’s free-agency class, with analysis arguing Kansas City may have whiffed on key bets. That frames the next 53-man cutdown as correction, not crowning—draft capital and internal promotions have to outplay the contracts that did not deliver.

Brand

Arrowhead name restored for 2026

Chiefs and G.E.H.A. announced the return of Arrowhead Stadium branding beginning with the 2026 season. Nice emotional reset for the fan base after 6-11; the football reset still has to happen between the tackles.

Market

Polymarket prices a climb, not a coronation

At roughly 9.5% to win the AFC and 6.7% to win the Super Bowl for 2027, the market is treating Kansas City as a live contender path with real doubt. That matches the tape from camp: talent at QB, questions everywhere it touches.

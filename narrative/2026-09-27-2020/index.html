The edge

Polymarket’s 8.9 percent Super Bowl ticket and a 72.6 percent ESPN FPI nod (Chiefs -6, 45.5 at DraftKings) are the right temperature for a 3-0 team that just won 24-10 while losing the first-down, third-down, and possession fights in Miami — Las Vegas is where that temperature either holds or last year’s 6-11 tape starts to look like a sequel.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

W
Miami Dolphins Week 3 · Sun, Sep 27 · 12:00 PM CT
KC 24–10

Kansas City opened Hard Rock Stadium with a 10-yard Kenneth Walker III run, answered Ollie Gordon II’s 3-yard score with a 5-yard Walker touchdown catch, then spent the middle of the game in a slog before Harrison Butker’s 34-yard field goal and Travis Kelce’s 11-yard catch from Patrick Mahomes at 2:55 of the fourth made it 24-10. It was a grind, not a coronation: 334 total yards, one turnover, and a defense that won the scoreboard while losing the clock.

The opening drive was the version of this offense Eric Bieniemy sold in September — Walker downhill, red-zone finish, 7-0. After that, Kansas City played like a team protecting a lead it did not yet have. Mahomes’ 20-of-24 is elite accuracy and a modest event: 246 yards means the ball was coming out on time into the first window, not the third. The interception is the only real scar on his night, but the scar on the unit is volume. Eighteen first downs after the Colts game had turned into a first-down factory is a choice, a Miami front that got off blocks, or both. On this tape it played like both.

Walker’s box (18 for 70, plus the 5-yard score as a receiver) is a closer’s night, not a hammer’s. He won the two possessions that put six on the board and otherwise ran into a stacked look that Kansas City never consistently broke. Rice (7-88) was the adult in the room between the hashes; Kelce was the adult in the red zone when the game finally needed closing. Third down at 3-of-7 is how you post 25:39 of possession in a game you control on the scoreboard. That is not a Mahomes problem. That is a stay-on-schedule problem — second-and-long, empty menu, punt, repeat.

Spagnuolo’s side will take 10 points allowed every week of the year, and the two Miami turnovers are the entire margin between a statement and a sweat. Credit the ball. Do not credit the third-down defense: 8-of-16, 19 first downs, 119 rush yards, 34:21 of clock. Bolton’s 11 tackles are a MIKE playing winning football and also a usage warning — too many snaps in space, too many rallies, not enough negative plays to make Miami uncomfortable. Pre-game, the pass rush was supposed to feast on a struggling Dolphins group after disappearing against Daniel Jones. Miami still netted 210 through the air and kept drives alive. The score says the defense won. The stopwatch says it survived.

What held up

  • Mahomes’ first-window accuracy (20-of-24, 246, 2 TD) kept drives from turning into disasters even when they stalled.
  • Walker as a closer: 10-yard run, 5-yard receiving score, 18 carries of physical tone even without the chunk yards.
  • Rice as the possession X — seven catches, 88 yards, the one skill player who consistently moved the sticks.
  • Kelce’s 11-yard touchdown at 2:55 of the fourth, the exact red-zone answer this offense is built to cash.
  • Two Miami turnovers and a 10-point ceiling, which is how you leave 3-0 when the clock is against you.

What broke

  • Third-down offense at 3-of-7, the simplest reason the possession time died.
  • The run game as a volume identity: 88 team rush yards after the Denver hammer and Walker’s 117-yard Colts night.
  • Third-down defense at 8-of-16 allowed, which handed Miami 34:21 of clock.
  • A pass rush that still did not close the show after being flagged as the Week 2 problem.
  • The hidden game: 18 first downs to 19, 25:39 of possession, one offensive turnover that kept a 14-10 game alive into the fourth.

Efficiency without the hammer

Mahomes at 20-of-24 with a 119.8 passer rating is a winning quarterback night. It is not a winning offensive identity if Walker is at 3.9 a carry and the unit is 3-of-7 on third down. Las Vegas will play the Miami script — dirty early downs, make Kansas City throw to stay alive — unless Bieniemy reclaims first-and-10.

Takeaways covered a long afternoon

Miami out-gained nobody and still won first downs, third downs, and possession. Two turnovers are why Arrowhead Pride could file a 24-10 and still say last year’s team almost showed up. That is not a sustainable model against a division opponent that will not donate the ball.

The scoring package still works

Walker from 10 yards out, Walker from five through the air, Kelce from 11 with the game in the fourth — the red-zone pairing is intact. Rice as the volume receiver (7-88) is the chain-moving piece that has to keep eating if the run game stays in the 80s. That trio is the 24 on the scoreboard. Everyone else is the 10 they allowed to linger.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

3-0

Kansas City is 3-0, on the road in the division next, and carrying two different tapes. The first two weeks looked like a new hammer. Miami looked like 2025 with better finishing. The next 10 days — Raiders, then a bye — decide which tape is the real one.

What we need to work on

Third-down offense that does not wait for 20-of-24

Three conversions in seven tries is how a surgical Mahomes night still produces only 18 first downs. Allegiant will be a man-and-pressure building; the mesh, boot, and screen answers have to be in the first two calls, not the fourth.

Walker as a first-down identity, not just a red-zone closer

Eighteen carries for 70 is honest work and not enough to keep a division front from pinning its ears back. Bieniemy’s downhill package has to show up on 1st-and-10 in Las Vegas or Mahomes will be asked to repeat a completion percentage that is not a weekly plan.

Getting off the field on third down

Miami went 8-of-16 and held the ball 34:21. Spagnuolo can live with 10 points; he cannot live with that snap count two weeks in a row. Simulated pressure has to become a takeaway engine, not a changeup, against a Raiders offense that will try to win the same clock fight.

Pass-rush answers that do not depend on the other team’s mistakes

The Week 3 preview from Arrowhead Addict framed Miami as the chance to get the rush back on track after Indianapolis. Two hundred ten net passing yards against a 0-2 team is not a full answer. Las Vegas, as a home underdog, will empty the protection menu. The front has to win without waiting for a gift fumble.

What to think about

Is 3-0 a new team or a better closer on last year’s tape?

Arrowhead Pride’s read after Miami — last year’s Chiefs almost coming out — is the debate in the building. The scoring pairing of Walker and Kelce still finishes. The early-down and third-down tape still stalls. Reid’s staff has one game before the bye to pick an identity and put it on film.

Tackle and safety availability after a long inactive list

Week 3 sat Josh Simmons and Diego Pounds (tackles), Chamarri Conner (safety), Jared Wiley (tight end), and Jack Pyburn and Tyreke Smith (defensive ends). That is not automatically next week’s injury report, but it is a depth chart the Raiders game will stress if any of those names remain out of the mix.

Justin Fields as a roster asset, not a subplot

Adam Schefter’s note that the Giants have interest, via Arrowhead Addict, is a market signal. It is not a reason to play Sunday distracted. If Kansas City is 3-0 with Mahomes playing this clean, Fields’ value is a general-manager conversation for the bye, not a game-week distraction.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Las Vegas Raiders

Noon on CBS at Allegiant is a division road game with a bye on the other side of it. Kansas City is a 6-point favorite for a reason, and Miami just showed every AFC West staff how to make this offense play small. The assignment is to impose Walker early, cash Mahomes’ rhythm into first downs instead of just completions, and refuse the 34-minute clock that made 24-10 look louder than it was.

How they match up

This is style versus scar tissue. Kansas City has the durable quarterback edge with Mahomes and a scoring package (Walker, Rice, Kelce) that has produced in all three games. Las Vegas, at home as an underdog in a rivalry building, will try to win the Miami game: dirty early downs, sit on the run, lengthen the game, and hope a 3-of-7 third-down offense blinks. The leverage is first-and-10. If Walker and the interior make the Raiders defend the run, play-action boots and Rice/Kelce in the short intermediate become the 20-of-24 night with actual points attached. If Kansas City gets behind the sticks, Allegiant becomes a pressure building and Spagnuolo’s defense is on the field again into the 30s of possession. The Chiefs should be the better team. They still have to prove they are the more physical one on the road.

Stay on schedule so third down is a choice

Script Walker inside zone and the easy screen until Las Vegas has to load the box. Then boot Mahomes. The Miami tape died on second-and-long; the Raiders tape cannot start there.

Make Kelce and Rice the third-down answer, not the panic button

Mesh, sit, and option routes for Kelce — the same family as the 11-yard closer — plus Rice on the move. If Kansas City is throwing 50-50 balls on 3rd-and-8, the Miami stall is back.

Two-high until Las Vegas proves it can run, then steal a down with simulated pressure

Cover-2 to take away the shot and let Bolton rally, which is what 11 tackles already said he will do. On obvious passing downs, Spagnuolo’s zone blitz has to manufacture the rush that did not arrive as a four-man in Miami.

Win the hidden game Miami stole

First downs, third downs, possession. Kansas City lost all three and still won because of takeaways. Do not ask the ball to bounce twice in a rivalry stadium. Finish drives with Walker and Kelce; get off the field with a negative play, not a 12-yard conversion.

  1. Open with a six-play run/play-action cluster: inside zone to Walker, the same zone again, then under-center boot to Rice or Kelce so Las Vegas cannot sit on the Miami run fits.
  2. On the first 3rd-and-5 or shorter, mesh with Kelce and Rice as the rub pair and Walker as the outlet — do not wait until the third series to install a conversion answer.
  3. If the Raiders pin their ears back on a true passing down, spacing screen to Walker with the line leading; that is the easy-offense button Miami never had to defend.
  4. Defensively, start in a Cover-2 shell on early downs to steal the shot and force Las Vegas to drive the length of the field the way Miami did — then tag a zone blitz at Bolton’s call on 3rd-and-6 or more.
  5. Red-zone pairing is non-negotiable: Walker from the 10 in, Kelce from the 12 in. Those two scores were the entire 24 in Miami. Do not get cute in a division game.
  6. Two-minute at the end of the first half is a live script, not a handshake. A 3-of-7 third-down team cannot donate a possession before the break on the road.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

After two wins that looked like a reinvention, Kansas City went to Hard Rock Stadium and won like a team trying not to beat itself. Patrick Mahomes was almost perfect on volume that never got loud — 20-of-24, 246 yards, two scores, one interception — Kenneth Walker III punched in a 10-yard run and a 5-yard throw, and Travis Kelce’s 11-yard catch at 2:55 of the fourth finally made 24-10 feel safe. The rest of the ESPN box is the hangover: 18 first downs, 88 rush yards, 3-of-7 on third down, 25:39 of possession. Miami answered with 19 first downs, 119 on the ground, 8-of-16 on third down, and 34:21 of clock. Two Dolphins turnovers are why this is filed as a win and not a referendum. Arrowhead Pride’s rapid reaction called it last year’s Chiefs almost coming out, and that is the honest read heading into a noon division road game at the Raiders.

Weeks 1 and 2 had an identity. Denver was the Bieniemy hammer; Indianapolis was Mahomes cashing the pass after the run had already spoken. Miami was neither. Walker still got 18 carries, but 70 yards is work, not a statement, and the passing game lived on completion percentage instead of explosives. Rashee Rice’s 7-for-88 was the chain-moving tape worth keeping. Everything else was complementary football failing in public: an efficient quarterback standing on the sideline while Nick Bolton piled up 11 tackles because Steve Spagnuolo’s unit could not get off the field.

That is the 3-0 problem, not the 3-0 celebration. The Chiefs are unbeaten and still the class of this start, but the Miami film is a checklist Andy Reid’s staff has seen before — stalled early downs, a thin third-down menu, a front that does not close drives, and a defense asked to tackle for half the afternoon. The pass rush was already the open item after Indianapolis; Miami’s 210 net passing yards and eight conversions did not close the argument. Takeaways covered it. Las Vegas will not volunteer two of those.

Sunday at the Raiders is the first AFC West road game of 2026, on CBS, with a bye sitting in Week 5. That calendar is a trap if the building treats Allegiant like a layover. DraftKings laying Kansas City -6 on a 45.5 total is respect, not a bye. ESPN FPI’s 72.6 percent is the same temperature: favored, not immune. The assignment is simple and unglamorous. Run Walker enough to make play-action real, convert a third down without waiting for Mahomes to go 20-of-24 again, and make Las Vegas earn every first down the way Miami did not have to. Then rest. Not before.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Walker downhill, first-and-10

Situation Opening series and every 1st-and-10 vs. Las Vegas after Miami held Kansas City to 88 rush yards

Eric Bieniemy’s identity vanished between the hashes in Miami (Walker 18 for 70) after it had defined Weeks 1 and 2. A home-underdog Raiders front will sit on that tape unless Kansas City punches first-and-10 and forces the box to load. That is the only way Mahomes’ 20-of-24 night turns into first downs instead of another 25-minute possession game.

Coaching point Walker: one cut, no bounce unless the back-side A-gap is a gift. Interior has to win first level on the first two snaps so Las Vegas cannot play the Miami fit all afternoon. Bieniemy should keep the fullback/tight-end combo on the field long enough that the Raiders have to declare run.

Offense

Sell Walker, move Mahomes

Situation 2nd-and-5 or 1st-and-10 after two inside-zone looks, Allegiant early downs

Mahomes was lethal when the first window was easy (20-of-24). He was ordinary when the drive died in long yardage. Boot off Walker is how Andy Reid turns a loaded Raiders box into Rice on the move or Kelce in the flat without asking the tackles — two of whom sat in Week 3 — to hold a five-step drop into a rivalry rush.

Coaching point Mahomes: sell the fake with the eyes, get the shoulders around, and take the first open man. Rice runs the over; Kelce the arrow. If the Raiders end comes up the field, throw it now — do not climb and recreate the Miami interception.

Offense

The 3-of-7 answer

Situation 3rd-and-4 to 3rd-and-6 vs. Las Vegas man/pressure, the down Miami won and Kansas City lost

Kansas City went 3-of-7 on third down in a game it controlled on the scoreboard. Kelce’s 11-yard touchdown is the same family of throw — tight window, after the rub, in rhythm. Rice as the opposite mesh runner (7-88 on the day) is the other adult. This is the conversion concept that keeps Spagnuolo’s defense off the field and avoids another 34-minute opponent clock.

Coaching point Kelce and Rice have to alter the timing so the linebackers collide; Walker checks if both meshers are covered. Mahomes: hitch once, throw. No hero ball on 3rd-and-5 on the road in the division.

Offense

Easy offense vs. a pinned-ear rush

Situation 3rd-and-7+ or 2nd-and-long when Las Vegas sells out after seeing the Miami stall

Home dogs in this rivalry will rush. Kansas City’s tackles were already a Week 3 availability story (Simmons, Pounds inactive), and the pass rush conversation on the other side of the ball is loud enough that Las Vegas will try to get home first. Walker as a screen runner — line leading in space — is how Bieniemy punishes that look without asking Mahomes to drop 40 times after an 88-yard rushing night.

Coaching point Set the look as protection, then get the ball out on the second step. Guards have to climb to the second level immediately; Rice and Kelce crack/clear so Walker can take the first tackle upfield, not sideways. This is a possession play, not a homerun call — five to eight yards wins the down.

Defense

Steal the shot, rally with Bolton

Situation Early downs and 3rd-and-medium vs. Las Vegas, after Miami went 8-of-16 and held the ball 34:21

Miami’s entire afternoon was drive football, not explosives, and Bolton still needed 11 tackles because the defense never got a three-and-out diet. Two-high takes away the shot a home underdog will hunt on first down and puts five underneath to rally. That is how Spagnuolo turns a 10-point night into an actual short-field night instead of another tackling drill.

Coaching point Corners squat and funnel; safeties split the post. Bolton: fit the run first, then rally to the checkdown — no fly-bys. The coaching point from Miami is simple: do not give up the conversion on 3rd-and-6 because somebody opened the deep middle hunting a sack.

Defense

Manufacture the rush Miami never felt

Situation 3rd-and-6 or longer vs. Las Vegas, the down the pass rush was supposed to own in Week 3 and did not

Arrowhead Addict framed Miami as the perfect chance to get the rush back on track after it disappeared against Indianapolis. Miami still netted 210 passing yards and converted eight third downs. Spagnuolo’s signature — show heat, drop a lineman, send a second-level runner — is how Kansas City creates a negative play without winning four one-on-ones, and how it avoids asking Bolton to tackle on every snap again.

Coaching point Sell four-down, then drop the end into the hook and send Bolton or the nickel off the edge. The dropped lineman has to occupy the throwing lane Mahomes’ counterpart will want on the hot. Finish: strip-clinch, two arms. Takeaways won Miami. Do not wait for Las Vegas to donate them.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Andy Reid vs. a division road noon

Reid has seen this movie in the AFC West: favored, coming off an ugly win, bye on the other side. The script should be boring on purpose — Walker, boot, Kelce — because Miami already proved the clever version stalls.

Eric Bieniemy’s early-down identity

Eighty-eight rush yards is not the Denver tape and not enough to protect a tackle rotation that sat two names in Week 3. Bieniemy has to win first-and-10 in Allegiant or Sunday becomes another completion-percentage night with no clock attached.

Steve Spagnuolo’s third-down menu

Cover-2 to take away the shot, zone blitz to manufacture the rush. Ten points allowed is the floor he will take; 8-of-16 and 34:21 is the ceiling he cannot live with against a rivalry opponent that will try to copy Miami’s clock.

Yards without a touchdown, then Payton on Monday night

Kansas City sat on the ball for 33 minutes against Seattle and still left with a 9–9 tie — now the real snaps start against Denver on ESPN.

Cold open

The edge

A short home favorite and a 42.5 total say this opener is a trench fight; the 9–9 tape says Kansas City only wins it if the red zone finally pays the run game.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

T
Seattle Seahawks Preseason Week 4 · Sat Aug 29
KC 9–9

At Arrowhead, Kansas City controlled the football and the line of scrimmage, then spent the night kicking field goals. Jason Myers opened with a 46-yarder, Butker answered from 28 and 24 before half, Jalen Milroe hit Nick Vannett from four yards in the third (Myers missed the extra point), and Butker’s 19-yarder in the fourth locked the 9–9 final. Nussmeier went 22-of-34 for 198 yards with an interception; Brashard Smith ran 16 times for 72; Andrew Armstrong caught five for 54; R Mason Thomas posted the defensive headline sack.

The tape is a Bieniemy calling card that never cashes. Twenty-one first downs and 155 rushing yards against a Seattle group that managed only 54 on the ground means Kansas City won early downs the old-fashioned way: under center, downhill, stay on schedule. Third down at 9-of-17 is not a broken offense. The broken part is what happened once they got into Butker’s range. Three makes from 28, 24, and 19 are the profile of a team that can move the chains and cannot finish a drive in the paint.

Nussmeier’s night was complementary, not catalytic. One hundred ninety-eight net passing yards on 34 attempts is a game-manager line, and the interception is the one drive that actually died on its own instead of at the 10-yard line. Armstrong as the leading receiver (5 for 54) tells you the passing game was a possession tool, not a shot chart. If you are installing an identity for Mahomes, that is useful: the run is real enough to sell play-action. If you are trying to convince yourself the red zone is solved, it is not. Seattle’s only touchdown was a four-yard throw; Kansas City never got there.

Steve Spagnuolo’s side did its preseason job. Seattle was held to 13 first downs, 4-of-11 on third down, 266 total yards, and 26:48 of possession. The run fit was clean — 54 yards is a win for a front that is still adding waiver pieces — and Thomas getting home is the one individual flash the pass rush needed on the last tape. The defense did not lose this game. It also did not have to face a finished Payton script. Treat the 9–9 as a complementary-football warning, not a defensive crisis: the unit can keep a modest total in play if the offense stops settling for three.

What held up

  • Downhill run game: 155 yards, Smith as the featured back, early-down control
  • Possession and third down: 33:12, 21 first downs, 9-of-17
  • Run defense: Seattle held to 54 yards and 13 first downs
  • R Mason Thomas as a pass-rush marker (1 sack)
  • Butker from chip-shot range (28, 24, 19)

What broke

  • Red-zone finishing: zero offensive touchdowns despite multiple trips inside Butker range
  • Explosive passing: 198 yards on 34 Nussmeier attempts, Armstrong as the leading receiver
  • Ball security: the Nussmeier interception was the one self-inflicted drive killer
  • Payoff on long, winning possessions — the definition of empty calories

The run game is the identity, not the closer

Brashard Smith’s 16-for-72 and the team’s 155 yards were the cleanest thing on the tape. Until someone punches it in from inside the 15, though, that identity is just a long field-goal operation. Denver will dare Kansas City to finish.

Third down without a payoff is still a problem

Nine conversions in 17 tries kept drives alive and kept the clock in Kansas City’s pocket for 33:12. Conversion rate is not the issue. Scoring rate is. The 19-yard Butker kick is the tell: they arrived, they stalled, they kicked.

The front flashed just enough to take into Week 1

Thomas’ sack and Seattle’s 54 rushing yards are the two defensive numbers that travel. The rest of the Week 1 pass rush still has to be proven against a Payton protection plan, especially with Thomas missing the last camp-style practice after that tape.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

6-11 (2025) · 0-0 (2026 regular season)

Kansas City is a 6-11 roster opening as a modest home favorite with a live run tape, a quarterback the opponent is treating as full, and a red zone that has not scored a touchdown when it mattered last. The 53 is set enough to play Denver; it is not set enough to assume the 9–9 problems vanished with the cutdown.

What we need to work on

Finish the inside-zone drives

The Seattle tape gave Bieniemy the look he wants — 155 yards, stay ahead of the chains — and then settled for three Butker kicks. Against Denver’s defense on a 42.5 total, those have to become six. Goal-line and 12-to-8-yard-line answers are the install this week, not more first-down theory.

Mahomes’ first live dropbacks of the year

Payton is already on record that he does not expect Mahomes to be limited after the leg surgery. That is a compliment and a warning: Denver will not vanilla the coverage or the rush. Timing with Worthy, the in-breaking mesh game, and the boot game off the run have to be sharp on snap one, not snap 40.

Trench availability on both sides

Trey Smith is playing through a hip that cost him a chunk of camp. Simmons’ back is the weekly watch. Thomas missed Thursday after being the sack on the last tape. If any of those three is a reduced Week 1, the 155-yard identity and the one-sack flash both shrink against a division opponent.

Clarify the receiver pecking order

Xavier Worthy is still the long-term swing skill in the passing game, and one insider is already selling Cyrus Allen as the best receiver on the roster. The Seattle leading receiver was Andrew Armstrong. Somebody has to win the red-zone and third-down jobs before Payton can sit on the run.

What to think about

Is the 42.5 total the real scouting report?

DraftKings posting 42.5 and FPI sitting at 58.5 percent for Kansas City is the market saying complementary football, not fireworks. If Reid scripts this like a shootout, he is fighting the tape and the number. If he scripts it like Seattle — run, possess, finish — he is playing the game that is actually in front of him.

How much of the 6-11 doubt is still in the building?

The 2025 collapse put the biggest stars under a new kind of scrutiny, and executives have the Chiefs outside the AFC’s top five. Week 1 will not settle that. It will tell you whether this team still plays scared of its own red zone or whether Mahomes and the run game can reassert the old hierarchy against a division rival.

What the fan fatal-flaw vote is actually pointing at

The final preseason Reacts poll asked Chiefs fans to name the potential downfall before a snap of regular-season football. Whatever the Kingdom circled, the tape already circled finishing. That is the debate worth having on the desk: scheme, personnel, or just the last 15 yards.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Denver Broncos

Division opener, Monday Night Football at Arrowhead, both quarterbacks coming off leg surgeries, and a three-point home number that only cashes if Kansas City turns the Seattle run tape into points. Payton will not gift a limited Mahomes; Reid cannot gift another field-goal diet.

How they match up

This is complementary football versus a Sean Payton operation that will test protection rules, play-action shots, and whether Spagnuolo can keep explosives off the board. Kansas City’s leverage is the under-center run it just showed (155 yards, Smith featured) plus home field and a defense that just held Seattle to 54 rushing yards. Denver’s leverage is Payton’s script against a patched tackle/interior picture (Simmons’ back, Smith’s hip) and a Chiefs red zone that has not scored a touchdown on the last tape. The 42.5 total is the honest overlay: whoever finishes inside the 20, not whoever wins first down, decides it.

Make Denver defend the boot and the inside zone on the same series

Bieniemy has to marry the 155-yard look to Mahomes moving. If the first-down run is real, the play-action boot is the easiest way to get a surgically repaired quarterback on the perimeter with defined reads instead of sitting in a muddy pocket.

Red-zone mesh and tight-end leak before Butker’s number is even up

The 19- and 24-yard field goals are the enemy. Versus man, mesh with a corner/sit combo and a back as the outlet is how you stop settling. Versus zone, leak the tight end off play-action. Call it before second-and-long from the 12.

Spagnuolo two-deep until Payton proves the run

Seattle never got going on the ground. If Denver’s backfield looks similar, sit in Cover-2, take away the shot, and tackle. The moment Payton starts converting early downs, flip to simulated pressure and make the protection declare.

Keep Thomas in the game as a rusher, not a spectator

He was the sack on the last tape and then missed Thursday. If he is active, he has to be a designated second-and-long piece, not a rotational afterthought. Waiver additions are depth; Thomas is the one named flash the pass rush has.

  1. Open 12/21 personnel under center: inside zone to Smith, same look twice if the first cut is plus-four.
  2. On the first plus-run, boot Mahomes to his strong side with the tight end leaking and Worthy as the over route — get a completion, get him breathing.
  3. Third-and-5 to 7: mesh, not a hero shot. Use the back as the outlet the way the Seattle possession game asked.
  4. Red zone: first-down run, second-down play-action, third-down mesh/sit. Do not let a 2nd-and-8 become a 19-yard field goal.
  5. Defensively, start Cover-2 on obvious Payton dropbacks; the first zone blitz should be a simulated five that drops a lineman, not a clean six-man that opens the seam.
  6. If Denver pins the ears back after an incompletion, spacing screen to Smith — punish the rush that is hunting a quarterback coming off leg surgery.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The last thing Kansas City put on tape in 2026 was a possession clinic that refused to become a touchdown. Against Seattle the Chiefs ran it 33:12, piled up 353 yards, converted 9-of-17 third downs, and still walked off in a 9–9 draw because the red zone kept turning into Harrison Butker. That is the hangover from a 6-11 season that has everyone suddenly doubting the biggest stars, and it is the problem Andy Reid and Eric Bieniemy have 16 days to solve before Denver arrives on Monday Night Football.

The market is not asking them to look like a Super Bowl favorite on September 15. ESPN FPI has Kansas City at 58.5 percent in the opener, DraftKings posts the Chiefs -3 with a 42.5 total, and Polymarket still prices this roster at 6.2 percent to win the 2027 title and 9.5 percent to win the AFC. Those numbers are a vote for Arrowhead and a grind, not for a finished product. NFL executives already have Kansas City outside the AFC’s top five, which is the honest shadow of last year’s collapse.

What the building is actually working with is more specific than the vibes. Sean Payton is not expecting Patrick Mahomes to be limited after offseason leg surgery, which means Denver will game-plan the real quarterback, not a managed one. Trey Smith is on the field after a hip issue that stole the back half of Missouri Western and a specialist visit, and he is already on record that you never feel 100 percent playing this sport. Josh Simmons’ back has been the weekly uneasiness, even as the team’s calm on him is supposed to tell you something. Rookie edge R Mason Thomas, who actually sacked Seattle, then missed the last camp-style practice. That is the Week 1 roster: a run identity that showed up, a quarterback the opponent is treating as full, and a handful of trenches questions that still have to hold for 60 minutes.

Brett Veach has not been idle around the edges. Kansas City claimed two defensive players off waivers, hosted a 10-player tryout with special-teams help in mind, signed international tight end Thomas Odukoya to the practice squad, and is sitting on about $4.7 million in cap space. None of that replaces a red-zone touchdown. The next Sunday that matters is not another dress rehearsal with Garrett Nussmeier; it is Mahomes, Payton, and a Broncos defense that will make Kansas City prove the 155-yard night was an identity and not a preseason mirage.

Film room

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-center inside zone, opening script

Situation 1st-and-10, first two series vs. Denver at Arrowhead — establish the Seattle run tape before Payton can sit on Mahomes

Kansas City just ran for 155 yards (Brashard Smith 16-for-72) and owned 33:12 of clock. Trey Smith is back on the field after the hip layoff, and a 42.5 total says this game will be won on early downs. If the zone cut is plus-four, Denver cannot hunt a quarterback coming off leg surgery.

Coaching point Bieniemy wants zone steps and one cut, not east-west. Smith has to press the line of scrimmage; Trey Smith has to play through the hip on the double team and not leak the back side. Reid should stay under center long enough that Payton’s front has to respect the iso before the first boot.

Offense

Play-action boot off the plus-run

Situation 2nd-and-5/6 after a Smith inside-zone gain — first live Mahomes drop of 2026 vs. a Payton defense that is not planning for a limited quarterback

Sean Payton has already said he does not expect Mahomes to be limited, so the rush will be full-speed. The cleanest protection for a surgically repaired leg is to move the pocket off the run Denver just had to stop. This is also how you stop a 198-yard, no-shot passing tape from becoming the regular-season identity.

Coaching point Sell the zone fake with the same footwork as the 155-yard tape. Mahomes’ first read is the leaking tight end; do not climb the boot and invite a hit on the plant leg. Worthy stretches the over. If the end crashes, throw it; if he holds, keep running — that is the point of the call.

Offense

Mesh to stop the field-goal diet

Situation Red zone, 2nd-and-7 or 3rd-and-5 from the 12 — the exact down-and-distance that became Butker from 19 and 24 against Seattle

Twenty-one first downs and zero touchdowns is the whole preseason warning. Worthy’s speed on the corner route and a rub from the opposite shallow is how you beat man when the run has already been stacked. Cyrus Allen’s buzz as a receiver only matters if someone actually wins this down. Armstrong’s 5-for-54 night was possession; this call is points.

Coaching point Bieniemy has to call it before the drive gets ugly, not after two stuffed runs. Teach the shallow to flatten and the sit to occupy the hook. The back is the outlet — Nussmeier needed that versus Seattle, and Mahomes will take it if the mesh is covered. No hero ball from the 12.

Offense

Smith spacing screen vs. the hunt

Situation 2nd-and-9 after an incompletion, when Denver pins the ears back at a quarterback they know is coming off leg surgery

Payton will not vanilla the rush if Kansas City gets behind the chains. The Seattle tape already showed a live ground game and a limited shot game; the screen is how you make an aggressive front pay without asking Mahomes to hold the ball. Smith just handled 16 carries — he can be the receiver in space while the line leads.

Coaching point Get Trey Smith and the playside tackle out in space; no holding on the first wing. Mahomes’ throw has to be on time so he is not taking a hit on the plant. If Simmons’ back is an issue in pass sets, this is also protection by scheme: you cannot sack what you cannot reach.

Defense

Spags Cover-2 on Payton’s first shot script

Situation 1st-and-10 and 3rd-and-7+ in the first quarter — take away the explosive Payton wants on Monday Night and keep the 42.5 total honest

Seattle managed only 13 first downs, 266 yards, and 54 on the ground against this defense. The next step is not more blitz; it is making Payton drive the field. Two-deep with five under matches a modest total and a Chiefs offense that may still be kicking field goals. Do not gift a seam shot while Mahomes is still finding his legs.

Coaching point Spagnuolo’s corners have to funnel, not gamble. Safeties split the post and rally to the tackle — the Seattle run defense only travels if the second level actually fits. Thomas, if active after missing Thursday, sets the edge rather than running upfield and opening the boot. Tackle, get off, punt.

Defense

Simulated pressure, Thomas as the extra

Situation 2nd-and-8 vs. Payton obvious pass — after Cover-2 has forced a long-yardage, now make the protection declare

Thomas was the sack on the last tape, Kansas City just claimed two defensive players off waivers, and Spagnuolo’s signature is showing heat while dropping a lineman. Payton’s protection rules are the chess match; a clean six-man is what he wants. A simulated five with a second-level rusher is how you get Mahomes a short field without exploding your own shell.

Coaching point Show five at the snap, drop the three-tech into the hook, send a linebacker. Thomas either contains or loops — do not let him disappear after one missed practice. If he is inactive, the waiver additions have to be the extra rusher, which is a thinner plan. The throwaway is a win; the seam is a loss.

The storylines

What we’re watching

QB

Payton is planning for full Mahomes

The Broncos coach is not expecting Patrick Mahomes to be limited after offseason surgery on a leg injury, and both quarterbacks are preparing to play. That removes the managed-snap fantasy. Kansas City has to script movement and easy completions so the first live pressure of 2026 is not a standing hit.

OL

Trey Smith’s hip is a Week 1 condition, not a camp anecdote

Smith missed a chunk of Missouri Western, saw a hip specialist, then got back on the field and said you never feel 100 percent playing football. The 155-yard run game needs him to be functional, not poetic. If the hip shows up in pass protection, Mahomes’ first night gets muddy fast.

EDGE

R Mason Thomas: sack on Saturday, missing on Thursday

The rookie edge was the defensive leader in the Seattle finale, then sat out the last training-camp-style practice. Spagnuolo’s Week 1 pass-rush plan has to have a version with him and a version without him. Denver will notice which one walks out for MNF.

WR

Worthy’s health vs. Allen’s buzz

A healthy Xavier Worthy is still the cleanest path to stabilizing the passing game. At the same time, an insider is already calling Cyrus Allen the best receiver on the roster after an offseason in which Veach largely left the room alone. Somebody other than Andrew Armstrong has to be the Week 1 answer.

LT

The building’s calm on Josh Simmons

Simmons missing practice with a back issue has become a familiar uneasiness, but the internal temperature is being sold as composure, not panic. Left tackle against a Payton opener is not a place for vibes. Watch the first obvious passing down: if Mahomes is stepping up, the back is a story; if he is scrambling left, it is a problem.

FRONT OFFICE

Waiver claims, tryouts, and $4.7 million

Veach claimed two defensive players, ran a 10-player tryout tilted toward special teams, and added Thomas Odukoya to the practice squad while sitting on roughly $4.7 million in cap space. That is how you open a season after 6-11: keep turning over the edges while the stars try to look like stars again.

Chess match

Coaching & adjustments

Reid vs. Payton on Monday Night

This is a script game between two staffs that will not pretend it is September football in the abstract. Reid has to open like the Seattle tape (run, possess) and close like a team that can still score touchdowns. Payton will test whether Mahomes is truly unlimited.

Bieniemy’s identity vs. the red-zone staff

The OC already has the downhill look. The remaining install is the last 15 yards: boot, mesh, and the decision to stop calling Butker’s number from the 12. That is a coaching choice, not a talent mystery.

Spagnuolo’s first-down patience

The Seattle tape rewards a two-deep start. The temptation on MNF is to hunt the quarterback with Thomas and the new waiver pieces. Spags wins this by making Payton drive 12 plays, then blitzing from a lead or a long-yardage look.

Last game

What the tape said

A real review of the most recent result — not a box-score dump, and not a skip-ahead.

W
Miami Dolphins Week 3 · Sun, Sep 27 · 12:00 PM CT
KC 24–10

Kansas City led 7-0 on Walker’s 10-yard run 2:06 into the game, answered Miami’s 3-yard touchdown with a 5-yard score for 14-7, then closed a 14-10 game with a 34-yard Butker field goal and an 11-yard touchdown for 24-10. Mahomes was 20-of-24 for 246, Walker had 18 carries for 70 and the opening score, Rice led all receivers at 7-for-88, and Nick Bolton posted 11 tackles while Miami held the ball 34:21.

The opening drive was the version of this offense that still scares people. Mahomes hit Travis Kelce deep middle for 48 yards to the Miami 19, J. Moore reported in as eligible, and Walker went up the middle for the 10-yard touchdown. That is Andy Reid’s identity in two snaps: a chunk over the top, then a downhill finish. Walker scored again early in the second quarter on a 5-yard touchdown catch from Mahomes that made it 14-7. The rest of the afternoon was more strained. Kansas City stalled on third down (3-of-7), turned it over on downs at the Miami 12 when H. Nourzad reported eligible and Walker was stuffed for no gain, and Mahomes’ deep-middle ball intended for Rice was intercepted by J. Rodriguez.

What traveled anyway was the deep-ball file Arrowhead Pride has been tracking. After Kelce’s 48, Mahomes added a 17-yard shot to Tyquan Thornton, a 17-yarder to Xavier Worthy, a 24-yarder to Noah Gray, and a 34-yarder to Rice that set up the 11-yard touchdown and a 24-10 lead. Twenty completions on 24 attempts with a 119.8 rating is not a scramble-drill tape. It is a quarterback who had answers when the run game topped out at 88 team rushing yards.

Spagnuolo won the takeaway math and lost the clock. Willis went 20-of-36 for 210 net yards, scrambled for chunk gains, and handed Ollie Gordon II 17 rushes and three catches (20 touches) as Miami posted 119 rushing yards on 31 carries. The stop that mattered was the reversed-and-recovered fumble: Willis up the middle, L. Sneed forcing and recovering. Karlaftis later picked Willis short right. Kansas City still never sacked Willis, landed one quarterback hit, and spent the day tackling Gordon, Malik Washington (seven touches), and a nine-carry Willis keep game.

The penalty tape is the other thing Reid will not ignore. L. Sneed’s defensive holding wiped a Miami incompletion. R. Thomas’ offside wiped another run. Creed Humphrey’s illegal block wiped a play in the fourth. Karlaftis’ illegal use of hands wiped C. Roland-Wallace’s interception. You can win 24-10 on the road and still hand a 3-0 division opponent a full menu of how to keep drives alive.

What held up

  • Mahomes 20-of-24, 246 yards, two passing touchdowns, 119.8 rating, 0 sacks
  • Explosive shots: Kelce 48, Thornton 17, Worthy 17, Gray 24, Rice 34
  • Walker’s 10-yard touchdown with J. Moore eligible, plus 18 carries and 20 touches
  • Rice as the volume receiver (7-for-88) when Kelce was on a two-catch day
  • L. Sneed forcing and recovering Willis’ fumble; Karlaftis intercepting Willis
  • Bolton’s 11-tackle game as the tackling hub against Gordon and Willis

What broke

  • Team rushing stuck at 88 yards on 25 carries after Walker’s 70
  • Third down at 3-of-7; turnover on downs at the Miami 12
  • Possession at 25:39 against Miami’s 34:21 and 19 first downs
  • Pass rush: 0 sacks, 1 quarterback hit on Willis
  • Five quarterback hits on Mahomes with 0 sacks allowed
  • Mahomes’ interception to J. Rodriguez on the deep middle intended for Rice

Efficiency covered a thin run chart

Mahomes’ 20-of-24 and Rice’s seven catches for 88 yards produced 246 net passing yards and 334 total yards. Walker’s 18 carries for 70 and 20 touches were the volume, but the team still finished at 88 rushing yards on 25 attempts, so the next room has to be more than one back’s workload.

Takeaways, not the pass rush, closed Miami

Kansas City recorded 0 sacks and 1 quarterback hit. The defense still created two Miami turnovers — L. Sneed’s forced-and-recovered fumble and the Karlaftis interception — against one Mahomes interception. That is a takeaway win, not a pressure win.

The clock is the honest film

Miami ran 19 first downs, 8-of-16 third downs, 31 rushes, and 34:21 of possession on nine drives. Kansas City answered with 18 first downs, 3-of-7, 25:39, and nine drives. A 14-point road win that loses those fights is still a win. It is not a control tape to take into the West.

Current state

Where we stand

After the last kickoff: the work list, and the questions the building should be arguing.

3-0

Kansas City is 3-0 with wins over Denver (31-10), Indianapolis (33-30), and Miami (24-10), and the live identity is still being argued in the building: explosive enough to win on the road at 20-of-24, not yet sturdy enough to keep the ball for 25:39 and call it control. Week 4 at Las Vegas is where that argument gets a division grade.

What we need to work on

Third down and the early-down run

A 3-of-7 third-down day and 88 team rushing yards will not travel against Crosby and a 3-0 Raiders front. Bieniemy has to get Walker downhill on early downs so Mahomes is not living in 20-of-24 hero ball to stay on schedule.

Pass-rush math after 0 sacks

One quarterback hit and zero sacks against Willis is the wrong tape to carry into Kirk Cousins, who will throw on time if the interior does not move. Spagnuolo needs a plan that actually arrives, not a hope that another fumble bails out a quiet rush.

Possession without blending last week’s clock

The Colts game was 37:00 of Kansas City football. The Miami game was 25:39. Those are not the same offense. If the Chiefs let Las Vegas do what Miami did — 30-plus rushes and a long clock — Cousins will find the tight ends and the favorite number will look thin.

Tackle and chip rules vs. Crosby

Kahlil Benson has started all three games at left tackle and just took Offensive Rookie of the Month, but Allegiant is a different assignment. The staff has to decide how much help he gets without stripping the run game of a hat.

What to think about

Control team or takeaway team?

Miami held the ball 34:21 and still lost 24-10 because Kansas City took it away twice. That works until it does not. A 3-0 club that just posted 18 first downs and 3-of-7 on third down has to decide whether the next script is about keeping Cousins on the sideline or hunting him.

Worthy’s two-catch game

Xavier Worthy had two receptions, one of them a 17-yard shot, on a day Rice saw seven targets land. Arrowhead Addict is already calling the slow start impossible to ignore. Is that a scheme choice behind Rice, Kelce, Gray, and Thornton, or a usage problem Reid has to answer this week?

How much simulated pressure vs. Cousins

Spagnuolo’s accountability message is public after two stressful defensive tapes. The call sheet vs. Cousins should not pretend the Miami film was a pressure tape. It was a takeaway tape. The question is whether the zone-blitz menu is there to rush the interior or just to change pictures.

Walker vs. the Raiders number

Walker leads the NFL in rushing through three weeks and just took AFC Offensive Player of the Month, but Arrowhead Addict notes no Chiefs back has reached 70 rushing yards against the Raiders in a Mahomes start. His 70 in Miami was individual; the team still had 88. Allegiant is where that drought either ends or defines the week.

Looking ahead

The next-game plan

How they match up, what Kansas City should script, and the keys that decide it.

Las Vegas Raiders

Week 4 at Las Vegas is 3-0 versus 3-0 on Sun Oct 4, 3:25 PM CT, CBS, with first place in the AFC West on the floor. Kansas City is a 69.6 percent ESPN FPI favorite and -4.5 at DraftKings on a 47.5 total — a real road-favorite price against a Raiders club that has already transformed itself into the Chiefs’ biggest early test.

How they match up

Mahomes’ deep-ball file (Kelce’s 48, Rice’s 34, Gray’s 24) meets a Raiders defense that will dare Kansas City to run into Crosby. Walker’s volume (20 touches, 70 rush yards in Miami) meets a historical drought against this franchise. On the other side, Cousins is exceeding early expectations, Linderbaum has the Raiders offensive line playing like a different unit, and Spagnuolo’s secondary is about to see its toughest tight-end test of the young season. Kansas City’s pass rush just posted 0 sacks and 1 hit; Las Vegas’ interior is the place to hunt if that changes.

Run Walker into the teeth of the drought

Do not ask Mahomes to go 20-of-24 again to cover 88 team rushing yards. Inside zone and early-down volume for Walker — 18 carries is the template, 70 individual yards is the floor you actually want to beat against this opponent — keep Crosby from pinning his ears back and keep Cousins on the sideline.

Attack the interior, don’t just rush the edge

The Raiders’ line has taken a leap with Linderbaum, and the published counter is still there: Kansas City can attack the interior. Jones, Karlaftis, and the second-level add-ons have to win the A-gap pictures so Cousins cannot live on tight-end sitters. This is a call sheet, not a hope that another Sneed fumble appears.

Two-deep answers for the tight ends

Arrowhead Addict framed this as the defense’s toughest tight-end test yet. Cover-2, with Bolton and the safeties rallying, is how you keep Cousins from splitting the field and turning this into a 19-first-down tracking game like Miami.

Move Mahomes after five hits

Miami did not sack him; it did record five quarterback hits. Boot and play-action movement, plus Rice on the move, give Benson help without turning every snap into a six-man protection that concedes the run.

  1. Open with under-center inside zone to Walker and a real hat for Benson, not an empty-set confession against Crosby.
  2. Tag a four-verticals shot off a run look — Kelce on the seam, Rice or Worthy on the boundary — to cash the deep-ball form from Miami.
  3. Install mesh vs. man as the third-down answer so Rice (seven catches last game) and Kelce are not waiting on late-down hero ball after a 3-of-7 tape.
  4. Boot Mahomes off play-action to the edge so the five-hit interior from Miami does not become Crosby’s tape.
  5. Live in Cover-2 vs. 12 personnel; make Cousins throw underneath to tight ends and rally with Bolton.
  6. Simulated pressure / zone blitz at the interior on early downs — drop a lineman, bring a second-level hat — as a picture-changer, not as a takeaway claim.

The story so far

Always looking ahead

The Chiefs left Hard Rock Stadium 3-0 with a 24-10 win that was cleaner on the scoreboard than it was on the clock. Patrick Mahomes went 20-of-24 for 246 yards, two touchdowns, and one interception, Rashee Rice caught seven balls for 88 yards, and Kenneth Walker III carried 18 times for 70 yards and the 10-yard score 2:06 into the game. Miami still won the possession fight at 34:21 to 25:39, edged first downs 19-18, and converted 8-of-16 third downs against Kansas City’s 3-of-7. That is the film that actually travels to Las Vegas.

Steve Spagnuolo’s defense did not sack Malik Willis, and it recorded one quarterback hit, but it took the ball twice: L. Sneed forced and recovered a Willis fumble, and Karlaftis intercepted a short throw intended for Ryan Miller. Those takeaways, plus Mahomes’ efficiency, covered a run game that finished at 88 yards on 25 carries and an offense that needed a 34-yard Butker field goal and an 11-yard touchdown in the fourth quarter to put the game away. Miami’s 119 rushing yards and 31 carries were a warning, not a trivia line.

A week earlier against Indianapolis, Kansas City had posted 29 first downs, 523 total yards, 11 drives, and 37:00 of possession in a 33-30 overtime win. Kansas City’s Miami box is a different animal: 18 first downs, 334 total yards, nine drives, and 25:39. Eric Bieniemy’s room cannot treat 20-of-24 as a substitute for early-down juice, not with Maxx Crosby across the line and another 3-0 AFC West team waiting.

The next Sunday is the first true West measuring stick of 2026. Kirk Cousins has the Raiders offense playing above the old script, Tyler Linderbaum has changed the interior, and Las Vegas’ tight ends are the coverage test Spagnuolo has not seen yet. ESPN FPI at 69.6 percent and Chiefs -4.5 on 47.5 is a road-favorite number, not a bye. Sole possession of first place in the division is on the table at Allegiant on Sun Oct 4, 3:25 PM CT.

The X’s & O’s

Concepts drawn up on the board — what it is, why it works, and the coaching point.

Offense

Under-center inside zone

Situation 1st-and-10, opening series vs. Las Vegas, 21 personnel with Walker in the backfield

Walker just carried 18 times for 70 and a 10-yard score; the team still finished at 88 rushing yards. You need downhill zone to start so Crosby cannot pin his ears and so Cousins sits.

Coaching point Reid and Bieniemy: zone steps, one cut, Walker through the first hat. Benson and Humphrey have to win the combination without asking Mahomes to bail out another 25:39 possession day.

Offense

Four verticals vs. Cover 3

Situation 2nd-and-medium after a Walker run, empty or 11 personnel vs. Las Vegas two-high rotate to three

Mahomes’ deep middle to Kelce went 48 yards in Miami, Rice took one 34 yards, and the deep ball is the 2026 differentiator. Stress the hashes so the Raiders cannot load the box for Crosby.

Coaching point Kelce bends the seam to the far hash; Rice and Worthy take the corners; Mahomes holds the safety with his eyes. Do not check it down early just because Miami recorded five hits.

Offense

Mesh vs. man

Situation 3rd-and-6 to 8 vs. Las Vegas, the down Kansas City just went 3-of-7

Rice caught seven balls for 88 when the third-down tape went 3-of-7. Rub-crossers give Mahomes an answer without needing another 20-of-24 perfect day to stay on the field.

Coaching point Rice and Kelce cross at six yards and do not pick up feet; Gray sits; Walker is the outlet. Bieniemy: if they press, throw the first open crosser and end the third-down drought.

Offense

Play-action boot

Situation 1st-and-10 vs. Las Vegas after a run look, move Mahomes away from Crosby’s edge

Miami did not sack Mahomes and still recorded five quarterback hits. Boot gets him on the perimeter, gives Benson a simpler set, and puts Gray or Kelce on an easy leak vs. a crashing Raiders front.

Coaching point Sell Walker. Mahomes gets depth and width. Gray leaks, Rice over the ball, Worthy late on the deep comeback. Throw on time — this is not a scramble drill.

Defense

Cover-2 shell vs. 12 personnel

Situation 2nd-and-7, Raiders 12 personnel, Cousins looking for the tight end in the hole

This is the tight-end test the last two tapes did not give Spagnuolo. Two deep, five under, Bolton rallies. Take away the shot and make Cousins dink into tackling, not 19-first-down tracking.

Coaching point Safeties split the posts. Corners squat and pass off. Bolton and Tranquill drive on the in-breaker. Spagnuolo: no free access to the window Kelce just hit for 48 in Miami.

Defense

Simulated pressure / zone blitz

Situation 1st-and-10 vs. Cousins, show five, drop a lineman, rush a second-level hat at the interior

The Raiders’ line has leaped with Linderbaum, and the counter is still the interior. Use the picture to move Cousins’ launch point after a 0-sack, 1-hit Miami film — this is a call, not a takeaway claim.

Coaching point Jones drops or stabs; Karlaftis and a linebacker take the interior rush lane the PBP will actually name when it happens. Spagnuolo: change the picture, cover the tight end in the vacated hook, tackle.
